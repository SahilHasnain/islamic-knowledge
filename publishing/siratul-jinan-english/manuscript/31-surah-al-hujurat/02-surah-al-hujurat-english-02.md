# Surah Al-Hujurat: English Translation

## Ayat 49:8

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=8; ayatId=4668; tafseerId=null; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** فَضْلًا مِّنَ اللّٰهِ وَ نِعْمَةًؕ-وَ اللّٰهُ عَلِیْمٌ حَكِیْمٌ(8)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** (They love faith because of) the Benevolence and Favour of Allah; and Allah is All-Knowing, Ever Wise.

## Ayat 49:9

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 53691

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=9; ayatId=4669; tafseerId=53691; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** وَ اِنْ طَآىٕفَتٰنِ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِیْنَ اقْتَتَلُوْا فَاَصْلِحُوْا بَیْنَهُمَاۚ-فَاِنْۢ بَغَتْ اِحْدٰىهُمَا عَلَى الْاُخْرٰى فَقَاتِلُوا الَّتِیْ تَبْغِیْ حَتّٰى تَفِیْٓءَ اِلٰۤى اَمْرِ اللّٰهِۚ-فَاِنْ فَآءَتْ فَاَصْلِحُوْا بَیْنَهُمَا بِالْعَدْلِ وَ اَقْسِطُوْاؕ-اِنَّ اللّٰهَ یُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِیْنَ(9)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** And if two groups of Muslims fight each other, so (O Muslims) make peace between them. Then if one oppresses the other, so fight with that oppressing group, until it reverts to the command of Allah. Then if it reverts, so reconcile between them with justice, and act justly (in every issue); indeed, Allah loves the just ones.

The occasion of revelation was that the Noble Prophet ﷺ was travelling on a donkey and paused near a gathering of the Ansar. The donkey urinated, and Abdullah ibn Ubayy held his nose. Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn Rawahah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ said, “The urine of the donkey of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is more fragrant than your musk.” The two exchanged words after the Noble Prophet ﷺ departed, their peoples fought and the matter reached blows. When the Noble Prophet ﷺ learned of it, he returned and made peace, and this verse was revealed. (Jalalayn with Sawi, 5/1992–1993; Madarik, p. 1153; Ruh al-Bayan, 9/73–76, excerpted.)

If two groups of believers fight, the Muslims must reconcile them. If one transgresses and refuses reconciliation, they must fight the transgressing group in support of the oppressed until it returns to Allah Almighty’s command. When it returns, the two groups must be reconciled with justice, without destroying the transgressing group; the purpose is to bring it firmly back to the straight path. Justice must be observed in every matter, and Allah Almighty loves the just.

Five matters follow: (1) Fighting and disputing are sins, but both parties were called believers, showing that sin is not disbelief. (2) Reconciling Muslims is a Sunnah of the Noble Prophet ﷺ and a high form of worship. (3) One who opposes or fights the Islamic ruler because of a misunderstanding is not a disbeliever or fasiq, but a believer. (4) The Islamic ruler must fight rebels until they stop their rebellion. (5) This fighting is not jihad; the rebels’ property is not spoils and their captives are not made slaves. Their strength is broken and they are treated as brothers.

The Quran and hadith repeatedly command reconciliation. Allah Almighty says: **وَ اِنِ امْرَاَةٌ خَافَتْ مِنْۢ بَعْلِهَا نُشُوْزًا اَوْ اِعْرَاضًا فَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَیْهِمَاۤ اَنْ یُّصْلِحَا بَیْنَهُمَا صُلْحًاؕ-وَ الصُّلْحُ خَیْرٌ** (Al-Nisa: 128), meaning that if a woman fears her husband’s ill-treatment or aversion, there is no blame if they make reconciliation, and reconciliation is better. He says: **یَسْــٴَـلُوْنَكَ عَنِ الْاَنْفَالِؕ -قُلِ الْاَنْفَالُ لِلّٰهِ وَ الرَّسُوْلِۚ-فَاتَّقُوا اللّٰهَ وَ اَصْلِحُوْا ذَاتَ بَیْنِكُمْ** (Al-Anfal: 1), commanding mutual reconciliation. He says: **لَا خَیْرَ فِیْ كَثِیْرٍ مِّنْ نَّجْوٰىهُمْ اِلَّا مَنْ اَمَرَ بِصَدَقَةٍ اَوْ مَعْرُوْفٍ اَوْ اِصْلَاحٍۭ بَیْنَ النَّاسِ** (Al-Nisa: 114), promising a great reward to one who reconciles people for Allah’s pleasure.

Sayyidatuna Umm Kulthum bint Uqbah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا reports that the Chief of the Messengers ﷺ said: “The one who reconciles people is not a liar: he conveys or says good.” (Bukhari, Kitab al-Sulh, 2/210, hadith 2692.) Sayyiduna Abu Darda رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ reports that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ asked whether he should tell them of an action better in rank than fasting, prayer and charity. When they said yes, he said: “Reconciling relations with one another.” (Abu Dawud, 4/365, hadith 4919.)

Reconciliation is permitted only within the limits of the Shariah. A settlement which makes the unlawful lawful or the lawful unlawful is not permitted. Three divorces make a woman unlawful for her former husband; merely making peace cannot make the unlawful lawful. Those who encourage such a settlement share in the sin of the unlawful relationship. May Allah Almighty grant Muslims sound understanding and the ability to reconcile within the limits of the Shariah.

Islam protects rights and social peace by commanding people to stop an oppressor and by warning the oppressor. Sayyiduna Anas رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ reports: “Help your brother whether he is an oppressor or oppressed.” When asked how to help an oppressor, the Noble Prophet ﷺ said: “Stop him from oppression; that is helping him.” (Bukhari, 4/389, hadith 6952.) Sayyiduna Ali al-Murtadha کَرَّمَ اللہ تَعَالٰی وَجْہَہُ الْکَرِیْم reports: “Beware the supplication of the oppressed; he asks Allah Almighty for his right, and Allah Almighty does not withhold the right of one entitled to it.” (Shu‘ab al-Iman, 6/49, hadith 7464.) Sayyiduna Abu Hurayrah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ reports that whoever has wronged his Muslim brother in honour or anything else should seek his pardon today, before the Day when there will be no dinar or dirham: his good deeds will be taken according to his wrongdoing, and if he has no good deeds, the oppressed person’s sins will be placed upon him. (Bukhari, 2/128, hadith 2449; Mishkat, 2/235, hadith 5126.)

Two virtues of supporting the oppressed are mentioned: Sayyiduna Anas reports that Allah Almighty writes seventy-three kinds of forgiveness for the one who answers the plea of an oppressed person; one repairs all his affairs and seventy-two raise his rank on the Day of Judgement. (Shu‘ab al-Iman, 6/120, hadith 7670.) Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn Umar reports that whoever goes with an oppressed person to help him will be given firmness on the day when feet slip. (Hilyat al-Awliya, Malik ibn Anas, 6/383, hadith 9012.)

Eight matters about the Companions and their apparent disagreements are important: (1) No saint of the Ummah, from the Tabi‘in until the Day of Judgement, can reach the rank of even the least-ranking Companion. (2) The nearness which the Companions possess in the court of Allah Almighty and the high ranks they will receive are unavailable to other Muslims. (3) Ahl al-Sunnat regard all Companions as exceptionally righteous and pious and consider investigating the details of who did what to whom and why to be impermissible. (4) If an action is reported which seems low in the eyes of the short-sighted, scholars explain it favourably and attribute it to sincere intention, remembering Allah’s true declaration **رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ عَنْهُمْ** rather than filling the heart with suspicion.

(5) Their rank is beyond our reason, so we cannot judge their matters. We must not favour one by insulting another or call one party worldly; all sought the welfare of the religion and the elevation of Islam and Muslims. They were also mujtahids: each followed the judgement which appeared more beneficial for Allah’s religion, the Shariah of the Noble Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims. Even when a judgement was mistaken, all remain deserving of respect, just as the mujtahids of Ahl al-Sunnat differ in subsidiary matters. (6) Muslims must not call the Companions misguided or fasiq because of these disputes and must not become enemies of any of them. All were devoted servants of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, honoured in the courts of Allah Almighty and His Messenger ﷺ and shining stars of guidance. (7) They were not Prophets or angels, so they were not protected from every human slip, but objecting to them in a manner contrary to the commands of Allah Almighty and His Messenger ﷺ is forbidden. (8) Allah Almighty promised good to both groups of Companions: those who spent and fought before the conquest and those who spent and fought afterwards. The first group had fewer resources and endured greater hardship, so it has greater rank, but Allah promised good to both. He says: **كُلًّا وَّعَدَ اللّٰهُ الْحُسْنٰى**. He also says that they are kept far from Hell, do not hear even its sound, remain in what their souls desire, are not saddened by the greatest terror, and are welcomed by the angels: **اُولٰٓىٕكَ عَنْهَا مُبْعَدُوْنَ**, **لَا یَسْمَعُوْنَ حَسِیْسَهَا**, **وَ هُمْ فِیْ مَا اشْتَهَتْ اَنْفُسُهُمْ خٰلِدُوْنَ**, **لَا یَحْزُنُهُمُ الْفَزَعُ الْاَكْبَرُ**, **وَ تَتَلَقّٰىهُمُ الْمَلٰٓىٕكَةُ**, **هٰذَا یَوْمُكُمُ الَّذِیْ كُنْتُمْ تُوْعَدُوْنَ** (Al-Anbiya: 101–103). Allah Almighty knows what they did and nevertheless promised them Paradise without punishment and honour and reward without reckoning. (Fatawa Razawiyyah, 29/357–363, excerpted.)

## Ayat 49:10

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 53692

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=10; ayatId=4670; tafseerId=53692; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** اِنَّمَا الْمُؤْمِنُوْنَ اِخْوَةٌ فَاَصْلِحُوْا بَیْنَ اَخَوَیْكُمْ وَ اتَّقُوا اللّٰهَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُرْحَمُوْنَ۠    (10)ٝ

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** The Muslims are brothers (to each other), therefore make peace between your two brothers, and fear Allah, that you may attain mercy.

Muslims are brothers because they are joined by religious connection and Islamic love, and this bond is stronger than worldly relationships. When two brothers quarrel, reconcile them and fear Allah Almighty, so that you may receive mercy. Piety produces love and affection among believers, and Allah’s mercy is upon the one who fears Him. (Khazin, 4/168; Madarik, p. 1153, excerpted.)

Three hadith about Muslim brotherhood: (1) Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn Umar رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ reports that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: “A Muslim is the brother of a Muslim: he neither oppresses him nor disgraces him. Whoever remains occupied fulfilling his brother’s need, Allah fulfils his need. Whoever removes a hardship from a Muslim, Allah removes one of his hardships on the Day of Judgement. Whoever conceals a Muslim’s fault, Allah will conceal his fault on the Day of Judgement.” (Bukhari, 2/126, hadith 2442.) (2) Sayyiduna Nu‘man ibn Bashir رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ reports: “All Muslims are like one person: when his eye suffers, the whole body suffers, and when his head aches, the whole body aches.” (Muslim, p. 1396, hadith 67 (2586).) (3) Sayyiduna Abu Musa Ash‘ari رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ reports: “A Muslim is to another Muslim like a building whose one brick strengthens another.” (Muslim, p. 1396, hadith 65 (2585).) May Allah Almighty grant Muslims understanding of their mutual relationship and the ability to fulfil its requirements.

## Ayat 49:11

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 53693

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=11; ayatId=4671; tafseerId=53693; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** یٰۤاَیُّهَا الَّذِیْنَ اٰمَنُوْا لَا یَسْخَرْ قَوْمٌ مِّنْ قَوْمٍ عَسٰۤى اَنْ یَّكُوْنُوْا خَیْرًا مِّنْهُمْ وَ لَا نِسَآءٌ مِّنْ نِّسَآءٍ عَسٰۤى اَنْ یَّكُنَّ خَیْرًا مِّنْهُنَّۚ-وَ لَا تَلْمِزُوْۤا اَنْفُسَكُمْ وَ لَا تَنَابَزُوْا بِالْاَلْقَابِؕ-بِئْسَ الِاسْمُ الْفُسُوْقُ بَعْدَ الْاِیْمَانِۚ-وَ مَنْ لَّمْ یَتُبْ فَاُولٰٓىٕكَ هُمُ الظّٰلِمُوْنَ(11)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** O believers! Men must not ridicule other men, it is likely that the ridiculed are better than the mockers; nor must the women ridicule other women, it is likely that the ridiculed women may be better than the mockers; and do not taunt one another, nor give evil nicknames. What a bad name it is to be called an evildoer after being a Muslim. And whoever does not repent, it is they who are the unjust.

The parts of this verse were revealed on different occasions. Sayyiduna Thabit ibn Qays رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ had weak hearing, so the Companions made space for him near the Noble Prophet ﷺ. One day he came late, moved people aside saying “Make room,” and reached near the Noble Prophet ﷺ. One man told him to sit where he had found a place. Thabit became angry, sat behind him, and later asked his name. When he named himself, Thabit called him “son of such-and-such woman,” a phrase then used to shame people, and this verse was revealed. It was also revealed concerning people of Banu Tamim who mocked poor Companions such as Ammar, Khabbab, Bilal, Suhayb, Salman and Salim. Wealthy people must not mock the poor, the well-born those of another lineage, the healthy the disabled, or the sighted one whose eye has a defect; the mocked person may be better in sincerity before Allah Almighty. (Khazin, 4/169.)

The signs of poverty are not a reason to mock anyone; the poor person may be superior in religion. Sayyiduna Anas reports that many people with dishevelled, dusty hair and only two worn garments have such rank before Allah Almighty that if they swear by Allah, Allah fulfils their oath. (Tirmidhi, 5/459, hadith 3880.) Sayyiduna Harith ibn Wahb al-Khuza‘i reports that the people of Paradise include every weak person whom people consider low; if he swears by Allah, Allah fulfils his oath. (Tirmidhi, 4/272, hadith 2614.)

The women’s part was revealed, according to one report, concerning the purified wives of the Noble Prophet ﷺ when they shamed Sayyidatuna Umm Salamah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا for her short stature. Another report says it concerned Sayyidatuna Safiyyah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا when a purified wife called her the daughter of a Jew. Safiyyah wept; the Noble Prophet ﷺ told her that she was the daughter of a Prophet, the niece of a Prophet and the wife of a Prophet, and asked Sayyidatuna Hafsah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا to fear Allah Almighty. (Tirmidhi, 5/474, hadith 3920.) Women’s joking with one another is not absolutely forbidden, but it is permitted only when it contains no obscenity, harm, disrespect to elders, rudeness to younger people, inappropriateness of time or place, or excess. (Fatawa Razawiyyah, 23/194.)

Mocking a Muslim through words, gestures, imitation or any other means, with the purpose of humiliation, is unlawful and sinful, because it humiliates and harms a Muslim and leads to Hell. (Jahannam ke Khatrat, p. 173.) The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: “Do not quarrel with your brother, mock him, or make a promise to him which you will break.” (Tirmidhi, 3/400, hadith 2002.) Sayyidatuna Aishah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا reports that he did not like imitating anyone, even in exchange for great wealth. (Abu Dawud, 4/353, hadith 4875.) Sayyiduna Hasan reports that on the Day of Judgement a door of Paradise will be opened for the one who mocked people and he will be called to enter; when he reaches it, it will close, and this will continue until, when it opens and he is called, he will not go because of despair. (Mawsu‘at Ibn Abi Dunya, 7/183, hadith 287.)

Mockery includes exposing faults, imitating a person, taunting him, shaming him, laughing at him, and using hurtful names. Those who belittle poor people and use insulting titles because of wealth or social status must repent, or they will be fasiq and deserving of Hell. A harmless joke which makes a person happy is permitted and can sometimes be Sunnah. The Noble Prophet ﷺ sometimes joked but always spoke the truth. Imam Ghazali says that permissible joking must contain only truth, must not hurt anyone, must not exceed limits and must not become a profession; excessive and constant joking produces excessive laughter, hardens the heart, may create hatred, and removes dignity. The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: “I also joke, but I speak only the truth.” (Mu‘jam al-Awsat, 1/283, hadith 995.) Joking merely to make people laugh can cause a person to fall into Hell farther than the Pleiades. (Musnad Ahmad, 3/366, hadith 9231; Ihya, 3/158.)

Four examples of the Noble Prophet’s ﷺ gentle humour are mentioned. Sayyidatuna Umm Ayman رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا said that her husband was calling him. He asked, “Who, the one with whiteness in his eye?” She denied that her husband had it, so he explained that every eye has whiteness around its black centre. (Subul al-Huda, 7/114.) He used to mix with the people and said to Sayyiduna Anas’s young brother, “O Abu Umayr, what did the little bird do?” (Bukhari, 4/134, hadith 6129.) When a man asked for a riding animal, he said, “We will give you the child of a she-camel.” The man asked what he would do with a she-camel’s child, and the Noble Prophet ﷺ replied that every camel is born from a she-camel. (Tirmidhi, 3/399, hadith 1999.) He told an old woman that no old woman would enter Paradise; when she became anxious, he recited **اِنَّاۤ اَنْشَاْنٰهُنَّ اِنْشَآءًۙ(۳۵) فَجَعَلْنٰهُنَّ اَبْكَارًا** (Al-Waqi‘ah: 35–36), meaning: “Indeed, We have created the women of Paradise in a special manner, and made them virgins.” (Mishkat, 2/200, hadith 4888.)

Taunting is condemned. The Noble Prophet ﷺ said that those who frequently curse will be neither witnesses nor intercessors on the Day of Judgement, and that a believer is not one who taunts, curses, speaks obscenely or uses foul language. (Muslim, p. 1400, hadith 85 (2598); Tirmidhi, 3/393, hadith 1984.) Giving evil nicknames includes shaming a person after he has repented from a sin, calling a Muslim a dog, donkey or pig, and using a title which reveals his defect and which he dislikes. True honourable titles such as Siddiq’s ‘Atiq, Umar’s Faruq, Uthman’s Dhu al-Nurayn, Ali’s Abu Turab and Khalid’s Sayf Allah are not forbidden; nor are established names such as A‘mash and A‘raj when their bearers do not dislike them. (Khazin, 4/170.) Calling oneself a fasiq after Islam by mocking, fault-finding or corrupting names is an evil name, and those who do not repent are unjust.

Three rulings follow: (1) No group of Muslims is contemptible; every believer has honour. (2) Greatness depends not merely on lineage but on piety. (3) Insulting a Muslim brother through lineage is unlawful and the way of the polytheists; this disease is widespread, especially among women, and they should learn that the one who is better before Allah Almighty is unknown to them.

## Ayat 49:12

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 53694

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=12; ayatId=4672; tafseerId=53694; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** یٰۤاَیُّهَا الَّذِیْنَ اٰمَنُوا اجْتَنِبُوْا كَثِیْرًا مِّنَ الظَّنِّ٘-اِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ اِثْمٌ وَّ لَا تَجَسَّسُوْا وَ لَا یَغْتَبْ بَّعْضُكُمْ بَعْضًاؕ-اَیُحِبُّ اَحَدُكُمْ اَنْ یَّاْكُلَ لَحْمَ اَخِیْهِ مَیْتًا فَكَرِهْتُمُوْهُؕ-وَ اتَّقُوا اللّٰهَؕ-اِنَّ اللّٰهَ تَوَّابٌ رَّحِیْمٌ(12)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** O believers! Avoid much suspicions; indeed, some suspicions become sin. And do not search for (hidden) faults, do not backbite one another; would any one amongst you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother? So, you will not tolerate this. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is the Great Acceptor of repentance, Ever Merciful.

Believers must avoid excessive suspicion, because some suspicion is sin. Suspicion may be obligatory, recommended, forbidden or permissible: good opinion of Allah Almighty is obligatory; good opinion of a righteous believer is recommended; bad opinion of Allah Almighty or a believer is forbidden; and judging an openly sinful person according to his known conduct is permissible. Sayyiduna Sufyan Thawri رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ said that one suspicion is expressed in the heart and on the tongue, and is sinful when it is evil towards a Muslim; another arises in the heart without being spoken, which is not itself a sin but should still be removed. Imam Razi explains that suspicion causes fault-finding and other evil deeds, while an apparently bad action may have a valid explanation. (Ibn Kathir, 7/352; Tafsir Kabir, 10/110; Baydawi, 5/218; Khazin, 4/170–171.)

Bad opinion of a Muslim is unlawful unless there is legal proof. As far as possible, his words and deeds must be interpreted correctly. If a clear indication establishes the suspected matter, it is no longer sinful to hold that view; one who repeatedly enters a place of accusation is responsible for exposing himself to suspicion. A righteous believer’s words must not be given a corrupt meaning when a sound meaning exists and his condition supports it. (Fatawa Razawiyyah, 6/486 and 20/278; Fatawa Amjadiyyah, 1/123; Khazain al-Irfan, p. 950.)

The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: “Avoid suspicion, for suspicion is the worst of lies. Do not search for one another’s outward or inward faults, do not be greedy, jealous or hateful, and do not turn away from one another; become brothers, O servants of Allah.” (Muslim, p. 1386, hadith 28 (2563).) Suspicion causes four religious harms: it may hurt the person if expressed before him; expressing it in his absence becomes backbiting; it leads to searching for faults; and it produces hatred and jealousy. Its worldly harms include enmity between brothers, hostility between mother-in-law and daughter-in-law, loss of trust and eventual divorce between spouses, broken sibling relationships, and the destruction of a peaceful home. A report cited in the source says that hostile thoughts, mental stress and anger increase the risk of heart disease and stroke by 86 percent. Imam Ghazali says that even if the smell of wine comes from someone, one must not punish him merely on that basis, because he may have rinsed his mouth after one sip or may have been forced to drink it; do not make Satan happy by confirming suspicion in the heart. (Ihya Ulum al-Din, 3/186, excerpted.)

Do not spy or search for hidden faults which Allah Almighty has concealed. The Noble Prophet ﷺ warned those whose tongues profess faith but whose hearts do not contain it: whoever searches out his Muslim brother’s hidden fault, Allah Almighty will search out his hidden fault and disgrace him, even inside his home. (Abu Dawud, 4/354, hadith 4880.) Searching for faults is a trait of hypocrites and ends in disgrace; whoever searches for another’s fault should fear that Allah Almighty may expose his own hidden faults.

Two virtues of concealing faults are mentioned: whoever conceals a Muslim’s fault, Allah conceals his faults on the Day of Judgement; and whoever sees something which should be concealed and conceals it is like one who gives life to a buried infant girl. (Bukhari, 2/126, hadith 2442; Abu Dawud, 4/357, hadith 4819.) A person should search for and correct his own faults rather than those of others. The Noble Prophet ﷺ said that a person sees a splinter in his brother’s eye while forgetting the branch in his own eye. Sayyiduna Ibn Abbas said that when intending to mention a companion’s fault, remember your own faults. (Shu‘ab al-Iman, 5/311, ahadith 6761 and 6758.)

Islam greatly values human honour, especially the honour of a Muslim. It forbids seeking out faults and displaying them without a Shariah-permitted need. Backbiting is to mention, in a person’s absence, a defect which he dislikes, even if it is true; if it is false, it is slander. The dead brother’s flesh is used as an example because cutting flesh causes pain just as disparaging a person causes pain to his heart, while honour is dearer than flesh. The occasion of revelation involved Sayyiduna Salman رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ, who had been assigned to serve two wealthy men. When he slept and could not prepare food, they sent him to the Noble Prophet’s ﷺ kitchen; Sayyiduna Usamah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ said he had no food. The two men said that Usamah had been miserly. When they came to the Noble Prophet ﷺ, he said, “I see the colour of meat in your mouths.” They said they had eaten no meat, and he said, “You backbit your brother; whoever backbites a Muslim eats his flesh.” (Khazin, 4/170–171, excerpted.)

Four hadith condemn backbiting. The Noble Prophet ﷺ defined it as mentioning about one’s brother what he dislikes; if the defect exists, it is backbiting, and if it does not exist, it is slander. (Muslim, p. 1397, hadith 70 (2589).) He said that backbiting is more severe than adultery: an adulterer may repent and Allah accepts his repentance, but the backbiter is not forgiven until the wronged person forgives him. (Shu‘ab al-Iman, 5/306, hadith 6741.) During the Ascension he saw people with copper nails tearing their faces and chests; Jibril عَلَیْہِ السَّلَام said that they were those who ate people’s flesh and violated their honour. (Abu Dawud, 4/353, hadith 4878.) Whoever ate his brother’s flesh in the world will be brought his brother’s flesh on the Day of Judgement and told to eat living flesh as he ate dead flesh; he will eat it screaming and distorting his face. (Mu‘jam al-Awsat, 1/450, hadith 1656.)

Backbiting can occur by speech, action, clear statement, insinuation, writing, head movements, eyes, lips, eyebrows, tongue or hands. Imitating a lame person’s walk or another person’s manner is also backbiting and may be worse because it vividly conveys the defect. Mentioning a deceased Muslim’s defect is backbiting, while insulting a protected non-Muslim is also impermissible; insulting a hostile disbeliever is not backbiting. Criticising someone to his face is even more unlawful when it harms him. Whoever hears backbiting must reject it verbally; if he fears harm, he must reject it in his heart, leave the gathering if possible, or change the subject, otherwise he shares the sin. (Bahar-e-Shariat, part 16, 3/532, 536–538.)

If the person who was backbitten learns of it, the backbiter must seek his pardon and specify what he said, then repent. If he did not learn of it, repentance and regret are sufficient. Scholars differ on the case where the person learns after repentance; in either case, slander requires repentance and correcting the falsehood before those who heard it. Seeking pardon should include sincere praise and showing affection to remove the pain from the person’s heart. If the apology was merely outward and insincere, there may still be accountability. If the wronged person has died or is absent, Imam Ghazali advises performing many good deeds, so that even if they are transferred for the backbiting, some remain. See also the books *Badgumani* and *Ghibat ki Tabahkariyan*. ([1], [2])
