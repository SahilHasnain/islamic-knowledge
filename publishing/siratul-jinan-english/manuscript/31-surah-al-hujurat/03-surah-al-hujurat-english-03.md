# Surah Al-Hujurat: English Translation

## Ayat 49:3

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 53686

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=3; ayatId=4663; tafseerId=53686; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** اِنَّ الَّذِیْنَ یَغُضُّوْنَ اَصْوَاتَهُمْ عِنْدَ رَسُوْلِ اللّٰهِ اُولٰٓىٕكَ الَّذِیْنَ امْتَحَنَ اللّٰهُ قُلُوْبَهُمْ لِلتَّقْوٰىؕ-لَهُمْ مَّغْفِرَةٌ وَّ اَجْرٌ عَظِیْمٌ(3)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** Indeed, those who lower their voices in the august court of Allah's (Beloved) Messenger, they are the ones whose hearts Allah has tested for piety; for them is forgiveness and great reward.

{Indeed, those who lower their voices in the august court of Allah's Messenger:} When the verse, “O believers! Do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet” was revealed, Sayyiduna Abu Bakr Siddiq, Sayyiduna Umar Farooq and some other Companions رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمْ adopted great caution and spoke very softly in the august presence of the Sovereign of both worlds ﷺ, as mentioned above. This verse was revealed concerning them, and their conduct was praised: Indeed, those who lower their voices in the august court of Allah Almighty’s Messenger ﷺ out of respect and reverence are the very people whose hearts Allah Almighty has tested for piety and made the piety within them apparent; for them there is forgiveness and a great reward in the Hereafter. (Jalalayn with Sawi, Al-Hujurat, under verse 3, 5/1987–1988.)

Five lessons from the verse “Indeed, those who lower their voices”:

(1) All other acts of worship are the piety of the body, while showing respect to the Noble Prophet ﷺ is the piety of the heart.

(2) Allah Almighty has tested the hearts of the Companions رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمْ for piety. Therefore, the person who, Allah forbid, considers them sinful is a denier of this verse.

(3) The Companions رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمْ were extremely pious and greatly fearful of Allah Almighty. Whoever believed the Noble Prophet ﷺ to be Allah Almighty’s Messenger and honoured him so greatly that he would not even raise his voice before him out of fear that his deeds might be ruined by raising his voice, must have had immense reverence and fear of Allah Almighty in his heart.

(4) The forgiveness of Sayyiduna Abu Bakr Siddiq and Sayyiduna Umar Farooq رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمَا is certain, because Allah Almighty has announced their forgiveness.

(5) The reward of these two revered personalities is beyond our imagination, because Allah Almighty has described it as great.

The rank of Sayyiduna Thabit رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ:

Despite his disability, Sayyiduna Thabit رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ made it binding upon himself that he would never raise his voice above the voice of the Noble Prophet ﷺ. Sayyiduna Anas رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ says: We used to see a man from the people of Paradise walking before us. When the battle against Musaylimah took place at Yamamah, Sayyiduna Thabit رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ saw that a group of Muslims had been defeated and said, “How regrettable these people are!” He then said to Sayyiduna Salim رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ, the freed slave of Sayyiduna Hudhayfah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ, “We did not fight the enemies of Allah in this manner alongside the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.” They both stood firm and continued fighting until they were martyred.

After Sayyiduna Thabit رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ was martyred, a Companion saw him in a dream. He said, “Such-and-such a person removed my armour and placed it beneath a stone cooking pot near a horse at the corner of the army camp. Go to Sayyiduna Khalid ibn Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ and inform him so that he may recover my armour. Then go to the Caliph of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, Sayyiduna Abu Bakr Siddiq رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ, and tell him that I have a debt, so that he may pay it, and that such-and-such of my slaves is free.” The Companion informed Sayyiduna Khalid ibn Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ, who found the armour and the horse exactly as Sayyiduna Thabit رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ had described. He took the armour and informed Sayyiduna Abu Bakr Siddiq رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ of the dream. Sayyiduna Abu Bakr Siddiq رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ carried out Sayyiduna Thabit’s bequest.

Sayyiduna Malik ibn Anas رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمَا says: I do not know of any bequest, other than this one, that was carried out after the death of its maker. (Sawi, Al-Hujurat, under verse 3, 5/1988.)

## Ayat 49:4

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 53687

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=4; ayatId=4664; tafseerId=53687; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** اِنَّ الَّذِیْنَ یُنَادُوْنَكَ مِنْ وَّرَآءِ الْحُجُرٰتِ اَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا یَعْقِلُوْنَ(4)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** Indeed, those who call you from outside the rooms (of your private residence), most of them do not have any sense (regarding your lofty status).

The occasion of revelation was that several people from Banu Tamim came to serve the Noble Prophet ﷺ at midday, while the Noble Prophet ﷺ was resting. They began calling him from outside the rooms. The Noble Prophet ﷺ came out, and this verse was revealed concerning them. It described the exalted rank of the Chief of the Messengers ﷺ and declared that calling him in this manner in his august court was ignorance and lack of sense. (Madarik, Al-Hujurat, under verse 4, p. 1151, excerpted.)

## Ayat 49:5

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 53688

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=5; ayatId=4665; tafseerId=53688; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** وَ لَوْ اَنَّهُمْ صَبَرُوْا حَتّٰى تَخْرُ جَ اِلَیْهِمْ لَكَانَ خَیْرًا لَّهُمْؕ-وَ اللّٰهُ غَفُوْرٌ رَّحِیْمٌ(5)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** And had they been patient until you yourself would come out (from your rooms) to them, so it would have been better for them. And Allah is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful.

{And had they been patient:} This verse teaches those people manners: instead of calling the Noble Prophet ﷺ, they should have exercised patience and waited until the Noble Prophet ﷺ himself came out of the sacred room to them and then they presented their request. Had they observed this obligatory etiquette, it would have been better for them. If those from whom this disrespect occurred repent, Allah Almighty is Forgiving towards them and Merciful to them. (Khazin, Al-Hujurat, under verse 5, 4/166; Ruh al-Bayan, Al-Hujurat, under verse 5, 9/68, excerpted.)

An etiquette when visiting scholars and teachers:

This verse indicates that when one visits the close servants of Allah Almighty and practising scholars, one should not show haste by knocking at the door of their residence. Rather, one should wait so that they may come out according to their usual routine. This was the practice of our righteous predecessors. The eminent scholar Sayyiduna Abu Ubayd رَحْمَۃُ اللّٰهِ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْهِ used to say: I never knocked at the door of any teacher. I continued waiting for him, and when he himself came out, I benefited from him.

Similarly, when Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn Abbas رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمَا went to the home of Sayyiduna Ubayy ibn Ka'b رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ to learn the Holy Quran from him, he would stand near the door and would not knock. Rather, he would silently wait until Sayyiduna Ubayy ibn Ka'b came out according to his usual routine. This conduct displeased Sayyiduna Ubayy ibn Ka'b, so he said to him, “Why did you not knock at the door, so that I could come out immediately and you would not have had to bear the difficulty of waiting?” Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn Abbas رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمَا replied, “A scholar among his people is like a Prophet among his nation,” meaning that a scholar is the heir of a Prophet. Since Allah Almighty said regarding His Noble Prophet ﷺ, “And had they been patient until you yourself would come out to them, so it would have been better for them,” I therefore waited for you to come out yourself instead of knocking at the door.

Imam Alusi رَحْمَۃُ اللّٰهِ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْهِ describes his own practice: I had read this account during childhood, and thereafter I continued to deal with my teachers in this manner throughout my life. (Ruh al-Ma‘ani, Al-Hujurat, under verse 5, 13/412.)

Two lessons from the verse “And had they been patient”:

(1) It is also known that Allah Almighty established and taught the etiquette of the august court of the radiant Prophet ﷺ. These manners do not apply only to human beings; they apply to jinn, human beings and angels alike. Nor are they limited to a particular time; they remain forever.

(2) Showing respect in the court of the elders takes a servant to high ranks and grants him the blessings of this world and the Hereafter.

## Ayat 49:6

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 53689

**Metadata:** surah=49; ayat=6; ayatId=4666; tafseerId=53689; para=26; volume=9

**Arabic:** یٰۤاَیُّهَا الَّذِیْنَ اٰمَنُوْۤا اِنْ جَآءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌۢ بِنَبَاٍ فَتَبَیَّنُوْۤا اَنْ تُصِیْبُوْا قَوْمًۢا بِجَهَالَةٍ فَتُصْبِحُوْا عَلٰى مَا فَعَلْتُمْ نٰدِمِیْنَ(6)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** O believers! If any sinner brings you some news, so you must investigate (it) in case you unknowingly cause suffering to some people then (later on) remain regretful for what you have done.

{O believers! If any sinner brings you some news, so you must investigate:} Allah Almighty says: O believers! If a fasiq brings you some report containing a complaint against someone, do not rely only upon his statement; rather, investigate whether it is true or not. The one who does not refrain from fisq will not refrain from lying either. Investigate, lest you unknowingly cause harm to a people and then, when their innocence becomes clear, have to regret what you did.

The commentators have stated this occasion of revelation: The Noble Prophet ﷺ sent Sayyiduna Walid ibn Uqbah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ to collect charity from Banu Mustaliq. There had been enmity between them in the era of ignorance. When Sayyiduna Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ came near their territory and the people learned of his arrival, many of them came out to welcome him, thinking that he had been sent by the Noble Prophet ﷺ and wishing to honour him. Sayyiduna Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ, however, thought that they had come to kill him because of the old enmity. He therefore returned and reported to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, according to his assumption, “They have refused to give charity and intend to kill me.” The Noble Prophet ﷺ sent Sayyiduna Khalid ibn Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ to investigate the situation. Sayyiduna Khalid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ saw that the people were calling the Adhan, performing prayer and had presented their charity. He brought the charity to the august presence and related what had happened. Thereupon this noble verse was revealed. (Khazin, Al-Hujurat, under verse 6, 4/166–167; Madarik, Al-Hujurat, under verse 6, p. 1152; Ruh al-Bayan, Al-Hujurat, under verse 6, 9/70–71, excerpted.)

Sayyiduna Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ was a Companion, and none of the Companions is a fasiq:

It should be remembered that this verse does not specifically call Sayyiduna Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ a fasiq. Rather, it states an Islamic law. Therefore, he cannot be called a fasiq on the basis of this verse. Allamah Ahmad Sawi رَحْمَۃُ اللّٰهِ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْهِ says: Although the incident of Sayyiduna Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ was the cause of this verse’s revelation, the word fasiq does not specifically refer to him, because he was not a fasiq but a great Companion. (Sawi, Al-Hujurat, under verse 6, 5/1990–1991, excerpted.)

Imam Fakhr al-Din Razi رَحْمَۃُ اللّٰهِ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْهِ says: In view of this verse, applying the term fasiq to Sayyiduna Walid رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ is a grave error, because he made an assumption and erred in it, and the one who makes a mistake is not called a fasiq. (Tafsir Kabir, Al-Hujurat, under verse 6, 10/98.)

The summary is that although the incident of Sayyiduna Walid ibn Uqbah رَضِیَ اللّٰهُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ was indeed the cause of this verse’s revelation, the verse does not specifically call him a fasiq. This is because no action had issued from him, in this incident or before it, which would justify calling him a fasiq. In this incident too, he attributed something to Banu Mustaliq because he believed, according to his assumption, that it was true, although it was actually incorrect. This is not something for which a person can be declared a fasiq.

Islam’s role in making society a cradle of peace:

This verse teaches that Islam prevents actions which obstruct social peace and commands actions through which society becomes a cradle of peace and tranquillity. If we keep the principle stated in this verse before us today, our society can become a cradle of peace. Fights and disorder occur among us for this very reason: when someone receives information, he does not verify it but immediately becomes angry and does something which leaves him troubled for the rest of his life. The family disputes among us are of the same nature, whether they concern a mother-in-law and daughter-in-law or a husband and wife: the matter is not verified and fighting begins.

Lessons from the verse “If any sinner brings you some news”:

(1) If a person is upright, his report is reliable.

(2) A ruler must not decide on the basis of one-sided testimony; he should decide only after hearing both parties.

(3) The statement of a backbiter and tale-bearer must never be accepted.

(4) One should not hurry in any matter, otherwise one may have to regret it later.
