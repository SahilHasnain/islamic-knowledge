# Surah An-Naas: English Translation

## Ayat 114:1

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54500
**Metadata:** surah=114; ayat=1; ayatId=6344; tafseerId=54500; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** قُلْ اَعُوْذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِۙ    (1)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** Say you (O Beloved), 'I take refuge in the One Who is the Lord of all the people.'

{قُلْ اَعُوْذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ: Say you (O Beloved), 'I take refuge in the One Who is the Lord of all the people.'} Allah Almighty is the Lord of all creation, but since man is the noblest of creation, he has been mentioned specifically. (Khazin, An-Naas, under verse 1, 4/430.)

### The Greatness and Nobility of Man

This also reveals the greatness and nobility of man: Allah Almighty has especially related His attribute of Lordship to him. The scholars have stated a point of wisdom here: the word “اَلنَّاسِ” occurs five times in this Surah. The wisdom is that, since a person receives nourishment and upbringing alone during childhood, the attribute of Lordship, “رَبِّ النَّاسِ”, meaning the attribute of nurturing, is mentioned first. When a person reaches youth, he becomes intoxicated with himself and strays from the way; at that time, legal restraint is needed, so “مَلِكِ النَّاسِ”, meaning the King of the people, is mentioned here. Since a person becomes occupied with worship in old age, the attribute of Allah Almighty’s Divinity and deserving of worship is mentioned in the third place, namely “اِلٰهِ النَّاسِ”. In the fourth place, “النَّاسِ” may refer to the righteous, because Satan generally tries to turn them away from worship through evil whispers. In the fifth place, “النَّاسِ” may refer to mischievous and corrupt people, because refuge from the evil of people is sought there.

{مَلِكِ النَّاسِ: The King of all the people.} That is, He is the One Who manages their affairs and is the true Sovereign and Owner of all. If anyone in the world receives government or ownership, he receives it only through His granting.

{اِلٰهِ النَّاسِ: The God of all the people.} Being worthy of worship is exclusive to Him, and He alone is the true God of all people.

{مِنْ شَرِّ الْوَسْوَاسِ ﳔ الْخَنَّاسِ: From the evil of the one who repeatedly inculcates evil thoughts and withdraws.} This refers to Satan. It is his habit that, when a person is heedless, he puts evil thoughts into his heart; and when a person remembers Allah Almighty, Satan cowers and withdraws.

### The Difference Between Evil Whispering and Inspiration

Remember that a bad thought is called a whisper, while good thoughts are called inspiration. Whispering comes from Satan, so one should recite لَا حَوْل upon it. Inspiration comes from Allah Almighty through an angel, so one should be grateful to Allah Almighty for it. Evil whispers are more frequent when the commanding self is dominant, while inspiration is more frequent when the tranquil self is dominant. Satan is such an enemy of ours that we do not see him: he sees us, but we do not see him. Therefore, seek refuge in that Powerful Lord عَزَّوَجَلَّ who sees him, while he does not see that Lord عَزَّوَجَلَّ. Seek refuge from a powerful enemy in the Powerful Lord عَزَّوَجَلَّ.

{اَلَّذِیْ یُوَسْوِسُ فِیْ صُدُوْرِ النَّاسِ: Who inculcates evil thoughts into the hearts of the people.} That is, Satan does not mislead through speech and voice; rather, he directly affects the heart and makes a bad thing appear good. He is himself an enemy, but comes wearing the clothing of a friend. Then he whispers to a person according to what that person is like.

{مِنَ الْجِنَّةِ وَ النَّاسِ: From among the jinn and mankind.} This explains that the Satan who whispers may be from among the jinn or from among mankind. Just as the devils among the jinn put evil whispers into people's hearts, the devils among mankind also come as advisers and put evil whispers into a person's heart. If a person accepts these whispers, their chain increases and they lead him far astray; but if he dislikes them, they withdraw and remain cowering.

### Seek Refuge from the Devils Among Jinn and Mankind

A person should seek refuge both from the evil of the devils among the jinn and from the evil of the devils among mankind. In this regard, a beneficial devotional practice is presented here. Sayyidatuna Aishah Siddiqah رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا states that when the Messenger of Allah صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہ وَ سَلَّمَ came to his blessed bed at night, he would join his two blessed hands, blow into them, recite Surah قُلْ هُوَ اللّٰهُ اَحَدٌ, قُلْ اَعُوْذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ, and قُلْ اَعُوْذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ, and then wipe his blessed hands over his entire sacred body, beginning with his blessed head and reaching as far as his blessed hands could reach. He would perform this action three times. (Bukhari, Book of the Virtues of the Quran, chapter on the virtue of the Mu'awwidhat, 3/407, hadith 5017.)

Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn Khubayb رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ states: We were caught in light rain, and darkness had fallen at that time. We were waiting for the Noble Prophet صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہ وَ سَلَّمَ to come and lead us in prayer. Then the Illuminated Presence صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہ وَ سَلَّمَ came to lead us in prayer and said, “Recite!” I asked, “What should I recite?” He said, “Recite قُلْ هُوَ اللّٰهُ اَحَدٌ, قُلْ اَعُوْذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ, and قُلْ اَعُوْذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ three times each in the morning and evening. Reciting them will protect you from every evil thing.” (Sunan an-Nasa'i, Book of Seeking Refuge, chapter 1, p. 862, hadith 5438.)

May Allah Almighty make this exegesis beneficial and useful for all Muslims, both the general public and the elite, and make it an excellent means of reforming the beliefs, knowledge, and actions of the Muslims. Through its blessing, may He grant limitless forgiveness and pardon to me, my parents, my dear relatives, my respected teachers, my friends and companions, my other associates, and my helpers. Ameen, and again Ameen.
