# Surah Al-Lahab: English Translation

## Ayat 111:1

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54495
**Metadata:** surah=111; ayat=1; ayatId=6327; tafseerId=54495; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** تَبَّتْ یَدَاۤ اَبِیْ لَهَبٍ وَّ تَبَّﭤ(1)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** May both the hands of Abu Lahab be destroyed, and he has been destroyed.

{تَبَّتْ یَدَاۤ اَبِیْ لَهَبٍ وَّ تَبَّ: May both the hands of Abu Lahab be destroyed, and he has been destroyed.} Abu Lahab's name was Abd al-'Uzza. He was the son of Abd al-Muttalib and the paternal uncle of the Noble Prophet ﷺ. He was a very fair and handsome man; for this reason, his kunya was Abu Lahab, and he became famous by this kunya.

The daughters of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, Sayyidatuna Ruqayyah and Sayyidatuna Umm Kulthum رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمَا, were married to his sons, 'Utbah and 'Utaybah. After this Surah was revealed, Abu Lahab caused those daughters to be divorced. The incident of 'Utbah is also highly instructive: along with divorcing the daughter of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, he insulted the Noble Prophet ﷺ. Therefore, the Beloved of both worlds ﷺ supplicated for his destruction. Consequently, despite making extensive preparations for his protection during a journey, a lion tore him apart.

The destruction of both hands of Abu Lahab in this verse means the destruction of his person. This blessed verse foretold Abu Lahab's destruction. Thus, he suffered a terrible death: he died one week after the Battle of Badr from a disease of black spots, called 'adasah by the Arabs. The Arabs regarded it as a contagious disease and kept far away from it. Therefore, this rejected man’s corpse remained there for three days. It swelled, burst, and began to give off a foul smell; then labourers were paid to remove it. (Ruh al-Bayan, Al-Masad, under verse 2, 10/534.)

## Ayat 111:2

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54496
**Metadata:** surah=111; ayat=2; ayatId=6328; tafseerId=54496; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** مَاۤ اَغْنٰى عَنْهُ مَالُهٗ وَ مَا كَسَبَﭤ(2)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** His wealth did not benefit him in the least, nor did whatever he earned.

{مَاۤ اَغْنٰى عَنْهُ مَالُهٗ وَ مَا كَسَبَ: His wealth did not benefit him in the least, nor did whatever he earned.} It is reported that when Abu Lahab heard the first verse, he said, “If what my nephew says is true, I will ransom my wealth and children for my life.” This verse refuted his notion, declaring that his thought was wrong and that nothing would be of benefit at that time. (Madarik, Al-Masad, under verse 2, p. 1381; Khazain al-Irfan, Al-Lahab, under verse 2, p. 1124.)

{سَیَصْلٰى نَارًا ذَاتَ لَهَبٍ: He will soon enter the Fire with layers of flames.} That is, after the Resurrection, Abu Lahab will enter Hell and suffer the punishment of the Fire. It is therefore known that Abu Lahab's being destined for Hell is certain.

## Ayat 111:4

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54497
**Metadata:** surah=111; ayat=4; ayatId=6330; tafseerId=54497; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** وَّ امْرَاَتُهٗؕ-حَمَّالَةَ الْحَطَبِۚ    (4)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** And his wife (too), carrying the bundle of wood on her head.

{وَّ امْرَاَتُهٗؕ-حَمَّالَةَ الْحَطَبِ: And his wife (too), carrying the bundle of wood on her head.} Umm Jamil, the daughter of Harb ibn Umayyah and the sister of Abu Sufyan, held intense enmity and hostility towards the Noble Messenger ﷺ. Despite being a very wealthy woman from a prominent family, she went to such an extreme in her enmity towards the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that she would herself carry a bundle of thorns on her head and place it in the path of the Noble Messenger ﷺ, so that it would cause pain and harm to the Illuminated Presence ﷺ and his Companions رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمْ. The harming of the Most Esteemed Prophet ﷺ was so dear to her that she would not even tolerate receiving anyone else's help in this work. (Baydawi, Al-Masad, under verses 4–5, 5/545; Khazin, Abu Lahab, under verse 4, 4/425, excerpted.)

{فِیْ جِیْدِهَا حَبْلٌ مِّنْ مَّسَدٍ: A rope made from date palm fibre is around her neck.} Umm Jamil had a rope made from date-palm fibre around her neck, with which she tied the bundle of thorns. One day, she was bringing this burden and sat upon a stone to rest because she had become tired. By Allah Almighty’s command, an angel pulled the bundle from behind her. It fell, and Umm Jamil was strangled by the rope around her neck, and she died. (Khazin, Abu Lahab, under verse 5, 4/425.)

This insolent, foul woman tasted punishment in this world as well, and she will also enter punishment in the Hereafter. In the Hereafter, chains of fire will be around her neck, and a bundle of the wood of Hell will be loaded upon her back.
