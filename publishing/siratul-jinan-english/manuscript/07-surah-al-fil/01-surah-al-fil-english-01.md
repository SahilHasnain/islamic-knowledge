# Surah Al-Fil: English Translation

## Ayat 105:1

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54485
**Metadata:** surah=105; ayat=1; ayatId=6293; tafseerId=54485; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** اَلَمْ تَرَ كَیْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِاَصْحٰبِ الْفِیْلِﭤ(1)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** O Beloved, did you not see how your Lord dealt with the People of the Elephant?

{اَلَمْ تَرَ كَیْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِاَصْحٰبِ الْفِیْلِ: O Beloved, did you not see how your Lord dealt with the People of the Elephant?} The summary of the incident mentioned in this Surah is that when Abraha, the king of Yemen and Abyssinia, saw people preparing to perform Hajj at the House of Allah during the Hajj season, he built a church in Sana'a for the purpose that those performing Hajj would come there instead of going to Makkah and would circumambulate this church.

The people of Arabia found this extremely objectionable. A man from the tribe of Banu Kinanah found an opportunity, relieved himself in that church, and defiled it. When Abraha learned of this, he became extremely enraged and swore that he would demolish the Exalted Ka'bah. Therefore, he set out with his army for this purpose. There were many elephants in this army, and at its front was a huge, mountain-like elephant with a large body, whose name was Mahmood.

When Abraha reached near Makkah, he seized the animals belonging to the people of Makkah. Among them were two hundred camels belonging to Sayyiduna 'Abdul-Muttalib رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ. When Sayyiduna 'Abdul-Muttalib رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ came to Abraha, Abraha honoured him, seated him beside himself, and asked, "For what purpose have you come here, and what is your request?" He replied, "My request is that my camels be returned to me." Abraha said, "I am very astonished after hearing you. I have come here to demolish this House of the Ka'bah, which is your revered and honoured place and that of your forefathers, yet you say nothing about it and are asking for the return of your camels!" He replied, "I am the owner of the camels, so I speak regarding them; and the One Who owns the Ka'bah will Himself protect it."

Hearing this, Abraha returned his camels to him. Sayyiduna 'Abdul-Muttalib رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ returned and informed Quraysh of the situation. He advised them to take refuge in the valleys and peaks of the mountains, so Quraysh did so. Sayyiduna 'Abdul-Muttalib رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ went to the door of the Ka'bah and supplicated in the Divine Court for the protection of the Ka'bah. After completing his supplication, he too went to join his people.

When Abraha ordered his army to prepare at dawn, the condition of the elephant named Mahmood was that it would walk when they directed it elsewhere, but when they turned it towards the Ka'bah, it would sit down. Allah Almighty sent armies of birds against Abraha's army from the direction of the sea. Each bird had three pebbles: two in its two feet and one in its beak. The birds came and began striking them with stones. Thus, when a bird dropped a pebble upon a person, that pebble would break through his helmet, emerge from his head, pierce his body, pass through the elephant, and reach the ground. The name of the person who was to be killed by each pebble was written upon it. In this way, the birds reduced Abraha's soldiers to the condition of the leftover consumed straw of animals. The Noble Prophet ﷺ was born in the very year in which this incident occurred. (خازن، الفیل، تحت الآیۃ: ۱-۵، ۴ / ۴۰۷-۴۱۰، ملخصاً)
