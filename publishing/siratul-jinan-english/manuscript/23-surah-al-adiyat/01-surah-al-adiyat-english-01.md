# Surah Al-Adiyat: English Translation

## Ayat 100:1

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54471

**Metadata:** surah=100; ayat=1; ayatId=6246; tafseerId=54471; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** وَ الْعٰدِیٰتِ ضَبْحًاۙ    (1)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** By the oath of those (horses) that run with a panting breath.

{وَالْعٰدِیٰتِ ضَبْحًا: By the oath of those horses that run with a panting breath.} The horses mentioned in this verse are the horses of warriors which run in jihad, causing sounds to emerge from their chests. (Abu Saud, Al-Adiyat, under verse 1, 5/896)

### Lessons from “By the oath of those horses that run with a panting breath”

Mufti Ahmad Yar Khan Naeemi رَحْمَۃُاللّٰہِ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ explained some beautiful points from this verse:

1. It is clear that the rank of warriors is extremely high, because Allah Almighty swore by their horses.

2. When a warrior's horse carries him on its back, the horse's rank rises. Therefore, when Sayyiduna Abu Bakr al-Siddiq رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ carried the Leader of the Messengers صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہ وَ سَلَّمَ on his shoulder on the night of the Migration; when Sayyiduna Ali al-Murtada كَرَّمَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی وَجْہَہُ الْکَرِیْم laid the Radiant Presence صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہ وَ سَلَّمَ on his knee at Sahba; and when Sayyidatuna Aishah Siddiqah رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا took the blessed head of the Noble Presence صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہ وَ سَلَّمَ onto her chest at the time of his passing away, then what must be the rank of that gentle lady Aminah and the foster mother Halimah who nursed the Noble Presence صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہٖ وَسَلَّمَ in their laps?

3. If the breath of a warrior's horse is blessed, since Allah Almighty swore by it, then the breath of one who remembers Allah is also blessed, and healing is attained through it.

{فَالْمُوْرِیٰتِ قَدْحًا: Then bring out sparks from stones by striking their hooves.} Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn Abbas رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمَا said: “This refers to horses which walk upon stony ground, causing sparks of fire to emerge from the friction of their hooves.” (Tafsir Kabir, Al-Adiyat, under verse 2, 11/259)

### Even a distant association with the accepted brings honour

One noteworthy point is that when the stone and flame became associated with the hoof of a warrior's horse, it became so beloved in the court of Allah Almighty that Allah Almighty mentioned it in an oath as well. This shows that even a distant association with those who are accepted is a cause of honour.

## Ayat 100:3

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54472

**Metadata:** surah=100; ayat=3; ayatId=6248; tafseerId=54472; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** فَالْمُغِیْرٰتِ صُبْحًاۙ    (3)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** And by those who raid (on enemies) at dawn.

{فَالْمُغِیرٰتِ صُبْحًا: And by those who raid at dawn.} The summary of this verse and the two verses after it is that Allah Almighty swore by those horses which attack the enemies of Islam at dawn, then raise dust while running, and at that very time enter the enemy army fearlessly.

When the fighters intended to attack an enemy of Islam, they would travel throughout the night and attack at dawn. This was beneficial because they could not be seen in the darkness of night, and at the time of the attack people were heedless and unprepared for battle. (Tafsir Kabir, Al-Adiyat, under verse 3, 11/260; Qurtubi, Al-Adiyat, under verse 3, 10/114, Part Twenty, excerpted)

### Lessons from verses 3 to 5 of Surah Al-Adiyat

Several matters are learned from these verses.

1. Jihad is generally blessed when carried out at dawn. Indeed, every religious and worldly task performed at that time contains blessing.

2. The dust raised by horses running during jihad is also beloved to Allah Almighty, because it is dust raised in the way of Allah Almighty.

3. Entering the enemy army fearlessly is also beloved to Allah Almighty.

## Ayat 100:6

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54473

**Metadata:** surah=100; ayat=6; ayatId=6251; tafseerId=54473; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** اِنَّ الْاِنْسَانَ لِرَبِّهٖ لَكَنُوْدٌۚ    (6)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** Indeed, man is very ungrateful towards his Lord.

{اِنَّ الْاِنْسَانَ لِرَبِّهٖ لَكَنُوْدٌ: Indeed, man is very ungrateful towards his Lord.} After mentioning the oaths by the horses of the warriors, Allah Almighty says that man is certainly very ungrateful towards his Lord عزَّوَجَلَّ. Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn Abbas رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمَا said that the ungrateful person is one who denies the blessings of Allah Almighty. Some exegetes have said that the ungrateful person means a sinful human being, while others have said that it means one who remembers calamities and forgets blessings. (Khazin, Al-Adiyat, under verse 6, 4/402)

{وَ اِنَّهٗ عَلٰى ذٰلِكَ لَشَهِیْدٌ: And indeed, he himself is witness to it.} One meaning of this verse is that man himself bears witness to his being ungrateful through his actions. A second meaning is that Allah Almighty Himself bears witness to man's ingratitude. (Khazin, Al-Adiyat, under verse 7, 4/402)

## Ayat 100:8

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54474

**Metadata:** surah=100; ayat=8; ayatId=6253; tafseerId=54474; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** وَ اِنَّهٗ لِحُبِّ الْخَیْرِ لَشَدِیْدٌﭤ(8)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** And indeed, he definitely loves wealth to the extreme.

{وَ اِنَّهٗ لِحُبِّ الْخَیْرِ لَشَدِیْدٌ: And indeed, he definitely loves wealth to the extreme.} The summary of this verse and the three verses after it is that man is extremely strong in his love and pursuit of wealth, while he is weak in worshipping Allah Almighty and giving thanks for His blessings. Does the person afflicted by love of wealth not know that when those in the graves are raised and presented before Allah Almighty, and what is in the chests is disclosed, Allah Almighty will recompense them for their good and bad deeds? Indeed, their Lord عزَّوَجَلَّ is fully informed, as He has always been, of their deeds, intentions, obedience and disobedience on the Day of Judgement, which is the Day of Decision. (Tafsir Kabir, Al-Adiyat, under verse 8, 11/262; Samarqandi, Al-Adiyat, under verses 8–11, 3/503–504; Madarik, Al-Adiyat, under verses 8–11, p. 1369, excerpted)

### The heedless person's hard heart

Nur al-Irfan states that the heedless person is hard-hearted because of love of wealth, since love of wealth causes hardness of the heart, whereas love of the Noble Prophet صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہ وَ سَلَّم is a cause of softness of the heart. Consider Yazid, Pharaoh and Shaddad: they were harder-hearted than animals, merely because of their love of wealth. Alternatively, the heedless person is hard-hearted regarding wealth but soft regarding religion, meaning that he cannot bear difficulty in religion. This is why people generally endure hardships for the world which they do not endure for religion.

### Love of wealth has four forms

Remember that love of wealth is of four kinds:

1. Faith-based love, such as wanting wealth for Hajj and similar purposes.
2. Self-based love, such as desiring wealth for one's own comfort and ease.
3. Rebellious love, such as loving wealth merely to accumulate it and leave it behind.
4. Satanic love, meaning love of wealth for sin and disobedience.

The last two forms are intended here. The first kind of love is worship. Sayyiduna Sulayman عَلَیْہِ الصَّلٰوۃُ وَالسَّلَام said: “اِنِّیْۤ اَحْبَبْتُ حُبَّ الْخَیْرِ”. The Noble Prophet صَلَّی اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَلَیْہِ وَاٰلِہ وَ سَلَّم had great love for the horses of jihad. Since wealth is a great means of goodness, it was called “good”. In the view of the Sufis, love of a blessing is blameworthy when it fills the heart so completely that no room remains for love of the Bestower. This is what is meant here: the heart should contain only the love of the Beloved, with no place for others, while other loves should remain outside the heart. A ship remains safe when the water is outside it; if water enters the ship, it sinks.
