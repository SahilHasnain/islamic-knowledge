# Surah Al-Falaq: English Translation

## Ayat 113:1

**Source:** Sirat-ul-Jinan tafseer 54499
**Metadata:** surah=113; ayat=1; ayatId=6338; tafseerId=54499; para=30; volume=10

**Arabic:** قُلْ اَعُوْذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِۙ   (1)

**Kanz-ul-Iman English translation:** Say you (O Beloved), 'I take refuge in the One Who is the Creator of the daybreak.'

{قُلْ اَعُوْذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ: Say you (O Beloved), 'I take refuge in the One Who is the Creator of the daybreak.'} Mentioning Allah Almighty with the attribute “Lord of the daybreak” when seeking refuge is because Allah Almighty creates the morning and removes the darkness of night; therefore, He is also able to remove from the one seeking refuge the circumstances that he fears. Just as a person waits for the morning to dawn during the dark night, a frightened person likewise waits for safety and comfort. In addition, morning is the time when the prayers of the helpless and distressed are made and accepted. Thus, the meaning of this verse is: “I seek the refuge of the One Who creates the time when ease is granted to those afflicted by distress and grief and prayers are accepted.” Another opinion is that “Falaq” is a valley in Hell. (Khazin, Al-Falaq, under verse 1, 4/429–430.)

{مِنْ شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ: From the evil of His entire creation.} This verse seeks refuge from the evil of every creature, whether living or lifeless, legally responsible or not legally responsible. Some commentators have said that “creation” here specifically refers to Iblis, than whom there is no creature worse. (Khazin, Al-Falaq, under verse 2, 4/430.)

{وَ مِنْ شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ اِذَا وَقَبَ: And from the evil of the darkness (of the night) when it sets in.} It is narrated from the Mother of the Believers, Sayyidatuna Aishah رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا, that the Noble Messenger ﷺ looked towards the moon and said to her, “O Aishah رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا, seek Allah Almighty’s refuge from its evil when it sets, for it becomes dark.” (Tirmidhi, Book of Tafsir, chapter on what has been reported concerning Surah Al-Mu'awwidhatayn, 5/240, hadith 3377.) This refers to the last nights of the month. When the moon disappears, the magical practices intended to make people ill are carried out at that time. (Khazin, Al-Falaq, under verse 3, 4/430.)

{وَ مِنْ شَرِّ النَّفّٰثٰتِ فِی الْعُقَدِ: And from the evil of those female (magicians) who blow onto knots.} That is, I seek refuge from the evil of female magicians who tie knots in strings, recite magical incantations over them again and again, and blow on them, as the daughters of Labid did in order to cast magic upon the Noble Prophet ﷺ. (Baghawi, Al-Falaq, under verse 4, 4/517.)

### An Important Sacred-Law Ruling Concerning Amulets

Remember that amulets and talismans made for impermissible purposes are impermissible and unlawful. However, making a talisman for a permissible purpose, tying knots on it, and reciting verses of the Holy Quran or the Names of Allah Almighty over it and blowing upon it are permissible. The majority of the Noble Companions and the Followers رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمْ hold this view. (Khazin, Al-Falaq, under verse 1, 4/429, abridged.) This is also established through the blessed practice and saying of the Noble Prophet ﷺ. Accordingly, it is narrated from Sayyidatuna Aishah Siddiqah رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا that she said: “Whenever anyone from the family of the Illuminated Presence ﷺ became ill, the Eminent Prophet ﷺ would recite the Mu'awwidhat (that is, Surah Al-Falaq and Surah An-Nas) and blow over him.” (Muslim, Book of Greetings, chapter on reciting the Mu'awwidhat and blowing over the sick, p. 1205, hadith 50 (2192).)

It is narrated from Sayyiduna Ubayd ibn Rifa'ah رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ that Sayyidatuna Asma bint Umays رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہَا said to the Noble Messenger ﷺ, “O Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the sons of Sayyiduna Ja'far رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ are quickly affected by the evil eye. Should I recite something and blow over them?” He said, “Yes, because if anything could overtake Divine Decree, the evil eye would certainly overtake it.” (Tirmidhi, Book of Medicine, chapter on what has been reported concerning incantation for the evil eye, 4/13, hadith 2066.)

{وَ مِنْ شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ اِذَا حَسَدَ: And from the evil of the envier when he envies me.} An envier is one who wishes that another person's blessing be taken away. Here, the Jews who envied the Noble Prophet ﷺ are specifically intended by “the envier”; or it refers specifically to the Jewish man Labid ibn al-A'sam. (Khazin, Al-Falaq, under verse 5, 4/430.) In general, this blessed verse is sufficient for seeking refuge from every envier. Envy is the worst quality, and it was the first sin committed in the heavens by Iblis and on earth by Cain. In contrast to envy is benevolent jealousy: this is when a person wishes for himself the same blessing that another person possesses, but does not wish for that blessing to be taken away from the other person. This blessed Surah teaches that magic and envy are among the worst crimes, since they have been mentioned specifically after the general evils.
