## Chapter 19: The Blessed Children (Continued)

### Sayyidina Ibrahim (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu)

He is the youngest among the Holy Prophet's ﷺ blessed children. He was born in Dhul Hijjah 8 AH, at a place called "Aaliya" near Madina, from the blessed womb of Sayyida Maria Qibtiyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). This is why Aaliya is also known as "Mashraba-e-Ibrahim." The news of his birth was brought to the prophetic court from Aaliya by the Prophet's freed slave, Sayyidina Abu Rafay'a (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). Upon hearing this good news, the Holy Prophet gave Sayyidina Abu Rafay'a a slave as a reward.

Immediately thereafter, Sayyidina Jibreel (Alaihissalam) descended and addressed the Prophet as "یَا أَبَا إِبْرَاهِیمَ" (O father of Ibrahim). The Holy Prophet was extremely happy. He sacrificed two rams for his Aqeeqah, gave silver equal to the weight of his hair in charity, buried his hair, and named him "Ibrahim." Then he entrusted him to Sayyida "Umme Saif" (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) for nursing. Her husband, Sayyidina Abu Saif (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), was a blacksmith by profession.

The Holy Prophet ﷺ had great love for Ibrahim and would sometimes visit him. Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "We went with the Messenger of Allah to the house of Sayyidina Abu Saif. At that time, Ibrahim was in the throes of departure." Seeing this scene, tears began to flow from the eyes of the Mercy for the Worlds ﷺ. Abdurrahman bin Awf (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) asked: "O Messenger of Allah! Do you also cry?" The Prophet replied: "O son of Awf! This is a tear of compassion." Then, as tears again flowed from his blessed eyes, these words came from his blessed lips:

"The eye weeps, the heart is grieved, but we only say what pleases our Lord. And surely, O Ibrahim! We are extremely grieved at your separation."

On the day Sayyidina Ibrahim (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) passed away, a solar eclipse occurred coincidentally. The Arabs had a belief from the time of ignorance that the sun and moon eclipse due to the death of a great person. Consequently, some thought that this eclipse was caused by Ibrahim's death. On this occasion, the Holy Prophet ﷺ delivered a sermon, refuting this belief of ignorance, and said:

"Indeed, the sun and moon are two signs of Allah. They do not eclipse due to anyone's death or life. When you see an eclipse, pray and perform the Salat al-Kusuf (eclipse prayer) until the eclipse ends." (Sahih Bukhari)

The Holy Prophet also said: "My son Ibrahim did not complete his nursing period and departed from this world. Therefore, Allah has appointed a wet nurse for him in Paradise who will continue to nurse him for the remainder of his nursing period." (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 254)

It is narrated that the Holy Prophet buried Sayyidina Ibrahim (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) in Jannatul Baqi near the grave of Sayyidina Usman bin Maz'oon (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) and sprinkled water on his grave with his own blessed hand. at the time of his death, Ibrahim's blessed age was 17 or 18 months. And Allah knows best. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 453)

### Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

She was the eldest among the Prophet's daughters. She was born in Makka 10 years before the proclamation of prophethood, when the Prophet's blessed age was 30. She accepted Islam in the early days of Islam. After the Battle of Badr, the Holy Prophet called her from Makka to Madina, and she migrated.

Before the proclamation of prophethood, she was married to her maternal cousin, Abul Aas bin Rabee'. Abul Aas was the son of Sayyida Haala (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), the sister of Sayyida Khadija (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). The Holy Prophet had married Zainab to Abul Aas at Khadija's request. Sayyida Zainab became Muslim, but Abul Aas remained on polytheism and disbelief.

In Ramadan 2 AH, Abul Aas was captured in the Battle of Badr and brought to Madina. Until then, Sayyida Zainab was still residing in Makka. To secure Abul Aas's release from captivity, she sent the necklace that her mother, Sayyida Khadija, had given her as dowry. Upon seeing this necklace, the Holy Prophet ﷺ was moved. At his signal, the noble Companions returned the necklace to Sayyida Zainab. The Holy Prophet freed Abul Aas on the condition that he would send Zainab to Madina upon reaching Makka.

Abul Aas kept his promise and sent Sayyida Zainab with his brother Kinana to the place "Batan-e-Yaajaj." The Holy Prophet had already sent Sayyidina Zaid bin Haarisa (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) with an Ansari Companion to that location. They brought Sayyida Zainab to Madina under their protection.

It is narrated that when Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was leaving Makka, the disbelievers of Quraish blocked her path. An ill-fated oppressor named "Hibaar bin Al Aswad" frightened her with a spear, causing her to fall from the camel, which resulted in a miscarriage. However, her brother-in-law Kinana drew arrows from his quiver and threatened: "Whoever follows Zainab's camel will not escape my arrows." Hearing this, the Quraish disbelievers backed off. Then the leader of Makka, Abu Sufyan, intervened and cleared the way for Sayyida Zainab to proceed to Madina.

Because of this painful affliction during her migration, the Holy Prophet said regarding her virtues: "Among my daughters, she holds the greatest virtue because she endured such a great hardship in migrating to me."

Afterward, in Muharram 7 AH, Abul Aas embraced Islam and migrated from Makka to Madina to live with Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 195-196)

Sayyida Zainab passed away in 8 AH. Sayyida Umme Aiman, Sayyida Sauda bint Zam'aa, and Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhun) performed her Ghusl. The Holy Prophet gave his blessed lower garment for her shroud and lowered her into the grave with his own blessed hands.

Sayyida Zainab had one son named "Ali" and one daughter named "Umaama." There is a narration that Ali died near the age of puberty during his mother's lifetime. However, Ibn Asakir states that some genealogists have mentioned that he was honored with martyrdom in the Battle of Yarmouk. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 197)

The Holy Prophet had great love for Sayyida Umaama. He would carry her on his blessed shoulders and take her to the Prophet's Mosque. It is narrated that once the King of Abyssinia, the Negus, sent the Prophet a suit of clothes as a gift, along with a gold ring with an Abyssinian gemstone. The Holy Prophet gave this ring to Sayyida Umaama.

Similarly, once someone presented the Holy Prophet with a very beautiful gold necklace. All the pure wives were amazed at its beauty. The Holy Prophet said to them: "I will give this necklace to the one among my household who is most beloved to me." All the pure wives thought he would certainly give it to Sayyida Ayesha, but the Holy Prophet called Sayyida Umaama close and placed the necklace around his beloved granddaughter's neck with his own blessed hands. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 197)

### Sayyida Ruqayya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

She was born 7 years before the proclamation of prophethood, when the Prophet's blessed age was 33. She accepted Islam in the early days of Islam. She was first married to Abu Lahab's son "Utba," but before she could go to her husband's house, the Surah "Tabbat Yada" (Chapter 111) was revealed. Hearing the mention of his permanent disgrace in the Quran, Abu Lahab became furious and forced his son Utba to divorce the Prophet's daughter, which Utba did.

Then the Holy Prophet married Sayyida Ruqayya to Sayyidina Usman bin Affan (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). After the marriage, Sayyidina Usman took her with him and migrated to Abyssinia. They returned from Abyssinia to Makka and then migrated to Madina. Both husband and wife were honored with the noble title "Saahibul Hijratain" (possessor of two migrations).

During the days of the Battle of Badr, Sayyida Ruqayya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was severely ill. Therefore, the Holy Prophet stopped Sayyidina Usman from participating in Badr and ordered him to nurse her. On the very day that Sayyidina Zaid bin Haarisa (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) brought the glad tidings of the Muslims' decisive victory in Badr to Madina, Sayyida Ruqayya passed away at the age of 20. The Holy Prophet could not attend her funeral due to being away for the Battle of Badr.

Although Sayyidina Usman did not participate in Badr, the Holy Prophet counted him among the mujahideen of Badr, gave him a share of the spoils equal to the other fighters, and gave him glad tidings of a great reward equal to that of the participants of Badr.

Sayyida Ruqayya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) had a son from Sayyidina Usman named "Abdullah." He passed away in 4 AH at the age of 6, after his mother's death. (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 198-199)

### Sayyida Umme Kulsoom (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

She was first married to Abu Lahab's other son "Utaiba." However, due to Abu Lahab's coercion, the ill-fated Utaiba divorced her before she could go to his house. This oppressor also showed extreme insolence toward the court of prophethood, to the extent that he spoke rudely and attacked the Mercy for the Worlds ﷺ, tearing his blessed garment. This insolence deeply pained the Prophet's tender heart. In anguish, these words came from his blessed lips: "O Allah! Set one of Your dogs upon him."

The effect of this prophetic supplication was that Abu Lahab and Utaiba both joined a trade caravan to Syria. At a place called "Zarqa," they halted by a monk for the night. The monk warned the caravan: "There are many wild beasts here. Be careful when you sleep." Hearing this, Abu Lahab said: "O people! Muhammad has supplicated for the destruction of my son Utaiba. Gather all the merchandise, place Utaiba's bed on top of it, and all of you sleep around him so that my son may be safe from the attack of beasts." The caravan people made thorough arrangements for Utaiba's protection. But in the dead of night, a lion came, sniffed everyone, jumped onto Utaiba's bed, and crushed his head. No matter how much the people searched for the lion, they could not find where it had come from or where it went. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 197-198)

Behold the glory of Allah! Both of Abu Lahab's sons, Utba and Utaiba, divorced the Prophet's daughters at their father's coercion. However, since Utba had not shown insolence at the prophetic court, he did not fall into divine wrath. On the contrary, on the day of the Conquest of Makka, he and his other brother "Mu'attib" both accepted Islam, pledged allegiance at the Prophet's blessed hand, and were honored with Companionship. But Utaiba, because of his wicked insolence at the prophetic court, fell into the wrath of the Almighty and the All-Compelling, and died a disbeliever, becoming the prey of a ferocious lion.

After Sayyida Ruqayya's death, the Holy Prophet married Sayyida Umme Kulsoom (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) to Sayyidina Usman Ghani (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) in Rabiul Awwal 3 AH. However, no child was born from this marriage. She passed away in Shaban 9 AH. The Holy Prophet led her funeral prayer, and she was buried in Jannatul Baqi. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 200)

### Sayyida Fatima (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

She is the youngest but the most beloved and cherished princess of the King of the Two Worlds ﷺ. Her name is "Fatima," and her titles are "Zahra" and "Batool." There is disagreement among historians regarding the year of her birth. Abu Umar says she was born one year before the proclamation of prophethood, when the Prophet was 41. Others have written she was born one year before the proclamation. Allama Ibnul Jauzi wrote that she was born 5 years before prophethood. And Allah knows best. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 202-203)

Allahu Akbar! What can be said of her virtues and excellences? The pages of hadith books are filled with accounts of her ranks and stations. We have written about them in our book "Haqqani Taqreerein." The Holy Prophet ﷺ said: "She is the leader of the women of the worlds and the leader of the women of Paradise." About her, the Prophet said: "Fatima is my daughter; she is a piece of my flesh. Whoever angers her has angered me." (Mishkat, p. 567, Manaqib-e-Ahl-e-Bait; Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 204)

She was married to Sayyidina Ali, the Lion of Allah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), in 2 AH. From her blessed womb, three sons — Sayyidina Hasan, Sayyidina Hussain, and Sayyidina Mohsin (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) — and three daughters — Zainab, Umme Kulsoom, and Ruqayya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhunn) — were born. Sayyidina Mohsin and Sayyida Ruqayya passed away in childhood. Sayyida Umme Kulsoom was married to the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), who had two children from her: a son named Zaid and a daughter named Ruqayya. Sayyida Zainab was married to Sayyidina Abdullah bin Jafar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 460)

The Prophet's ﷺ passing deeply pained Sayyida Fatima (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). After the Prophet's departure, she was never seen laughing. Six months after the Prophet's departure, on Tuesday night, 3 Ramadan 11 AH, she answered the call of death. Sayyidina Ali or Sayyidina Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) led her funeral prayer. The most correct and preferred view is that she was buried in Jannatul Baqi. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 461)

### The Number of Paternal Uncles

Historians disagree on the number of the Holy Prophet's ﷺ paternal uncles. Some say nine, some say ten, and some say eleven. However, the author of Mawahibe Ladunniyah, quoting from "Zakhairul Uqba Fi Manaqibe Zawil Qurba," has written that the Holy Prophet's respected father, Sayyidina Abdullah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), had 13 brothers.
