## Chapter 14: The Ninth Year of Hijra — 9 AH

The ninth year of Hijra is filled with many remarkable events. Historians have recorded them in great detail. In this brief book, we will touch upon the most significant ones under separate headings.

### The Verses of Takhyeer and Eela

"Takhyeer" (choice) and "Eela" (vow of abstinence) are two technical terms of the Shariah. The incident behind them is as follows:

Some of the noble wives of the Beloved asked him for additional household expenses. This weighed heavily on the Prophet's delicate heart and disturbed his peace of mind. He became so distressed that he took a vow of Eela — that for one whole month, he would not even approach his wives.

By a remarkable coincidence, during these very days, the Beloved fell from a horse, spraining his blessed ankle. Due to this discomfort, he secluded himself in an upper room for a month.

Meanwhile, rumours spread that the Beloved had divorced all his wives. Hazrat Umar was away. When he returned and heard the rumours, he was extremely alarmed. He went to the mosque and saw the Companions sitting sadly. Without entering his own home, he rushed straight to the Prophet's apartment.

After some exchange, the Beloved told Hazrat Umar: "I have taken a vow of Eela for one month. If I had not taken the vow, I would have gone to my wives." Hazrat Umar then asked permission to inform the Companions that the divorce news was false. When he did, everyone was overjoyed.

When the month passed and the vow was fulfilled, the Beloved went to his wives. But the situation remained unsettled until the verses of Takhyeer were revealed:

*"O Prophet! Say to your wives: If you desire the life of this world and its adornment, then come, I will give you a provision and set you free in a handsome manner. But if you desire Allah, His Messenger, and the abode of the Hereafter, then indeed Allah has prepared for the doers of good among you a great reward."* (Al-Ahzab: 28-29)

When this verse was revealed, the Beloved went first to Hazrat Aisha and presented the choice. She replied: "Do I need time to consider this? I choose Allah, His Messenger, and the Hereafter." Every wife made the same choice — none could bear separation from the Beloved.

### Appointment of Zakat Collectors

In Muharram, 9 AH, the Beloved sent various officials and collectors to different tribes for collecting Zakat and Sadaqat. Among those appointed were:

(1) Hazrat Uyaina bin Hasan to Bani Tameem
(2) Hazrat Yazeed bin Hussian to Bani Asad
(3) Hazrat Amr bin Aas to the tribe of Fazaara
(4) Hazrat Ali to Najran

### Ghazwa-e-Tabuk

#### The Cause

The Ghassani dynasty of Arabia, which ruled Syria under Caesar's influence, was Christian. Caesar decided to use them to march on Madina. Syrian merchants who sold olive oil in Madina brought news that Caesar had gathered a massive army in Syria, including not only Romans but also the tribes of Lakhm, Judham, and Ghassan. Their advance guard had already reached Balqa.

#### The Call to Jihad

The Beloved announced the call for Jihad in a time of extreme hardship — the season of intense heat, when the dates were ripening in the orchards. It was a long journey and the enemy was powerful. To encourage contributions, he said: "Whoever equips the army of Usra will have Paradise." (Usra means "hardship" — this campaign is also called Jaish-ul-Usra.)

#### Contributions of the Companions

Hazrat Abu Bakr brought all his wealth — 4,000 dirhams. When the Beloved asked if he had left anything for his family, he replied: "I have left Allah and His Messenger for them."

Hazrat Umar brought half his wealth. He had thought he would surpass Abu Bakr this time, but the Beloved said: "O Umar! What you have left for your family is also half."

Hazrat Usman equipped 10,000 soldiers at his own expense, providing 900 camels, 100 horses, and 1,000 dinars. The Beloved said: "O Allah! I am pleased with Usman. You too be pleased with him." Hazrat Usman became extremely wealthy from this blessed prayer.

The women donated their jewellery. Hazrat Aasim bin Adi brought many loads of dates. Abu Aqeel Ansari, a poor man, brought only one sa'a (measure) of dates, saying: "O Messenger of Allah! I had no more. I worked all night carrying water and earned this much." The hypocrites mocked him, saying Allah had no need of this small amount. But the Beloved accepted it gladly.

#### Those Left Behind

Some sincere Companions, known as the "Weepers" (Bakka'oon), came requesting mounts, but the Beloved had none to give. They wept bitterly in their helplessness. The Quran mentions them:

*"Nor on those who, when they came to you that you might give them mounts, you said: I cannot find anything on which to carry you. They turned back while their eyes overflowed with tears of grief that they could not find anything to spend."* (At-Tawbah: 92)

The hypocrites, on the other hand, made false excuses. Some came to the Prophet for permission to stay, while others simply stayed behind without any excuse. Abdullah bin Ubayy (the chief hypocrite) also stayed back.

Hazrat Abu Khaisama noticed that the army had left and the hypocrites were staying behind enjoying the shade and cool water. Filled with zeal, he loaded some dates on his camel and rushed to catch up. When the army saw a rider in the distance, the Beloved said: "That is Abu Khaisama." So he too joined the army.

#### The Miracles of the Journey

On the way, they passed the ruined settlements of the nations of Aad and Thamud, destroyed by divine punishment. The Beloved ordered: "Do not enter their dwellings weeping, lest you suffer what they suffered."

**The Prophecy about Abu Zar:** The Beloved saw Hazrat Abu Zar Ghifari walking alone and said: "He will live alone, die alone, and be raised alone." Later, during Usman's caliphate, Abu Zar was sent to Rabza. When he was dying, he told his wife and servant: "Wash me, shroud me, and place me by the roadside. Tell the first caravan that passes to pray over me." The first caravan belonged to Hazrat Abdullah bin Mas'ood, who recognized Abu Zar, stopped, and buried him with full honour. He said: "Truly the Messenger of Allah spoke the truth: You will live alone, die alone, and be raised alone."

**The Lost Camel:** At one stage, the Prophet's camel went missing. A hypocrite named Zaid bin Lusait said: "Muhammad claims he receives news from heaven but cannot find his own camel!" The Beloved said: "By Allah's oath! I know exactly where my camel is. She is in that valley, her reins entangled in a tree." When they went there, they found it exactly as he had said. (Zurqani)

**The Spring of Tabuk:** When the army neared Tabuk, the Beloved said: "Tomorrow you will reach the spring of Tabuk after sunrise. Do not let anyone touch the water before I arrive." When they arrived, only a thin trickle was flowing. The Beloved took some water, washed his face and hands, and rinsed his mouth. He ordered the water poured back into the spring. Immediately, a powerful stream gushed forth, providing enough water for the entire army of 30,000 and all their animals. (Zurqani)

#### The Roman Army Retreats

When the Prophet reached Tabuk and ordered camp, there was no sign of the Roman army. Caesar's spies had reported that the Messenger of Allah was approaching with 30,000 men. Such terror gripped the Romans that they lost heart and did not dare leave their homes.

The Beloved stayed 20 days in Tabuk, displaying the might of the Islamic army and instilling awe of Islam in the hearts of the disbelievers. No battle took place.

During this stay, the ruler of Aila, named Yahunna, appeared at court. He agreed to pay Jizya and presented a white mule as a gift. The Beloved gave him his blessed cloak in return and issued a document of protection for his people to continue their maritime trade.

Similarly, the Christians of Jarba and Azrooh came and agreed to pay Jizya.

#### The Expedition to Dummatul Jandal

The Beloved sent Hazrat Khalid bin Waleed with 120 horsemen to Dummatul Jandal, whose king was Ukaidir bin Abdul Malik. He instructed: "You will find him hunting wild cows on a moonlit night. Capture him alive." Hazrat Khalid found Ukaidir and his brother Hassan hunting. When Hassan resisted, Khalid killed him and captured Ukaidir, releasing him on the condition that he go to Madina and make peace. Ukaidir came to Madina, and the Beloved granted him amnesty.

### The Return to Madina

On returning to Madina, the hypocrites came swearing false excuses. The Beloved accepted their apparent excuses but inwardly knew the truth. However, three sincere Companions — Hazrat Ka'ab bin Malik, Hazrat Hilaal bin Umayya, and Hazrat Muraara bin Rabee'a — were truthful about their failure to join the expedition. The Beloved ordered their boycott for 50 days. Their repentance was later accepted, and verses of the Quran were revealed about them. (A detailed account is in the book "Irfaani Taqreerein".)

As the Beloved neared Madina and saw Mount Uhud, he said: "This is Uhud. It loves us and we love it." The people of Madina — women, children, and servants — poured out to welcome him with songs of praise. On reaching Madina, the Beloved prayed two rakats in the mosque.
