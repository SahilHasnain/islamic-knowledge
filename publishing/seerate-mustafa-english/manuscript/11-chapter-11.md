## Chapter 11: The Sixth Year of Hijra — 6 AH

### Baiturrizwan and the Treaty of Hudaibiya

Among all the events of this year, the most important and glorious is Baiturrizwan and the Treaty of Hudaibiya. This event holds great significance in Islamic history, for the secret of all of Islam's future progress is tied to its folds. This is why, although outwardly it appeared to be a surrender-like treaty, the Lord of the Worlds has given it the title of "Fathe Mubeen" (Manifest Victory) in the Holy Quran.

In Zulqada, 6 AH, the Beloved ﷺ set out for Makka with 1,400 Companions, having assumed the state of Ihram for Umrah. The Beloved ﷺ was concerned that the disbelievers of Makka might prevent them from performing Umrah, so he had first sent a man from the Khuza'a tribe to Makka to bring news of the Quraish's intentions.

When the Prophet's caravan reached near the place of Asfaan, that man brought the news that the disbelievers of Makka had gathered all the tribes of Arabia and declared that the Muslims would never be allowed to enter Makka. The Quraish had gathered all their allied tribes, prepared an army, and camped at Baladah outside Makka to block the Muslims. Khalid bin Waleed and Ikrima (Abu Jahl's son), with 200 selected horsemen, had advanced as far as Ghameem.

When the Beloved ﷺ saw the dust of Khalid bin Waleed's horsemen on the road, he left the highway and took a different route, eventually reaching Hudaibiya and camping there.

### The Miracle of Water at Hudaibiya

Water was extremely scarce at this place. There was only one well, which dried up within a few hours. When the Companions became restless with thirst, the Beloved ﷺ put his blessed hand into a large bowl, and a spring of water began to flow from his blessed fingers. Then he put the water from his ablution and an arrow into the dry well. So much water gushed up in the well that the entire army and all the animals drank to their fill from that well for several days.

### Baiturrizwan (The Pledge of Ridwan)

At Hudaibiya, the Beloved ﷺ saw that a vast army of the Quraish was ready for battle, while the Muslims had assumed Ihram and could not even strike a blade of grass. So he thought it appropriate to send someone to Makka for peace negotiations. He selected Hazrat Umar for this task, but Hazrat Umar excused himself, saying: "O Messenger of Allah! The Quraish are my bitter enemies, and there is no one from my tribe in Makka who could protect me from them." So the Beloved ﷺ sent Hazrat Usman instead.

Hazrat Usman went to Makka and conveyed the Beloved's message of peace to the Quraish. Due to his wealth and his tribe's support, Hazrat Usman was highly respected in the eyes of the Quraish. They did not mistreat him, but said: "We permit you to perform Tawaf of the Kaaba and Sa'i of Safa and Marwa and complete your Umrah, but we will never let Muhammad ﷺ near the Kaaba."

Hazrat Usman refused, saying: "I can never perform my Umrah without the Messenger of Allah ﷺ." This led to an argument, and the disbelievers detained him in Makka.

In the field of Hudaibiya, news spread that the Quraish had martyred Hazrat Usman. When the Beloved received this news, he said: "It has become obligatory to avenge Usman's blood." Saying this, he sat under a babool tree and said to the Companions: "All of you pledge allegiance to me on my hand that you will remain faithful and self-sacrificing until the very end."

All the Companions, with immense enthusiasm and fervour, pledged their self-sacrifice on the Beloved's hand. This is the pledge known in Islamic history as Baiturrizwan. Allah Ta'ala has mentioned this pledge and this tree in Surah Al-Fateh of the Holy Quran:

*"Indeed, those who pledge allegiance to you, they are pledging allegiance to Allah. The hand of Allah is over their hands."* (Al-Fateh: 10)

In another place in the same Surah, Allah praises those who made this pledge:

*"Certainly, Allah was pleased with the believers when they pledged allegiance to you under the tree. He knew what was in their hearts, so He sent down tranquillity upon them and rewarded them with an imminent victory."*

But after Baiturrizwan, it became known that the news of Hazrat Usman's martyrdom was false. He was alive and well in Makka and later returned safely to the Beloved's service.

### How the Treaty of Hudaibiya Came About

The first person to come to the Beloved ﷺ at Hudaibiya was Budail bin Warqa Khuzai. Although his tribe had not yet become Muslim, they were allies of the Beloved and extremely sincere well-wishers. Budail informed the Beloved that the Quraish had gathered a large army and even had milch camels for provisions. They intended to fight and would not allow him to reach the Kaaba.

The Beloved ﷺ said: "Convey my message to the Quraish: We have not come to fight. We only want to perform Umrah. The Quraish have suffered great loss of life and property in continuous battles. It is in their interest to make a truce with me for a fixed period, leaving me to deal with the Arabs. If they agree, it will be better for them. If they choose to fight, I swear by Allah in Whose power is my life, I will fight them until my head is severed from my body."

Budail took the message to the Quraish. The hot-headed youngsters among the Quraish shouted: "No! We have no need to hear his message!" But the sensible elders allowed Budail to speak. After hearing the message, a very old and respected chief of the Quraish, Urwah bin Masood Saqafi, stood up and said: "O Quraish! Am I not your father?" They replied, "Yes." "Am I not your elder?" Again, "Yes." "Do you have any doubt about me?" They said, "No, never." Then Urwah said: "Muhammad has proposed a very wise and good solution. Allow me to go and negotiate with him."

He went to Hudaibiya and addressed the Beloved: "We received your message through Budail. If you destroy the Quraish and wipe them off the earth, has any Arab ever destroyed his own people? And if the Quraish prevail, these people with you — I see faces among them who will abandon you and flee."

Hearing this, Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddique could not contain himself. He said: "O Urwah! Be quiet! May your goddess Lat's private parts be sucked! Would we ever abandon the Messenger of Allah?"

Urwah asked who this was. He was told it was Abu Bakr. Urwah said: "By the oath of Him in Whose hand is my life, O Abu Bakr! If you had not done me a favour that I have yet to repay, I would have answered your harsh words."

Urwah considered himself a great man. Whenever he spoke to the Beloved, he would reach out and touch the Prophet's blessed beard. Hazrat Mugheera bin Shayba, who stood behind the Beloved with a drawn sword, could not tolerate this. Whenever Urwah reached for the Prophet's beard, he would strike his hand with the sword's hilt and say: "Remove your hand from the Prophet's beard!" Urwah asked who he was. When told it was Mugheera bin Shayba, he said angrily: "O treacherous one! Am I not trying to settle your breach of faith?" (Mugheera had killed some men, and Urwah had paid their blood money.)

Then Urwah looked at the Companions, surveyed the entire camp, and left. The scene of the Companions' amazing and astonishing devotion and love that he had witnessed at Hudaibiya left a deep impression on his heart. He returned to the Quraish and expressed his feelings:

"O my people! By Allah's oath! When Muhammad spits, it falls into the palm of some Companion, who rubs it on his face, hands, and skin with devotion. When he commands something, they rush to obey it. When he performs ablution, they fight over the used water as if swords were being drawn. When he speaks, all the Companions fall silent. Such is the awe of him in their hearts that no one can even look him in the eye.

"O my people! By Allah's oath! I have seen the courts of many kings. I have visited the courts of Caesar, Chosroes, and the Negus. But by Allah! I have never seen any king's courtiers show such respect to their king as Muhammad's Companions show to Muhammad."

After hearing this, a man of the Kinana tribe named Halees said: "Allow me to go to him." When he approached the court of prophethood, the Beloved ﷺ told the Companions: "This man belongs to a tribe that honours sacrificial animals. So bring the sacrificial animals before him and all start reciting the Talbiyah."

When the man saw the sacrificial animals and heard the Companions reciting the Talbiyah in the state of Ihram, he said: "SubhanAllah! How can these people be prevented from reaching the House of Allah?" He immediately returned and said to the Quraish: "I have seen the sacrificial animals with my own eyes. They are in Ihram. I can never advise that they be stopped from the Kaaba."

Then a man named Markaz bin Hafs stood up and said: "Send me too." As he approached, the Beloved said: "This is Markaz, a very rude man." The Beloved began speaking with him, but before his conversation ended, Suhail bin Amr suddenly arrived. Seeing him, the Beloved said, as a good omen: "Suhail has come. Now your matter has become easy (Suhail means easy)."

Suhail said: "Now let us write a document of treaty between us." The Beloved agreed and called Hazrat Ali to write. There was a long discussion about the terms. Finally, an agreement was reached.

The Beloved told Hazrat Ali: "Write: 'Bismillahir Rahmanir Rahim.'" Suhail objected: "We do not know 'Rahman.' Write 'Bismika Allahumma' as our old custom." The Muslims insisted on writing the full Bismillah, but the Beloved accepted Suhail's request.

Then the Beloved dictated: "These are the terms on which Muhammadur Rasulullah has made peace with the Quraish." Suhail objected again: "By Allah! If we acknowledged you as Allah's Messenger, we would neither have stopped you from the Kaaba nor fought you. Write: 'Muhammad bin Abdullah.'"

The Beloved said: "By Allah's oath! I am both Muhammadur Rasulullah and Muhammad bin Abdullah. The fact is that you deny my prophethood." He then told Hazrat Ali: "Erase 'Rasulullah' and write 'Muhammad bin Abdullah.'"

Who could be more obedient to the Prophet than Hazrat Ali? But sometimes in love, there comes a moment when even a true lover has to refuse out of love for the Beloved. Hazrat Ali said: "O Messenger of Allah! I will never erase your name." The Beloved said: "Show me where my name is." Hazrat Ali pointed to the spot, and the Beloved himself erased the word "Rasulullah."

The treaty was completed with these terms:

(1) No fighting between the two parties for 10 years.
(2) The Muslims would return this year without performing Umrah.
(3) They could come for Umrah the following year, staying only three days in Makka.
(4) They could bring no weapon except a sword, which must be kept in its sheath and bag.
(5) They could not take with them any Muslim residing in Makka. If any Muslim wanted to stay in Makka, he would not be prevented.
(6) If anyone from the disbelievers went to Madina, he would be returned. But if a Muslim returned to Makka from Madina, he would not be sent back.
(7) Arab tribes were free to ally with either party.

These terms were clearly against the Muslims, and the noble Companions were extremely displeased. But they were helpless against the command of prophethood.

#### The Incident of Abu Jandal

Strangely, the treaty had been written but not yet signed by both parties when suddenly Suhail's own son, Hazrat Abu Jandal, appeared among the Muslims dragging his chains. Suhail said: "O Muhammad! My first condition for signing is that you return Abu Jandal to me."

The Beloved said: "The treaty is not yet signed. It will take effect after both sides sign." But Suhail insisted: "Then I make no treaty with you." The Beloved said: "O Suhail! At least permit me to keep Abu Jandal with me." Suhail refused.

Seeing that he would be returned to Makka, Abu Jandal appealed to the Muslims: "O Muslims! I am being returned to the polytheists although I am a Muslim!" He showed the marks of torture on his body to stir the Muslims.

Hazrat Umar was overcome with emotion. He came to the court of prophethood, his teeth chattering, and submitted: "Are you not truly the Messenger of Allah?" The Beloved replied: "Of course I am." "Are we not on the truth and our enemies on falsehood?" "Of course." "Then why are we being humiliated in our faith?" The Beloved said: "O Umar! I am Allah's Messenger and I do not disobey Him. He is my Helper."

Hazrat Umar then asked: "Did you not promise us that we would soon perform Tawaf of the Kaaba?" The Beloved replied: "Did I tell you that you would do so this very year?" "No." "Then I tell you again, you will certainly reach the Kaaba and perform Tawaf."

Hazrat Umar then went to Hazrat Abu Bakr and repeated his concerns. Hazrat Abu Bakr said: "O Umar! He is Allah's Messenger. He does whatever Allah commands. He never disobeys Allah, and Allah is his Helper. By Allah's oath! He is on the truth. Stay with him."

Hazrat Umar regretted all his life the words he had spoken to the Prophet in that moment of emotion. He continually repented, prayed extra prayers, fasted, gave charity, and freed slaves as expiation.

This was a time of severe trial. On one side, Abu Jandal was pleading with the Muslims, and every Muslim was so charged that if not for the respect of the Prophet, their swords would have leaped from their sheaths. On the other side, the treaty had been signed, and the responsibility of fulfilling it remained.

The Chief of the Worlds, considering the delicacy of the situation, said to Abu Jandal: "Be patient. Soon Allah will find a way for you and other oppressed people. We have made a treaty, and we cannot break our word." So Abu Jandal had to return to Makka in chains.

When the treaty was finalized, the Beloved ordered the Companions: "Arise, sacrifice your animals, and shave your heads to remove Ihram." Such was the Muslims' displeasure and anger that not one person moved — yet out of respect, no one spoke a word either.

The Beloved mentioned this to Hazrat Umm Salama. She advised: "Do not speak to anyone. Go yourself, sacrifice your animal, and have your head shaved." The Beloved did exactly that. When the Companions saw the Beloved sacrificing and removing Ihram, they realized he would not change his decision. So they began sacrificing and shaving each other's heads — but with such grief that it seemed they would kill one another.

Then the Messenger of Allah set out for Madina with his Companions.

### Fathe Mubeen (The Manifest Victory)

All the Companions considered this treaty a humiliating surrender, and you have read of Hazrat Umar's distress. But after this, the following verse was revealed:

*"Indeed, We have granted you (O Beloved) a manifest victory."* (Al-Fateh: 1)

The Holy Lord called this treaty a "Manifest Victory." Hazrat Umar submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Is this a victory?" The Beloved replied: "Yes, this is a victory."

Although the Companions' initial thoughts about the treaty were not positive, subsequent events proved that this treaty was indeed the key to all future conquests. Until now, Muslims and disbelievers lived separately with no opportunity for interaction. But this treaty opened the door to free travel, dialogue, and exchange of ideas. Disbelievers would come to Madina, stay for months, and closely study the Muslims' character and actions — listening to Islamic teachings and the beauty of Islam. When Muslims went to Makka, their conduct, chastity, and devotion left such an impression on the disbelievers' hearts that they naturally inclined toward Islam.

History bears witness that from the Treaty of Hudaibiya to the Conquest of Makka, so many people embraced Islam as had never before done so. Khalid bin Waleed (the Conqueror of Syria) and Amr bin Al Aas (the Conqueror of Egypt) also became Muslims during this period, travelling from Makka to Madina of their own accord.

### The Oppressed Muslims of Makka

After the Hijra, those who became Muslims in Makka endured great hardships. They were chained and whipped by the disbelievers. Whenever one of them found an opportunity, he would secretly come to Madina. The Treaty of Hudaibiya closed this door, because one of its terms stated that if a Muslim from Makka came to Madina, he would be returned.

### The Feat of Hazrat Abu Baseer

After the Beloved returned to Madina from the Treaty of Hudaibiya, the first person to migrate from Makka was Hazrat Abu Baseer. The Quraish immediately sent two men demanding his return according to the treaty. The Beloved ﷺ said: "We have made a treaty and cannot break our word. Go with them." Hazrat Abu Baseer submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Will you return me to the disbelievers?" The Beloved said: "Go, Allah will create a way for you and the oppressed."

So Abu Baseer went with them. On the way, when the escort stopped to rest, Abu Baseer asked one of them for his sword, saying he admired its quality. When the man handed it over, Abu Baseer killed him with it. The other escort fled to Madina and reported to the Prophet that his companion had been killed.

As he was speaking, Abu Baseer arrived with a drawn sword and submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! You fulfilled your obligation under the treaty by returning me. Now Allah has freed me." The Beloved said: "This man would kindle a war if he had more supporters."

Understanding the implication, Abu Baseer left Madina and went to the seashore near the trade route. Other Muslims who had been detained in Makka began joining him. Soon a band of oppressed Muslims gathered there. Whenever they heard of a Quraish trade caravan passing, they would attack and plunder it. The Quraish, unable to bear the losses, finally wrote to the Beloved ﷺ requesting him to call Abu Baseer and his men to Madina, abandoning the treaty condition about returning refugees.

The Beloved sent a letter to Abu Baseer asking him to come to Madina. But sadly, the letter arrived when Abu Baseer was in his death throes. He passed away with the blessed letter in his hands. His companions buried him and then came to Madina. (`Madarijun Nubuwwah`)

### The Letters of the Beloved to Kings

In 6 AH, after the Treaty of Hudaibiya, the Beloved ﷺ sent messengers to various kings and rulers with letters inviting them to Islam. Some of the notable messengers were:

(1) Hazrat Dihya Kalbi — to the Emperor of Rome (Caesar)
(2) Hazrat Abdullah bin Hudhafa — to Khusru Parwez, the Emperor of Persia
(3) Hazrat Hatib — to Muqawqis, the ruler of Egypt
(4) Hazrat Amr bin Umayya — to the Negus, the King of Abyssinia
(5) Hazrat Saleet bin Amr — to Hawzah, the King of Yamama
(6) Hazrat Shuja bin Wahab — to Haris Ghassani, the Governor of Ghassan

#### The Letter to Caesar

Hazrat Dihya Qalbi took the Beloved's blessed letter to Basra and presented it to the Roman governor of Syria, Haris Ghassani. He forwarded it to Caesar (Heraclius) in Baitul Maqdis. When Caesar read it, he was deeply moved. He ordered that anyone from Arabia be found to tell him about this Prophet. Abu Sufyan happened to be in Syria with a trade caravan. He was brought before Caesar.

Caesar asked Abu Sufyan about the Prophet's lineage, truthfulness, social status, followers, and other matters. The famous hadith in Bukhari records this conversation. After questioning Abu Sufyan, Caesar said:

"If what you say is true, he is indeed a prophet. I knew a prophet was coming, but I did not expect him to be from among you. If I could, I would go to him. If I am with him, I would wash his feet." But Caesar did not accept Islam because he feared losing his throne and the pressure from the clergy.

#### Khusru Parwez's Arrogance

A similar letter was sent to Khusru Parwez, the Emperor of Persia. When he received the letter beginning with "Bismillahir Rahmanir Rahim," he was furious at being addressed before his own name. He tore the letter and said disdainfully: "My slave dares to write to me like this!" When the Beloved ﷺ heard of this, he said: "Allah will tear apart his kingdom."

Khusru then ordered his governor of Yemen, Bazaan, to send men to arrest "this man from Hijaz." Bazaan sent two men to Madina. When they arrived, the Beloved asked them: "Who are you?" They said they were from Bazaan. The Beloved smiled and said: "Go and tell your master that last night, Allah killed Khusru Parwez." They were astonished and returned to Yemen. When they arrived, news of Khusru's assassination by his own son Sheerwaih had indeed reached Bazaan. Bazaan and the people of Yemen accepted Islam.

#### The Negus of Abyssinia

The Negus (Asmaha) received the letter with respect. He accepted Islam and died in 9 AH. The Beloved offered his funeral prayer in absentia in Madina. However, the Negus who succeeded him is not known to have accepted Islam.

#### Muqawqis of Egypt

Muqawqis received the letter with honour but did not accept Islam. He sent gifts to the Beloved, including the Coptic slave girl Maria, who later became the mother of the Beloved's son Ibrahim.

#### Hawzah of Yamama

Hawzah replied: "What you say is very good. If you give me a share in your rule, I will follow you." The Beloved said: "Islam has not come for the greed of ruling. I would not give him even a piece of land."

#### Haris Ghassani

Haris Ghassani became enraged upon receiving the letter and threatened to march on Madina. But before he could act, he lost his kingdom.

### Various Expeditions of 6 AH

Before the Treaty of Hudaibiya, the Beloved ﷺ sent small detachments in various directions to defend against disbelievers' attacks. The historians differ much about the exact dates and sequence of these expeditions, so only the prominent ones are mentioned here.

#### Sariyya-e-Najd: The Story of Samama bin Usaal

In 6 AH, the Beloved ﷺ sent a detachment under Hazrat Muhammad bin Maslama towards Najd. They captured Samama bin Usaal, the chief of Banu Hanifa, and brought him to Madina. He was tied to a pillar in the Prophet's Mosque. The Beloved came to him and asked: "O Samama! What do you think of yourself?" Samama replied: "O Muhammad! My condition is good. If you kill me, you will kill a man of blood. If you favour me with your generosity, you will favour a grateful one. If you desire wealth, name your price."

The Beloved left him and came back the next day — the same conversation. The third day, the same. Then the Beloved ordered the Companions to release him. Samama went to a nearby garden, bathed, returned to the mosque, and recited the Shahada. He said: "By Allah's oath! No face on earth was more hateful to me than yours. But today, no face is more beloved to me than yours. No religion was more detestable to me than yours. But today, no religion is more beloved to me than yours. No city was more despicable to me than yours. But today, no city is more beloved to me than yours.

"O Messenger of Allah! I was going to Makka for Umrah when your men captured me. What is your command?" The Beloved gave him glad tidings and told him to proceed to Makka for Umrah. When he reached Makka and began Tawaf, a Quraish man said: "O Samama! Have you become Sabi (apostate)?" Samama replied courageously: "I have not become an apostate. I have become a Muslim. And listen, O people of Makka! By Allah's oath! As long as the Messenger of Allah ﷺ does not permit, not a single grain of wheat from my land (Yamama) will reach you." (Bukhari)

#### The Assassination of Abu Raafe

Among the events of 6 AH is the killing of Abu Raafe, the Jewish chieftain. His name was Abdullah bin Abu Huqaiq (or Salam bin Al Huqaiq). He was an extremely wealthy and influential trader, but a bitter enemy of Islam, notorious for his disrespect towards the Prophet. This was the same man who, along with Huyay bin Akhtab, had gone to Makka and incited the Quraish and other tribes, gathering an army of 10,000 for the Battle of Khandaq. He was the one who spurred Abu Sufyan to become their commander.

Huyay bin Akhtab was killed after the Battle of Khandaq during Ghazwa-e-Bani Quraiza, but Abu Raafe escaped and remained actively engaged in harming Islam. The Aus and Khazraj tribes constantly competed in good deeds. Since the Aus tribe had killed the enemy of Allah, Ashraf Yahoodi, in 3 AH, the Khazraj now desired to kill Abu Raafe to earn equal merit.

Hazrat Abdullah bin Ateeq, Abdullah bin Anees, Abu Qatada, Haris bin Ribai, Masood bin Sinan, and Khuzai bin Aswad volunteered. The Beloved ﷺ permitted them, appointed Hazrat Abdullah bin Ateeq as their leader, and instructed them not to kill women or children.

Abdullah bin Ateeq reached Abu Raafe's palace at night, entered secretly, found his bed in the darkness, and killed him. As he was leaving, he fell from a ladder and broke his foot. He bound it with his turban and somehow made his way back to Madina. When he reported the killing to the Beloved, he was told to stretch out his foot. The Beloved passed his blessed hand over it, and the broken bone instantly healed. (Bukhari)

#### Other Small Expeditions of 6 AH

The following expeditions also took place before Hudaibiya, though their exact dates are disputed:
(1) Sariyya-e-Qurta (2) Ghazwa-e-Bani Lihyan (3) Sariyyatul Ghamar (4) Sariyya-e-Zaid towards Wadiyul Qura (5) Sariyya-e-Zaid towards Ayes (6) Sariyya-e-Zaid towards Wadiyul Qura (7) Sariyya-e-Ali towards Saad (8) Sariyya-e-Zaid towards Umme Qarafa (9) Sariyya-e-Ibne Rawaha (10) Sariyya-e-Ibne Muslima (11) Sariyya-e-Zaid towards Taraf (12) Sariyya-e-Akal wa Uraina (13) Ba'as Zamri

These names are recorded with some variation by different historians. In all these, the Beloved did not personally participate except where noted. (`Zurqani Alal Mawahib`)
