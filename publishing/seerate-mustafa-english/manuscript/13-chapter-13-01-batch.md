## Chapter 13: The Eighth Year of Hijra — 8 AH

### The Battle of Muta

Muta is a place in the land of Syria. In 8 AH, that magnificent battle between disbelief and faith took place here, in which only 3,000 devoted Muslims pitted their lives against a 100,000-strong army of the disbelievers. This battle has remained an eternal landmark in Islamic history, and in it some of the most resolute personalities among the noble Companions were honoured with martyrdom.

#### The Cause of the Battle

The Chief Prophet ﷺ had sent a letter to the ruler of Basra (or the Roman Caesar) through Hazrat Haris bin Umair. On the way, Shurhbeel bin Amr Ghassani, the ruler of Balqa and a tributary of Caesar, seized the Prophet's messenger and brutally tied him with rope and killed him. When this tragic news reached the court of prophethood, the blessed heart was deeply grieved. The Beloved immediately prepared an army of 3,000 Muslims, tied a white banner with his own blessed hand, and gave it to Hazrat Zaid bin Haarisa, appointing him commander. He instructed:

"If Zaid is martyred, Hazrat Ja'far bin Abi Talib will be the commander. If he too is honoured with martyrdom, then Hazrat Abdullah bin Rawaha will bear the banner. After him, the Muslim army may choose whomever they wish."

The Beloved himself accompanied the army to Saniyyatul Wida to see them off. He commanded the commander: "Go to the place where our messenger Hazrat Haris bin Umair was martyred. First invite the disbelievers there to Islam. If they accept, they are your brothers in faith. Otherwise, seek Allah's help and fight them."

As the army set out, the Muslims prayed aloud: "May Allah bring you back safely and successfully."

When the army had advanced some distance from Madina, news came that Caesar himself had camped in the land of Balqa with a massive army of 100,000 polytheists. Hearing this, Hazrat Zaid bin Haarisa ordered a halt, intending to send word to the Beloved and await orders. But Hazrat Abdullah bin Rawaha said:

"Our purpose is not victory or spoils. Our objective is martyrdom — for martyrdom is the believer's aim, not wealth nor conquest. And this noble goal can be attained at any time and in any condition."

His speech filled every fighter with the spirit of jihad. All marched forward into the land of Muta. There they saw a vast army in silk and gold embroidered uniforms, standing in battle readiness beyond measure. What comparison could 3,000 have against over 100,000? But the Muslims, trusting in Allah, stood firm.

#### The Battle Scene

First, the Muslim commander Hazrat Zaid bin Haarisa advanced and invited the disbelievers to Islam. Their response was a hail of arrows and sword strikes. Seeing this, the Muslims prepared for battle. Hazrat Zaid dismounted and plunged into the battlefield on foot. The Muslims fought with immense fervour. But the disbelievers surrounded Hazrat Zaid with spears and lances, and after fighting heroically, he was martyred.

Hazrat Ja'far bin Abi Talib immediately seized the banner of Islam. But a Roman polytheist struck him with such a sword blow that his body was cut in two. People later said: We saw his body. There were more than 90 wounds from spears and swords, every one on the front — not a single wound on his back.

After Hazrat Ja'far, Hazrat Abdullah bin Rawaha took the banner. His cousin offered him a piece of meat on a bone, saying: "Brother! You have not eaten anything. Have this." He took one bite when the disbelievers' overwhelming horde fell upon him. He threw away the bone, drew his sword, plunged into the enemy ranks reciting Rajaz verses, and fought with extreme bravery until, weakened by wounds, he fell and was honoured with the cup of martyrdom. (Bukhari)

Then, by the army's consensus, Hazrat Khalid bin Al Waleed took the banner. He fought with such valour that nine swords broke in his hand, one after another. Through his military skill and expertise, he extricated the Muslim army from the enemy's encirclement.

In this battle, twelve noble Companions were martyred:
(1) Hazrat Zaid bin Haarisa
(2) Hazrat Ja'far bin Abi Talib
(3) Hazrat Abdullah bin Rawaha
(4) Hazrat Masood bin Aus
(5) Hazrat Wahab bin Saad
(6) Hazrat Ubaad bin Qaia
(7) Hazrat Haris bin Nomaan
(8) Hazrat Suraqa bin Umar
(9) Hazrat Abu Kaleeb bin Umar
(10) Hazrat Jabir bin Umar
(11) Hazrat Umar bin Saad
(12) Hazrat Haubja Zibbi

The Muslim army killed many disbelievers, captured some spoils, and returned safely to Madina.

#### The Miracle of Prophetic Vision

While the battle raged at Muta, the Chief Prophet ﷺ in Madina witnessed the entire battlefield. All veils were lifted, and his prophetic eyes observed the events. Bukhari records that the Beloved informed his Companions of the martyrdoms of Zaid, Ja'far, and Abdullah bin Rawaha before the news arrived from the battlefield.

In a state of extreme grief, before the congregation of Companions, he said: "Zaid took the banner and was martyred. Then Ja'far took the banner and was martyred. Then Abdullah bin Rawaha took the banner and was martyred. Then the banner was taken by one of Allah's swords — Khalid bin Waleed." The Beloved continued giving these reports while tears flowed from his eyes. (Bukhari, Ghazwa-e-Muta)

Moosa bin Uqba writes in Maghazi: When Hazrat Ya'ala bin Umayya brought the news of Muta to the court of prophethood, the Beloved asked him: "Will you tell me the news, or shall I tell you?" Hazrat Ya'ala submitted: "You tell me, O Messenger of Allah." When the Beloved described the entire scene in detail, Hazrat Ya'ala said: "By the Being Who sent you with the truth, you have not left out a single thing that I could narrate." (`Zurqani`)

Hazrat Asma binte Umais, the wife of Hazrat Ja'far, relates: "I had bathed and adorned my children, applied oil and kohl, and was kneading dough to bake bread for them, when suddenly the Messenger of Allah ﷺ entered my home and said, 'Bring Ja'far's children to me.' When I presented them, he hugged and kissed them, and tears streamed from his eyes onto his blessed cheeks. I asked, 'Has any news come about Ja'far and his companions?' He replied, 'Yes. They have been martyred today.' Hearing this, I cried out, and my house filled with women. Then the Beloved went to his apartment and told the Mothers of the Believers, 'Prepare food for Ja'far's family.'"

Hazrat Khalid bin Waleed approached Madina with his army. The Beloved mounted his horse and went out to welcome them. The people of Madina, including small children, ran out to greet the returning mujahideen. Hazrat Hassan bin Saabit recited a heart-wrenching elegy for the martyrs of Muta that made everyone weep. (`Zurqani`)

Regarding Hazrat Ja'far, whose hands were severed during the battle, the Beloved said: "Allah has given Ja'far two wings in place of his hands, with which he flies in Paradise wherever he wishes." This is why Hazrat Abdullah bin Umar would greet Hazrat Ja'far's son, Hazrat Abdullah, saying: "Peace be upon you, O son of the one with two wings." (Bukhari)

### Sariyya-e-Khabat (The Expedition of Leaves)

Between the Battle of Muta and the Conquest of Makka, the Beloved sent several small detachments to various locations to defend against the disbelievers. Among these expeditions, Sariyya-e-Khabat is the most famous. Imam Bukhari calls it Ghazwa-e-Saiful Behar.

In Rajab, 8 AH, the Beloved appointed Hazrat Abu Ubaida bin Al Jarrah as commander of 300 Companions and sent them towards the seashore to keep watch over the mischief of the Juhaina tribe. Provisions ran so short that the commander would distribute one date per day to each mujahid. Eventually even the dates ran out, and the men, desperate with hunger, began eating tree leaves. This is why historians named it Sariyya-e-Khabat or Jaishul Khabat — for "Khabat" in Arabic means tree leaves.

#### A Miraculous Fish

Hazrat Jabir relates: We had to stay in this journey for about a month. When hunger became severe and we began eating leaves, Allah created a means of sustenance from the unseen. The waves of the sea cast ashore a fish so enormous that it was like a mountain. Three hundred Companions ate its meat for eighteen days, rubbed its fat on their bodies, and when they departed, they even carried its meat back to Madina.

When they reached the court of prophethood and related this incident, the Beloved said: "This was Allah's provision for you." He then asked for some of the fish and ate from it. The fish was so large that the commander, Hazrat Abu Ubaida, planted two of its ribs into the ground, and a loaded camel could pass beneath the arch they formed.

### The Conquest of Makka

(Ramadan, 8 AH — January 630 CE)

Ramadan, 8 AH is an immensely glorious chapter in the history of prophethood. This is the golden chapter whose splendour will bring joy to every believer's heart until the Day of Judgement. For it was on this date, eight years earlier, that the Chief of the Worlds had left Makka in the darkness of night with his Companion of the Cave, in extreme grief, calling his beloved city desolate. As he departed, he cast a longing glance at the sacred House of Allah and said: "O Makka! By Allah's oath! You are dearer to me than all the cities of the world. If my people had not driven me out, I would never have left you."

But eight years later, on this joyful date, he entered Makka as a great conqueror, entered the Kaaba, and through the beauty and majesty of his prostration, honoured the sacred House of Allah.

#### The Breaking of the Treaty by the Quraish

But the question arises: The Treaty of Hudaibiya stipulated ten years of peace. Why then, after only two years, did the Beloved march on Makka with a great army?

The answer is the Quraish's breach of faith and treachery against the Treaty of Hudaibiya.

As mentioned in the account of the Treaty of Hudaibiya, one of its terms was that any Arab tribe wishing to ally with the Quraish could do so, and any wishing to ally with the Prophet could do so.

Accordingly, the tribe of Bani Bakr allied with the Quraish, and the tribe of Bani Khuza'a allied with the Prophet. Both tribes were settled near Makka, but a long-standing enmity existed between them.

For some time, the Quraish and other Arab tribes had exhausted their strength fighting the Muslims. The peace brought by Hudaibiya gave them respite. Now the Bani Bakr saw an opportunity to settle old scores with Bani Khuza'a. Together with their allies the Quraish, they launched a completely sudden attack on Bani Khuza'a. All the Quraish chiefs — Ikrima bin Abi Jahl, Safwan bin Umayya, Suhail bin Amr, and other great leaders — openly participated in killing the Bani Khuza'a.

The helpless Bani Khuza'a could not withstand this terrible onslaught and fled for refuge to the sacred sanctuary of the Kaaba. The common people of Bani Bakr refrained from attacking inside the Haram, respecting its sanctity. But their chief, Naufal, was so consumed with vengeance that he continued killing Bani Khuza'a mercilessly even within the Haram, shouting: "This opportunity will never come again!"

These savage, beastly men trampled upon the sanctity of Allah's House and shed the blood of Bani Khuza'a within its boundaries. The Quraish too participated fully in this massacre and plunder. (`Zurqani`)

Clearly, the Quraish had practically broken the Hudaibiya covenant by this attack. Attacking Bani Khuza'a, who were allied with the Prophet, was tantamount to attacking the Prophet himself. Twenty-three men of Bani Khuza'a were killed in this attack.

### Seeking Help from the Chief of the Worlds

After this incident, the chief of Bani Khuza'a, Amr bin Salim Khuzai, came to Madina with a delegation of forty men to plead for help. This became the prelude to the Conquest of Makka.

Hazrat Bibi Maimuna relates: One night, the Chief Prophet was performing ablution in his apartment when suddenly, three times, he said in a loud voice: "Labbaik, Labbaik, Labbaik! (I am present for you, again and again!)" Then three times he said: "Nusrat, Nusrat, Nusrat! (Help has come to you!)"

When he finished ablution, I asked: "O Messenger of Allah! With whom were you speaking in solitude?" He replied: "O Maimuna! The matter has become grave. My allies, Bani Khuza'a, have been attacked by Bani Bakr and the Quraish. In this time of distress and helplessness, they have called out to me for help from afar, and I heard their call and have answered them to reassure them."

On the third day after this incident, when the Beloved went to the mosque for Fajr prayer, the oppressed people of Bani Khuza'a suddenly began reciting Rajaz verses in a loud voice. The Beloved and the noble Companions listened to their heart-rending appeal.

The poetry included these verses (translated meaning):

"O Allah! I remind Muhammad of the covenant between us and his forefathers of old. May Allah guide you aright. Help us fully. Call the servants of Allah, and they will come to help. Among those helpers is the Messenger of Allah, who is in a state of anger — if the stain of humiliation touches him, his countenance changes.

"Those people (Bani Bakr and Quraish) attacked us at the place of Wateer while we were asleep, and killed us mercilessly even in the state of bowing and prostration. Certainly, the Quraish have broken their promise to you and torn apart the strong covenant."

Hearing these verses, the Beloved consoled them: "Do not fear. I am ready to help you." (`Zurqani`)

### The Prophet's Love for Peace

Despite this, the Beloved sent a messenger to the Quraish with three options, asking them to accept any one:

(1) Pay blood money for the slain of Bani Khuza'a.
(2) Dissociate from Bani Bakr and abandon their support.
(3) Declare the Hudaibiya treaty null and void.

When the messenger presented these terms, Qurta bin Abde Amr, as the Quraish's representative, replied: "We will neither pay blood money nor abandon our allies Bani Bakr. However, the third term is acceptable to us — we declare the Hudaibiya treaty null and void."

But after the messenger left, the Quraish regretted their answer. Some chiefs went to Abu Sufyan and said: "If this matter is not resolved, Muhammad will surely attack us." Abu Sufyan told them: "My wife Hind binte Utba has seen a dream — a river of blood flowing from Hujoon to Khandama, which then suddenly disappeared." The Quraish considered this dream extremely ominous. Terrified, they pressured Abu Sufyan to go to Madina immediately and renew the Hudaibiya treaty.

#### Abu Sufyan's Efforts

Abu Sufyan rushed to Madina. First, he went to the home of his daughter, Hazrat Umme Habeeba (a Mother of the Believers). As he was about to sit on a bed, she quickly folded it away. Surprised, Abu Sufyan asked: "Daughter! Why did you remove the bed? Did you not consider me worthy of it?" She replied: "This is the Messenger of Allah's bed. You are a polytheist and impure. I could not bear you sitting on it." Stung by this, Abu Sufyan left hurt.

He then presented himself before the Beloved and stated his purpose. The Beloved gave no reply. Abu Sufyan then went to Hazrat Abu Bakr, Hazrat Umar, and Hazrat Ali. All of them said: "We can do nothing."

When Abu Sufyan approached Hazrat Ali, Hazrat Fatima and Hazrat Imam Hasan were also present. Abu Sufyan pleaded: "O Ali! You are very kind-hearted among the people. We have come with a purpose. Will we return empty-handed? We only want you to intercede for us with Muhammad."

Hazrat Ali said: "O Abu Sufyan! We have no authority to interfere in the Beloved's will and intention."

Desperate, Abu Sufyan turned to Hazrat Fatima: "O Fatima! This five-year-old child (Imam Hasan), if he could just say once that he has made peace between the two parties, this child would be called the chief of Arabia."

Hazrat Fatima replied: "What involvement do children have in these matters?"

Finally, Abu Sufyan said: "O Ali! The matter seems very difficult. Suggest some way out."

Hazrat Ali said: "I cannot give you any useful advice in this matter. But you are the chief of Bani Kinana. Go yourself and announce to the people that you have renewed the Hudaibiya treaty."

Abu Sufyan asked: "Can my unilateral declaration be of any use?"

Hazrat Ali replied: "Obviously, a unilateral declaration cannot be of any real use. But what other option do you have?"

Abu Sufyan went to the Prophet's Mosque and loudly announced: "I have renewed the treaty of Hudaibiya." But no Muslim responded.

Abu Sufyan returned to Makka. The Quraish asked: "What happened in Madina?" After hearing the whole story, they asked: "When you announced the renewal of the Hudaibiya treaty on your side, did Muhammad accept it?" Abu Sufyan said: "No." The Quraish said: "Then this is nothing. This is neither peace that we may sit in comfort nor war that we may prepare for battle."

### The March on Makka

After this, the Beloved ordered the people to prepare for war. He told Hazrat Aisha to prepare the weapons of war. He sent orders to allied tribes as well, but he told no one where he intended to strike — not even Hazrat Abu Bakr.

Hazrat Abu Bakr came to Aisha and saw her bringing out weapons. He asked: "Has the Beloved ordered this?" She replied: "Yes." He asked: "Do you know where he intends to go?" She said: "By Allah! I do not."

With utmost secrecy, the Beloved prepared for war, intending to take the people of Makka completely by surprise.

#### The Letter of Hazrat Hatib bin Balta'a

Hazrat Hatib bin Balta'a, a noble Companion, wrote a letter to the Quraish informing them: "The Messenger of Allah is preparing for war. Be on your guard." He sent this letter to Makka through a woman.

But Allah had granted His Beloved knowledge of the unseen. Through this divine knowledge, the Beloved learned of Hatib's action. He immediately sent Hazrat Ali, Hazrat Umar, Hazrat Zubair, and Hazrat Miqdad, instructing: "Go to Rauza-e-Khaakh. There you will find a woman with a letter. Seize that letter and bring it to me."

The four Companions rode swift horses to Rauza-e-Khaakh and found the woman. When they demanded the letter, she denied having any. Hazrat Ali said: "By Allah's oath! The Messenger of Allah never speaks falsehood, nor are we liars. Hand over the letter, or we will strip and search you."

Forced, the woman retrieved the letter from her hair bun. When the Companions brought it to the Beloved, he summoned Hazrat Hatib and asked: "O Hatib! What have you done?"

Hatib submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Do not be hasty with me. I have neither changed my faith nor become an apostate. The only reason I wrote this letter is that my wife and children are in Makka. I have no relatives there to look after them. All other mujahideen have family or tribes in Makka who care for their dependents. So I thought I would put the Quraish under an obligation so they would treat my family well.

"O Messenger of Allah! My faith is firm — Allah will surely defeat these disbelievers. This letter of mine cannot benefit them in any way."

The Beloved accepted Hatib's excuse. But Hazrat Umar, seeing the letter, was enraged beyond control. He submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Allow me to strike off the head of this hypocrite!" Other Companions too were filled with anger. But the Mercy of the Worlds showed not the slightest frown of displeasure. He said: "O Umar! Do you not know that Hatib is among the people of Badr? Allah has addressed the people of Badr saying: 'Do as you wish, for you are forgiven.'"

Hearing this, Umar's eyes filled with tears, and he fell silent, saying: "Allah and His Messenger know best."

On this occasion, the following verse of the Quran was revealed:

*"O you who believe! Do not take My enemy and your enemy as friends..."* (Al-Mumtahina: 1)

In any case, the Beloved forgave Hazrat Hatib. (Bukhari)

### The Attack on Makka

On 10th Ramadan, 8 AH, the Chief Prophet set out from Madina towards Makka with an army of 10,000. Some narrations say 12,000 — but there is no contradiction. It is possible that 10,000 left Madina, and other tribes joined on the way, making it 12,000 by the time they reached Makka.

The Beloved and all the noble Companions were fasting. When they reached "Kadeed," he asked for water, and while mounted on his camel, in full view of the entire army, he drank during the daytime, ordering everyone to break their fast. So the Beloved and his Companions did not fast due to being on a journey and in jihad. (Bukhari)

#### Meeting with Hazrat Abbas

When they reached Zuhafa, the Beloved's uncle Hazrat Abbas came to serve him with his family. He had become Muslim long before this and was residing in Makka by the Beloved's permission, holding the honoured position of providing Zamzam water to pilgrims.

Two other men came: Haris bin Abdul Muttalib's son (named Abu Sufyan bin Al Haris, who was also Hazrat Umme Salama's stepbrother). When the Beloved learned of their arrival, he initially refused to meet them, for they had caused him much hurt — particularly Abu Sufyan bin Al Haris, his cousin, who had been among his most devoted companions before prophethood but afterward composed such shameful and obscene satires against the Beloved that his heart was wounded.

Hazrat Umme Salama strongly interceded for them. Abu Sufyan bin Al Haris said: "If the Messenger of Allah does not forgive me, I will take my small children to the desert of Arabia and perish there with them from hunger and thirst."

Hazrat Umme Salama then submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Will your cousin and your aunt's son remain more unfortunate than all mankind? Will they receive no share of your mercy?"

These heart-rending words from his loving wife stirred the ocean of mercy in the heart of the Mercy of the Worlds. Then Hazrat Ali advised them: "Both of you go and suddenly stand before the court of prophethood and say what Hazrat Yusuf's brothers said:

*'By Allah! Indeed Allah has preferred you above us, and we were indeed sinful.'* (Yusuf: 91)"

The two men did exactly this. The Mercy of the Worlds' blessed countenance lit up with a thousand stars of compassion, and he replied with the very words of Hazrat Yusuf:

*'There is no reproach upon you today. May Allah forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of the merciful.'* (Yusuf: 92)

After being forgiven, Abu Sufyan bin Al Haris composed verses in praise of the Beloved and apologized for his past satires. For the rest of his life, he remained a true and steadfast Muslim, but due to shame, he could never raise his head before the Beloved. The Beloved loved him greatly and said: "I hope that Abu Sufyan bin Al Haris will take my uncle Hazrat Hamza's place."

#### Miles of Fire

The Muslim army camped at Marruzzahran, one stage from Makka. The Beloved ordered that every mujahid light a separate fire. When 10,000 mujahideen lit their fires, for miles around, the entire plain of Marruzzahran was ablaze with light.

#### The Spies of the Quraish

Although the Quraish's spies had learned that armies were approaching from Madina, they sent Abu Sufyan bin Harb, Hakeem bin Hizam, and Budail bin Warqa as spies to ascertain the exact situation.

Hazrat Abbas was extremely anxious about the Quraish's fate. He thought: if the Beloved enters Makka as a conqueror with this great army, the Quraish will be annihilated. So he mounted the Beloved's white mule and set out for Makka at night to warn them and persuade them to seek forgiveness and make peace.

According to a Bukhari narration, the Quraish knew the Beloved had left Madina but did not know the army had reached Marruzzahran. The three spies were still searching for the Muslim army when they neared Marruzzahran and saw fire stretching for miles.

Astonished, Abu Sufyan said: "I have never seen such widespread fire burning in this plain in my life. Which tribe could this be?" Budail said: "It seems to be Bani Amr." Abu Sufyan said: "No. Bani Amr cannot be so numerous as to fill the entire plain of Marruzzahran with their fires."

Hazrat Abbas met the three, and Abu Sufyan asked: "O Abbas! Where are you coming from? What is this fire?" Abbas replied: "This is the fire of the Messenger of Allah's army."

Hazrat Abbas told Abu Sufyan: "Mount behind me on my mule, otherwise the Muslims will see you and kill you immediately." As they entered the camp, Hazrat Umar and some other Muslims who were guarding saw Abu Sufyan. Umar could not control his vengeful spirit and exclaimed: "There is Allah's enemy, Abu Sufyan!" He rushed to the court of prophethood and submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Abu Sufyan has fallen into our hands. Permit me to cut off his head."

But Hazrat Abbas also arrived, bringing the three polytheists, and interceded for their lives, saying: "O Messenger of Allah! I have granted them all protection."

#### Abu Sufyan Accepts Islam

Abu Sufyan's enmity toward Islam was no secret — the repeated injuries to the Beloved in Makka, the repeated attacks on Madina, the incitement of Arab tribes, the numerous conspiracies to assassinate the Beloved, the collusion with Jews and all Arab disbelievers to annihilate Islam — these were unforgivable crimes that cried out demanding his execution.

But the kind Prophet, whom the Quran calls "Ra'oofur Raheem" (Kind and Merciful), whispered mercy into Abu Sufyan's ears: "O criminal! Do not fear. This is not the court of worldly kings. This is the court of the Mercy of the Worlds."

According to a Bukhari narration, Abu Sufyan immediately accepted Islam upon presenting himself and thus saved his life. (Bukhari)

Another narration says Hakeem bin Hizam and Budail bin Warqa accepted Islam that very night, but Abu Sufyan recited the Kalima the following morning. (`Zurqani`)

Yet another narration records a conversation between them:

The Beloved: "O Abu Sufyan! Do you not yet believe that Allah is One?"
Abu Sufyan: "Why not? If there were another god, he would have helped us today."
The Beloved: "Do you have any doubt that I am Allah's Messenger?"
Abu Sufyan: "As to that, I still have some doubt."

But afterward, he recited the Kalima. At the time his faith may have been shaky, but eventually he became a true Muslim. He later participated in the Battle of Taif in the Muslim army and lost one of his eyes fighting the disbelievers.
