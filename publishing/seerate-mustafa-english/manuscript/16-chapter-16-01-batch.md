## Chapter 16: The Eleventh Year of Hijra — 11 AH

### The Army of Usama (Jaish-e-Usama)

This army was also called Sariyya-e-Usama. It was the very last military expedition that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ ordered to be dispatched. On Monday, the 26th of Safar, 11 AH, the Holy Prophet ﷺ ordered preparations for war against the Romans. The next day, he summoned Sayyidina Usama bin Zaid (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) and said: "I have appointed you as the commander of this army. Proceed to the place 'Ubna', where your father was martyred. March with great speed and launch a surprise attack upon the disbelievers so that they cannot prepare for war." Despite his own blessed self being unwell, the Prophet ﷺ himself tied the banner with his own blessed hands and handed this standard of Islam to Sayyidina Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), instructing: "Fight in the name of Allah and in the path of Allah, and wage war against the disbelievers."

Sayyidina Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) appointed Sayyidina Buraida bin Al Husaib (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) as the standard-bearer. He left Madina and encamped at a place called "Jaraf", one league away, so that the entire army could assemble there. The Holy Prophet ﷺ ordered all the eminent personalities among the Ansar and Muhajirun to also join this army.

Some people found it burdensome that such an army, which included the great leaders and pillars of the Ansar and Muhajirun, should be commanded by an inexperienced youth not more than twenty years old. When the Prophet ﷺ learned of this objection, it deeply affected his noble heart. Despite his illness, with a bandaged head and wrapped in a shawl, he ascended the pulpit and delivered a sermon in which he said: "If you are objecting to Usama's commandership, then you had also objected to his father's commandership before this. By Allah! His father (Zaid bin Haritha) was worthy of commandership, and after him, his son (Usama bin Zaid) is also worthy of commandership. He is among the dearest of people to me, just as his father was among the dearest of my companions. Therefore, accept my advice regarding Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), for he is among the best of you."

After delivering this sermon, the Prophet ﷺ went to his home, and his illness intensified further.

Sayyidina Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), fulfilling the prophetic command, proceeded to the place of Jaraf, where the Islamic army continued to assemble until a great force was ready. On the 10th of Rabiul Awwal, 11 AH, the distinguished men who were to depart for Jihad came to seek leave from the Prophet ﷺ. After bidding farewell, they went to Jaraf. The next day, the Prophet's ﷺ illness grew even more severe. Sayyidina Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) also came to the noble presence to inquire about the Prophet's health and to seek leave. The Prophet ﷺ saw Sayyidina Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) but due to weakness could not speak. He repeatedly raised his blessed hands toward the sky and passed his sacred hand over Usama's body. Sayyidina Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "From this, I understood that the Prophet ﷺ was praying for me."

After this, Sayyidina Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) took leave and returned to his army. On the 12th of Rabiul Awwal, 11 AH, he announced the order to march. He was preparing to mount when a messenger from his mother, Sayyida Umme Aiman (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), arrived with the news that the Prophet ﷺ was in the throes of departure. Upon hearing this soul-stirring news, Sayyidina Usama, Sayyidina Umar, Sayyidina Abu Ubaida (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum), and others immediately came to Madina. They saw that the Prophet ﷺ was in the agony of departure, and that very day at noon or mid-afternoon, he passed away.

Hearing this news, Sayyidina Usama's army returned to Madina. However, when Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) assumed the mantle of caliphate, despite the opposition of some people, he dispatched that army in the final days of Rabiul Aakhir. Sayyidina Usama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) proceeded to "Ubna", where after a very bloody battle, the Islamic army emerged victorious. He killed his father's murderer and other disbelievers, and after forty days returned to Madina with countless spoils of war.

(Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, pp. 409-411; Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 107-112)

### The Demise of the Beloved Prophet ﷺ

The Chief of Mercy for All Worlds, the Prophet ﷺ, came into this world solely to deliver Allah's final and conclusive message — the commandments of the religion of Islam — to His servants and to establish Allah's ultimate proof. How did the Prophet ﷺ accomplish this mission? And what success did he achieve? The concise answer is this: since this world came into existence, thousands of prophets and messengers (peace be upon them) came to this world to accomplish this great task. But if the preaching achievements of all the prophets and messengers are gathered together, they would appear before the preaching triumphs of the Chief of the Universe ﷺ like a lamp before the radiant sun, like a speck before a vast desert, like a drop before a mighty ocean.

The preaching of the Prophet ﷺ brought about such a revolution in the world that it elevated every lowliness of existence to the heights of perfection, transformed the earth of humiliation into the sky of honor. The sacred and luminous palace of the true religion, for whose construction all the prophets and messengers from Adam (Alaihissalam) to Eisa (Alaihissalam) were sent as architects, was perfected by the Seal of the Prophets ﷺ to such an extent that Allah Almighty stamped it with "Perfected Religion."

When Islam was completed and the purpose of the Prophet's ﷺ coming into this world was fulfilled, the time arrived for the fulfillment of Allah's firm promise (the verse: "Indeed, you are to die, and they are to die").

### The Prophet's Knowledge of His Demise

The Prophet ﷺ had gained knowledge of his departure long before, and he had informed people of it on various occasions. During the Farewell Pilgrimage, he bid farewell to the people saying: "Perhaps I will not be able to perform Hajj with you after this."

Similarly, in the sermon of Ghadir Khum, words of a similar nature were uttered by his blessed tongue. Although in both sermons he used the word "La'alla" (perhaps) to somewhat veil the news of his departure, upon returning from the Farewell Pilgrimage, in the sermons he delivered, he did not use the word "perhaps" but clearly and with certainty informed people of his departure.

In Sahih Bukhari, it is narrated from Sayyidina Uqba bin Aamir (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) that one day the Prophet ﷺ came out of his home and prayed over the graves of the martyrs of Uhud in the manner one prays over the deceased. Then he turned and ascended the pulpit, saying: "I am your forerunner (one who dies before you) and I am your witness. By Allah! I am looking at my Fountain (Kausar) at this very moment." (Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 975)

This incident occurred before the onset of his final illness. At the time of relating this incident, the Prophet ﷺ had already gained certain knowledge of when and at what time he would depart from this world. After the final illness began, he clearly informed his daughter Sayyida Fatima (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) about his departure without using the word "perhaps". In Sahih Bukhari, it is narrated that during his final illness, the Prophet ﷺ called Sayyida Fatima (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) and spoke to her quietly, at which she wept. Then he called her again and spoke to her quietly, at which she laughed. When the pure wives asked her about this, she said: "The Prophet ﷺ told me quietly that he would die in this illness, so I wept. Then he told me quietly that after him, I would be the first among his family to join him, so I laughed." (Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 638)

In any case, the Prophet ﷺ had knowledge of the time of his departure before it occurred. And why should he not, when Allah had informed him of the times of death of other people? If Allah, the Knower of the Unseen, informed him of the time of his own departure beforehand, there is nothing improbable in this. Allah Almighty had granted the Prophet ﷺ knowledge of what has been, what is, and what will be. We have elaborated on this subject in our book "Qur'ani Taqreerein."

### The Onset of Illness

There is disagreement among historians as to when the illness began and for how many days the Prophet ﷺ remained ill. In any case, on the 20th or 22nd of Safar, 11 AH, the Prophet ﷺ went to Jannatul Baqi (the cemetery of the common Muslims) in the middle of the night. When he returned, his blessed temperament became unwell. This was on the day of Sayyida Maimuna's (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) turn. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 417; Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 110)

On Monday, the Prophet's ﷺ illness became very severe. At his request, all the pure wives gave permission for him to stay in the apartment of Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). Thus, Sayyidina Abbas and Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) supported him and brought him to the blessed chamber of Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). As long as he had strength, he would himself lead the prayers in Masjid-e-Nabawi. When his weakness grew extreme, he ordered that Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) should lead the prayer in his place. Thus, Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) led seventeen prayers.

One day, at the time of Zuhr prayer, the Prophet ﷺ felt some improvement. He ordered that seven water skins be poured over him. When he had performed Ghusl, Sayyidina Abbas and Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) held his blessed arm and brought him to the mosque. Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) was leading the prayer. Hearing the footsteps, he began to step back, but the Prophet ﷺ signaled him to stay and sat beside him, leading the prayer. Seeing the Prophet ﷺ, Sayyidina Abu Bakr (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) and the other followers continued performing the acts of prayer.

After the prayer, the Prophet ﷺ delivered a sermon in which he gave many instructions and commandments of Islam, spoke about the virtues and rights of the Ansar, and recited Surah Wal-Asr and another verse.

(Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 425; Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 639)

He had seven dinars kept at home. The Prophet ﷺ said to Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha): "Bring those dinars so that I may spend them in the path of Allah." Thus, through Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), the Prophet ﷺ distributed those dinars and did not leave behind a single atom of gold or silver in his home.

(Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 424)

The Prophet's illness fluctuated. On the day of his departure, Monday, his condition was relatively better. His room adjoined the mosque. He lifted the curtain and saw the people praying Fajr. Seeing this, he smiled with joy. The people thought he wanted to come to the mosque. Overcome with happiness, everyone became restless, but the Prophet ﷺ signaled them to stop, entered his room, and drew the curtain. This was the last time the noble Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) saw the blessed countenance of prophethood. Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "The Prophet's blessed face appeared like a page of the Quran" — that is, it had become radiantly white. (Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 640)

After this, he began to lose consciousness repeatedly. In her intense grief, Sayyida Fatima Zahra (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) exclaimed: "Oh, the distress of my father!" The Prophet ﷺ said: "O my daughter! Your father will have no distress after today." (Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 641)

Then, repeatedly, the Prophet ﷺ was saying: "With those upon whom Allah has bestowed His favor" and sometimes he would say: "O Allah! The Highest Companion (Ar-Rafeeq al-A'la)" and also recite, and he said: "Indeed, death has its agonies."

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "When the Prophet ﷺ was healthy, he often used to say: 'Prophets are given a choice — whether to accept departure or to continue living in this world.' When these words were uttered by the blessed tongue of the Prophet ﷺ, I understood that he had chosen the Hereafter." (Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 640)

Shortly before his departure, Sayyida Ayesha's brother, Abdurrahman bin Abu Bakr (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), arrived holding a fresh miswak. The Prophet ﷺ looked at it intently. Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) understood that he desired the miswak. She immediately took the miswak, softened it with her teeth, and placed it in his blessed hand. The Prophet ﷺ used the miswak. It was mid-afternoon when a rattling sound began to be heard from his blessed chest. Then his blessed lips moved, and the people heard these words: "Prayer! And take care of those whom your right hands possess."

Nearby was a basin of water. He repeatedly dipped his hand into it and wiped his blessed face, reciting the Kalima. Sometimes he would draw the blessed sheet over his face and sometimes remove it. Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was sitting with his sacred head resting against her chest. Suddenly, the Prophet ﷺ raised his hand, made a gesture with his finger, and said three times: "Not this — rather, the Highest Companion." These words were on his blessed tongue when, alas, his blessed hand fell limp and his eyes remained open, fixed on the ceiling. His sacred soul had departed to the realm of purity.

(Bukhari, Vol. 2, pp. 640-641)

*Surely, to Allah we belong and to Him we shall return.*
*O Allah, send blessings and peace upon our master Muhammad, his family, and all his Companions.*

There is considerable disagreement among historians regarding the exact date of departure, but all scholars of Seerah agree that it was a Monday in the month of Rabiul Awwal. It is commonly accepted that on Tuesday, the 12th of Rabiul Awwal, 11 AH, in the mid-afternoon, the Prophet ﷺ passed away. (And Allah knows best.)
