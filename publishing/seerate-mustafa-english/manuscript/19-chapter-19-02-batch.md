## Chapter 19: The Pure Wives (Continued)

### Sayyida Hafsa (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

The respected father of the Mother of the Believers, Sayyida Hafsa (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), is the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Umar Ibnul Khattab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), and her respected mother is Sayyida Zainab bint Maz'oon (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), who is a famous female Companion. Sayyida Hafsa was first married to Sayyidina Khunais bin Huzafa Sahmi (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), and she migrated to Madina with her husband. However, her husband was wounded in the Battle of Badr or Uhud and passed away, leaving her a widow. Then, in 3 AH, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ married her, and she was honored to reside in the prophetic household as a Mother of the Believers.

She was a very distinguished, high-spirited, and generous-hearted lady. In speaking the truth, being articulate, and possessing insight, she had inherited the temperament of her great father. She often observed fasts and remained occupied with the recitation of the Holy Quran and other forms of worship. There was some sternness in her temperament, which is why the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Umar bin Al Khattab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), was always concerned lest her harsh speech cause any hurt to the Holy Prophet ﷺ. He would repeatedly say to her: "O Hafsa! Whatever you need, ask me for it. Beware, never demand anything from the Holy Prophet ﷺ, and never, ever cause him any hurt. Remember, if the Holy Prophet ﷺ becomes displeased with you, you will fall into Allah's wrath."

Along with being a great worshipper, she also held a distinguished rank in jurisprudence and hadith. She narrated 60 hadith from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, of which 5 are recorded in Sahih Bukhari, while the rest are recorded in other books of hadith.

Many Companions and Successors appear in the list of her students in hadith, among whom her own brother, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), is very famous. She passed away in Madina in Shaban 45 AH, during the governorship of Muawiya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) when Marwan bin Hakam was the governor of Madina. He led her funeral prayer and carried her bier for some distance. Then Sayyidina Abu Huraira (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) continued shouldering her bier to the grave. Her two brothers, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umar and Sayyidina Aasim bin Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma), and her three nephews, Sayyidina Salim bin Abdullah, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abdullah, and Sayyidina Hamza bin Abdullah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum), lowered her into the grave. She was laid to rest in Jannatul Baqi beside the other pure wives. At the time of her death, she was 60 or 63 years old. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 236-237)

### Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

Her name is Hind, and her kunyah is "Umme Salma," but she is more famous by her kunyah. Her father's name is "Huzaifa" (according to some historians, "Sahl"). However, all historians agree that her mother is "Aatika bint Aamir." She was first married to Sayyidina Abu Salma Abdullah bin Abdul Asad (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), who was the Holy Prophet's foster brother. Both husband and wife accepted Islam soon after the proclamation of prophethood. They were among the first to migrate to Abyssinia.

Later, they returned from Abyssinia to Makka and intended to migrate to Madina. Sayyidina Abu Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) saddled a camel, seated Sayyida Umme Salma and their son Salma in the howdah, and set off. However, the Banu Mughira, Sayyida Umme Salma's family, rushed after them and said: "We will never let this girl of our family go to Madina," and forcibly pulled her off the camel. Seeing this, Sayyidina Abu Salma's family also became enraged and said: "You are stopping Umme Salma only because she is your family's daughter, but we will never let her son 'Salma' stay with you, because he is a member of our family." Saying this, they snatched the child from his mother's lap.

However, Sayyidina Abu Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) did not abandon his intention of migration. He left both his wife and child and went alone to Madina. Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), separated from her husband and child, sat on the rocky ground of Makka, weeping bitterly from morning to evening. Seeing her condition, one of her paternal cousins took pity on her. He persuaded the Banu Mughira: "Why have you separated this poor woman from her husband and child? Why don't you allow her to go to her husband with her child?" Eventually, the Banu Mughira agreed to let her go to Madina. Then the Banu Abdul Asad (Abu Salma's family) also returned the child to her. Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) took her child in her lap, mounted the camel, and set off alone for Madina.

When she reached the place called "Tan'im," she met Usman bin Talha, a very noble and respected man of Makka. He asked: "O Umme Salma! Where are you headed?" She replied: "I am going to Madina to my husband." He asked: "Is there no one with you?" Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) replied in a pained voice: "No, there is no one with me except Allah and this child." Hearing this, Usman bin Talha's sense of honor was stirred, and he said: "By Allah's oath! It does not befit me to leave a noble woman like you, the wife of a noble man, alone." Saying this, he took the camel's reins in his hand and began walking on foot.

Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "By Allah's oath! I have never found any Arab more noble than Usman bin Talha. Whenever we halted at a place, he would lie down separately under a tree, while I slept near my camel. When we were to depart, I would take my child in my lap and mount the camel, and he would take the reins and start walking." In this manner, he accompanied her all the way to Quba, and from there he returned to Makka, saying: "Now go on; your husband is in this village." Thus, Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) reached Madina safely. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 239)

Both husband and wife lived peacefully in Madina. However, in 4 AH, when her husband, Sayyidina Abu Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), passed away, despite having several children, the Holy Prophet ﷺ married her. She came to live in the prophetic household with her children and was honored with the noble title of Mother of the Believers.

Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was an unparalleled example of beauty, intelligence, and wisdom. Imamul Haramain states: "I do not know any woman whose opinion always proved correct." On the day of the Treaty of Hudaibiya, when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ ordered the people to sacrifice their animals and remove their ihram and return to Madina without performing Umrah (as this was the condition of the treaty), the people were so grieved and distressed that not a single person was willing to sacrifice. The Holy Prophet ﷺ felt spiritual distress at the behavior of the noble Companions. He discussed the matter with Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), and she advised: "O Messenger of Allah! Do not say anything to anyone. Go and sacrifice your own animal and remove your ihram yourself." The Holy Prophet ﷺ did exactly that. When the noble Companions saw that the Holy Prophet ﷺ had removed his ihram, they realized the Prophet ﷺ would never break the treaty of Hudaibiya. So all the Companions also sacrificed their animals and removed their ihram, and everyone returned to Madina.

Along with beauty, intelligence, and sound judgment, her expertise in jurisprudence and hadith was particularly distinguished. She narrated 378 hadith from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Many Companions and Successors were her students in hadith. Among her students are included Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas and Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum). She passed away in Madina at the age of 84, and the year of her death is 53 AH. Sayyidina Abu Huraira (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) led her funeral prayer, and she was buried in Jannatul Baqi in the cemetery of the pure wives. Some historians say her death occurred in 59 AH, Ibrahim Harbi said 62 AH, and some say after 63 AH. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 238-242) And Allah knows best.

### Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

Her real name is "Ramla." She is the daughter of the leader of Makka, Abu Sufyan bin Harb, and her mother's name is Safiyya bint Abul Aas, who is the paternal aunt of the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Usman (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu).

She was first married to Ubaidullah bin Jahsh. Both husband and wife accepted Islam and migrated to Abyssinia. However, in Abyssinia, such misfortune befell her husband Abdullah bin Jahsh that he apostatized from Islam and became a Christian, and he died drinking wine in that state of apostasy.

Ibn Sa'ad narrates from Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) that in Abyssinia, she saw a dream one night in which her husband Ubaidullah bin Jahsh's appearance had suddenly become extremely ugly and disfigured. She was very frightened by this dream. When morning came, she saw that her husband Ubaidullah bin Jahsh had apostatized from Islam and adopted the Christian religion. Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) warned her husband by telling him her dream and called him back to Islam, but that ill-fated man paid no heed and died in a state of apostasy. Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) remained steadfast in her Islam.

When the Holy Prophet ﷺ learned of her situation, it grieved his tender heart. To console her, he sent Sayyidina Amr bin Umayya Zamri (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) to the King of Abyssinia, the Negus, with a letter saying: "You become my representative and perform my marriage with Sayyida Umme Habeeba." When the Negus received this prophetic decree, he sent his special maidservant, named "Abraha," to Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) and informed her of the Messenger of Allah's ﷺ proposal. Sayyida Umme Habeeba was so overjoyed at this glad tiding that she gave some of her jewelry to the maidservant Abraha as a reward. She appointed Sayyidina Khalid bin Saeed bin Abul Aas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), her maternal cousin, as her representative for the marriage and sent him to the Negus.

The Negus solemnized the marriage assembly in his royal palace. He invited Sayyidina Jafar bin Abi Talib and other noble Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) who were present in Abyssinia to this gathering. He himself delivered the sermon and, before everyone, performed the marriage of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ with Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). He paid 400 dinars as dower from his own pocket and handed it over to Sayyidina Khalid bin Saeed (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) then and there. When the noble Companions were about to rise from the marriage assembly, the Negus said: "Please remain seated. This is the way of the prophets — food is served at weddings." Saying this, the Negus called for food, and all the noble Companions ate to their fill and then departed to their homes. Thereafter, the Negus sent Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) to the Holy Prophet ﷺ in Madina with Sayyidina Shurahbil bin Hasana (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). She entered the prophetic sanctuary and received the noble title of Mother of the Believers.

Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was a lady of pure character, possessing commendable qualities, extremely high-spirited, generous-natured, and very strong in faith. When her father, Abu Sufyan, was still in a state of disbelief and came to Madina for the renewal of the Treaty of Hudaibiya, he unceremoniously entered her house and sat on the prophetic bed. Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) paid no regard to her father and removed him from the bed, saying: "This is the bed of prophethood. I can never allow an impure polytheist to sit on this pure bed."

Sayyida Umme Habeeba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) narrated 65 hadith from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Among them, 2 hadith are found in both Bukhari and Muslim, and one hadith is narrated exclusively by Muslim. The remaining hadith are found in other books of hadith. Among her students, her brother Sayyidina Amir Muawiya, her daughter Sayyida Habeeba, and her nephew Abu Sufyan bin Saeed (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) are very famous.

She passed away in Madina in 44 AH and was buried in Jannatul Baqi near the other pure wives. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 242-245; Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, pp. 481-482)

### Sayyida Zainab bint Jahsh (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

She is the daughter of the Holy Prophet's ﷺ paternal aunt, Sayyida Umaima bint Abdul Muttalib. The Holy Prophet ﷺ had married her to his freed slave, Sayyidina Zaid bin Haarisa (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). However, because Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was a very distinguished lady of the Quraish tribe, unmatched in beauty among Quraish women, and although the Holy Prophet had freed Zaid and made him his adopted son, he had formerly been a slave, so Sayyida Zainab was not happy with him. Discord frequently arose between husband and wife, to the extent that Sayyidina Zaid (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) divorced her.

This incident naturally pained the Holy Prophet's tender heart. When her waiting period (iddat) ended, solely to console Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), the Holy Prophet ﷺ sent her a proposal of marriage. It is narrated that upon receiving this glad tiding, Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) performed two rak'ats of prayer and, prostrating, prayed: "O Allah! Your Messenger has sent me a proposal of marriage. If I am worthy in Your sight to enter his wedlock, then O Allah! Perform my marriage with him." Her prayer was immediately accepted, and this verse was revealed:

> فَلَمَّا قَضَىٰ زَيْدٌ مِّنْهَا وَطَرًا زَوَّجْنَاكَهَا

**Translation:** "When Zaid fulfilled his need of her (divorced her and her iddat ended), We gave her (Zainab) to you in marriage." (Al-Ahzab: 37)

After the revelation of this verse, the Holy Prophet ﷺ smiled and said: "Who will go to Zainab and give her the good news that Allah has performed my marriage with her?" Hearing this, one of the Prophet's maidservants rushed to Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) and gave her the glad tidings by reciting this verse. Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was so overjoyed at this good news that she removed her jewelry and gave it to the maidservant as a reward, fell into prostration, and observed fasting continuously for two months in gratitude for this blessing.

It is narrated that the Holy Prophet ﷺ thereafter went unexpectedly to Sayyida Zainab's house. She submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! You have married me without a sermon and without witnesses?" He replied: "Your marriage with me has been performed by Allah Almighty, and Sayyidina Jibreel (Alaihissalam) and other angels are witnesses to this marriage." The Holy Prophet ﷺ gave such a grand wedding feast (Walima) for this marriage that he did not give such a grand feast for any other pure wife. At the Walima for his marriage with Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), the Prophet ﷺ fed the noble Companions bread and meat.

Some hadith are also narrated concerning her virtues. It is narrated that one day the Holy Prophet said: "After my death, among my wives, the one with the longest hand will meet me first." Hearing this, all the pure wives measured their hands against a stick. Sayyida Sauda's hand turned out to be the longest. However, after the Holy Prophet's ﷺ passing, among the pure wives, Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was the first to die. Then the Companions realized that "long hand" meant abundant charity, for Sayyida Zainab would perform handiwork and give its income in charity to the poor and needy.

She was of a very generous nature and possessed a very hot temperament. Once, Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) sent her some garments as a gift. She distributed them all among her relatives and did not keep any for herself. When the Holy Prophet heard this, he was extremely pleased.

When she passed away, people realized that the "long hand" meant abundant charity, because her handiwork and charity were exceptional. Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) himself led her funeral prayer and she was buried in Jannatul Baqi. She passed away in 20 AH or 21 AH at the age of 53 in Madina. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, pp. 476-478, etc.)
