## Chapter 7: The Second Year of Hijra — 2 AH

Like the first year of Hijra, the second year of Hijra also witnessed many important events. Some of the major events are as follows.

### The Change of the Qibla

As long as the Beloved ﷺ remained in Makka, he continued to pray facing the Kaaba. But after the migration, when he came to Madina Munawwara, Allah Ta'ala commanded him to face Baitul Muqaddas as his Qibla in prayers. So for sixteen or seventeen months, he continued to pray facing Baitul Muqaddas. But the desire of his heart was that the Kaaba itself should be made the Qibla. He would often raise his face towards the sky, waiting for divine revelation for this.

One day, Allah Ta'ala revealed this verse of the Quran to fulfil the heartfelt wish of His Beloved:

*"We see the turning of your face to the heaven. So We shall surely turn you to a Qibla with which you shall be pleased. So turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque."* (Al-Baqarah: 144)

So the Master of the Prophets was leading the Zuhr prayer at the mosque of the Bani Salamah tribe. In the very state of prayer, this revelation came. During the prayer itself, the Beloved turned away from Baitul Muqaddas and faced the Kaaba. All the followers followed him. This mosque, where this incident occurred, is called "Masjidul Qiblatain". Even today, this historic mosque is a place of visitation for the elite and the common, located approximately 2 kilometres north-west of the city of Madina.

This change of Qibla is called "Tahweel-e-Qibla". The change of Qibla caused great distress to the Jews. As long as the Beloved ﷺ continued to pray facing Baitul Muqaddas, the Jews were very happy and used to say with pride, "Muhammad also worships facing our Qibla." But when the Qibla changed, the Jews became so angry and displeased that they began to taunt: "Since Muhammad opposes us in every matter, he has changed the Qibla merely to oppose us."

Similarly, the group of hypocrites also began to find all sorts of faults and raise all kinds of objections. To silence both these groups and shut their mouths, Allah the Generous revealed these verses:

*"The foolish among the people will say, 'What has turned them away from their Qibla, which they used to face?' Say, 'To Allah belongs the east and the west. He guides whom He wills to a straight path.'"* (Al-Baqarah: 142)

*"And thus We have made you a middle nation that you may be witnesses over the people and the Messenger may be a witness over you. And We did not make the Qibla which you used to face except that We might make evident who would follow the Messenger from who would turn back on his heels. And indeed, it is difficult except for those whom Allah has guided. And Allah would never let your faith be lost. Indeed, Allah is, to the people, Kind and Merciful."* (Al-Baqarah: 143)

In the first verse, the objection of the Jews was answered: in the worship of Allah, no particular direction is essential. East, west, north, south — all directions are equal for His worship. Allah Ta'ala may appoint whichever direction He wishes as the Qibla for His servants. Therefore, no one has any right to object.

In the second verse, the hypocrites were silenced. After the change of Qibla, they had begun to propagate everywhere that the Prophet of Islam himself was confused about his religion — sometimes he regarded Baitul Muqaddas as the Qibla, sometimes he said the Kaaba is the Qibla. The verse explained the wisdom behind the change of Qibla: the hypocrites, who were merely pretenders to Islam and used to pray, turned away as soon as the Qibla changed and deviated from Islam. In this way, it became clear who is sincere in Islam, who is a hypocrite, who follows the Messenger of Allah, and who turns away from the religion.

### The Beginning of Battles

Until now, the Beloved ﷺ had only been commanded to invite people to Islam with proofs and good counsel. The Muslims were commanded to bear patiently the harm inflicted by the disbelievers. So the disbelievers had inflicted great mountains of oppression and tyranny upon the Muslims, but the Muslims never took up arms for revenge. They always bore the harm and troubles of the disbelievers with patience and forbearance.

But after the migration, when the entire Arabia and the Jews became mortal enemies of this handful of Muslims and resolved to annihilate them, Allah the Holy gave the Muslims permission: "If people initiate war against you, you too may fight them."

The 12th of Safar, 2 AH, is that memorable day in the history of Islam when Allah the Creator gave the Muslims permission to draw the sword against the disbelievers and revealed this verse:

*"Permission [to fight] is given to those who are being fought, because they were wronged. And indeed, Allah is competent to give them victory."* (Al-Hajj: 39)

Imam Muhammad bin Shihab Zohri says that this was the first verse revealed regarding the permission for jihad. However, in Tafseer Ibne Jareer, it is mentioned that the first verse revealed about jihad was:

*"Fight in the way of Allah those who fight you but do not transgress. Indeed, Allah does not like transgressors."* (Al-Baqarah: 190)

In any case, in 2 AH, Allah Ta'ala gave the Muslims permission to fight the disbelievers. However, initially this permission was conditional — that is, permission was given to fight only those disbelievers who attacked the Muslims. The Muslims had not yet been permitted to initiate war on their own.

But after the truth became clear and falsehood was exposed, since the propagation of truth and the dissemination of divine commandments were obligatory upon the Beloved ﷺ, the command for jihad was revealed against all those disbelievers who obstinately refused to accept the truth, whether they initiated fighting against the Muslims or not. Because after the truth had become manifest, compelling people to accept the truth and forcing them to abandon falsehood was extremely necessary for the wisdom and well-being of the human race.

In any case, there is no doubt that if all the battles that took place after the migration are examined with deep insight, it becomes clear that all these battles were forced upon the Muslims by the disbelievers. The poor Muslims were compelled to take up arms out of necessity. Just look at the following incidents with a critical eye:

**(1)** The Beloved ﷺ and his Companions left everything in Makka and came to Madina in a state of utter helplessness. It should have been that the disbelievers of Makka now sat in peace — their enemy, the Beloved, and the Muslims had left their city. But what happened was that the rage of these disbelievers increased so much that they now became mortal enemies of the people of Madina as well. Just a few days after the migration, the disbelievers of Makka sent a threatening letter to the Ansar chief Abdullah bin Ubai.

Abdullah bin Ubai was the person before the migration whom all the people of Madina had agreed to make their king, and preparations for his coronation had been made. But after the Beloved came to Madina, this plan was abandoned. Due to this grief and anger, Abdullah bin Ubai remained a chief of the hypocrites throughout his life, secretly opposing Islam and remaining engaged in all kinds of conspiracies against Islam and the Muslims. (`Bukhari, 2/924`)

The contents of the letter that the disbelievers of Makka wrote to this enemy of Islam were: "You have given shelter to our man Muhammad. We swear by Allah — either kill him or expel him from Madina. Otherwise, we will all attack you, kill all your fighting men, and take possession of your women." (`Abu Dawood, 2/67`)

When the Beloved ﷺ came to know of this threatening and dangerous letter, he met Abdullah bin Ubai and said, "Will you kill your brothers and sons?" Since most of the Ansar had entered the fold of Islam, Abdullah bin Ubai understood this point and could not act upon the command of the disbelievers of Makka.

**(2)** Around the same time, Hazrat Saad bin Muaaz, the chief of the Aus tribe, went to Makka to perform Umrah. Due to old ties, he stayed at the house of Umayya bin Khalaf. When Umayya took him for tawaf of the Kaaba at midday, Abu Jahl happened to come across them. He scolded Umayya, saying, "Who is this with you?" Umayya said, "This is Saad bin Muaaz from Madina." Hearing this, Abu Jahl furiously said, "You people have given shelter to those irreligious people (Muhammad and the Companions). By Allah's oath! If you were not with Umayya, you would not have returned alive." Hazrat Saad bin Muaaz replied with great courage and boldness: "If you stop us from visiting the Kaaba, we will stop your trade route to Syria." (`Bukhari`)

**(3)** The disbelievers of Makka did not stop at these threats. They began preparing to attack Madina and started planning the mass killing of the Beloved and the Muslims. The Beloved would spend his nights awake, and the Companions would take turns guarding him. Due to their influence over all of Arabia, the disbelievers of Makka had inflamed all the tribes with the idea that it was necessary to attack Madina and annihilate the Muslims.

In the presence of these three factors, any sensible person would have to admit that in these circumstances, it was essential for the Beloved to take some measures for self-defence so that the survival and safety of the Ansar, the Muhajireen, and his own life could be ensured.

Therefore, after learning of the dangerous intentions of the disbelievers of Makka, the Beloved decided to implement two measures for the self-defence of himself and the Companions.

**First:** Interference should be made in the Syrian trade of the Quraish, on which their livelihood depended, so that they would abandon the idea of attacking Madina and be forced to make peace.

**Second:** Treaties of peace and security should be made with the tribes settled around Madina so that the disbelievers of Makka would not be able to contemplate attacking Madina.

So, with these two measures in view, the Beloved began to send small armies of Companions around Madina. He himself also went with some of these armies. These small armies of Companions would sometimes go to monitor the movements of the disbelievers of Makka, sometimes to make peace treaties with certain tribes, and sometimes for the purpose of blocking the trade route of the disbelievers of Makka to Syria. In this connection, confrontations between the Muslims and the disbelievers of Makka and their allies began, and a series of small and large battles commenced. These battles are recorded in Islamic history under the titles of Ghazwat and Saraya.

### The Difference Between Ghazwa and Sariyya

Here, it is necessary to remember the terminology used by the writers of Seerah: the military expedition in which the Beloved himself participated is called a Ghazwa, and the troop detachments in which the Beloved did not participate are called Sariyya. (`Madarijun Nubuwwat`)

Historians differ on the number of Ghazwat (expeditions in which the Beloved participated). In Mawahib-e-Ladunniya, the number is 27. In Rauzatul Ahbab, it is written that some say 21, some say 24, some 25, and some 26.

However, the narration recorded by Imam Bukhari from Hazrat Zaid bin Arqam states the total number of Ghazwat as 19. Among these, the 9 in which actual fighting took place are:

(1) Battle of Badr (2) Battle of Uhud (3) Battle of Ahzab (4) Battle of Banu Quraiza (5) Battle of Banu Al Mustaliq (6) Battle of Khaibar (7) Conquest of Makka (8) Battle of Hunain (9) Battle of Taif

As for the Saraya (expeditions in which the Beloved did not participate), according to some historians their number is 47, and according to others 56. Imam Bukhari narrated from Muhammad bin Is'haq that the first Ghazwa was Abwa and the last Ghazwa was Tabook. The first Sariyya sent from Madina for battle was Sariyya-e-Hamza, whose mention will come later.

Nearly the entire period after the migration was spent in organizing and managing Ghazwat and Saraya. If we count the minimum number of Ghazwat that appear in narrations — 19 — and the minimum number of Saraya — 47 — then in 9 years, the Beloved had to face 66 small and large battles. Therefore, the topic of Ghazwat and Saraya is a very important part of the blessed Seerah.

By the grace of Allah Ta'ala, the complete details of all these Ghazwat and Saraya, their causes and reasons, are recorded in Islamic histories. But this is such a vast subject that the narrow scope of this book is insufficient to cover all these topics. However, the difficulty is that if we omit these topics entirely, the Seerah of the Messenger would surely remain incomplete and deficient. Therefore, it is essential to briefly mention some famous Ghazwat and Saraya here so that this important chapter of the blessed Seerah may also be presented before the readers.

### Sariyya-e-Hamza

After the revelation of the verses of jihad, the first small army that the Master of the Prophets sent against the disbelievers was called "Sariyya-e-Hamza". The Beloved gave his uncle Hazrat Hamza bin Abdul Muttalib a white flag and sent only 30 Muhajireen under that flag to face an army of disbelievers numbering 300, led by Abu Jahl.

Hazrat Hamza reached Saiful Bahr, and both sides lined up for battle. But a man named Majdi bin Amr Juhanni, who was an ally of both parties, intervened and stopped the fight. (`Madarijun Nubuwwat`)

### Sariyya-e-Ubaida bin Al Haaris

In the same year, with 60 or 80 Muhajireen, the Beloved sent Hazrat Ubaida bin Al Haaris as commander with a white flag to Rabigh. The standard-bearer of this Sariyya was Hazrat Mustah bin Asasa. When this army reached the place of Saniyyatul Murrah, Abu Sufyan and Abu Jahl's son Ikrima had gathered 200 Quraish disbelievers.

The two armies confronted each other. Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas shot an arrow at the disbelievers. This was the very first arrow shot by the Muslims at the disbelievers of Makka. Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas shot a total of 8 arrows, and every arrow hit its target perfectly. The disbelievers, terrified by the shooting of these arrows, fled. Therefore, no battle took place. (`Madarijun Nubuwwat`)

### Sariyya-e-Saad Bin Abi Waqqas

In the same year, in the month of Zul Qada, the Beloved sent Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas with 20 horsemen to intercept an army of Quraish disbelievers. The flag of this Sariyya was also white, and Hazrat Miqdad bin Aswad was its standard-bearer. This army travelled at night. When they reached the place of Khiraar on the fifth day, they found that the disbelievers of Makka had already fled one day earlier. Therefore, no clash occurred.

### Ghazwa-e-Abwa

This Ghazwa is also called Ghazwa-e-Wadaan. It is the first Ghazwa — that is, the first time the Beloved ﷺ went out with the intention of jihad. In Safar 2 AH, he took 60 Muhajireen out of Madina, appointing Hazrat Saad bin Obada as his deputy in Madina and giving the flag to Hazrat Hamza. He pursued the disbelievers up to the place of Abwa, but the Quraish disbelievers had already fled, so no battle took place.

Abwa is a village 80 miles from Madina where the blessed mother of the Beloved, Hazrat Aamina, is buried. Staying there for a few days, he made a written pact of mutual assistance with Makhshi bin Amr Zamri, the chief of the Banu Zamra tribe, and returned to Madina. In this Ghazwa, the Beloved remained outside Madina for 15 days.

### Ghazwa-e-Bawaat

In the 13th month after migration, in 2 AH, the Beloved appointed Hazrat Saad bin Muaaz as governor of Madina and set out with 200 Muhajireen with the intention of jihad. The flag of this Ghazwa was also white, and the standard-bearer was Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas. The purpose of this Ghazwa was to intercept a trade caravan of the Quraish disbelievers. The leader of that caravan was Umayya bin Khalaf Jamahi, and it carried 100 Quraish disbelievers and 1500 camels. The Beloved went up to the place of Bawaat in search of that caravan, but the Quraish disbelievers were not encountered anywhere. So the Beloved returned to Madina without any battle.

### Ghazwa-e-Safwan

In the same year, Qurz bin Jabir Fihri raided the pastures of Madina and drove away some camels. The Beloved appointed Hazrat Zaid bin Haarisa as his deputy in Madina and Hazrat Ali as standard-bearer, and with a group of Companions, pursued the raider to the valley of Safwan. But the raider fled with such speed that he could not be caught, and the Beloved returned to Madina.

The valley of Safwan is near Badr, which is why some historians have named this Ghazwa as Ghazwa-e-Badr-e-Oola. It should be remembered that Ghazwa-e-Safwan and Ghazwa-e-Badr-e-Oola are two names for the same Ghazwa. (`Madarijun Nubuwwat`)

### Ghazwa-e-Zil Ushaira

In the same year, 2 AH, a caravan of the Quraish disbelievers was going from Makka to Syria with trade goods. The Beloved took 150 or 200 Muhajir Companions and went up to the place of Zil Ushaira, near the port of Yambu, to intercept that caravan. But upon arriving there, it was found that the caravan had already moved far ahead. Therefore, no confrontation took place.

However, when this same caravan returned from Syria and the Beloved set out to intercept it, the Battle of Badr occurred, whose detailed account follows.

### Sariyya-e-Abdullah bin Jahash

In the month of Rajab, 2 AH, the Beloved appointed Hazrat Abdullah bin Jahash as commander and sent a group of 8 or 12 Muhajireen under his leadership. Two people shared each camel. The Beloved gave Hazrat Abdullah bin Jahash a sealed letter in a wrapper and said, "After travelling for two days, open this wrapper and read it, and act upon the instructions written in it."

When he opened and read the letter after two days, it contained the following: "Stop at the place of Nakhla, between Taif and Makka, keep watch on the caravans of the Quraish, and keep us informed of the situation."

This was an extremely dangerous task. Staying in the enemy's stronghold for intelligence work was like walking into the jaws of death. But all these self-sacrificing Companions reached Nakhla without hesitation. By a strange coincidence, they arrived at Nakhla on the last date of Rajab. On that very day, a trade caravan of the Quraish disbelievers arrived. It carried Amr bin Al Hazrami, Abdullah bin Mugheera's two sons Usman and Naufal, Hakam bin Qaisan, and others. The camels were loaded with dates and other trade goods.

The commander of the Sariyya, Hazrat Abdullah bin Jahash, said to his companions: "If we leave these caravan people alone, they will reach Makka and inform the people of Makka of our presence here, and we will be killed or captured. But if we fight these people, today is the last date of Rajab, a sacred month. The sin of fighting in the sacred month will be upon us."

Finally, it was decided that they should fight these people to avert the danger to their lives. Hazrat Waqid bin Abdullah Tameemi shot a well-aimed arrow that struck Amr bin Al Hazrami, killing him instantly. Usman and Hakam were captured. Naufal escaped. Hazrat Abdullah bin Jahash took the camels and the goods loaded on them as booty and returned to Madina. He presented one-fifth of this booty to the Beloved ﷺ. (`Zurqani Alal Mawahib`)

Those who were killed or captured belonged to very noble families. Amr bin Al Hazrami, who was killed, was the son of Abdullah Hazrami. Amr bin Al Hazrami was the first disbeliever to be killed by the hands of the Muslims. Among the captives, Usman was the grandson of Mugheera, who was considered a very great chief of the Quraish, and Hakam bin Kaisan was the freed slave of Hisham bin Al Mugheera.

For this reason, this incident turned all the Quraish disbelievers into a raging fire. The slogan of "Blood for blood" echoed in every street and market of Makka. In reality, the Battle of Badr was a reaction to this very incident. Hazrat Urwah bin Zubair states that the Battle of Badr and all the battles that took place with the Quraish disbelievers had their root cause in the killing of Amr bin Al Hazrami by Hazrat Waqid bin Abdullah Tameemi's arrow. (`Tarikh-e-Tabri`)

### The Battle of Badr

Badr is a village approximately 80 miles from Madina Munawwara, where an annual fair was held in the time of ignorance. There was also a well there whose owner was named Badr, after whom this place was named.

At this location, the great battle of Badr took place — a fierce bloody confrontation between the Quraish disbelievers and the Muslims. The Muslims were blessed with that magnificent, clear victory, after which the banner of Islam's honour and glory rose so high that the grandeur and might of the disbelievers of Quraish were utterly humbled.

Allah Ta'ala named the day of the Battle of Badr as "Yawmul Furqan" (the Day of Distinction). In Surah Al-Anfal of the Quran and briefly in other Surahs, this battle is mentioned repeatedly. Reminding the Muslims of His favour regarding this clear victory, Allah the All-Knowing says in the Noble Quran:

*"And Allah had certainly helped you at Badr when you were weak and helpless. So fear Allah that you may be grateful."* (Aale Imran: 123)

**The Cause of the Battle of Badr**

As we have already written, the real cause of the Battle of Badr was the intense rage among the Quraish disbelievers over the killing of Amr bin Al Hazrami. Every disbeliever had but one slogan on his lips: "We will take blood for blood."

But suddenly, an unexpected situation arose. The very caravan that the Beloved had gone in search of up to Zil Ushaira but had not caught, news suddenly reached Madina that this same caravan was now returning from Syria to Makka. It was also learned that the caravan consisted of Abu Sufyan bin Harb, Makharima bin Naufal, Amr bin Al Aas, and about 30 or 40 men, carrying a huge amount of Quraish trade goods.

The Beloved said to his Companions: "The bands of Quraish disbelievers are constantly roaming around Madina with the intention of plunder. Qurz bin Jabir Fihri has come to the very pastures of Madina and committed robbery and banditry. So why should we not attack this caravan of the Quraish disbelievers and loot it, so that the Syrian trade of the Quraish disbelievers is stopped and they are forced to make peace with us?" Hearing this blessed command of the Beloved, the Ansar and the Muhajireen became ready for it.

**Departure from Madina**

So on the 12th of Ramadan, people set out with great haste. Each person went in whatever state he was in. In this army, neither were there many weapons nor any significant amount of military supplies or rations, because no one had any idea that a major battle would take place on this journey.

But when news spread in Makka that the Muslims were armed and had set out to loot the Quraish caravan, excitement spread in Makka. The army of the Quraish disbelievers immediately mobilized to attack the Muslims.

When the Beloved received this information, he gathered the esteemed Companions, informed them of the situation, and said clearly: "On this journey, we may encounter the Quraish caravan, and it is also possible that we may have to confront the army of the disbelievers of Makka."

Hearing this, Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddique, Hazrat Umar, and other Muhajireen expressed great enthusiasm. But the Beloved was looking at the Ansar, because the Ansar, while pledging allegiance on the Beloved's blessed hand, had only covenanted that they would take up arms when the disbelievers attacked Madina. But now the matter involved going outside Madina to fight.

Hazrat Saad bin Obada, the chief of the Khazraj tribe among the Ansar, seeing the face of the Beloved, spoke up: "O Messenger of Allah! Are you indicating towards us? By Allah's oath! We are those self-sacrificing people that if you command us to jump into the sea, we will do so." Similarly, another great chief of the Ansar, Hazrat Miqdad bin Aswad, submitted passionately: "O Messenger of Allah! We will not say as the people of Hazrat Moosa said — 'You and your Lord go and fight.' Rather, we will fight on your right, on your left, in front of you, and behind you." Hearing the speeches of both these chiefs of the Ansar, the face of the Beloved shone with joy.

One mile outside Madina, the Beloved reviewed his army. He ordered the return of those who were young, saying, "What business do children have on a dangerous battlefield?"

**The Young Soldier**

Among these children was Hazrat Umair bin Abi Waqqas, the younger brother of Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas. When he was told to return, he became agitated and burst into tears. He absolutely refused to go back. Seeing his restlessness and weeping, the delicate heart of the Beloved was moved, and he allowed him to accompany them. Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas hung a sword around the neck of that young soldier.

At the time of departure from Madina, the Beloved had appointed Ibne Umme Maktoom as the imam of Masjid Nabawi for prayers. But when he reached the place of Roha, he sensed some danger from the hypocrites and the Jews, so he appointed Hazrat Abu Lubaba bin Abdul Munzir as governor of Madina and ordered him to return to Madina. He also ordered Hazrat Aasim bin Adi to keep watch over the villages near Madina.

After these arrangements, the Beloved proceeded towards Badr, from where the Makkan disbelievers were expected to arrive. The total number of the army was now 313, of whom 60 were Muhajireen and the rest were Ansar. Journeying stage by stage, when he reached the place of Safra, he sent two men for intelligence to find out where the caravan was and how far it had reached.

**Abu Sufyan's Cunning**

Meanwhile, the spies of the Quraish disbelievers were also doing their work very diligently. When the Beloved left Madina, Abu Sufyan received this information. He immediately sent Zam Zam bin Amr Ghifari to Makka to inform the Quraish so that they could arrange for the protection of their caravan. He himself changed the route and took the caravan towards the coast.

When Abu Sufyan's messenger Zam Zam bin Amr Ghifari reached Makka, according to the custom of the time, when someone had to deliver fearful news, the messenger would tear his clothes, sit on the back of a camel, and shout the news. Zam Zam bin Amr Ghifari tore his shirt, stood on the back of a camel, and began to shout loudly: "O people of Makka! All your trade goods are in Abu Sufyan's caravan, and the Muslims have resolved to intercept this caravan and loot it! Hurry! Arm yourselves and rush to save your caravan!"

**The Enthusiasm of the Quraish Disbelievers**

When this fearful news reached Makka, such a commotion arose that all peace and tranquillity of Makka was destroyed. All the tribes of the Quraish rushed out of their homes. Among the chiefs of Makka, only Abu Lahab did not go due to his illness. All the other chiefs of the Quraish set out fully armed. And since the incident of Nakhla was still fresh — in which Amr bin Al Hazrami had been killed by the Muslims and his caravan looted — the Quraish disbelievers were beside themselves with the spirit of revenge.

An army of a thousand strong, every soldier fully armed with double weapons. For the army's provisions, the wealthy people of the Quraish — Abbas bin Abdul Muttalib, Utba bin Rabiya, Haris bin Aamir, Nazr bin Al Haaris, Abu Jahl, Umayya and others — would slaughter 10 camels every day in turn and feed the entire army. Utba bin Rabiya, the greatest chief of the Quraish, was the commander of this army.

**Abu Sufyan Escapes**

When Abu Sufyan left the common route and took the coastal route, and was far from the dangerous areas, he sent a letter to the Quraish through a swift messenger: "You left your homes armed to protect your goods and people. Now return to your homes, for we have escaped the attack and plunder of the Muslims and are safely reaching Makka with our lives and goods."

**Difference Among the Disbelievers**

Abu Sufyan's letter reached the disbelievers of Makka when they were at the place of Zuhafa. After reading the letter, the chiefs of the Banu Zahra and Banu Adi tribes said, "Now there is no need to fight the Muslims. We should turn back." Hearing this, Abu Jahl became furious and said, "By Allah's oath! We will go to Badr with this same preparation. We will slaughter camels there, eat and feed, drink wine, hold dance parties, so that the stamp of our grandeur and might is set upon all the Arab tribes and they always fear us."

The Quraish disbelievers acted upon Abu Jahl's opinion, but the Banu Zahra and Banu Adi tribes returned. Except for these two tribes, all the other Quraish tribes participated in the Battle of Badr. (`Seerat Ibne Hisham, 2/618`)

**The Disbelievers in Badr**

Since the Quraish disbelievers reached Badr before the Muslims, they had occupied suitable positions. When the Beloved neared Badr, in the evening he sent Hazrat Ali, Hazrat Zubair, and Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas towards Badr to bring news of the Quraish disbelievers. These Companions captured two slaves of the Quraish who were assigned to fetch water for the army of the disbelievers.

The Beloved asked both slaves, "Tell me, which chiefs of the Quraish are in that army?" Both slaves named them: "Utba bin Rabia, Shaiba bin Rabia, Abul Bakhtari, Hakeem bin Hizaam, Naufal bin Khuwailid, Haaris bin Aamir, Nazr bin Al Haaris, Jamma bin Al Aswad, Abu Jahl bin Hisham, Umayya bin Khalaf, Suhail bin Amr, Amr bin Abde Wud, Abbas bin Abdul Muttalib and others — all are present in this army."

Hearing this list, the Beloved turned to his Companions and said: "Muslims! Listen! Makka has thrown the very pieces of its liver towards you." (`Muslim, 2/102`)

**The Emperor of Both Worlds in the Field of Badr**

When the Beloved camped at Badr, he set up at a place where there was neither any well nor any spring, and the ground was so sandy that the horses' legs would sink in. Seeing this, Hazrat Hubab bin Munzir submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Is the place you have chosen for camp by divine revelation or by military strategy?" The Beloved said, "No revelation has come regarding this." Hazrat Hubab bin Munzir said, "Then in my opinion, from a military strategy perspective, it would be better that we move forward and take control of the water springs, so that the wells controlled by the disbelievers become useless."

The Beloved approved his opinion and acted upon it. By Allah's power, rain also fell, which settled the dust and sand of the field, making movement easy for the Muslims. The ground of the disbelievers became muddy. The Muslims stored the rainwater and made reservoirs here and there so that this water could be used for bathing and ablution. Allah the All-Knowing mentions this favour in the Quran: "And when He caused drowsiness to overcome you as a reassurance from Him, and sent down water from the sky to purify you thereby, and to remove the impurity of Satan from you, and to strengthen your hearts and make your feet firm." (Al-Anfal: 11)

**The Night Vigil of the Chief of Creation**

It was the night of the 17th of Ramadan, 2 AH. The entire army was sleeping in peace and comfort. But the Chief of Creation was the one who spent the whole night beseeching the Lord of the Worlds in prayer. When dawn appeared, he woke the people for prayer. After prayer, he recited the verses of jihad and delivered such a thrilling and inspiring sermon that every drop of blood in the veins of the Muslim warriors turned into an ocean of fervour, raising stormy waves, and they began to prepare for the battlefield.

**Who, When, and Where Will Be Killed?**

At night, the Beloved inspected the battlefield with a few self-sacrificing Companions. He had a stick in his blessed hand. He drew lines on the ground with that stick and said: "This is where so-and-so disbeliever will be killed. Tomorrow his corpse will be found lying here." And so it happened — wherever the Beloved had indicated a disbeliever's place of death, that disbeliever's corpse was found exactly there. Not one of them deviated even a hair's breadth from the line. (`Abu Dawood, 2/364`)

This hadith clearly and explicitly establishes that knowledge of who, when, and where will die — these unseen matters — Allah Ta'ala had granted to His Beloved.

**War Was About to Be Avoided, Then It Was Decided**

The Quraish disbelievers were eager to fight, but among them were also some sensible, thoughtful people who did not desire bloodshed. Hakeem bin Hizam, who later became a Muslim, was a very gentle and mild-tempered person. He said to the army commander Utba bin Rabia: "After all, what is the benefit of this bloodshed? I give you a very sincere advice. The demand of the Quraish is the blood of Amr bin Al Hazrami, and he was your ally. Pay his blood money. This way, this battle will be avoided, and today will become a memorial of your good name in your historical life — that through your prudence, a very dangerous and bloody battle was averted."

Utba himself was a very prudent and noble-natured man. He gladly accepted this sincere advice. But Abu Jahl's approval was also necessary. So Hakeem bin Hizam took Utba bin Rabia's message to Abu Jahl. Abu Jahl's vein of ignorance swelled up. He gave a venomous taunt: "Yes, yes! I understand very well that Utba's courage has failed him. Since his son Hudhaifa has become a Muslim and come with the Islamic army, he is shirking from war so that his son is not harmed."

Not stopping there, Abu Jahl called Aamir bin Al Hazrami, the brother of the slain Amr bin Al Hazrami, and said: "Look, the entire scheme to avenge your slain brother's blood is being ruined because the commander of our army, Utba, is showing cowardice." Hearing this, Aamir bin Al Hazrami, according to Arab custom, tore his clothes, put dust on his head, and began to cry out, "Waa Umraah! Waa Umraah!" (Alas for Amr!). This act set the entire army of the Quraish disbelievers ablaze, and the whole army echoed with the slogan "Blood for blood!" Every soldier, beside himself with passion, became eager and restless for war.

When Utba heard Abu Jahl's taunt, he too became filled with rage and said, "Tell Abu Jahl that the battlefield will show who is the coward." Saying this, he asked for an iron helmet, but his head was so large that no helmet would fit properly. He had to wrap cloth around his head and arm himself for war.

**The Lines of the Mujahideen**

On Friday, the 17th of Ramadan, 2 AH, the Beloved ordered the Muslim warriors to form battle lines. He had a stick in his blessed hand, with which he straightened the ranks so that no one was ahead or behind. He also ordered that no one should make any noise or commotion except for the remembrance of Allah.

At this very time, when the drum of war was about to beat, two incidents occurred that were extremely instructive and full of advice.

**The Kiss of the Blessed Stomach**

The Beloved was straightening the ranks with his stick when he noticed that Hazrat Sawaad Ansari's stomach was slightly protruding from the line. The Beloved poked his stomach with the stick and said, "O Sawaad! Stand straight." Hazrat Sawaad said, "O Messenger of Allah! You have struck my stomach with the stick. I must take retaliation (qisas) from you." Hearing this, the Beloved lifted his blessed garment and said, "O Sawaad! Here is my stomach. Strike it with the stick and take your retaliation."

Hazrat Sawaad rushed forward, kissed the blessed stomach, and then embraced the Beloved with immense fervour. The Beloved asked, "O Sawaad! Why did you do this?" He submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! At this moment, I am standing in the battle line with my head in my hands. Perhaps my time of death has come. At this moment, the desire surged in my heart that at the time of death, my body should touch your pure body." Hearing this, the Beloved appreciated Hazrat Sawaad's sentiment and prayed for goodness and blessings for him. Hazrat Sawaad, apologizing at the court of prophethood, forgave his retaliation. All the Companions looked at Hazrat Sawaad in wonder.

**Fulfilment of a Covenant**

It so happened that Hazrat Hudhaifa bin Al Yamaan and Hazrat Haseel were coming from somewhere. On the way, the disbelievers stopped them and said, "You are going to the field of Badr to help Muhammad." Both denied this and made a covenant that they would not participate in the battle. So the disbelievers let them go. When both presented themselves at the court of prophethood and narrated the incident, the Beloved separated them from the battle lines and said: "We will fulfil the covenant in every situation. We only need Allah's help."

O readers! Ponder! The world knows how valuable every soldier is on the battlefield, especially when facing a huge army of enemies. But the King of Both Worlds preferred to deprive his small army of two brave and daring mujahideen rather than allow any Muslim to break his covenant, even with a disbeliever.

Allahu Akbar! O kings of the nations of the world! Tell me, by Allah, does any such shining page exist in the great volumes of your historical lives? O moon-and-sun-eyed observers! Tell me, for Allah's sake! Have your eyes ever seen such an example of the fulfilment of a covenant on the pages of existence? By Allah's oath! I am certain that you can say nothing but "No."

**Both Armies Face to Face**

Now the time had come. In the field of Badr, both lines of truth and falsehood stood before each other. The Quran declares: "There is a sign of admonition for you in the two armies that met — one fighting in the way of Allah, and the other denying Allah."

After completing the battle lines of the Muslim warriors, the Beloved went to a hut that the Companions had made for his sitting. Now the question of guarding this hut was extremely important, because the real target of the Quraish attacks was the person of the King of Both Worlds. No one dared to guard this hut. But at this moment too, it was the fortune of his Companion of the Cave, the respected Siddique, that he stood near this hut with an unsheathed sword. Hazrat Saad bin Muaaz also guarded around the hut with some Ansar.

**The Prophetic Supplication**

In this critical moment, the Beloved stood before the Creator, raising his hands with tears, beseeching: "O Lord! Fulfil the promise You made with me today." He was so overwhelmed and absorbed that the blessed sheet would fall from his blessed shoulders and he would not know. Sometimes he would place his head in prostration and pray: "O Allah! If these few souls are destroyed, then until the Day of Judgement, there will be no one to worship You on the face of the earth." (`Seerat Ibne Hisham, 2/627`)

Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddique, the Companion of the Cave, seeing him so restless, lost his own peace and calm. He picked up the sheet, placed it on the Beloved's blessed shoulder, held his blessed hand, and submitted in a choked voice with great respect: "Master! That is enough now. Allah will surely fulfil His promise."

Accepting the words of his self-sacrificing Companion of the Cave, the Beloved ended his supplication. His blessed tongue began to recite this verse: "Soon the army will be defeated and they will turn their backs." (Al-Qamar: 45). He kept reciting this verse repeatedly, which indicated the glad tidings of a clear victory.

**How the Battle Began**

The battle began in this way. First, Aamir bin Al Hazrami, who was restless to avenge his slain brother Amr bin Al Hazrami, advanced for battle. To face him, the Beloved's slave Hazrat Mahja'a stepped into the field and was honoured with martyrdom while fighting.

Then Hazrat Harisa bin Suraqa Ansari was drinking water from a reservoir when suddenly an arrow of the disbelievers struck him and he too became a martyr. (`Seerat Ibne Hisham, 2/627`)

**Hazrat Umair's Passion for Martyrdom**

While delivering the sermon of jihad, the Master of the Prophets said: "Muslims! March towards that Paradise whose width is equal to the heavens and the earth." Hazrat Umair bin Al Hamam Ansari spoke up: "O Messenger of Allah! Is Paradise's width equal to the heavens and the earth?" He said, "Yes." Hearing this, Hazrat Umair exclaimed, "Excellent! Excellent!" The Beloved asked, "Why did you say 'Excellent'?" He submitted, "O Messenger of Allah! Only with the hope that I too may enter Paradise." The Beloved gave him glad tidings: "O Umair! You are indeed among the people of Paradise."

Hazrat Umair was eating some dates at that time. Hearing this glad tiding, he threw away the dates, stood up, and immediately charged at the army of the disbelievers with his sword. Fighting bravely, he was martyred. (`Muslim, Kitabul Jihad, 2/139`)

**The Commander of the Disbelievers is Killed**

The commander of the disbelievers, Utba bin Rabia, had an ostrich feather on his chest. Together with his brother Shaiba bin Rabia and his son Waleed bin Utba, he came out from his line, filled with rage, and issued a challenge for single combat. Hazrat Auf, Hazrat Muaaz, and Abdullah bin Rawaha stepped forward from the Islamic lines. Utba asked their names and lineage. When he learned they were Ansar, he said: "We have no business with you."

Then Utba shouted: "O Muhammad! These people are not our equals. Send the nobles of the Quraish to fight us." The Beloved commanded Hazrat Hamza, Hazrat Ali, and Hazrat Ubaida to go out and face these three. So these three lions of Islam stepped into the field. Since they were wearing helmets covering their faces, Utba did not recognize them and asked who they were. When all three gave their names, Utba said: "Yes, now they are our equals."

When the battle began, Hazrat Hamza, Hazrat Ali, and Hazrat Ubaida displayed such faith-inspired bravery that the ground of Badr trembled and the hearts of the disbelievers shuddered. The result of their combat was that Hazrat Hamza fought Utba — both fought with great bravery — until Hazrat Hamza struck Utba repeatedly with his sword and laid him on the ground. Waleed fought with Hazrat Ali — both attacking each other murderously — but the Lion of Allah's Zulfiqar struck Waleed down, and he was killed in disgrace.

But Utba's brother Shaiba wounded Hazrat Ubaida so severely that he collapsed on the ground, unable to bear the wounds. Seeing this, Hazrat Ali rushed forward, killed Shaiba, and brought Hazrat Ubaida on his shoulders to the court of prophethood. His shin was shattered and the marrow was flowing out. In this condition, he submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Have I been deprived of martyrdom?" The Beloved said: "No, certainly not! Rather, you have been honoured with martyrdom." Hazrat Ubaida said: "O Messenger of Allah! If only my uncle Abu Talib were alive today, he would acknowledge that I am the embodiment of his verse: 'We will hand over Muhammad to the enemies only when we have been struck down fighting around him, and we will forget our sons and wives.'"

**Zubair's Historic Spear**

After this, Ubaida bin Sayeed bin Al Aas came out, covered from head to toe in iron armour and weapons. Challenging the Islamic army, he said, "I am Abu Karash." Hearing this arrogant challenge, the Beloved's cousin Hazrat Zubair bin Al Awwam stepped forward with his spear. But he saw that except for the eyes, no part of that body was uncovered by iron. Hazrat Zubair aimed his spear with such force at the man's eye that he fell and died. The spear pierced through the eye and stuck in the skull bone.

When Hazrat Zubair placed his foot on his corpse and pulled with full force, the spear came out with great difficulty, but it had bent and curved. This spear remained a historic memento for years. The Master of the Prophets asked Hazrat Zubair for that spear and always kept it with him. After the Beloved, it was passed to the four Rightly-Guided Caliphs, then to Hazrat Zubair's son Hazrat Abdullah bin Zubair. Until 73 AH, when the oppressive Umayyad governor Hajjaj bin Yusuf Saqafi martyred him, this spear remained with him. Then it went into the possession of Banu Umayya, after which it disappeared.

**Abu Jahl Killed in Disgrace**

Hazrat Abdurrahman bin Auf says: "I was standing in a line. On my right and left were two young boys. One asked me quietly, 'Uncle! Do you know Abu Jahl?' I said, 'Why, nephew! What business do you have with Abu Jahl?' He said, 'Uncle! I have made a covenant with Allah that wherever I see Abu Jahl, I will kill him or be killed fighting him, because he is a very great enemy of Allah's Messenger.'

Hazrat Abdurrahman says: 'I was looking at the young man in wonder when the other young man also said the same to me. Meanwhile, Abu Jahl appeared, twirling his sword. I gestured that this is Abu Jahl. That was it. Both of them swooped on him with their swords like a falcon on its prey. They struck Abu Jahl repeatedly until they laid him on the ground.'

These two boys were Hazrat Muawwaz and Hazrat Muaaz, the sons of Afra. Abu Jahl's son Ikrima attacked his father's killer, Hazrat Muaaz, striking his left shoulder from behind. His arm was severed, leaving only a little skin, and the hand hung loose. Hazrat Muaaz pursued Ikrima, chasing him far, but Ikrima escaped.

Even in this condition, Hazrat Muaaz kept fighting. But the severed hanging hand was causing difficulty. He pressed his severed hand with his foot and pulled with such force that the skin tore off completely. Then he continued fighting with one hand.

Hazrat Abdullah bin Mas'ood passed by Abu Jahl. At that time, Abu Jahl still had some life left in him. Hazrat Abdullah bin Mas'ood trampled his neck with his foot and said, "You are Abu Jahl. Tell me, how has Allah disgraced you today?" Even in this state, Abu Jahl said with arrogance: "This is no great achievement for you. My being killed is nothing more than a man killed by his own people. Yes! I only regret that I wish someone other than farmers had killed me."

Since Hazrat Muawwaz and Hazrat Muaaz were Ansari, and the Ansar worked in agriculture, and the Quraish looked down upon farmers, Abu Jahl considered being killed by farmers as regrettable.

After the battle ended, the Master of the Prophets passed by Abu Jahl's corpse with Hazrat Abdullah bin Mas'ood and said, pointing towards the corpse: "Abu Jahl is the Firaun of this nation." Then Abdullah bin Mas'ood cut off Abu Jahl's head and placed it at the feet of the King of Both Worlds. (`Bukhari, Ghazwa-e-Badr`)

**The Death of Abul Bakhtari**

Before the battle began, the Beloved had already said that there were some people in the army of the disbelievers who had been brought under pressure by the Makkan disbelievers. Such people should not be killed. He had also named these people. Among them was Abul Bakhtari, who had not come willingly to fight the Muslims but had been forced by the Quraish.

During the battle, Hazrat Majzar bin Ziyaad saw Abul Bakhtari with his close friend Junada bin Maliha, both on horseback. Hazrat Majzar said, "O Abul Bakhtari! The Beloved has forbidden us from killing you. So I will let you go." Abul Bakhtari asked, "What about my companion Junada?" Hazrat Majzar said clearly: "We cannot let him go alive." Hearing this, Abul Bakhtari became angry and said, "I cannot bear the taunt of Arab women that Abul Bakhtari abandoned his companion to save his own life."

Saying this, Abul Bakhtari recited a verse of rajaz: "A noble-born man can never abandon his companion until he himself is killed or finds his way out." Then fighting ensued, and both were killed.

**The Destruction of Umayya**

Umayya bin Khalaf was a great enemy of the Messenger. When the two armies of disbelief and Islam became entangled in the Battle of Badr, Umayya, due to his old ties, clung to Hazrat Abdurrahman bin Auf, saying, "Save my life!" Hazrat Abdurrahman bin Auf took pity and wanted to let Umayya escape.

But Hazrat Bilal saw Umayya. When Hazrat Bilal was Umayya's slave, Umayya had tortured him severely. In the spirit of revenge, Hazrat Bilal called out to the Ansar. The Ansar suddenly attacked. Hazrat Abdurrahman bin Auf told Umayya to lie down, and he lay down. Hazrat Abdurrahman bin Auf lay over him to hide and protect him. But Hazrat Bilal and the Ansar thrust their hands under his legs and thrust swords into his armpits, killing him. (`Bukhari, 1/308`)

**The Army of Angels**

In the Battle of Badr, Allah Ta'ala sent down an army of angels from the heavens to help the Muslims. First, a thousand angels came, then three thousand, and then five thousand. (Quran, Surah Aale Imran and Anfal)

When the fierce fighting began, the angels were not visible to anyone, but the effects of their blows were clearly seen. Some disbelievers were found with marks of beatings on their noses and mouths. Sometimes a head would fall severed without any sword blow. These were the feats of the army of angels that came from the heavens.

**The Disbelievers Laid Down Arms**

With the destruction of Utba, Shaiba, Abu Jahl and other Quraish chiefs, the backbone of the Makkan disbelievers was broken. Their feet slipped, they laid down their arms and fled. The Muslims began to capture them. In this battle, 70 disbelievers were killed and 70 were captured. The rest fled, leaving behind their belongings. The disbelievers of Makka suffered such a crushing defeat that their military power was completely destroyed. The great famous chiefs of the Quraish, who were known for bravery and chivalry — Utba, Shaiba, Abu Jahl, Abul Bakhtari, Jamma, Aas bin Hisham, Umayya bin Khalaf, Munabbe bin Al Hajjaj, Uqba bin Abi Mueet, Nazr bin Al Haaris and others, the crowned heads of the Quraish — were all killed one by one.

### The Martyrs of Badr

In the Battle of Badr, a total of 14 Muslims were honoured with martyrdom — 6 Muhajireen and 8 Ansar. The names of the Muhajir martyrs are:

(1) Hazrat Ubaida bin Al Haaris (2) Hazrat Umair bin Abi Waqqas (3) Hazrat Zushshimalain Umair bin Abde Amr (4) Hazrat Aaqil bin Abi Bukair (5) Hazrat Mahja (6) Hazrat Safwan bin Baija

And the list of the Ansar martyrs is:

(7) Hazrat Saad bin Khaisma (8) Hazrat Mubasshir bin Abdul Munzir (9) Hazrat Harisa bin Suraqa (10) Hazrat Muawwaz bin Afra (11) Hazrat Umair bin Hamaam (12) Hazrat Raafe bin Mualla (13) Hazrat Auf bin Afra (14) Hazrat Yazeed bin Haaris (`Zurqani`)

Among these martyrs of Badr, thirteen were buried in the field of Badr itself. However, Hazrat Ubaida bin Haaris passed away at the stage of Safara on the return journey from Badr, so his blessed grave is at the stage of Safara. (`Zurqani`)

### The Pit of Badr

It was the constant practice of the Master of the Prophets that wherever he saw a corpse, he would have it buried. But in the Battle of Badr, the number of disbelievers killed was so large that burying each one separately would have been a difficult task. Therefore, the Beloved ordered that all the corpses be thrown into a pit at Badr. The Companions dragged all the corpses and threw them into the pit.

Umayya bin Khalaf's corpse had swollen. When the Companions tried to drag him, his body parts began to separate. So his corpse was covered with earth right there. (`Bukhari, 2/566`)

### Addressing the Corpses of the Disbelievers

When the corpses of the disbelievers had been thrown into the pit of Badr, the Chief of the Worlds stood at the edge of that pit and called out by name: "O Utba bin Rabiya! O Shaiba bin Rabiya! O so-and-so! O so-and-so! Did you find your Lord's promise true? I certainly found my Lord's promise absolutely true."

When Hazrat Umar Farooq saw that the Beloved was addressing the corpses of the disbelievers, he was very surprised. He submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Are you speaking to lifeless bodies?" Hearing this, the Beloved said: "O Umar! By the oath of Allah in Whose power is my soul! You alive people cannot hear my words better than them. But the difference is that these dead cannot reply." (`Bukhari`)

**Important Note:** This hadith of Bukhari and others establishes that when the dead disbelievers can hear the speech of the living, then the believers — especially the saints, martyrs, and prophets — after their death, certainly hear the greetings and speech of us living beings, as well as our supplications. Since the Beloved called out to the dead corpses of the disbelievers, then why would calling out to Allah's chosen servants — the saints, martyrs, and prophets — after their death not be permissible and correct? This is why when the Master of the Prophets went to the cemetery of Madina, he would turn his blessed face towards the graves and say:

*"Peace be upon you, O people of the graves. May Allah forgive us and you. You have gone before us, and we are to follow you."*

And the Beloved commanded his Ummat as well, teaching the Companions: "When you go to visit the graves, greet them." (See Mishkaat, Bab Ziyaratul Quboor)

These hadiths make it clear that the dead hear the greetings and speech of the living. Otherwise, what benefit could there be in greeting those who do not hear?

### Return to Madina

After the victory, the Beloved stayed in Badr for three days. Then he set out with all the booty and the captured disbelievers. When he reached the valley of Safara, he distributed the booty among the mujahideen.

Hazrat Usman Ghani's honourable wife, Hazrat Bibi Ruqayya — who was the Beloved's daughter — was ill at the time of the Battle of Badr. The Beloved had ordered Hazrat Usman Ghani to stay in Madina to nurse her. Therefore, he could not participate in the Battle of Badr. But the Beloved gave him a share of the booty equal to the mujahideen of Badr and gave him the glad tidings of equal reward. Therefore, Hazrat Usman Ghani is also counted among the companions of Badr.

### The Welcome of the Mujahideen of Badr

After the victory, the Master of the Prophets sent Hazrat Zaid bin Haarisa to Madina with the glad tidings of the great victory. When Hazrat Zaid bin Harisa arrived in Madina with this glad news, all the people of Madina remained restless with joy, awaiting the Beloved's arrival. When news of his arrival came, the people of Madina went ahead and welcomed him enthusiastically at the place of Roha. (`Ibne Hisham, 2/643`)

### Treatment of the Prisoners

When the captives of the Makkan disbelievers were brought to Madina as prisoners of war, a huge crowd gathered to see them. People kept making various remarks. The Beloved's honourable wife Hazrat Bibi Saudah came to see the captives. She saw that among them was her close relative Suhail. Involuntarily, she exclaimed: "O Suhail! Even you have put on shackles like women! Could you not have been killed like brave men?" (`Seerat Ibne Hisham, 2/645`)

The Beloved distributed these prisoners among the Companions and ordered that they be kept with comfort. So two or four prisoners stayed in the houses of the Companions. The Companions treated them so well that they fed them the best meat and bread they could afford while they themselves ate only dates.

Among the prisoners was the Beloved's uncle, Hazrat Abbas. He had no shirt on his body, and he was such a tall man that no one's shirt would fit him. Abdullah bin Ubayy (the chief of the hypocrites) was of the same height, so he gave his own shirt to him. There is a narration in Bukhari that the blessed shirt that the Beloved gave to Abdullah bin Ubayy for his shroud was in return for this favour. (`Bukhari, 1/422`)

**The Fate of the Prisoners of War**

The Beloved consulted the Companions about what should be done with these captives. Hazrat Umar gave the opinion that all these enemies of Islam should be killed, with each person killing his close relative with his own sword. But Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddique advised: "After all, these people are our own dear relatives. So do not kill them. Rather, release them in exchange for some ransom amount. The financial condition of the Muslims is very weak. The ransom money will provide financial support for the Muslims. And perhaps in the future, Allah Ta'ala may grant them the ability to accept Islam."

The Beloved, the Mercy to the Worlds, approved of Hazrat Abu Bakr's thoughtful opinion. He released those captives in exchange for four thousand dirhams each. Those who could not pay due to poverty were released without any ransom. Among these captives, for those who knew how to write, the ransom was that each one would teach writing to ten boys of the Ansar.

**Ransom of Hazrat Abbas**

The Ansar requested the Beloved: "O Messenger of Allah! Hazrat Abbas is our maternal nephew. Please forgive his ransom." But the Beloved did not accept this request.

Hazrat Abbas was among those ten wealthy chiefs of the Quraish who had taken the responsibility of providing provisions for the army of the disbelievers. For this purpose, Hazrat Abbas had twenty uqiya of gold. Since it was not yet Abbas's turn to feed the army, that gold was still with him. The Beloved included that gold in the booty and demanded that Abbas pay the ransom for himself, his two nephews Aqeel bin Abi Talib and Naufal bin Haaris, and his ally Utba bin Amr Jahdam.

Hazrat Abbas said, "I have no wealth. Where shall I pay the ransom from?" Hearing this, the Beloved said: "Uncle! Where is that wealth which you gave to your wife Ummul Fazl when setting out for the Battle of Badr, saying: 'If I am killed in this battle, give so much wealth to my sons from this'?"

Hearing this, Hazrat Abbas said: "By the oath of that Allah Who has sent you with the truth! You are indeed the Messenger of Allah. Because no one knew of that wealth except me and my wife Ummul Fazl." So Hazrat Abbas paid the ransom for himself, his two nephews, and his ally, and secured their release. After this, Hazrat Abbas, Hazrat Aqeel, and Hazrat Naufal all became blessed with Islam.

### Hazrat Zainab's Necklace

Among the prisoners of the Battle of Badr was the Beloved's son-in-law, Abul Aas bin Arrabi. He was the son of Haala bint Khuwailid, and Haala was the real sister of Hazrat Bibi Khadija. So Hazrat Bibi Khadija, after consulting with the Messenger of Allah, had married her daughter Hazrat Zainab to Abul Aas Arrabi.

When the Beloved proclaimed his prophethood, his daughter Hazrat Zainab accepted Islam, but her husband Abul Aas did not become a Muslim, nor did he separate from Hazrat Zainab. Abul Aas bin Arrabi sent a messenger to Hazrat Zainab asking her to send the ransom amount. Hazrat Zainab also sent a valuable necklace that her mother Hazrat Bibi Khadija had given her as dowry, along with the ransom money.

When the Beloved's eyes fell on that necklace, the memory of Hazrat Bibi Khadija and his love for her affected his blessed heart with such tenderness that he wept. He said to the Companions: "If you wish, return this memento of her mother to my daughter." All the Companions bowed in submission, and the necklace was sent back to Hazrat Bibi Zainab in Makka.

Abu Aas was released and came from Madina to Makka. He then sent Hazrat Bibi Zainab to Madina. Abul Aas was a great trader. He went from Makka to Syria with his trade goods, earned great profit, and was returning to Makka when the Muslim mujahideen attacked his caravan and looted all his goods and belongings. The booty had already been distributed among all the soldiers.

Abul Aas came secretly to Madina and stayed in Hazrat Zainab's house, and she gave him protection. The Beloved said to the Companions: "If you wish, return Abul Aas's goods and belongings." Understanding the Beloved's indication, all the mujahideen brought the goods to Makka, accounted for every penny to all his trade partners, paid each person his share, and then Abul Aas announced his acceptance of Islam. He told the people of Makka: "I am going to Madina after settling everyone's accounts fully so that no one can say that Abul Aas became a Muslim and fled to Madina out of fear of repayment." After this, Hazrat Abul Aas came to Madina and lived with Hazrat Bibi Zainab.

### The Mourning of the Slain of Badr

When the news of the crushing defeat of the Quraish at Badr reached Makka, such a calamity arose that every house became a house of mourning. But fearing that the Muslims would mock them, Abu Sufyan announced throughout the city: "Beware! No one is to weep!"

In this battle, Aswad bin Al Muttalib had lost two sons — Aqeel and Jamma — and a grandson Haris bin Jamma. This heart-rending shock had shattered Aswad's heart. He wanted to weep bitterly for his slain ones to relieve his heart, but out of tribal honour he could not weep. He kept choking and suffocating inside, and from constant weeping, he became blind.

One day, a woman's crying was heard in the city. He sent his slave to find out who was crying. Had permission been given to weep for the slain of Badr? He too was restless to weep. The slave reported that a woman's camel was lost and she was crying over it. Aswad was a poet. Hearing this, his tongue involuntarily uttered these painful verses, every word of which drips blood:

*"Is that woman crying over a lost camel? And sleeplessness has taken away her sleep. She may not cry over a camel, but let her cry over Badr, where fate was unkind."*

*"If you must weep, weep for Aqeel, and weep for Haris who was a lion among lions. And weep for them all, but do not take their names. And Abu Hakima — Jamma had no equal."*

### The Dreadful Conspiracy of Umair and Safwan

One day, Umair and Safwan were sitting in the Hateem of the Kaaba, shedding tears over the slain of Badr. Suddenly, Safwan said: "O Umair! Remembering how my father and other chiefs of Makka were killed in Badr, my heart is shattered. There is no pleasure left in life."

Umair said: "By Allah's oath! If I were not in debt and worried about my children, I would saddle a swift horse right now, go to Madina, and in the blink of an eye, kill Muhammad by surprise and escape."

Hearing this, Safwan said: "O Umair! Do not worry at all about your debt and children. I make a covenant in the House of Allah that I will pay all your debts and take responsibility for your children's upbringing."

After this pact, Umair went straight home, sharpened his sword, poisoned it, mounted his horse, and rode to Madina. When he neared Masjid Nabawi, Hazrat Umar caught him, seized him by the neck, and brought him to the court of prophethood.

The Beloved asked: "Why, Umair? With what intention have you come?" He replied, "To secure the release of my son." The Beloved said: "Did you and Safwan not sit in the Hateem of the Kaaba and conspire to kill me?" Hearing this secret, Umair was stunned. He said: "I bear witness that you are indeed the Messenger of Allah. By Allah's oath! No one knew of this secret except me and Safwan."

Meanwhile, in Makka, Safwan was extremely restless, waiting to hear news of the Beloved's assassination, counting the days for Umair's return. But when he suddenly heard that Umair had become a Muslim, the ground slipped from beneath his feet in sheer astonishment.

Hazrat Umair, after becoming a Muslim, returned to Makka. Just as he was once thirsty for the blood of Muslims, he now became an enemy to the lives of the disbelievers. He began to preach Islam in Makka with such courage and boldness that through his invitation to Islam, the dark hearts of great disbelievers were illuminated with the light of faith. This same Umair came to be known as the Companion of the Messenger, Hazrat Umair.

### The Virtues of the Companions of Badr

The Companions who participated in the jihad of the Battle of Badr hold a special distinction among all the Companions. One very great virtue of these fortunate people is that the Master of the Prophets said about them: "Indeed, Allah Ta'ala is aware of the people of Badr and has decreed: 'Do whatever deeds you wish now. Without doubt, Paradise has become obligatory for you.' Or He said: 'I have forgiven you.'"

### Abu Lahab's Instructive Death

Abu Lahab could not participate in the Battle of Badr. When the disbelievers of Quraish returned to Makka after their defeat, hearing the circumstances of the Battle of Badr from people, Abu Lahab was extremely grieved and distressed. After this, he was afflicted with smallpox, which covered his entire body. He died on the eighth day. The Arabs were very afraid of smallpox and considered those who died of it very unlucky. Therefore, even his sons did not touch his corpse for three days.

Eventually, fearing people's taunts, they dug a pit, dragged the corpse with sticks, threw it in, and covered it with earth. Some historians have written that people from a distance threw so many stones into that pit that his corpse was hidden under the stones. (`Zurqani`)

### Ghazwa-e-Bani Qainuqa

In Ramadan of 2 AH, the Beloved returned to Madina from the Battle of Badr. Shortly after, on the 15th of Shawwal, 2 AH, the incident of Ghazwa-e-Bani Qainuqa occurred. We have already written that three large Jewish tribes lived around Madina: Banu Qainuqa, Banu Nazeer, and Banu Quraiza. The Muslims had a treaty with all three.

But after the Battle of Badr, the first tribe to break the treaty was the Jews of Banu Qainuqa, who were the bravest and wealthiest. The incident occurred when a veiled Arab woman came to the Jewish market. The shopkeepers played mischief and exposed the woman. All the Jews laughed loudly. When the woman screamed, an Arab came and killed the shopkeeper. This led to a fight between the Jews and the Arabs.

When the Beloved came to know, he arrived and began to reproach the Jews for their disgraceful behaviour. The evil Jews of Banu Qainuqa became angry and said: "Do not be proud of the victory of Badr. The people of Makka were inexperienced in warfare, so you defeated them. If you ever face us in battle, you will know what war really is and what warriors are like."

When the Jews broke the treaty, the Beloved attacked them on Saturday, the middle of Shawwal, 2 AH. The Jews could not withstand the battle. They shut the gates of their fort and were besieged. After a 15-day siege, the Jews were eventually defeated and forced to surrender. On the advice of the Companions, the Beloved expelled these Jews from the city. These treacherous, covenant-breaking Jews settled in the place of Azaraat in Syria. (`Madarijun Nubuwwat`)

### Ghazwa-e-Saweeq

We have already written that after the Battle of Badr, every house in Makka was in mourning for the slain Quraish chiefs. Every child in Makka was restless and eager for revenge. Therefore, battles like Ghazwa-e-Saweeq and the Battle of Uhud were the result of this very spirit of revenge.

After the killing of Utba and Abu Jahl, the supreme chief of the Quraish was Abu Sufyan. The greatest task for this position was to avenge the Battle of Badr. Abu Sufyan took an oath that until he avenged the slain of Badr, he would not take a bath after janabat nor put oil in his hair.

Two months after the Battle of Badr, in Zulhijja 2 AH, Abu Sufyan advanced towards Madina with an army of 200 mounted camel riders. He had great confidence in the Jews' support against the Muslims. Hoping for this, Abu Sufyan first went to the Jew Huyay bin Akhtab, but he did not even open his door.

Disappointed from there, he met Salam bin Mishkam, the chief of the Banu Nazeer Jews and manager of the Jewish treasury. Salam welcomed Abu Sufyan enthusiastically and informed him of all the Beloved's military secrets. In the morning, Abu Sufyan attacked the settlement of Areez, three miles from Madina. In this attack, Abu Sufyan martyred an Ansari Companion named Saad bin Amr, cut down some trees, and set fire to some houses and gardens of Muslims.

In his estimation, these actions fulfilled his oath. When the Master of the Prophets came to know, he pursued him. But Abu Sufyan fled so frantically that to lighten his load, he threw away sacks of saweeq (barley porridge) that he had brought for his army's rations. These were seized by the Muslims. In Arabic, saweeq means barley porridge, so this Ghazwa was named Saweeq.

### The Marriage of Hazrat Fatima

In this same year, 2 AH, the Beloved's dearest daughter, Hazrat Fatima, was married to Hazrat Ali. This marriage was conducted with great dignity and simplicity. The Beloved ordered Hazrat Anas to invite Hazrat Abu Bakr, Hazrat Umar, Hazrat Usman, Hazrat Abdurrahman bin Auf, and some other Muhajireen and Ansar. When the Companions gathered, the Beloved delivered a sermon and performed the marriage.

The list of items that the King of Both Worlds gave as dowry to the Princess of Islam, Hazrat Fatima, is as follows: one blanket, one cot made of palm branches, a leather mattress stuffed with date-palm bark instead of cotton, one leather water-bag, one water-skin, two grinding mills, and two earthen pots.

Hazrat Harisa bin Noman Ansari donated his house to the Beloved so that Hazrat Ali and Hazrat Fatima could live there. When Hazrat Bibi Fatima left for her new home, after the Isha prayer, the Beloved came to the house. He asked for a vessel of water, rinsed his mouth, and sprinkled water on Hazrat Ali's chest and arms. Then he called Hazrat Fatima and sprinkled water on her head and chest. Then he prayed: "O Allah! I place Ali and Fatima and their progeny under Your protection, that they remain safe from the mischief of Satan."

### Miscellaneous Events of 2 AH

(1) In this year, the commandments regarding the obligation of fasting and zakat were revealed. Like prayer, fasting and zakat became obligatory upon the Muslims.

(2) In this year, the Beloved offered the Eidul Fitr prayer with the congregation at the Eidgah for the first time. Before this, the Eidul Fitr prayer had not been performed.

(3) The command to give Sadaqah Fitr was also issued in this year.

(4) In this year, on the 10th of Zulhijja, the Beloved performed the Eidul Adha prayer and sacrificed two rams after the prayer.

(5) In this year, the expeditions of Ghazwa-e-Quratul Kudr and Ghazwa-e-Bahraan and a few other small expeditions also took place, in which the Beloved participated, but no battle occurred.
