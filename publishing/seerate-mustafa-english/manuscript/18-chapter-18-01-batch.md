## Chapter 18: The Prophetic Character

### The Character of the Prophet

Need anyone ask about the beautiful character of the Prophet ﷺ when the Creator of character Himself has declared: "O Beloved! Indeed, you are upon an exalted standard of character." (Al-Qalam: 4)

Today, nearly 1400 years have passed — what courage do the enemies of the Prophet have to call him ill-mannered, when the Prophet ﷺ demonstrated his practical character even among his enemies? Allah Almighty proclaimed in the Quran: "(O Beloved!) It is by the mercy of Allah that you deal gently with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have dispersed from around you." (Aal-e-Imran)

The enemies of the Prophet heard this divine declaration from the tongue of the Quran, but none dared to contradict it or deny a truth more brilliant than the sun. Indeed, even the greatest enemies of the Prophet ﷺ acknowledged that he was of exceedingly exalted character, gentle-natured, compassionate, and generous.

In short, the noble Prophet ﷺ encompassed all the excellences of character: forbearance and forgiveness, mercy and compassion, justice and equity, generosity and munificence, altruism and sacrifice, hospitality, absence of harshness, bravery, fulfillment of promises, good dealings, patience and contentment, gentle speech, cheerfulness, friendliness, equality, sympathy for grief, simplicity and informality, humility and self-effacement, and modesty. He attained such high stations in all these that Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) captured the true picture in one sentence: "His character was the practical embodiment of the Quranic teachings."

We have written a detailed discourse on the Prophetic character in our book "Haqqani Taqreerein." Here, too, we present some blossoms from the branches of the "Tree of Eternity" of the Prophetic character, so that we may implement them and make our Islamic life complete and perfect, becoming complete Muslims in the world of Islam.

### The Prophet's Intellect

Since all intellectual, practical, and moral perfections revolve around the intellect, it is essential to write something about the Prophet's intellect. In this regard, we present just one reference:

Wahb bin Munabbih (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) said: "I have read in seventy-one books that if the intellects of all human beings from the creation of the world until the Day of Judgment were compared to the blessed intellect of the Prophet ﷺ, the relationship of all human intellects to his blessed intellect would be like that of a single grain of sand to all the deserts of the world. That is, all human intellects are equal to one grain of sand, and the Prophet's blessed intellect is equal to all the deserts of the world." This hadith was narrated by Abu Nuaim in Hilya and by Ibn Asakir. (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 250; Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 42)

### Forbearance and Forgiveness (Hilm Wa Afw)

Sayyidina Zaid bin Sa'ana (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), who was originally a great Jewish scholar, had purchased dates from the Prophet ﷺ. With one or two days remaining before the delivery date, he came in a crowded gathering and demanded payment with extreme bitterness and harshness. He grabbed the Prophet's cloak and shawl, looked at him with fierce eyes, and shouted: "O Muhammad! All the children of Abdul Muttalib have this habit — you always delay paying people's rights! Delaying and evading has become your custom!"

Seeing this scene, Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) lost control and said with furious, venomous looks: "O enemy of Allah! Are you being so insolent to the Messenger of Allah? By Allah! If I did not respect the Prophet's ﷺ dignity, I would cut off your head with my sword this instant!"

Hearing this, the Prophet ﷺ said: "O Umar! What are you doing? You should have encouraged me to fulfill the right and advised him to demand gently — thus helping both of us." Then the Prophet ﷺ ordered: "O Umar! Give him dates equal to his right, and give him some extra as well."

When Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) gave more dates than his due, Sayyidina Zaid bin Sa'ana (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) asked: "O Umar! Why are you giving me more than my right?" He replied: "Because I looked at you with fierce and crooked eyes and frightened you, the Prophet ﷺ ordered me to give you extra to console your heart."

Hearing this, Sayyidina Zaid bin Sa'ana said: "O Umar! Do you recognize me? I am Zaid bin Sa'ana." Sayyidina Umar asked: "Are you the same Zaid bin Sa'ana, the greatest scholar of the Jews?" He said: "Yes." Sayyidina Umar asked: "Then why did you behave so insolently with the Prophet?"

Sayyidina Zaid bin Sa'ana replied: "O Umar! The fact is that I had read in the Torah all the signs of the Prophet of the End Times, and I have seen all of them in his person. But two signs remained for which I needed to test him. One is that his forbearance overcomes ignorance — the more ignorance he is confronted with, the more his forbearance increases. I have now witnessed both signs in him. I bear witness that he is indeed the true Prophet. And O Umar! I am a very wealthy man. I make you my witness that I have given half my wealth in charity to the Ummah of the Prophet." Then he came to the court of prophethood and entered the fold of Islam by reciting the Kalima. (Dalayilun Nubuwwah, Vol. 1, p. 23; Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 253)

Sayyidina Jubair bin Mut'im (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "On the return from the Battle of Hunain, Bedouins surrounded the Prophet ﷺ, demanding wealth. They pressed him so much that he was forced back against a thorny acacia tree. A Bedouin snatched his blessed cloak. The Prophet ﷺ stood and said: 'Return my cloak to me. If I had as many cattle as these thorny bushes, I would distribute them all among you. You will find me neither miserly, nor a liar, nor a coward.'" (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 446)

Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates: "I was walking with the Prophet ﷺ, who was wearing a Najrani cloak with thick, coarse edges. Suddenly, a Bedouin grabbed him and pulled the cloak so violently that the coarse edge scratched the Prophet's delicate neck. The Bedouin then said: 'Command that some of the wealth Allah has given you be given to me.' The Prophet of Mercy ﷺ turned to the Bedouin and, with complete forbearance, smiled at him and ordered that he be given something." (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 446)

In the Battle of Uhud, Utba bin Abi Waqqas broke the Prophet's ﷺ blessed tooth, and Abdullah bin Qamia wounded his blessed face, causing it to bleed. But the Prophet ﷺ said nothing more than: "O Allah! Guide my people, for they do not know."

At Khaibar, a Jewish woman named Zainab poisoned the Prophet ﷺ, but he did not take revenge against her. Lubaid bin Aasam cast a spell on the Prophet, and through revelation the entire matter became known, but the Prophet did not punish him. Ghauras bin Al Haris, intending to kill the Prophet, drew his sword from its sheath. When the Prophet awoke from sleep, Ghauras said: "O Muhammad! Who will save you from me now?" The Prophet replied: "Allah." The awe of prophethood caused the sword to fall from his hand, and the Prophet picked up the sword and said: "Speak! Who will save you from me now?" Ghauras pleaded, "You save my life!" The Prophet of Mercy released him and forgave him. Ghauras went to his people and said: "O people! I have come from the best of all the people in the world." (Shifa Qazi Ayaz, Vol. 1, p. 62)

The disbelievers of Makka had done every kind of oppression against the Prophet ﷺ. But on the day of the Conquest of Makka, when all these tyrants of Quraish were besieged by the armies of the Ansar and Muhajirun, trapped and helpless, trembling in fear and terror within the Sacred Sanctuary — every hair on their bodies shaking with the fear of revenge — the Prophet of Mercy forgave them, saying: "There is no accountability today. Go, you are all free."

A disbeliever was brought to the Prophet, caught by the noble Companions. They said: "O Messenger of Allah! This man intended to kill you." The man was trembling with fear. The Mercy to the Worlds ﷺ said: "Do not fear. If you intended to kill me, so what? You could never have overcome me, for Allah Almighty has promised my protection." (Shifa Qazi Ayaz, Vol. 1, p. 63)

In short, there are thousands such incidents in the blessed life of the noble Prophet ﷺ that prove that forbearance and forgiveness — tolerating harm and letting off criminals despite having the power to take revenge — was his noble habit, a magnificent masterpiece of his beautiful character, unparalleled in the world.

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ never took revenge for himself. However, if anyone violated the sanctity of Allah, he would certainly hold them accountable."

### Humility (Tawazo'a)

The Prophet's ﷺ humility was unique in all the world. Allah Almighty granted him the choice: "O Beloved! If you wish, you may live a royal life, and if you wish, you may live the life of a servant." He chose to live as a servant.

Sayyidina Israfeel (peace be upon him), seeing this humility, said: "O Messenger of Allah! Because of this humility, Allah Almighty has granted you the exalted rank that you are the most venerable and highest-ranking among all the children of Adam. On the Day of Judgment, you will be raised first from your grave, and you will be the first to intercede." (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 262; Shifa, Vol. 1, p. 86)

Sayyidina Abu Umama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates: "The Holy Prophet ﷺ came out of his house leaning on his staff. We all stood up out of respect. Seeing this, he said humbly: 'Do not stand up as the non-Arabs stand up to honor one another. I am a servant; I eat like a servant and sit like a servant.'" (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 86)

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "The Prophet of Both Worlds ﷺ would sometimes seat one of his servants behind him on his mount." According to a narration from Tirmizi, on the day of the Battle of Quraiza, the reins of his mount were made of palm fiber. (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 264)

Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "The Prophet ﷺ would accept the invitation of even a slave. If invited to barley bread and stale fat, he would accept. He would visit the sick among the poor, sit with the needy, and mix freely with his Companions." (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 77)

Sayyidina Abu Saeed Khudri (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) said: "The Prophet ﷺ would do his own household chores with his own blessed hands. He would sit and eat with his servants and help them with household work." (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 77)

A man came to the court of prophethood and was overcome with awe, trembling with fear. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not be afraid. I am not a king, nor a tyrant ruler. I am the son of a woman of Quraish who used to eat dried meat." (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 286; Shifa, Vol. 1, p. 78)

On the day of the Conquest of Makka, when the Prophet ﷺ entered the city with his armies in a triumphant procession, such was his humility and self-effacement that he sat on his she-camel with his head bowed, his blessed head touching the front part of the saddle. (Shifa, Vol. 1, p. 77)

Similarly, during the Farewell Pilgrimage, when the Prophet ﷺ went with a hundred thousand moths of the candle of prophethood on his final Hajj, his camel had an old saddle, and he wore a cloak worth no more than four dirhams. On that same camel's back, in that same attire, as the honorable deputy of Allah Almighty and the Sovereign of Both Worlds, he delivered his imperial sermon, listened to by over a hundred thousand children of monotheism with utmost attention. (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 268)

Sayyidina Abdullah bin Aamir (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates: "Once the strap of the Prophet's sandal broke, and he began repairing it with his own hands. I said: 'O Messenger of Allah! Give it to me; let me fix it.' He replied: 'It is true that you will fix it, but I do not like to show superiority over you.'" Similarly, the noble Companions, seeing the Prophet busy with some work, would repeatedly request: "O Messenger of Allah! Do not do this work yourself; let us do it." But the Prophet would say: "It is true that you will do all my work, but I cannot tolerate living among you with any special distinction." (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 265)

### Good Treatment (Husne Mu'aashrat)

The Holy Prophet ﷺ treated his pure wives, his friends, his Companions, his relatives, and his neighbors with such excellent manners and affability that every one of them was captivated by and full of praise for his beautiful character.

His special servant, Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), says: "I had the honor of serving the Prophet ﷺ for ten years, both during travel and at home. He never scolded me or rebuked me, nor did he ever say, 'Why did you do that?' or 'Why didn't you do that?'" (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 266)

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "No one had better manners than the Prophet ﷺ. Whenever any of his Companions or family members called him, he would reply, 'Labbaik (I am here).'" Sayyidina Jarir (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "Ever since I became Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ never stopped me from coming to him. Whenever he saw me, he would smile. He was cheerful with his Companions, mixing freely with everyone, and conversing with each one. He was also cheerful with the children of the Companions, seating them in his blessed lap. He would accept the invitations of free men, slaves, servants, and the poor. He visited the sick in the remote parts of Madina and accepted the excuses of those who offered them." (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 71)

Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates: "If someone whispered something in the Prophet's ear, he would not turn his head away until that person finished speaking. He never sat with his legs stretched out in the company of his Companions. He would take the initiative in greeting anyone who came to him. He would shake hands with visitors. Generally, he would spread his cloak or offer his seat to visitors. He called his Companions by their patronymics (kunyat) and good names. He never interrupted anyone's speech. He met everyone cheerfully and with a smile.

The servants and maids of Madina would bring water in vessels in the morning so that the Prophet would dip his blessed hand in their vessels, making the water blessed. Even in the severe winter season, the Prophet would dip his sacred hand into each vessel, not depriving anyone despite the cold." (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 72)

Sayyidina Amr bin Sahib (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) said: "Once I was present in the Prophet's service. His foster father (the husband of Sayyida Halima) came, and the Prophet spread a portion of his garment for him to sit on. Then his foster mother Sayyida Halima came, and he spread the remaining portion of his garment for her. Then his foster brother came, and he seated him in front of himself. The Prophet would always send clothes and other things to Sayyida Suwaiba (the freed slave-girl of Abu Lahab who had also nursed him for a few days)." (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 75)

The Prophet did not keep a separate bed for himself; he would rest on the beds of his pure wives. He always kept his blessed wives happy with his love and affection. Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "When I drank water from a cup and then gave it to the Prophet, he would place his lips where my lips had been and drink. When I chewed meat from a bone and gave it to him, he would place his mouth where my mouth had been." (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 269)

Every day, the Prophet would visit his pure wives and also honor his daughters' homes with his presence, inquiring about their well-being. He repeatedly showered his grandchildren with love and affection, consoling and keeping everyone happy. He would converse with children, taking pleasure in their talk and entertaining them. He also looked after his neighbors and treated them with utmost generosity and kindness. In short, through his conduct and holy way of life, the Prophet established such an Islamic society that if the world were to follow his blessed example today, a river of peace, love, and mercy would flow throughout the world, the hellfire of conflict, hatred, and discord would be extinguished, and the world would become a paradise of peace, comfort, and love.
