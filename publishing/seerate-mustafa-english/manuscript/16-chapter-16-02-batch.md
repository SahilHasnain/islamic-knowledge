## Chapter 16: The Eleventh Year of Hijra — 11 AH (Continued)

### The Impact of the Prophet's Departure

What a tremendous shock the departure of the Holy Prophet ﷺ was to the noble Companions and the revered Ahl al-Bayt (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum)! What state befell the people of Madina? Thousands of pages could not even begin to depict it. Those moths of the candle of prophethood — whose hearts would become restless and eyes tearful if they did not behold the blessed countenance for even a few days — how heart-rending and agonizing must their eternal separation from the Beloved of the Universe ﷺ have been?

The eminent Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) lost their senses, to say the least. Their wits left them, their voices fell silent, and they became so stupefied that it became difficult for them to think of what to say or do. Sayyidina Usman Ghani (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) was struck with such a stupor that he wandered aimlessly here and there, neither speaking to anyone nor listening to anyone. Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) sat so overwhelmed by grief and sorrow that he had no strength left to rise, sit, or move. Sayyidina Abdullah bin Anees (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) received such a blow to his heart that he could not bear it, and his heart gave out.

Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) was so beside himself that he drew his sword and, with the naked blade in hand, roamed the streets of Madina, saying: "If anyone says that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ has died, I will cut off his head with this sword."

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) relates that after the Prophet's departure, Sayyidina Umar and Sayyidina Mughira bin Sh'aba (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) sought permission and entered the room. Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) looked at the Prophet ﷺ and said: "A very deep state of unconsciousness has overcome him." When they were leaving, Sayyidina Mughira (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) said: "O Umar! Do you know what has happened? The Prophet ﷺ has passed away." Hearing this, Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) lost control and exclaimed passionately: "O Mughira! You lie! The Prophet ﷺ cannot pass away until every single hypocrite in the world is eliminated."

It is narrated in Mawahib Ladunniyyah from Tabari that at the time of the Prophet's departure, Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) was in "Sukh", a place one mile from Masjid-e-Nabawi where his wife Sayyida Habiba bint Khariza (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) lived. Since there had been some improvement in the illness on Monday morning, the Prophet ﷺ had granted Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) permission to go to Sukh to see his family.

According to Sahih Bukhari and other sources, Sayyidina Abu Bakr (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) came from Sukh on his horse, without speaking to or hearing from anyone. He went straight to Sayyida Ayesha's (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) apartment, removed the sheet from the Prophet's ﷺ blessed face, leaned over him, and kissed him between the eyes with intense emotion, saying: "You are pure in both life and departure. May my parents be sacrificed for you. Allah will never cause two departures to meet you. The departure that was decreed for you, you have died it."

After this, Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) went to the mosque, where Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) was addressing the people. Sayyidina Abu Bakr (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) said: "O Umar! Sit down." Sayyidina Umar refused to sit, so Sayyidina Abu Bakr left him and began to address the people, turning their attention to him:

"O people! Whoever among you worshipped Muhammad ﷺ, let him know that Muhammad has passed away. And whoever among you worshipped Allah Almighty, then Allah is Alive and will never die." Then Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) recited this verse from Surah Aal-e-Imran:

> "And Muhammad is but a Messenger; many Messengers have passed away before him. If then he departs (from this world) or is martyred, will you turn back on your heels? And he who turns back on his heels will not harm Allah in the least. And soon Allah will reward the grateful." (Aal-e-Imran: 144)

Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) says: "When Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) recited this verse, it seemed as though no one had ever known it. After hearing it from him, everyone began reciting it." (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 166; Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 433)

Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "When I heard this verse of Surah Aal-e-Imran from the tongue of Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique, I realized that indeed the Prophet ﷺ had passed away." Then Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) withdrew from his earlier proclamation — made in a state of agitation with his drawn sword — that the Prophet had not died. His son, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma), says: "It was as if a veil had been cast over us, and our attention was not drawn to this verse. The sermon of Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique lifted that veil." (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 434)

### Preparation and Shrouding

Since the Holy Prophet ﷺ had instructed that his preparation and shrouding be performed by his Ahl al-Bayt and family members, this service was carried out by the members of his own household. Sayyidina Fadl bin Abbas, Sayyidina Qusam bin Abbas, Sayyidina Ali, Sayyidina Abbas, and Sayyidina Usama bin Zaid (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) collectively performed his Ghusl. Where water droplets and moisture had collected around the blessed navel and eyelids, Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), in his intense love and devotion, licked and consumed them with his tongue. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, pp. 438-439)

After Ghusl, a shroud of three cotton garments was prepared, made of "Suhool" cloth from a village. There was no shirt or turban among them. (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 169)

### Funeral Prayer

When the blessed body was ready, people rushed to offer the funeral prayer. First the men, then the women, then the children offered the funeral prayer. The blessed body remained inside the sacred chamber. Groups entered one after another, offered prayer, and left, but there was no Imam. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 240; Ibn Majah, p. 118)

### The Blessed Grave

Sayyidina Abu Talha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) prepared the grave, which was of the "baghli" type (a niche dug sideways). The blessed body was lowered into the grave by Sayyidina Ali, Sayyidina Fadl bin Abbas, Sayyidina Abbas, and Sayyidina Qusam bin Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum). (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 442) However, narrations from Abu Dawood indicate that Sayyidina Usama and Abdurrahman bin Auf (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) also descended into the grave. (Abu Dawood, Vol. 2, p. 458)

There arose a difference of opinion among the noble Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) as to where the Prophet ﷺ should be buried. Some suggested he should be buried in Masjid-e-Nabawi, while others thought he should be buried in the cemetery of the Companions. At this juncture, Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) said: "I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say that every Prophet is buried at the place where he dies." Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) says: "Upon hearing this hadith, the people lifted the Prophet's bedding and prepared his grave at that very spot (the chamber of Sayyida Ayesha), and he was buried there." (Ibn Majah, p. 118)

The moths of the candle of prophethood must have been immensely restless to participate in the honor of the Prophet's Ghusl and shrouding. However, as we have written, since the Prophet ﷺ had himself instructed that his Ghusl and preparation be performed only by his Ahl al-Bayt, and the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), also decreed that this was the right of the Ahl al-Bayt, Sayyidina Abbas and the Ahl al-Bayt closed the door and performed the Ghusl and enshrouding. But from beginning to end, the Commander of the Faithful himself and all other noble Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) remained present outside the sacred chamber. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 437)

### The Prophet's Legacy

The holy life of the Prophet ﷺ was so ascetic that he kept nothing for himself. So what could he have left behind after his departure?

Sayyidina Amr bin Al Haris (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates that at the time of his departure, the Prophet ﷺ left behind neither a dirham nor a dinar, neither a slave-girl nor a slave, nor anything else — only his white mule, his weapons, and some land which he had given in charity to the general body of Muslims. (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 382)

Nevertheless, his legacy included three notable things: (1) The lands of Banu Nazeer, Fidak, and Khaibar, (2) riding animals, (3) weapons.

**Land:** The income from the orchards and lands of Banu Nazeer, Fidak, and Khaibar was used by the Prophet ﷺ for his own and his pure wives' annual expenses, as well as for the needy, the poor, and the general needs of Muslims. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 445; Abu Dawood, Vol. 2, p. 412)

After the Prophet's departure, Sayyidina Abbas, Sayyidina Fatima (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma), and some of the pure wives desired that these properties be distributed as inheritance among the heirs. They presented this request before the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). However, he and Sayyidina Umar and other senior Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) informed them of this hadith: "We (the Prophets) leave no inheritance; whatever we leave behind is charity for the Muslims."

In light of this hadith, they clearly stated that according to the Prophet's will, these properties had already been endowed as a waqf. Therefore, no change could be made in the purposes and expenditures for which the Prophet ﷺ used their income during his blessed life. During his caliphate, Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), at the insistence of Sayyidina Abbas and Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), appointed both of them as trustees of the Banu Nazeer property on the condition that they would spend its income on the same purposes as the Prophet ﷺ had. Later, some discord arose between them, and they expressed the wish that the Banu Nazeer property be divided — half under the trusteeship of Sayyidina Abbas and half under Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). However, Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) did not accept this request. (Abu Dawood, Vol. 2, p. 413; Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 436)

The lands of Khaibar and Fidak remained in the hands of the Caliphs until the time of Sayyidina Umar bin Abdul Azeez (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). The governor of Madina, Marwan bin Al Hakam, had made them his fief, but Sayyidina Umar bin Abdul Azeez (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) restored the same practice that had been followed during the caliphates of Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique and Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma).

**Riding Animals:** According to Zurqani and other sources, the Prophet ﷺ owned seven horses, five mules, three donkeys, and two she-camels. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 386-391) However, it is not specified how many of these animals were present at the time of his departure, as the Prophet ﷺ would give animals to others, buy new ones, and also receive them as gifts.

According to authentic narrations, the riding animals present at the time of the Prophet's departure included: a horse named "Laheef", a white mule named "Duldul" (which lived a very long life — it survived until the time of Sayyidina Ameer Muawiya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), having lost all its teeth and eventually gone blind; according to the history of Ibn Asakir, Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) also rode it in the battle against the Khawarij), an Arab donkey named "Afeee", and a she-camel named "Azba" or "Qaswa" — the same camel the Prophet ﷺ had purchased from Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) at the time of the Hijra, on whose back he performed the Hijra, and on whose back he delivered the sermons at Arafat and Mina during the Farewell Pilgrimage. (And Allah knows best.)

**Weapons:** Since the need for Jihad was ever-present, the Prophet's arsenal contained nine or ten swords, seven iron coats of mail, six bows, one quiver, one shield, five spears, two helmets, three jerkins, one large black flag along with smaller white and yellow flags, and one tent.

Regarding the swords, Shaykh Abdul Haq Muhaddith Dehlawi (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote that he does not know whether all these swords were in the Prophet's possession simultaneously or at different times.

**Utensils and Various Items:** There were several cups and vessels, including one made of glass. There was a wooden cup that had cracked, so Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) had bound it with a silver chain. (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 438) There was a leather bucket, an old water-skin, a stone trough, a large cup named "Alsa'a", a leather bag in which the Prophet ﷺ kept a mirror, scissors, and miswak, a comb, a surma container, a very large cup named "Al-Ghara", and measuring vessels for Sa'a and Mud.

Additionally, there was a cot with legs of black wood, which Sayyidina As'ad bin Zararah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) had presented as a gift. The bedding and pillow were of leather stuffed with date-palm fibers, and there were the blessed sandals. This is a list of the Prophet's belongings mentioned in various hadith.

### Blessed Relics (Tabarrukat-e-Nubuwwat)

In addition to these belongings, there were also some commemorative blessed relics that the lovers of the Prophet kept preserved in their homes with intense devotion, cherishing them more than their own lives. Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) preserved in his home three blessed relics: the blessed hair, the blessed sandals, and a wooden cup bound with silver wire. (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 438)

Similarly, Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) had a thick woolen blanket, which she kept as a blessed relic and would show to people. Sayyidina Abu Darda (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates: "We had the honor of visiting Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), and she brought out a thick blanket, saying: 'This is the same blanket in which the Prophet ﷺ passed away.'" (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 438)

One of the Prophet's swords, named "Zulfiqar", was with Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). After him, it remained in his family until it was with Sayyidina Imam Hussain (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) at Karbala. After his martyrdom, it remained with his son and successor, Imam Zainul Aabideen (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). When Imam Zainul Aabideen (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) took leave from Yazid bin Muawiya and returned to Madina, the famous Companion Sayyidina Miswar bin Makhrama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) visited him and said: "If you have any need or any service I can render, please command me, for I am ready to fulfill your orders." Imam Zainul Aabideen (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) replied that he had no need. Then Sayyidina Miswar bin Makhrama (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) requested: "Could you grant me the sword of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ (Zulfiqar) that is with you? I fear that Yazid's people may overpower you and this relic may be taken from you. If you give me this sacred sword, by Allah! As long as I have a single breath left, those people will never reach this sword." However, Imam Zainul Aabideen (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) did not consent to part with the sacred sword. (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 438)

The Prophet's ring and blessed staff remained in the possession of the successive Caliphs — Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique, Sayyidina Umar Farooq, and Sayyidina Usman Ghani (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) — during their respective caliphates. However, the ring fell from Sayyidina Usman's (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) hand into a well and was lost. That well is called "Beere Urais", also known as "Beere Khaatim". (Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 872)

As for the blessed staff, it was lost in this manner: The Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Usman Ghani (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), was delivering a sermon on the pulpit of Masjid-e-Nabawi, holding the sacred staff of the Prophet in his blessed hand, when suddenly an unfortunate man named "Jahjah Ghifari" arose and, snatching this sacred relic from his hand, broke it. Due to this insolence, divine punishment descended upon him: his hand became cancerous, the entire hand rotted and fell off, and he perished in this torment. (Dalayilun Nubuwwah, Vol. 3, p. 211)

**Note:** According to our research, Sayyidina Jahjah bin Saeed Ghifari (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) is a Companion of the Prophet, and we have not found any statement denying his Companionship. Therefore, such words should never be used for him.

**Author's Apology:** It cannot even be imagined that a common Muslim would intentionally use inappropriate words for a Companion. Surely, the author (may Allah have mercy on him) was unaware that this person was a Companion, because the incident here relates to the breaking of Sayyidina Usman Ghani's staff, which may have led the author to a leniency in expression. Otherwise, he would never have written such a thing about a Companion of the Prophet, especially since the author himself has mentioned the virtues of the noble Companions in his books, which proves that he was a firm Sunni of correct creed and a lover of the noble Companions (may Allah be pleased with them all).

**Islamic Creed Regarding the Companions:** The position of Ahl al-Sunnah regarding the noble Companions is: (1) It is absolutely forbidden to delve into the incidents that occurred between the noble Companions. Muslims should regard all of them as devoted servants and sincere lovers of the Chief of the Universe ﷺ. (2) The noble Companions were neither Prophets nor angels to be infallible. Some of them made mistakes, but to hold anyone accountable for anything they did...

There were many other such blessed relics preserved with various Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum), mentioned in various places in hadith and books of Seerah. The noble Companions and the great Tabi'un (may Allah have mercy on them) had such passionate love for these sacred relics that they cherished them more than their own lives.
