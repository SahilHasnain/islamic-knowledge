## Chapter 19: Ancestors, Servants, and Attendants (Continued)

### Paternal Uncles of the Holy Prophet ﷺ

Apart from Sayyidina Abdullah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), Abdul Muttalib had 12 sons. Their names are:

1. Harith
2. Abu Talib
3. Zubair
4. Hamza
5. Abbas
6. Abu Lahab
7. Ghaidaq
8. Maqoom
9. Zaraar
10. Qasm
11. Abdul Ka'aba
12. Zahl

Among these, only Sayyidina Hamza and Sayyidina Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) accepted Islam. Sayyidina Hamza (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) was extremely strong and brave. The Holy Prophet ﷺ honored him with the distinguished title of "Asadullah" and "Asadur Rasool" (the Lion of Allah and the Lion of His Messenger). He was martyred in the Battle of Uhud in 3 AH and became known as "Sayyidush Shuhada" (the Chief of Martyrs). His illuminated shrine, three miles from Madina, in the battlefield of Uhud, is a place of visitation for the Islamic world.

Numerous hadith have been narrated concerning the virtues of Sayyidina Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). The Holy Prophet gave him and his children many glad tidings and blessed supplications. He passed away in 32 or 33 AH at the age of 87 or 88 and was buried in Jannatul Baqi. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 270-285; Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 288)

### Paternal Aunts of the Holy Prophet ﷺ

The number of his paternal aunts is 6. Their names are:

1. Aatika
2. Umaima
3. Umme Hakeem
4. Barrah
5. Safiyya
6. Arawi

Among these, all historians agree that Sayyida Safiyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) accepted Islam. She is the mother of Sayyidina Zubair bin Al Awwam (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). She was a very brave and courageous lady. In the Battle of the Trench, she single-handedly killed an armed and attacking Jew with a wooden pole. This account has been mentioned in the Battle of the Trench. It is also narrated that in the Battle of Uhud, when the Muslim army had scattered, she stood alone, throwing a spear at the disbelievers. The Holy Prophet was extremely amazed at her extraordinary bravery and said to her son, Sayyidina Zubair (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu): "Look at this woman's courage and self-sacrifice!" She passed away in Madina in 20 AH at the age of 73 and was buried in Jannatul Baqi. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 287-288)

Regarding the acceptance of Islam by Arawi, Aatika, and Umaima besides Safiyya, historians differ. Some have written that these three also became Muslim, while according to others, their Islam is not established. And Allah knows best. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 287)

### Special Servants (Khuddame Khaas)

Although all the noble Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) are special servants of the prophetic court, the following Companions are particularly notable in this blessed list:

**1. Sayyidina Anas bin Maalik (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He is the most famous and distinguished servant of the Holy Prophet ﷺ. He had the honor of serving the Prophet faithfully for 10 continuous years in travel and at home. The Holy Prophet made a special supplication for him: "O Allah! Grant abundance in his wealth and children, and enter him into Paradise." Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "I have witnessed the acceptance of two of these three supplications. Every person's garden bears fruit once a year, but my garden bears fruit twice a year, and the fruits have the fragrance of musk. My children number 106 — 70 sons and the rest daughters. And I hope to witness the acceptance of the third supplication as well — that I will enter Paradise." He narrated 2286 hadith from the Holy Prophet, and his students in hadith are very numerous. He lived over 100 years and passed away in Basra in 91, 92, or 93 AH. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 296-297)

**2. Sayyidina Rabi'a bin Ka'ab Aslami (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He would prepare water and Miswak for the Holy Prophet's ablution (Wuzoo). The Prophet gave him glad tidings of Paradise. He passed away in 63 AH. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 297)

**3. Sayyida Aiman bint Umme Aiman (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha):** She was the Prophet's small water-skin (Mashk) which he used for Istinja and Wuzoo, and she always kept it in her custody. She was honored with martyrdom on the day of the Battle of Hunain. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 297)

**4. Sayyidina Abdullah ibn Mas'ood (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He kept the Prophet's blessed sandals, Wuzoo vessel, cushion, and Miswak. He always performed this service in travel and at home. He lived over 60 years and passed away in 32 or 33 AH — according to some, in Madina, and according to others, in Kufa. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 297-298)

**5. Sayyidina Uqba bin Aamir Zuhni (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He would hold the reins of the Prophet's riding mule. He was very proficient in the Quranic sciences and the laws of inheritance (Fara'id). He was a highly eloquent orator and a fiery poet. Sayyidina Amir Muawiya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) appointed him governor of Egypt during his caliphate. He passed away in Egypt in 58 AH. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 299)

**6. Sayyidina Asal bin Shareek (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He would saddle the howdah on the Prophet's camel.

**7. Sayyidina Abu Zar Ghifari (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He was an early Companion. He was extremely detached from worldly life, a devoted worshipper and ascetic, and a very special servant of the prophetic court. Several hadith have been narrated concerning his virtues. He passed away in 31 AH in a village called "Rabza," some distance from Madina. Sayyidina Abdullah bin Mas'ood (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) led his funeral prayer. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 300)

**8. Sayyidina Muhajir, freedman of Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma):** He was the freed slave of the Mother of the Believers, Sayyida Umme Salma. Along with the honor of Companionship, he also had the honor of serving the Holy Prophet for 5 years. He was a very brave mujahid, part of the army that conquered Egypt. He lived in Egypt for some time, then moved to "Yaha" (a place), where he remained until his death. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 301)

**9. Sayyidina Hunain, freedman of Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma):** He was originally the Prophet's servant, serving him day and night. Then the Prophet gave him to his uncle, Sayyidina Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). But after a few days, Abbas freed him so that he could remain in the prophetic court day and night and continue serving. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 301)

**10. Sayyidina Nu'am bin Rabi'a Aslami (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He was also counted among the special attendants of the prophetic court. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 301)

**11. Sayyidina Abul Hamra (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** His name was Hilaal bin Al Harith. He was the Prophet's freed slave and special servant. After the Prophet's departure, he moved from Madina to "Hims" (Syria), where he died. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 301)

**12. Sayyidina Abussam'a (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He was the Prophet's slave. Then the Prophet freed him, but he did not separate from the prophetic court and remained engaged in service. He used to give the Prophet his Ghusl bath. His name was "Iyaad." (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 301)

### Special Guards

Since the disbelievers were the sworn enemies of the Holy Prophet ﷺ and were always on the lookout for an opportunity to assassinate him — indeed, they had made murderous attacks multiple times — some self-sacrificing Companions would take turns guarding the Prophet's various resting and halting places at night with drawn swords. This continued until this verse was revealed: "Allah will protect you from the people." After this verse was revealed, the Prophet said: "There is no longer any need for guarding. Allah has promised me that He will protect me from all my enemies."

Among these devoted guards, the following fortunate Companions are particularly notable:

1. Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique
2. Sayyidina Sa'ad bin Muaaz Ansari
3. Sayyidina Muhammad bin Maslama
4. Sayyidina Zakwan bin Abde Qais
5. Sayyidina Zubair bin Al Awwam
6. Sayyidina Sa'ad bin Abi Waqqas
7. Sayyidina Abbad bin Bishr
8. Sayyidina Abu Ayyub Ansari
9. Sayyidina Bilal
10. Sayyidina Mughira bin Sha'aba

(Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum Ajmayin)

### Poets of the Prophetic Court

Many noble Companions were honored with composing poems in praise of the Holy Prophet. However, the special poets of the prophetic court are three, who not only composed naat but also gave crushing replies to the poetic attacks of the disbelievers.

**1. Sayyidina Ka'ab bin Maalik Ansari Sulami (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He was one of those who did not participate in the Battle of Tabuk and were reprimanded. Later, the acceptance of his repentance was revealed in the Holy Quran. He said: "The Holy Prophet said to us: 'Satirize the polytheists, for the believer continues to strive with his life and wealth, and your verses are like arrows against the disbelievers.'" He passed away during the caliphate of Sayyidina Ali or the reign of Sayyidina Amir Muawiya.

**2. Sayyidina Abdullah bin Rawaha Ansari Khazraji (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** Several hadith have been narrated regarding his virtues. The Holy Prophet awarded him the title "Sayyidush Shu'ara" (Chief of the Poets). He was honored with martyrdom in the Battle of Mauta.

**3. Sayyidina Hassan bin Sabit bin Munzir bin Amr Ansari Khazraji (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):** He is the most famous among the poets of the prophetic court. The Holy Prophet made a supplication for him: "O Allah! Support him through Sayyidina Jibreel." He also said: "As long as he replies to the disbelievers of Makka on my behalf with his verses, Sayyidina Jibreel remains with him." He lived 120 years — 60 in the time of ignorance and 60 in the service of Islam. It is a historical curiosity that he, his father "Sabit," his grandfather "Munzir," and his great-grandfather "Hiraam" all lived 120 years. He passed away in 54 AH. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 372-373)

### Special Muezzins

The Holy Prophet had four special muezzins:

1. Sayyidina Bilal bin Rabaah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu)
2. Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umme Maktoom (the blind) (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu)
3. Sayyidina Sa'ad bin A'iz (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) — known as "Sa'ade Qaraz," the muezzin of Masjid Quba
4. Sayyidina Abu Mahzoora (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) — he would call the Adhan in the Sacred Mosque of Makka. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 269-271)
