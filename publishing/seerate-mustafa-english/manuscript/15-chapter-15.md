## Chapter 15: The Tenth Year of Hijra — 10 AH

### Hajjatul Wida (The Farewell Pilgrimage)

Among all the events of this year, the most splendid and significant was the "Farewell Pilgrimage". This was the final Hajj of the Prophet ﷺ, and it was also his first Hajj after the Hijra. In the month of Zul Qadah, 10 AH, the Prophet ﷺ announced his intention to depart for Hajj. This news spread across the entire Arabian Peninsula like lightning, and people from all over Arabia rushed forward to earn the honor of accompanying him.

The Holy Prophet ﷺ performed Ghusl in Madina on Thursday at the end of Zul Qadah, donned an Izar (lower garment) and a Chadar (upper wrap), offered Zuhr prayer in Masjid-e-Nabawi, and then departed from Madina Munawwara. He instructed all his pure wives (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhunn) to accompany him as well.

Six miles from Madina Munawwara, at the Miqat of the people of Madina called "Zul Haleefa", he halted for the night. He then performed Ghusl for Ihram, and Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) applied perfume to his blessed body with her own hands. The Prophet ﷺ then offered two Rak'ats of prayer, mounted his she-camel "Qaswa", recited the Talbiyah ("Labbaik") in a loud voice, and set forth. Sayyidina Jabir (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates: "I raised my gaze and looked — ahead, behind, to the right and to the left, as far as the eye could see, there was a forest of people." According to a narration of Baihaqi, there were 114,000 Muslims with the Prophet during the Farewell Pilgrimage, and according to another narration, 124,000. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 106; Madarij, Vol. 2, p. 387)

On the 4th of Zul Hijja, the Prophet ﷺ entered Makka Mukarrama. When the boys of his clan, Bani Hashim, heard the news of his arrival, they ran out in joy, and the Prophet ﷺ, with immense love and affection, seated some in front and some behind on his she-camel. (Nisai)

The Prophet ﷺ offered Fajr prayer at a place called "Zee Tuwa" and performed Ghusl. He then entered Makka Mukarrama and at the time of Chasht (mid-morning, when the sun had risen high), he entered the Masjid al-Haram. When his blessed eyes fell upon the Kaaba, he recited this supplication:

"O Allah! You are the Giver of peace and from You comes peace. O Lord! Keep us alive in peace. O Allah! Increase the grandeur, honor, and reverence of this House, and increase the honor and dignity of the one who performs Hajj or Umrah of this House."

When the Prophet ﷺ reached before the Black Stone (Hajr Aswad), he placed his hand upon it and kissed it. He then performed Tawaf of the Kaaba. In the first three circuits he performed Ramal (walking briskly with short steps), and in the remaining four circuits he walked at a normal pace. In each circuit, when he reached before the Black Stone, he would point toward it with his staff and then kiss the staff. Sometimes he would touch the Black Stone with his staff, sometimes he would touch it with his hand and then kiss his hand, sometimes he would place his blessed lips on the Black Stone and kiss it, and it is also established that he sometimes touched the Yemeni Corner (Rukn Yamaani) as well. (Nisai, Vol. 2, pp. 30-31)

When he completed Tawaf, he came to Maqam-e-Ibrahim and offered two Rak'ats of prayer there. After completing the prayer, he again touched the Black Stone and proceeded toward Safa through the front door.

When he drew near, he recited this verse:

> "Indeed, Safa and Marwah are among the symbols of Allah." (Al-Baqarah: 158)

He then performed Sa'ee between Safa and Marwah. Since the Prophet ﷺ had sacrificial animals with him, he did not remove his Ihram after performing Umrah.

On the 8th of Zul Hijja, Thursday, the Prophet ﷺ went to Mina. He offered five prayers — Zuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha, and Fajr — in Mina. On the 9th of Zul Hijja, Friday, he proceeded to Arafat.

During the Days of Ignorance (Jahiliyya), the Quraish considered themselves superior to all other Arabs. They would halt at Muzdalifa instead of Arafat, while the rest of the Arabs would stay at Arafat. However, Islamic equality did not permit this distinction for the Quraish, and Allah revealed the command:

> "Then return from where the people return, and ask forgiveness of Allah. Indeed, Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful." (Al-Baqarah: 199)

Upon reaching Arafat, the Prophet ﷺ set up camp in a woolen tent. When the sun passed its zenith, he mounted his she-camel "Qaswa" and delivered the sermon. In this sermon, he proclaimed many essential Islamic rulings and, while abolishing all evils and absurd customs of the Jahiliyya, declared:

"Listen! All customs of the Jahiliyya are crushed beneath my two feet."

(Abu Dawood, Vol. 1, p. 263; Muslim, Vol. 1, p. 397)

Similarly, shattering the idols of Jahiliyya — such as familial pride, racial superiority, and notions of high and low among tribes — and raising high the banner of Islamic equality, the Sovereign of Both Worlds ﷺ stated in this historic sermon:

"O people! Indeed, your Lord is One, and indeed, your father (Adam, peace be upon him) is one. Listen! No Arab has superiority over a non-Arab, and no non-Arab has superiority over an Arab; no red-skinned person has superiority over a black-skinned person, and no black-skinned person has superiority over a red-skinned person, except through piety."

Likewise, to establish peace and security throughout the world, the Emperor of Peace and Security, the Sovereign of Both Worlds ﷺ issued this divine decree:

"Your blood and your wealth are as sacred to you, until the Day of Judgment, as this day of yours, this month of yours, and this city of yours are sacred."

(Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood)

While delivering his sermon, the Prophet ﷺ asked the listeners: "You will be asked about me in the presence of Allah Almighty — what will you say?" All the listeners replied: "We will tell Allah that you delivered His message and fulfilled the duty of prophethood." Hearing this, the Prophet ﷺ raised his finger toward the sky and said three times: "O Allah! Be my witness." (Abu Dawood, Vol. 1, p. 263)

At that very moment, while the Prophet ﷺ was delivering the sermon and fulfilling his duty of prophethood, this verse was revealed:

> "This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as your religion." (Al-Maida: 3)

### The Throne of the Sovereign of the Two Worlds

This astonishing and instructive incident is also worth remembering: at the time when the Sovereign of the Two Worlds, in his capacity as the honorable deputy of Allah Almighty and the greatest Caliph, was proclaiming the divine command, his royal throne — the saddle of his she-camel and his sweat-absorbing cloth — was perhaps not worth more than ten rupees. There was no splendid saddle on that she-camel, no haudaj, no mahmil, no canopy, no crown.

Has any other king in the history of the world ever presented such an example of simplicity? The answer is only this and only this: "No."

*This is the ascetic kingship*
*Which is the distinctive mark of none but the Sovereign of the Two Worlds ﷺ.*

After the sermon, the Prophet ﷺ offered Zuhr and Asr prayers with one Adhan and two Iqamahs. He then went to the "Mauqif" (the standing place) and remained engaged in supplications beneath the Mount of Mercy until sunset. After sunset, amid the throng of over 100,000 pilgrims, he proceeded from Arafat to Muzdalifa. There he offered Maghrib and then Isha with one Adhan and two Iqamahs. He spent the entire night near the Sacred Monument (Mush'ar al-Haram), supplicating for his Ummah. Before sunrise, he left Muzdalifa for Mina, traveling through the valley of Muhassir.

At Mina, the Prophet ﷺ came to the Jamarah (stoning pillar) and threw pebbles. He then proclaimed in a loud voice:

"Learn the rites of Hajj! I do not know if I will perform Hajj again after this."

(Muslim, Vol. 1, p. 419)

At Mina, the Prophet ﷺ also delivered a lengthy sermon in which, like the sermon at Arafat, he announced many rulings and commandments. He then proceeded to the place of sacrifice. The Prophet ﷺ had 100 camels for sacrifice. He slaughtered some with his own blessed hands and entrusted the rest to Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). He instructed that the meat, skin, saddle cloths, and halters all be given in charity, and said: "The butcher's wages should not be paid from it, but should be given separately."

### The Blessed Hair

After the sacrifice, the Prophet ﷺ had his head shaved by Sayyidina Muammar bin Abdullah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). He gave a portion of his blessed hair to Sayyidina Abu Talha Ansari (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) and ordered the remaining blessed hair to be distributed among the Muslims. (Muslim, Vol. 1, p. 421)

After this, the Prophet ﷺ went to Makka and performed Tawaf-e-Ziyarat.

### At the Zamzam Well

He then came to the Zamzam well. The members of the Abdul Muttalib clan were serving Zamzam to the pilgrims. The Prophet ﷺ said: "If I were not afraid that seeing me do this, others would also snatch the bucket from your hands and draw water themselves, I would draw water with my own hands and drink." Sayyidina Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) presented Zamzam Sharif to the Prophet ﷺ, and he drank Zamzam Sharif while standing, facing the Qibla.

He then returned to Mina and stayed there until the 12th of Zul Hijja, throwing pebbles at the Jamarat every day after sunset. On Tuesday, the 13th of Zul Hijja, after sunset, he departed from Mina, spent the night at "Muhassib", offered Fajr prayer in the morning at the Kaaba's mosque, performed Tawaf-e-Wida, and set out for Madina Munawwara along with the Ansar and Muhajirun.

### The Sermon of Ghadir Khum

At a place on the route called "Ghadir Khum", where there was a pond, the Prophet ﷺ gathered all his companions and delivered a brief sermon, the translation of which is as follows:

"After praise and thanks: O people! I too am a human being. It is possible that the angel of Allah Almighty (the Angel of Death) may soon come and I will have to accept his call. I leave behind among you two weighty things: one is the Book of Allah Almighty, which contains guidance and light, and the second is my Ahl al-Bayt (family). I remind you of Allah Almighty regarding my Ahl al-Bayt." (Muslim, Vol. 1, p. 279)

In this sermon, the Prophet ﷺ also said: "Whoever I am his master (Maula), Ali is also his master. O Allah! Love whoever loves Ali, and be hostile to whoever is hostile to Ali."

(Mishkat, p. 566, Manaqib-e-Ali)

There is no clear explanation in the Hadith as to why it was necessary to mention the virtues and excellences of Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) in the sermon of Ghadir Khum. However, a narration in Bukhari indicates that Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) had done something on his own initiative during his return from Yemen that was disapproved of by some of his companions from Yemen, to the extent that one of them complained to the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ replied: "Ali has the right to even more than this." It is possible that to dispel such doubts and misgivings from the hearts of the Yemeni Muslims, the Prophet ﷺ mentioned the virtues of Sayyidina Ali and the Ahl al-Bayt (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) on this occasion.

(Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 223; Tirmidhi, Manaqib-e-Ali)

### A Misunderstanding of the Rawafid

Some Shia have written on this occasion that the sermon of "Ghadir Khum" was a declaration of Sayyidina Ali's (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) immediate caliphate (Khilafat bila Fasl). However, it is clear to any discerning person that this is nothing but a fabrication. If the intent had truly been to declare immediate caliphate for Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), the sermons at Arafat or Mina — where over 100,000 Muslims were gathered — would have been far more appropriate, rather than at Ghadir Khum, where no one was present except the people of Yemen and Madina.

Approaching Madina, the Prophet ﷺ spent the night at the place of Zul Haleefa and entered Madina Munawwara in the morning with great honor and dignity.
