## Chapter 17: The Prophet's Noble Features and Attributes (Continued)

### His Attire

The Prophet ﷺ mostly wore cotton clothes. Occasionally he also wore wool and linen. He did not restrict himself to any particular type of clothing. He wore jubba, qaba, shirt, izaar, hulla, chadar, turban, cap, and socks. He liked the paijama (trousers) and even bought one from the market of Mina, but it is not confirmed that he ever actually wore it.

### The Turban

The Prophet ﷺ would let the tail of his turban hang down, sometimes over one shoulder and sometimes between both shoulders. His turban was white, green, saffron-colored, and black. On the day of the Conquest of Makka, he wore a black turban. (Shamayil Tirmizi, p. 9)

He always wore a cap under his turban and said: "The difference between our turbans and those of the polytheists is that we tie our turbans over caps." (Abu Dawood, Bab al-Imama, p. 209)

### The Cloak

The Prophet ﷺ greatly liked cotton cloaks made in Yemen, called "Hibarah" or "Burde Yamani", and wore them frequently. Occasionally he also wore a green cloak. (Abu Dawood, Vol. 2, p. 207)

### The Woolen Blanket

The Prophet ﷺ frequently used a woolen blanket, to the extent that he was wrapped in one at the time of his departure. Sayyidina Abu Bardah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates that Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) brought out a thick blanket and a thick cloth for the lower garment and said: "The Prophet ﷺ passed away in these two garments." (Tirmizi, Vol. 1, p. 206)

### His Blessed Sandals

The shape and design of the Prophet's ﷺ sandals was exactly like the chappals of India. They had a leather sole with straps attached. The sandals generally had two straps made of cured leather. (Shamayil Tirmizi, p. 7)

### Preferred Colors

The Prophet ﷺ wore white, black, green, and saffron-colored clothes. However, white was his most beloved and preferred color. He greatly disliked red clothes. Once, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) came to the sacred court wearing red clothes. The Prophet ﷺ expressed displeasure and asked: "What kind of garment is this?" So Abdullah burned them. The Prophet ﷺ, upon hearing this, said: "There was no need to burn them; you should have given them to some woman, as there is no harm for women to wear red clothes." Similarly, the Prophet ﷺ once passed by a man wearing two red garments. The man greeted the Prophet, but he did not respond to his greeting. (Abu Dawood, Vol. 2, pp. 207-208)

### The Ring

When the Prophet ﷺ decided to send letters of invitation to Islam to the kings, the Companions said that kings do not accept letters without a seal. So the Prophet ﷺ had a silver ring made, on which was engraved "Muhammadur Rasoolullah" in three lines. (Shamayil Tirmizi, p. 7)

### Fragrance

The Prophet ﷺ loved fragrance very much and always used perfume, although his blessed body itself emitted such fragrance that the street through which he passed would become perfumed. He said: "Men's perfume should be such that its scent spreads but its color is not visible, and women's perfume should be such that its color is visible but its scent does not spread." Whenever someone sent him a gift of fragrance, he never refused, saying: "Do not refuse a gift of fragrance, for it is from Paradise." (Shamayil Tirmizi, p. 15)

### Surma (Kohl)

The Prophet ﷺ would apply "Ismid" kohl every night. He had a kohl container from which he would apply three strokes to each eye, saying: "Apply Ismid kohl, for it brightens and sharpens the eyesight and promotes eyelash growth." (Shamayil Tirmizi, p. 5)

### Riding

The Prophet ﷺ loved riding horses immensely. Besides horses, he also rode camels, mules, and donkeys (the Arab donkey, which is more beautiful than a horse). (Sahihain, etc.)

### Refinement and Cleanliness

The Prophet's ﷺ temperament was extremely refined and he loved cleanliness. Seeing a man in dirty clothes, he expressed displeasure, saying: "Cannot this person wash his clothes?" Seeing another man with disheveled hair, he said: "Does this person not have anything (oil or comb) to tidy his hair?" (Abu Dawood, Vol. 2, p. 207)

Similarly, a man came to the Prophet ﷺ in very poor clothing. The Prophet asked him: "Do you possess any wealth?" He replied: "Yes, I have camels, goats, horses, and slaves." The Prophet ﷺ said: "When Allah has given you wealth, some sign of His blessings should be seen upon you (i.e., wear good and clean clothes)." (Abu Dawood, Vol. 2, p. 207)

### Preferred Foods

Since the holy life of the Prophet ﷺ was a complete model of asceticism, patience, and contentment, he never desired rich or elaborate foods, to the extent that he never even ate a chapati (flatbread). Yet some foods he liked and ate with relish. For example, "Hais" — an Arabian dish made of ghee, cheese, and dates cooked together — which he greatly enjoyed.

He frequently ate barley bread, which was coarse. Among condiments, he liked meat, vinegar, honey, olive oil, and especially gourd (pumpkin). When meat contained gourd, he would search for gourd pieces in the dish and eat them.

He ate the meat of goat, sheep, camel, zebra, rabbit, chicken, quail, and fish. He also frequently consumed dates and Sattu (ground barley). He would eat watermelon with dates, cucumber with dates, and sometimes dates with bread. He also ate grapes, pomegranates, and other fruits.

Cold water was much loved by him. Sometimes he mixed water with milk, and sometimes drank pure milk. Occasionally he would soak raisins and dates in water and drink the syrup. Whatever he drank, he did so in three breaths. He never ate food at a table; he always ate from a cloth or leather spread placed on the floor. He never ate while reclining against a cushion or pillow, nor did he approve of this practice. He ate only with his fingers, never using a spoon or fork. However, he would sometimes cut boiled meat with a knife before eating. (Shamayil Tirmizi)

### Daily Routine

Study of the noble hadith reveals that the Prophet ﷺ divided his day and night into three parts: one for the worship of Allah Almighty, one for the general creation, and one for himself.

His general practice was that after Fajr prayer, he would sit on his prayer mat until the sun had risen high. This was the special time for meeting people. People would come to his noble presence with their needs and requirements. He would fulfill their needs and teach them the rulings and commandments of Islam. He would also interpret the dreams of people (both his own and theirs). After this, he would engage in various conversations. Sometimes people would recall the stories and customs of the Jahiliyya and laugh, and the Prophet ﷺ would also smile. Sometimes the noble Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) would recite poetry to him. (Mishkat, Vol. 2, p. 406; Abu Dawood, Vol. 2, p. 318)

During this time he would also distribute spoils of war and stipends. When the sun had risen high, he would sometimes offer four and sometimes eight rak'ats of Chasht (Ishraq) prayer. Then he would go to the apartments of his pure wives and attend to household needs, helping his wives with domestic work. (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 93)

After Asr prayer, the Prophet ﷺ would honor all his pure wives with a visit, spending a little time conversing with each. After Isha, he would go to the mosque and then return to rest. He did not like conversation after Isha.

### Sleep and Wakefulness

After Isha prayer, he would generally sleep. Before sleeping, he would recite certain surahs of the Quran and some supplications. He would then lie on his right side, reciting: "O Allah! In Your name I die and live."

Upon waking, he would recite: "All praise is for Allah Who has given us life after death, and to Him is the resurrection."

He would rise from bed at midnight or in the latter part of the night, use a miswak, perform wudu, and engage in worship. He would recite the Quran, perform various supplications, especially Tahajjud prayer. In Tahajjud, he would sometimes recite long surahs and sometimes short ones. In his later years, he would offer some rak'ats while sitting. After Tahajjud, he would pray Witr, and after the break of true dawn, he would offer the Sunnah of Fajr and go to the mosque for Fajr prayer. Sometimes he would sleep and wake several times during the night, reciting verses of the Quran, and sometimes conversing with his pure wives. (Sihah Sitta, etc.)

### His Gait

The Prophet ﷺ walked with great dignity. Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "When walking, the Prophet ﷺ would walk with a slight forward lean, as if descending from a height." Sayyidina Abu Huraira (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "He walked so swiftly that it seemed the earth was being folded beneath his feet. We would become breathless and struggle to keep pace with him, but the Prophet ﷺ would continue walking swiftly without any difficulty." (Shamayil Tirmizi, p. 9)

### His Speech

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) said: "The Prophet ﷺ did not speak rapidly or hurriedly. Rather, he spoke with great dignity and composure, pausing between words. His speech was so clear and distinct that listeners could understand and memorize it. If something was important, he would repeat it three times so that the listeners could commit it to memory." The Prophet ﷺ was blessed with the miracle of "Jawamiul Kalam" (comprehensive speech) — he could express vast meanings in concise phrases. Sayyidina Hind bin Abu Haala (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "The Prophet ﷺ did not speak unnecessarily; rather, he remained silent most of the time." (Shamayil Tirmizi, p. 15)

### The Court of Prophethood

The court of the Sovereign of Both Worlds ﷺ was not like the courts of kings and emperors. It was entirely free from the formalities of thrones and crowns, chamberlains and gatekeepers, guards and bodyguards. In the courtyard of Masjid-e-Nabawi, the noble Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) had built a small earthen platform — this was the royal throne of the Sovereign of Prophethood ﷺ. A mat would be spread upon it, and the Sovereign of Both Worlds and Emperor of the Universe ﷺ would grace it. Despite this simplicity, everyone in that court appeared awestruck by the majesty of prophethood. In Sahih Bukhari and other narrations, it is stated that when people sat in the Prophet's court, it seemed as though birds were perched on their heads — no one would make the slightest movement. (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 398)

In this court, the Prophet ﷺ would first attend to those in need, hear everyone's requests, and fulfill their needs. He would meet with delegates of tribes. All those present would sit with their heads bowed in perfect manners. When the Prophet ﷺ spoke, silence would descend upon the assembly, and everyone would listen attentively to the prophetic words of the Sovereign of the Universe. (Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 380)

There was no restriction on who could enter the Prophet's court. Rich and poor, city-dweller and Bedouin — all kinds of people would attend. They would ask and answer in their own dialects. No one, however poor or humble, could be interrupted when speaking, even by a great and wealthy person.

Those who exceeded proper bounds in questioning were tolerated by the Prophet ﷺ with perfect forbearance. He would continue teaching and advising about Islamic rulings and commandments, and would consult his special Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum). He would issue important commands regarding peace, war, and the organization of the Ummah. In this court, he would also judge cases.

### The Sermons of the Sovereign of Both Worlds

Since a prophet is a caller to the faith and the preacher of the Shariah, and sermons and oratory are the best means of religious instruction, every prophet must necessarily be a preacher and orator. This is why, when Allah Almighty honored Hazrat Moosa (Alaihissalam) with prophethood and sent him to Firaun, Moosa prayed: "O my Lord! Expand my chest for me, make my task easy for me, and remove the knot from my tongue so that they may understand my speech."

Since the Chief of All Prophets ﷺ is the leader of all messengers and the Seal of the Prophets, the Holy Allah granted him such matchless excellence in oratory and speech that he became the most eloquent of all Arabs. He was blessed with the miracle of Jawamiul Kalam — in every single word that issued from his blessed tongue, an ocean of meanings and subjects could be seen surging. The impact of his eloquence would bring about great revolutions in the hearts of listeners.

Besides the sermons of Friday and the two Eids, the Prophet ﷺ delivered hundreds of such eloquent and effective sermons and impactful exhortations on various occasions that the masters of Arab eloquence were left astonished. By the effects and impacts of these sermons, the hearts of even the most hard-hearted melted like wax, and the world of their hearts changed in an instant.

Since the Prophet ﷺ combined many roles, these different roles influenced his style of delivery in sermons. He was a caller to faith, a conqueror, a commander of armies, a reformer of the people, and a sovereign. Therefore, based on these roles, his sermons displayed various kinds of emphasis and different styles of passionate expression. Such was the passion of his delivery that often during sermons, his eyes would become red, his voice would rise very high, and the signs of divine majesty would appear on his blessed face. He would repeatedly raise his finger, making gestures, as if he were challenging an army. (Muslim, Vol. 1, p. 284)

Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), painting a vivid picture of his passionate sermons, says: "I heard the Prophet ﷺ delivering a sermon on the pulpit. He was saying: 'The Omnipotent Lord will take the heavens and the earth in His hand, then He will say: I am the Omnipotent! I am the King! Where are the tyrants? Where are the arrogant?' While saying this, the Prophet ﷺ would clench and open his fist, and his blessed body would sway right and left in his passion, to such an extent that I saw the lower part of the pulpit shaking so much that I thought (in my heart): 'Will this pulpit fall with the Prophet on it?'" (Ibn Majah, p. 326)

The Prophet ﷺ delivered sermons on the pulpit, on the ground, and standing on the back of a camel, as the occasion demanded. Sometimes his sermons were lengthy, but generally they were concise yet comprehensive.

On the battlefield, the Prophet ﷺ would deliver sermons leaning on a bow. During Friday sermons in mosques, he would hold a staff in his blessed hand. (Ibn Majah, p. 79)

The impact of his sermons was such that at times, in the most intensely heated situations, his few words would unleash a river of love. Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) relates: "One day the Prophet ﷺ delivered such an impactful and soul-stirring sermon that I had never heard the like before. In the midst of the sermon, he said: 'O people! If you knew what I know, you would laugh little and weep much.' The moment this sentence left his blessed tongue, the listeners became such that people hid their faces in their garments and began sobbing profusely." (Bukhari, Vol. 2, p. 665)

### The Worship of the Chief of the Universe

Despite countless engagements, the Prophet ﷺ was such a great worshipper that it would be difficult to find a parallel even in the holy lives of all the prophets and messengers. Indeed, the truth is that regarding the earlier prophets, it cannot even be known with certainty what their method of worship was and which times were dedicated for worship. Among all the communities of the prophets, only the Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) of the Seal of the Prophets ﷺ have the honor and distinction that they have preserved every single detail of their beloved Prophet's worship — its methods, times, and states.

The worship performed inside homes and in the darkness of the night was observed and remembered by the pure wives (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhunn), who then conveyed it to the entire Ummah. The worship performed outside the home was meticulously observed and memorized by the noble Companions (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum). They preserved in the treasuries of their memory his standing and sitting, bowing and prostrating, their quantities and modalities, his remembrances and supplications with their exact words, and even the states of his humility and concentration. They then disseminated these details among the Ummah to such an extent that not only were they preserved in the pages of books, but every individual of the Ummah — even the veiled women — became aware of them. Today, every Muslim child, wherever he may be in any corner of the earth, knows the complete details of his Prophet's worship and follows them with faith and passion in emulation of his Prophet. A brief outline of his worship is as follows:

### Prayer

Even before the proclamation of prophethood, the Prophet ﷺ was engaged in the worship of Allah Almighty in the Cave of Hira through standing, meditation, remembrance, and reflection. After the revelation of the Quran, the method of prayer was taught to him. Then, on the Night of Ascension (Me'raj), the five daily prayers were made obligatory. Besides the five daily prayers, the Prophet ﷺ also offered Ishraq, Chasht, Tahiyyatul Wudu, Tahiyyatul Masjid, Salatul Awwabin, and other Sunnah and Nafl prayers. He would rise during the night to pray. He remained consistent in Tahajjud prayer throughout his life.

There are various narrations about his nightly nafl prayers. Some narrations indicate that after Isha he would sleep a little, then wake up and pray for a while, then sleep again, then wake up and pray again. He would continue in this state until morning. Sometimes he would wake after two-thirds of the night had passed and remain engaged in prayer until the break of true dawn. Sometimes he would rise after midnight and not lie down again for the rest of the night. He would recite long surahs in prayer, sometimes making the bowing and prostration long, sometimes the standing long. Sometimes he would pray six rak'ats, sometimes eight, sometimes less, sometimes more. In his later years, he would offer some rak'ats standing and some sitting. He would pray Witr along with Tahajjud.

During Ramadan, especially in the last ten days, his worship would increase greatly. He would stay awake all night and remain apart from his pure wives. He would wake his family members for prayer. He would generally observe I'tikaf (seclusion in the mosque). Along with prayers, he would also supplicate with intense humility, weeping and sobbing while standing, sitting, and prostrating during the nights. During Ramadan, he would also recite the entire Quran with Sayyidina Jibreel (peace be upon him). Along with the recitation of the Quran, he would also engage in various supplications. Sometimes he would remain standing all night in prayer and supplication, until his blessed feet would swell. (Sihah Sitta and other books of Hadith)

### Fasting

Besides the fasts of Ramadan, the Prophet ﷺ would also fast nearly the entire month of Shaban. Similar was his practice in the remaining months of the year — if he began fasting, it seemed he would never stop; then if he stopped, it seemed he would never fast again. In particular, he would fast three days each month (the Ayyam-e-Beed, i.e., 13th, 14th, 15th), on Mondays and Thursdays, on Ashura (10th of Muharram), during the first ten days of Zul Hijja, and six days of Shawwal. Occasionally, he would practice "Saum-e-Wisal" (continuous fasting, day and night, for several days), but he forbade his Ummah from doing so. Some Companions said: "O Messenger of Allah! You practice Wisal." He replied: "Who among you is like me? I spend the night with my Lord, and He feeds me and gives me to drink." (Bukhari and Muslim, Saum-e-Wisal)

### Zakat

Since Allah Almighty did not make Zakat obligatory upon the prophets, the Prophet ﷺ was not obligated to pay Zakat. (Zurqani, Vol. 8, p. 90) However, such was his state of charity and almsgiving that he kept nothing for himself — neither gold nor silver, nor any merchandise, nor any herds of cattle. Whatever came to him, he would distribute it all in the path of Allah to those deserving. He could not bear that any wealth should remain overnight in the house of prophethood. Once, so much revenue came that despite distributing it all day, it could not be finished by evening, so he spent the night in the mosque. When Sayyidina Bilal (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) came and informed him that all the money had been distributed, only then did he step into his home. (Abu Dawood)

### Hajj

After the proclamation of prophethood, the Prophet ﷺ performed two or three Hajj while in Makka Mukarrama. (Tirmizi) After the Hijra, in 10 AH, he performed one Hajj, known as the Farewell Pilgrimage (Hajjatul Wida), a detailed account of which has already been given. Besides Hajj, after the Hijra, he also performed four Umrah. (Tirmizi and Bukhari)

### Remembrance of Allah

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "The Prophet ﷺ was engaged in the remembrance of Allah at all times, every moment." (Abu Dawood)

Whether rising or sitting, walking or traveling, eating or drinking, sleeping or waking, performing wudu, wearing new clothes, mounting or dismounting, going on a journey or returning, entering or leaving the lavatory, entering or leaving the mosque, during battle, in wind, rain, or thunder — at every time and in every state, supplications were on his tongue. At times of joy and grief, at the break of dawn, at sunset, upon hearing the rooster's crow, upon hearing the donkey's bray — indeed, there was no occasion for which he did not have a supplication. Not only during the day, but also in the silence of the night, he remained constantly engaged in supplication and remembrance of Allah. Even at the time of his departure, the phrase that repeatedly remained on his blessed tongue was the supplication: "O Allah! The Highest Companion (Ar-Rafeeq al-A'la)." (Sihah Sitta, Hasan Husain, and other books of Hadith)
