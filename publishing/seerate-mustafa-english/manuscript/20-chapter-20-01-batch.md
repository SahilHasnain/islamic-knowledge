## Chapter 20: Miracles of Prophethood (Mo'jizate Nubuwwat)

### What is a Miracle?

When something astonishing appears from the noble prophets (Alaihimussalatu Wassalam) to demonstrate the truth of their prophethood — something that does not happen ordinarily — this occurrence, which is contrary to the normal course of nature, is called a "miracle" (Mo'jiza).

Since a miracle is a divine sign to manifest the truth of a prophet, it is essential that the miracle be extraordinary — that is, entirely contrary to apparent causes and means, and to the normal ongoing patterns. Otherwise, it is obvious that the disbelievers could say: "This happened because of such-and-such a cause, and such things always happen naturally." Therefore, this is a necessary condition for a miracle — indeed, it is part of the very concept of a miracle — that it be, in some respect, contrary to normal means and ongoing patterns, and completely above the intervention of apparent causes and means. This is so that when the disbelievers see it, they are compelled to admit: "Since this thing has no apparent cause and has never happened ordinarily before, its appearance from a person is undoubtedly a feat beyond human power. Therefore, this person is surely sent by Allah and is His prophet."

### Four Types of Miracles

Since it is a necessary and essential condition for a miracle to be, in some respect, beyond human power and contrary to ongoing patterns, upon careful consideration, from the perspective of being extraordinary, miracles can be divided into four categories:

**First:** The thing itself is completely contrary to apparent causes and patterns — for example, the Holy Prophet ﷺ splitting the moon into two pieces; Sayyidina Moosa's (Alaihissalam) staff turning into a serpent and swallowing the serpents of the magicians; Sayyidina Eisa (Alaihissalam) bringing the dead back to life, etc.

**Second:** The thing itself is not extraordinary, but its appearance from the prophet at a particular time, completely unexpectedly, becomes extraordinary in this respect, and therefore it is also called a miracle. For example, in the Battle of the Trench, the sudden onset of a terrifying windstorm that uprooted the tents of the disbelievers, overturned heavy cooking pots from their stoves, and flung them far away. Or in the Battle of Badr, an army of 313 Muslims defeating a fully armed force of 1000 formidable disbelievers, who were killed or captured. Obviously, a windstorm arriving or an army being defeated is not in itself extraordinary — such things happen all the time. But on this particular occasion when the Prophet felt a special need for divine support, the windstorm's sudden appearance without any apparent cause, and the disbelievers' defeat despite their numerical superiority at the hands of a small Muslim force — this can be attributed to nothing other than divine support and unseen assistance. In this respect, it is undoubtedly contrary to ongoing patterns and above apparent causes and means, and therefore certainly a miracle.

**Third:** There is also a situation where neither the event itself is contrary to nature, nor does anything extraordinary occur at the time of its occurrence. However, the manner in which the event manifests is extremely rare and extraordinary. For example, through the supplications of the prophets, rain suddenly falls, the sick are healed, and calamities are averted. Obviously, these things are not contrary to nature, nor is there any specific time for their occurrence — such things happen all the time. However, the ways and means by which they occurred — the prophet suddenly made a supplication and these things appeared immediately — in this respect, all these things are undoubtedly extraordinary, separate from apparent causes, and above them. Therefore, these things will also be called miracles.

**Fourth:** Sometimes it happens that neither the event itself is contrary to ongoing patterns, nor is its manner of manifestation extraordinary. However, the prophet's acquisition of knowledge of the unseen about the event before it occurs, without any apparent cause, and the prophet informing about the event before its occurrence — this is extraordinary. For example, the reports of the unseen that the prophets gave long before events occurred — all these events are extraordinary and miraculous in this respect.

As narrated in Sahih Muslim: one day a very powerful windstorm arose. At that time, the Holy Prophet was outside Madina. He said then and there: "This windstorm has arisen for the death of a hypocrite of Madina." And so it happened — when people reached Madina, they learned that a hypocrite had been killed by that windstorm. (Mishkat Shareef, Vol. 2, p. 537)

Reflect: in this incident, neither the windstorm blowing nor a person being killed by it is contrary to nature — windstorms occur all the time and people die in them. However, the Holy Prophet's knowledge of this event in advance, and his informing people of this unseen news beforehand — this is undoubtedly among the extraordinary and miraculous.

### Miracles of Previous Prophets and the Seal of the Prophets

Since every prophet's miracle serves as proof of his prophethood, Allah Almighty honored each prophet with miracles appropriate to the environment of his era and the intellectual capacity and understanding of his people.

For example, in the time of Sayyidina Moosa (Alaihissalam), magic and sorcery had reached their highest peak of development. Therefore, Allah granted him the miracles of the "White Hand" (Yad-e-Baiza) and the "Staff" (Asa), through which he dominated the magical feats of the sorcerers so completely that all the magicians fell into prostration and believed in his prophethood.

Similarly, in the time of Sayyidina Eisa (Alaihissalam), the science of medicine had reached its zenith. The physicians and doctors of that era had stunned everyone with their skill in treating major diseases. Therefore, Allah granted Sayyidina Eisa (Alaihissalam) the miracle of healing the congenitally blind and lepers, and reviving the dead. Seeing this, the physicians and doctors of the Christian era were dumbfounded and stood bewildered. Ultimately, acknowledging these miracles as beyond human capability, they affirmed his prophethood.

Similarly, in the time of Sayyidina Saaleh (Alaihissalam), the arts of stone carving and sculpture were widely renowned. Therefore, the Holy and Pure Allah granted him this miracle: he pointed toward a mountain, and a rock split open, from which emerged a very beautiful and healthy she-camel with her calf. He said: "This is Allah's she-camel, a sign for you." Seeing this miracle, the people of Saaleh believed.

In short, each prophet was granted miracles according to the environment of his era, the temperament of his people, and the inclination of their natures — one received one miracle, another two, and yet another more. However, our Holy Prophet, the Prophet of the End Times ﷺ, since he is the prophet of all prophets and his sacred character is the summary of the holy lives of all prophets, and his teachings are the essence of the teachings of all noble prophets, and he came into the world with a universal and eternal religion, and all peoples and nations of the first and last in the universe were the addressees of his holy call — therefore, Allah made his holy personality a collection of all the miracles of the previous prophets and blessed him with countless miracles of various kinds that were necessary for every class, group, nation, and all people of different intellectual capacities and understandings.

This is why his character and appearance, his Sunnah and Shariah, his manners and habits, his daily and nightly practices — in short, every single gesture and every single thing about his person and attributes contains a world of miracles in its fold. The Book revealed to him is his greatest and eternal miracle, remaining till the Day of Judgment — every verse of which is a book of clear signs, and every line of which is a register of miracles.

His miracles manifested in the higher and lower realms of creation in such a way that from the earth to the Throne, the drum of the grandeur of his miracles beats. On the face of the earth, in all the realms of minerals, plants, and animals, the flag of his all-encompassing authority and sovereignty of various kinds of miracles waved in such a manner that even great deniers had to bow their heads before his truthfulness and prophethood. Except for the obstinate, every human being — regardless of which nation or religion he belongs to and however high the levels of his intellectual temperament and mindset may be — seeing the abundance, variety, and grandeur of his miracles, must believe that undoubtedly he is the true prophet and the real messenger of Allah.

If one looks at his God-given physical and spiritual powers, one finds that the mind-boggling feats of the various periods of his holy life are themselves the greatest of miracles. Sometimes wrestling with the unconquerable wrestlers of Arabia and throwing them down; sometimes, in the blink of an eye, traveling from the carpet of the earth to Sidratul Muntaha and beyond, touring the Exalted Throne; sometimes splitting the moon into two pieces at the gesture of his fingers; sometimes making the setting sun return; sometimes striking a pickaxe on a rock in the trench (Khandaq) and showing his Ummat waving the flags of Islam over the kingdoms of Rome and Persia; sometimes causing springs of water to flow from his fingers; sometimes feeding a hungry army with a handful of dates so that every soldier ate to his fill — and so on and so forth. These are undoubtedly such miraculous events that no sane person in the world can remain unaffected by them.

### A Few of the Numerous Miracles

It is extremely difficult to count the miracles of the Holy Prophet ﷺ in thousands. Because, as we have written, his holy personality is the sum total of the miracles of all previous prophets. Besides these, Allah Almighty has granted him countless other miracles that were not given to any prophet or messenger. Therefore, it is a truth more brilliant than the sun that every moment of his holy life is, in reality, a world of miracles and a vast universe of extraordinary events.

It is obvious that when the authors of great and voluminous books could not collect all the miracles of the Holy Prophet in their books, how can the narrow confines of our brief book accommodate numerous miracles? However, the famous principle is: "What cannot be fully obtained should not be entirely abandoned." Therefore, I deemed it appropriate to mention some miracles in this brief book as well, so that the pages of this book may not remain completely deprived of the colorful flowers of the miracles of prophethood.

Since we have stated that the miracles of our Holy Prophet, the Prophet of the End Times, are not limited to the lower realm but the authority of his miracles prevails in both the lower and higher realms, we present a few examples of various types of miracles under different headings.

### Heavenly Miracles

**The Splitting of the Moon (Shaqqul Qamar):**

Among the miracles of the Seal of the Prophets ﷺ, the miracle of "Shaqqul Qamar" (the splitting of the moon) is a very significant and decisive miracle. It is mentioned in hadith that the disbelievers of Makka demanded that he show them a miracle and a sign as proof of his prophethood. At that time, the Prophet showed them the miracle of "Shaqqul Qamar" — the moon appeared split into two pieces.

Sayyidina Abdullah bin Mas'ood, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas, Sayyidina Anas bin Maalik, Sayyidina Jubair bin Mut'im, Sayyidina Ali bin Abi Talib, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umar, Sayyidina Huzaifa bin Yamaan (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum), and others have narrated this incident. (Zurqani Alal Mawahib, Vol. 5, p. 124)

Among these narrations, the most authentic and reliable is that of Sayyidina Abdullah bin Mas'ood (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), which is recorded in Bukhari, Muslim, Tirmizi, etc. Sayyidina Abdullah bin Mas'ood was present on that occasion and witnessed this miracle with his own eyes. He says: "During the time of the Holy Prophet, the moon split into two pieces. One piece appeared above the mountain and the other below the mountain. The Prophet showed this scene to the disbelievers and said, 'Bear witness, bear witness!'" (Bukhari, Vol. 2, pp. 721-722)

Besides these blessed hadith, mention of this great miracle is also found in the Holy Quran. The divine command is:

> اقْتَرَبَتِ السَّاعَةُ وَانشَقَّ الْقَمَرُ (1) وَإِن يَرَوْا آيَةً يُعْرِضُوا وَيَقُولُوا سِحْرٌ مُّسْتَمِرٌّ (2)

**Translation:** "The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split. And if they see a sign, they turn away and say, 'This is continuous magic.'" (Al-Qamar: 1-2)

The clear and explicit meaning of this verse is that the Hour has drawn near and only a small portion of the world's life remains, because the splitting of the moon, which was among the signs of the Hour, occurred during the time of the Holy Prophet. But even after seeing this clearest and most decisive miracle, the disbelievers of Makka did not become Muslim; rather, the oppressors said: "Muhammad has cast a spell on us, and such things of magic have always been happening."

**Removal of a Misconception:**

Some atheists who deny the miracle of Shaqqul Qamar believe that this "splitting of the moon" refers purely to the moon splitting on the Day of Judgment when the sky will split and the moon and stars will fall and scatter. However, it is clear to people of understanding that this nonsense of the atheists is utterly baseless and completely absurd. First, in this case, without any contextual indicator, the past tense verb "انشق" (split) would have to be interpreted in the future tense as "ينشق" (will split), which is completely unnecessary. Second, after mentioning the moon splitting, it is said:

> وَإِن يَرَوْا آيَةً يُعْرِضُوا وَيَقُولُوا سِحْرٌ مُّسْتَمِرٌّ

**Translation:** That is, upon seeing the great sign of Shaqqul Qamar, the disbelievers said: "This is magic that has always been happening."

Obviously, when the disbelievers of Makka saw the miracle of Shaqqul Qamar, they called it magic. Otherwise, it is an open fact that on the Day of Judgment, when the sky splits and the moon and stars break into pieces and fall, and all people die, who will be there to call it magic? Therefore, undoubtedly, these are the only meanings of this verse: the moon split during the Holy Prophet's time, and seeing this miracle, the disbelievers called it a feat of magic.

**A Question and Its Answer:**

Yes, a question arises here that people often ask: if the miracle of Shaqqul Qamar occurred in Makka, why was it not seen in other lands and cities?

The answer to this question is: First, besides Makka, people of other cities also saw this miracle, as established by hadith. Sayyidina Masrooq narrates from Sayyidina Abdullah bin Mas'ood (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) that after seeing this miracle, the disbelievers of Makka said: "The son of Abu Kabsha (Muhammad) has cast a spell on you." Then they decided among themselves to ask the travelers arriving from outside: "Let us see what they say about this, for Muhammad's magic cannot affect all people." Consequently, the travelers coming from outside also testified: "We have seen the moon split." (Shifa Qaazi Ayaaz, Vol. 1, p. 183)

And even if it is accepted that the inhabitants of other lands and cities did not see this miracle, the fact that something is not seen by some does not mean it did not happen. Various phenomena appear in the sky daily — for example, clouds of different colors, rainbows, shooting stars — but these are seen only by those who happen to be looking at the sky at that time; others do not see them.

Similarly, another reason why this miracle may not have been seen in other lands and cities is the difference in the rising times of the moon. Due to the difference in longitudes, in some places the moon rises at a certain time while in other cities the moon has not risen at all. This is why when a lunar eclipse occurs, it is not visible in all lands. Sometimes, in other countries and cities, clouds or mountains obstruct the view of the moon.

**The Miracle of the Sun's Return:**

Among the heavenly miracles of the Holy Prophet ﷺ, the miracle of the sun's return is also a very great miracle and a clear sign of the truth of prophethood. Its account is that Sayyida Asma bint Umais (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "Near Khaibar, at the resting place of Sahba, the Holy Prophet lay down to sleep after Asr prayer, placing his blessed head in the lap of Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), and revelation began to descend upon him. Sayyidina Ali remained sitting with the Prophet's head in his lap until the sun set, and he realized that his Asr prayer had become qada (missed). Upon this, the Prophet made this supplication: 'O Allah! Surely Ali was in obedience to You and Your Messenger. Therefore, return the sun so that Ali may perform his Asr prayer.'"

Sayyida Asma bint Umais says: "I saw with my own eyes that the sun, which had already set, returned, and sunlight spread over the mountain peaks and the earth all around." (Zurqani, Vol. 5, p. 113; Shifa, Vol. 1, p. 185; Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 252)

There is no doubt that this miracle is not mentioned in Bukhari. But remember, the absence of a hadith from Bukhari is not proof that the hadith is completely baseless. Imam Bukhari had memorized 600,000 hadith. From these, he selected and wrote 9,082 hadith in Bukhari (including repetitions and supporting narrations). If repetitions and supporting narrations are excluded, the total number of hadith is 2,861. (Muqaddama Fathul Baari)

The remaining hadith that Imam Bukhari (Alaihirrahma) had memorized — obviously they were not baseless or fabricated but were certainly authentic or at least good (Hasan). After all, where did all those hadith go? And what happened to them? One would have to say that other muhadditheen wrote those hadith, along with some others, in their respective books. Consequently, the hadith of the sun's return at Sahba for Ali's Asr prayer has been recorded by many muhadditheen in their books.

As Shaikh Abdul Haque Muhaddis Dhelwi (Alaihirrahma) stated: Imam Abu Ja'afar Tahawi, Ahmad bin Saaleh, Imam Tabarani, and Qaazi Iyaaz have written this hadith in their respective books. Imam Tahawi also wrote that Imam Ahmad bin Saaleh, who was a contemporary of Imam Ahmad bin Hambal, used to say: "This narration is among the greatest miracles and signs of prophethood. Therefore, the people of knowledge should not lag behind in remembering it nor should they be negligent."
