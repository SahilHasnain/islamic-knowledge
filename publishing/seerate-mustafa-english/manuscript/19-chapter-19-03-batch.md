## Chapter 19: The Pure Wives (Continued)

### Sayyida Zainab bint Jahsh (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) — Conclusion

When Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) passed away, people realized that "long hand" meant abundant charity, for she used to do handiwork and give its income in charity to the poor and needy.

When the news of her death reached Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), she said: "Alas! A praiseworthy woman, beneficial to all, who would delight orphans and elderly women, has departed from this world today." Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) also said: "I have not seen any woman better than Sayyida Zainab in goodness, truthfulness, and kindness to relatives."

It is narrated that Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) used to say to the other pure wives: "Allah Almighty has granted me a virtue that none of the other pure wives have received. All the pure wives were married to the Holy Prophet by their fathers and grandfathers, but my marriage with the Holy Prophet was performed by Allah Himself."

She narrated 11 hadith from the Holy Prophet ﷺ, of which 2 are recorded in both Bukhari and Muslim. The remaining 9 hadith are written in other books of hadith.

It is narrated that when the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), learned of Sayyida Zainab's death, he ordered: "Proclaim in every street and market of Madina that all people of Madina should gather for the funeral prayer of their holy mother." Sayyidina Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) himself led her funeral prayer, and she was buried in Jannatul Baqi. She passed away in 20 AH or 21 AH at the age of 53 in Madina. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, pp. 476-478, etc.)

### Sayyida Zainab bint Khuzaima (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) — "Ummul Masakeen"

In the time of ignorance (Jahiliyyah), because she used to feed the poor and needy abundantly, she was given the title "Ummul Masakeen" (Mother of the Poor). She was first married to Sayyidina Abdullah bin Jahsh (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), but when he was martyred in the Battle of Uhud, the Holy Prophet ﷺ married her in 3 AH. However, after the marriage, she lived only for 2 or 3 months and passed away at the age of 30 in Rabiul Aakhir 4 AH. She was buried in Jannatul Baqi beside the other pure wives. On her mother's side, she is the sister of Sayyida Maymuna (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 249)

### Sayyida Maymuna (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

Her father's name is Harith bin Hazan, and her mother's name is Hind bint Awf. Sayyida Maymuna's original name was "Barrah," but the Holy Prophet ﷺ changed it to "Maymuna" (Blessed One).

She was first married to Abu Ruham bin Abdul Uzza. When the Holy Prophet ﷺ went to Makka for the Umratul Qada in 7 AH, she was already a widow. Sayyidina Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) spoke to the Holy Prophet about her, and the Prophet married her. On the return from Umratul Qada, at the place called "Saraf," he honored her with his companionship.

Sayyida Maymuna (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) had 4 full sisters:

1. **Ummul Fazl Lubabatul Kubra** — the wife of the Holy Prophet's uncle, Sayyidina Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), and the mother of Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu).
2. **Lubabatus Sughra** — the mother of Sayyidina Khalid bin Al Waleed, the Sword of Allah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu).
3. **Asma** — married to Ubayy bin Khalaf. She accepted Islam and is counted among the female Companions.
4. **Izzah** — also a female Companion, who was in the household of Ziyad bin Malik.

Apart from these full sisters, Sayyida Maymuna had 4 half-sisters (from the mother's side only):

1. **Asma bint Umais** — first married to Sayyidina Jafar bin Abi Talib (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), from whom she had three sons: Abdullah, Aun, and Muhammad. After Jafar's martyrdom at the Battle of Mauta, Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique married her, and she gave birth to Muhammad bin Abu Bakr. After Abu Bakr's death, Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) married her, and she bore a son named "Yahya."

2. **Salma bint Umais** — first married to the Chief of Martyrs, Sayyidina Hamza (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), from whom she had a daughter named "Ummatullah." After Hamza's martyrdom, Sayyidina Shaddad bin Alhaad married her, and she had two sons: Abdullah and Abdurrahman.

3. **Salamah bint Umais** — married to Abdullah bin Kaab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu).

4. **Sayyida Zainab bint Khuzaima** — the Mother of the Believers known as "Ummul Masakeen," whose account has already passed.

It was commonly said about Sayyida Maymuna's mother, "Hind bint Awf," that in terms of sons-in-law, no old woman on the face of the earth was more fortunate than her. Her list of sons-in-law includes:
1. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ
2. Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique
3. Sayyidina Ali
4. Sayyidina Hamza
5. Sayyidina Abbas
6. Sayyidina Shaddad bin Alhad (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum)

All these great personalities were sons-in-law of "Hind bint Awf" (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 251; Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 484)

A total of 76 hadith are narrated from Sayyida Maymuna (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). Of these, 7 are found in both Bukhari and Muslim, one exclusively in Bukhari, one exclusively in Muslim, and the rest in other books of hadith.

She was the last wife of the Holy Prophet ﷺ — after her, he did not marry any other woman. There is disagreement among historians regarding her year of death. The famous saying is that she passed away in 51 AH at the place "Saraf," where the Holy Prophet had first honored her with his companionship. Ibn Sa'ad narrates from Waqidi that she died in 61 AH, and Ibn Ishaq says 63 AH. And Allah knows best.

At the time of her death, her nephew, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma), was present. He led her funeral prayer and lowered her into the grave. The narrator Ata says: "We were with Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas at the funeral of Sayyida Maymuna. When the bier was lifted, Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas said in a loud voice: 'O people! She is the wife of the Messenger of Allah. Carry her bier very, very gently and do not jolt her sacred body.'" Sayyidina Yazeed bin Asam (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "We buried Sayyida Maymuna at the place Saraf, in the very same hut where the Messenger of Allah had first honored her with his proximity." (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 253)

### Sayyida Juwayriyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

She is the daughter of the great chief of the Banu Mustaliq tribe, Harith bin Abu Zarar. In the Battle of Muraise'a (Banu Mustaliq), when the disbelievers were captured by the Muslims, Sayyida Juwayriyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) was among the prisoners. When the captives were distributed as slaves among the mujahideen, she fell into the share of Sayyidina Sabit bin Qais (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu). She entered into a contract of Kitabat (manumission by payment) with him — that is, she agreed to pay a certain amount of money to be freed.

Sayyida Juwayriyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) came to the court of the Messenger ﷺ and submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! I am the daughter of the great chief of my tribe, Harith bin Abu Zarar. I have accepted Islam. Sabit bin Qais has made a Kitabat contract with me, but I do not have enough money to pay for my freedom, as my entire family has been captured in this battle and all our wealth and possessions have become war booty in Muslim hands. I am in a state of complete destitution and helplessness."

The Mercy for the Worlds ﷺ was moved by her plea. He asked: "If I treat you better than this, would you accept it?" She asked: "O Messenger of Allah! What better treatment would you give me?" The Prophet said: "I wish to pay the entire amount of your Kitabat myself and then, after freeing you, marry you myself, so that your family's honor and dignity may be preserved."

Hearing this, Sayyida Juwayriyya's happiness knew no bounds. She gladly accepted this honor. The Holy Prophet paid the full Kitabat amount, freed her, included her among his pure wives, and she was honored with the title of Mother of the Believers.

When news spread through the Islamic army that the Messenger of Allah had married Sayyida Juwayriyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), all the mujahideen said with one voice: "The family into which the Holy Prophet has married — no member of that family can remain a slave." Consequently, all the slaves of that family that were in possession of the Muslim fighters were freed immediately.

This is why Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) used to say: "No woman's marriage in the world proved more blessed than the marriage of Juwayriyya, because through this marriage, the entire tribe of Banu Mustaliq was saved from slavery." (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 254)

Sayyida Juwayriyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "Three nights before the Holy Prophet came to my tribe, I saw a dream in which a moon came from the direction of Madina and fell into my lap. I did not tell anyone about this dream, but when the Messenger of Allah married me, I understood that this was the interpretation of that dream." (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 254)

Her original name was "Barrah" (righteous), but because this name expressed seniority and superiority, the Prophet changed it to Juwayriyya (little girl). She was a very devout woman, always engaged in her litanies and devotions from Fajr prayer until the Chasht (forenoon) prayer. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 479)

Sayyida Juwayriyya had two brothers — Amr bin Al Harith and Abdullah bin Harith — and one sister, Amrah bint Harith. All three also accepted Islam and were honored with the rank of Companionship.

The story of her brother Abdullah bin Harith's acceptance of Islam is very astonishing and interesting as well. He came to the prophetic court to secure the release of his tribe's prisoners, bringing some she-camels and a maidservant. He hid them in a mountain valley and appeared alone at the prophetic court, requesting the release of the war captives.

The Holy Prophet asked: "What have you brought for the ransom of the prisoners?" He said: "I have nothing." Hearing this, the Prophet said: "What about your she-camels and your maidservant whom you have hidden in such-and-such valley?" Hearing this testimony of the unseen from the prophetic tongue, Abdullah bin Harith was astounded: "How did the Holy Prophet know about my maidservant and camels?" Immediately, the light of the Holy Prophet's truthfulness and prophethood illuminated his dark heart, and he instantly accepted Islam by reciting the Kalima.

Sayyida Juwayriyya narrated 7 hadith from the Messenger of Allah. Among them, 2 are in Sahih Bukhari, 2 in Sahih Muslim, and 3 in other books. Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umar, Sayyidina Ubaid bin Sabaaq, and her nephew Sayyidina Tufail (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum) have narrated from her. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 481; Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 255)

She passed away in Madina in 50 AH at the age of 65. The governor of Madina, Marwan, led her funeral prayer, and she was buried in Jannatul Baqi. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 255; Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 481)

### Sayyida Safiyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha)

Her original name was Zainab. The Messenger of Allah changed it to Safiyya. She was the daughter of the great chief of the Jewish tribe of Banu Nazeer, Huyaiy bin Akhtab, and her mother's name was Zarah bint Samuil. She belongs to the tribe of Banu Israel, from the descendants of Sayyidina Haroon (Alaihissalam), the brother of Sayyidina Moosa (Alaihissalam). Her husband, Kinana bin Abil Huqaiq, was also the great chief of Banu Nazeer, who was killed in the Battle of Khaibar.

In Muharram 7 AH, when the Muslims conquered Khaibar and all the war captives were gathered together, Sayyidina Dahiyya bin Khaleefa Kalbi (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) came to the prophetic court and requested a maidservant. The Prophet said: "Take any maidservant you like from these captives." He chose Sayyida Safiyya. But a Companion submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! Sayyida Safiyya is the princess of Banu Quraiza and Banu Nazeer. Her family honor demands that you include her among your pure wives." Consequently, the Prophet took her from Sayyidina Dahiyya Kalbi and gave him another maidservant in exchange. Then he freed Sayyida Safiyya and married her. On the return from Khaibar, at the resting place of Sahba, he honored her with his companionship within his tent. For the Walima feast, he fed the noble Companions a dish of dates, ghee, and cheese. The detailed account of this has already passed in the Battle of Khaibar.

The Holy Prophet ﷺ showed special attention and extremely gracious favor to Sayyida Safiyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) and was so considerate of her that it would sometimes stir Sayyida Ayesha's (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) jealousy.

It is narrated that once Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) remarked about Sayyida Safiyya: "She is of short stature." The Holy Prophet ﷺ said: "O Ayesha! You have said something which, if mixed with the sea, would alter it." (i.e., this is backbiting, which is a very evil thing.)

Similarly, on one journey, Sayyida Safiyya's camel became injured, and Sayyida Zainab (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) had an extra camel. The Holy Prophet said: "O Zainab! Give your camel to Safiyya." Zainab said angrily: "I will not give my anything to this Jewess." Hearing this, the Holy Prophet was so displeased with Sayyida Zainab that he did not set foot on her bed for two or three months. (Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 483)

Tirmizi narrates that one day the Prophet saw Sayyida Safiyya crying. He asked the reason. She said: "O Messenger of Allah! Sayyida Ayesha and Sayyida Hafsa say that they are far more honored in the prophetic court than me because their families are related to you." Hearing this, the Holy Prophet said: "O Safiyya! Why did you not say to them: 'How can you both be better than me? Sayyidina Haroon (Alaihissalam) is my father, Sayyidina Moosa (Alaihissalam) is my uncle, and Sayyidina Muhammad ﷺ is my husband.'" (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 259)

She also narrated 10 hadith from the Holy Prophet. Among them, 1 hadith is found in both Bukhari and Muslim, and the remaining 9 are recorded in other books.

There is disagreement on the year of her death. Waqidi says she died in 50 AH, and Ibn Sa'ad says 52 AH. At the time of her death, she was 60 years old. She too was laid to rest in Madina's famous cemetery, Jannatul Baqi. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 259; Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 2, p. 483)

### Summary of the Timeline

These are the 11 pure wives of the King of Madina ﷺ on whom all historians agree. Among them, Sayyida Khadija had passed away before the Hijrah. Sayyida Zainab bint Khuzaima, known as "Ummul Masakeen," passed away 2-3 months after the marriage, as we have written earlier. At the time of the Prophet's ﷺ passing, 9 wives were present. The Prophet used to fix turns for 8 of them, as Sayyida Sauda (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) had gifted her turn to Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). Among these 9 holy wives, the first to pass away after the Prophet's demise was Sayyida Zainab bint Jahsh (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), and the last among them was Sayyida Umme Salma (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), who passed away in 62 or 63 AH. After her death, the world became devoid of the Mothers of the Believers.

### Holy Maidservants

Apart from the aforementioned pure wives, the Holy Prophet ﷺ also had 4 maidservants who were under his possession. Their names are as follows:

**1. Sayyida Maria Qibtiyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha):**
She was presented as a gift to the prophetic court by Maqooqas Qibti, the king of Egypt and Alexandria, along with some other gifts and offerings. Her mother was Roman and she was Egyptian, so she was very beautiful and fair. She is the Umm Walad (mother of the Prophet's child) of the Holy Prophet ﷺ because his son Sayyidina Ibrahim (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) was born from her blessed womb.

Despite being a maidservant, the Holy Prophet ﷺ kept her in purdah. He had a separate house built for her in the place called Aaliya, near Madina, where she lived, and the Holy Prophet would visit her. Waqidi states that after the Holy Prophet, the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Abu Bakr Siddique (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), arranged for her sustenance throughout his life. After him, the Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Umar Farooq (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), continued this service. She passed away in 15 or 16 AH. The Commander of the Faithful, Sayyidina Umar Farooq (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), specially gathered people to participate in her funeral prayer, led it himself, and buried her in Jannatul Baqi. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 271-272)

**2. Sayyida Raihana (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha):**
She was from the Jewish tribe of Banu Quraiza. She was captured and came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. However, she did not accept Islam for some days, which made the Holy Prophet ﷺ displeased with her. But one day, a Companion came and gave the glad tidings: "O Messenger of Allah! Raihana has accepted Islam." The Prophet was extremely pleased and said to her: "O Raihana! If you wish, I will free you and marry you." But she requested: "O Messenger of Allah! Please keep me as your maidservant. That will be better and easier for both of us."

She passed away in the Prophet's own lifetime, in 10 AH, when he returned from the Farewell Pilgrimage, and was buried in Jannatul Baqi. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 273)

**3. Sayyida Nafeesa (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha):**
She was originally the maidservant of Sayyida Zainab bint Jahsh (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), who presented her as a gift to the Holy Prophet's service. She lived in the prophetic household as a maidservant. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 274)

**4. The Fourth Maidservant:**
Apart from the aforementioned maidservants, the Holy Prophet had a fourth maidservant about whom historians have generally written that her name is not known. She too was captured in some battle and came to the prophetic court. She remained honored as a maidservant of the Holy Prophet and blessed with his companionship. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 274)

### Blessed Children (Aulad-e-Kiram)

All historians agree that the number of the Holy Prophet's ﷺ blessed children is 6: two sons — Sayyidina Qasim and Sayyidina Ibrahim — and four daughters — Sayyida Zainab, Sayyida Ruqayya, Sayyida Umme Kulsoom, and Sayyida Fatima (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhum). However, some historians have stated that the Prophet also had a son named Sayyidina Abdullah, whose titles were Tayyib and Tahir. Based on this view, the Prophet's blessed children total 7 — 3 sons and 4 daughters. Shaikh Abdul Haque Muhaddis Dhelwi (Rahmatullahi Ta'ala Alaih) considers this latter view more correct. Apart from this, there are other sayings regarding the Prophet's blessed children, the mention of which is not free from length.

Among these seven blessed children of the Holy Prophet, Sayyidina Ibrahim (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) was born from the womb of Sayyida Maria Qibtiyya (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). All the remaining blessed children were born from the blessed womb of Sayyida Khadijatul Kubra (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha). (Zurqani, Vol. 2, p. 193; Madarijun Nubuwwah, Vol. 3, p. 451)

Now we shall shed light on these blessed children with some detail.

**Sayyidina Qasim (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):**
He is the first child, born in the blessed lap of Sayyida Khadija (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), before the proclamation of prophethood. The Holy Prophet's kunyah, Abul Qasim, is derived from his name. The majority of scholars say that he had just learned to walk when he passed away. Ibn Sa'ad states that his blessed age was 2 years, but Allama Ghalabi says he lived only 17 months. And Allah knows best. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 194)

**Sayyidina Abdullah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu):**
His titles are Tayyib and Tahir. He was born after the proclamation of prophethood. He was also called "Tayyib" and "Tahir" because he was born in Islam.
