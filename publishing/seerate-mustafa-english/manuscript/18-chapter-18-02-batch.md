## Chapter 18: The Prophetic Character (Continued)

### Modesty (Haya)

Regarding the modesty of the Holy Prophet ﷺ, Allah Almighty's own declaration in the Quran is the greatest witness: "Indeed, your (this) statement hurts the Prophet, but he is modest before you (and cannot say anything to you)." (Al-Ahzab)

Describing the Prophet's modesty, an honored Companion, Sayyidina Abu Saeed Khudri (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), said: "The Prophet was more modest than a virgin girl in her seclusion." (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 284; Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 503)

Thus, throughout his life, the hem of his purity remained clean from every ugly word and deed, and no action contrary to dignity and honor ever occurred from him. Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) said: "The Prophet was neither foul-mouthed nor obscene, nor did he shout in the marketplaces. He did not repay evil with evil, but rather forgave. She also said: 'Due to his extreme modesty, I never saw the Prophet fully unclothed.'" (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 69)

### Fulfillment of Promises (Eefa-e-Ahad)

Fulfillment of promises and covenants is also a very important and flourishing branch of the tree of character. In this quality as well, the Arab Prophet's ﷺ great character is unparalleled.

Sayyidina Abul Hamsa (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "Before the proclamation of prophethood, I purchased some goods from the Prophet, and some amount remained due from me. I said to him: 'Wait right here; I will bring the money from home and give it to you.' The Prophet agreed to wait. I went home but forgot my promise. Three days later, when I remembered, I went to that place with the money. What did I see? The Prophet was still there! No wrinkle had appeared on his forehead, and he said nothing except: 'O young man! You have put me to hardship. I have been waiting here for three days according to my promise.'" (Shifa Sharif, p. 74)

### Justice (Adl)

The sacred Messenger of Allah was the most trustworthy, the most just, the most righteous, and the most truthful in the entire world. This is a luminous truth acknowledged even by his greatest enemies. Before the proclamation of prophethood, all the people of Makka remembered him by the honored titles "Sadiqul Wa'ad" (Truthful in Promise) and "Ameen" (Trustworthy).

Sayyidina Rabi'a bin Khasim (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) says: "The people of Makka were agreed that the Prophet was of the highest rank in trustworthiness and justice. Therefore, before the proclamation of prophethood, the people of Makka would have their cases and disputes decided by him, and they would accept all his decisions with the utmost respect, without any choice or objection, saying: 'This is the decision of the Trustworthy One.'" (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, pp. 78-79)

A narration from Sahih Bukhari stands as the greatest witness to the Prophet's justice. A woman from the Banu Makhzum clan of the Quraish committed theft. In Islam, the punishment for theft is that the thief's right hand be cut off at the wrist. The Quraish became deeply concerned about this incident — if this woman's hand was cut off, it would leave an indelible stain on their family honor, and they would be humiliated in the eyes of all Arabs. They decided that a powerful intercession should be made in the court of prophethood so that the Prophet would not cut off her hand.

They persuaded Sayyidina Usama bin Zaid (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma), who was extremely beloved in the eyes of prophethood, to intercede. Influenced by the insistence of the Quraish nobles, he presented the request in the sacred court. Hearing this, the signs of divine wrath appeared on the Prophet's blessed forehead, and he said in a very angry tone: "O Usama! Do you intercede regarding one of the punishments prescribed by Allah?" Then the Prophet stood and delivered a sermon, in which he said:

"O people! Those before you were destroyed because when a noble person among them committed theft, they would let him go, but when a weak person stole, they would impose the punishment. By Allah! If Fatima, the daughter of Muhammad, were to steal, Muhammad would certainly cut off her hand."

### Dignity (Waqaar)

Sayyidina Khariza bin Zaid (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) used to say: "The dignity with which the noble Prophet ﷺ graced his gatherings cannot be matched even in the courts of the greatest kings." Sayyidina Jabir bin Samurah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) used to say: "The Prophet's assembly was characterized by forbearance, modesty, goodness, and trustworthiness. No one could speak loudly in his assembly. When the Prophet spoke, all present would bow their heads and listen attentively, as if birds were perched on their heads."

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "The Prophet spoke with such dignity and composure that if someone wanted to count his words, they could." (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, pp. 80-81; Bukhari, Vol. 1, p. 503)

In his sitting and rising, dignified speech, and every gesture, a purely prophetic dignity was evident, through which the majesty and grandeur of his prophethood shone before everyone like the radiant sun.

### Ascetic Life (Zahidana Zindagi)

Although the Prophet ﷺ was the Sovereign of the Universe and the Chief of Both Worlds, he lived such an ascetic and simple life that no parallel can be found in the history of prophethood. In food, clothing, housing, furnishings, lifestyle — in every aspect of his blessed life — his asceticism and detachment from the world was so pronounced that one could only say that the pleasures and comforts of the world were more insignificant than a mosquito's wing in his prophetic eyes.

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "Three consecutive days never passed in the Prophet's life when he ate his fill of bread. Sometimes a whole month would pass without a fire being lit in the house of prophethood, and the family's only food was dates and water." Allah Almighty had said to him: "O Beloved! If you wish, I will turn the mountains of Makka into gold that will accompany you, and you may spend it as you please." But the Prophet did not accept this and said in the court of Allah: "O my Lord! I prefer to be hungry one day and satiated the next, so that in hunger I may humbly supplicate to You, and in satiety I may praise and thank You."

Sayyida Ayesha (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) said: "The bed on which the Prophet slept was a leather mattress stuffed with tree bark instead of cotton."

Sayyida Hafsa (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha) says: "On my turn, the Holy Prophet ﷺ would sleep on a coarse mat that I would fold double and spread. Once I folded it into four layers and spread it. In the morning, the Prophet said: 'Spread it as before, double-folded, for I fear that the softness of this bedding may cause me to fall into deep sleep, disrupting my Tahajjud prayer.'"

It is narrated that sometimes the Prophet would rest on a cot made of coarse palm-fiber ropes. When he lay on it without a mattress, the delicate marks of the ropes would appear on his body. (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, pp. 82-83)

### Bravery (Shuja'at)

The matchless bravery of the Chief of the Prophets ﷺ was such that Sayyidina Ali (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu), himself a fearless warrior, said: "When the battle grew fierce and even the bravest warriors' eyes would become bloodshot with fear, we would seek shelter near the Prophet. He would be the foremost among us, advancing closest to the enemy. Among us, the bravest was considered the one who stayed closest to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in battle."

Sayyidina Abdullah bin Umar (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) used to say: "I have never seen anyone braver, stronger, more generous, or more likable than the Prophet ﷺ."

Sayyidina Bara bin Aazib and other noble Companions related: "In the Battle of Hunain, the army of 12,000 Muslims fled before the attacks of the disbelievers. Arrows were raining continuously from the enemy side. Even then, the Prophet ﷺ did not retreat a single step. He was mounted on a white mule, with Sayyidina Abu Sufyan bin Al Haris holding its reins, advancing alone toward the dense enemy ranks, reciting: 'I am the Prophet — this is no lie; I am the son of Abdul Muttalib.'"

### Physical Strength (Taaqat)

The Prophet's physical strength was also at the level of miracle. He displayed such astonishing feats and perfections through this miraculous strength that the human mind is bewildered at the mere thought. During the Battle of the Trench (Ahzab), when the noble Companions were digging the trench, a huge rock appeared that no one could break. When the Prophet struck it with his miracle-working pickaxe, it crumbled like grains of sand, as detailed in the account of the Battle of the Trench.

### The Wrestler Rukana

Rukana, a famous wrestler of Arabia, was passing by the Prophet. The Prophet invited him to Islam. Rukana said: "O Muhammad! If you wrestle me and throw me down, I will accept your call." The Holy Prophet agreed, wrestled him, and threw him down. Rukana challenged him again. The Prophet threw him a second time with such force that he could not get up for a long time. Astounded, Rukana said: "O Muhammad! By Allah! Your glory is extraordinary. No Arab wrestler has ever pinned me to the ground, but you have thrown me twice in an instant." Some historians say Rukana immediately became Muslim, while others write that he accepted Islam on the day of the Conquest of Makka. (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 291)

### Contest with Yazeed bin Rukana

Rukana's son, Yazeed bin Rukana, was also a renowned wrestler. He came to the court of prophethood with 300 goats and said: "O Muhammad! Wrestle with me." The Prophet said: "If I throw you, how many goats will you give me?" He said: "I will give you 100 goats." The Prophet agreed, and as soon as they locked hands, he threw him to the ground. Astounded, Yazeed gave him 100 goats. He challenged him again, and the Prophet threw him a second time, receiving another 100 goats. A third time he challenged, and the Prophet threw him so hard that he fell on his back. Yazeed gave the remaining 100 goats but said: "O Muhammad! All Arabia is witness that no wrestler has ever defeated me until today. The way you have thrown me three times has convinced my heart that you are indeed a prophet of Allah." He then recited the Kalima and entered the fold of Islam. The Prophet ﷺ was extremely pleased and returned all 300 goats to him. (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 292)

### Test of Strength with Abul Aswad

Abul Aswad Jamhi was such a mighty wrestler that he would sit on a piece of leather, and ten wrestlers would try to pull it out from under him, but the leather would tear into pieces rather than come out. He also came to the sacred court and challenged: "If you throw me in wrestling, I will become Muslim." The Prophet stood up to wrestle him. As soon as he gripped his hand, he threw him to the ground. Astounded by this prophetic strength, he immediately became Muslim. (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 292)

### Generosity (Sakhawat)

The generosity of the Holy Prophet ﷺ needs no description. Sayyidina Abdullah bin Abbas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) says: "The noble Prophet was more generous than all people. Especially in Ramadan, his generosity would increase to such an extent that he became more generous than the rain-bearing winds."

Sayyidina Jabir bin Abdullah (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhuma) says: "The Prophet ﷺ never said 'No' to anyone who asked, no matter how great the thing requested." (Shifa Sharif, Vol. 1, p. 65)

This is the theme that the Tabi'i poet Farzadaq (d. 110 AH) expressed so beautifully:

*"You never see him saying 'No' to a petitioner —*
*Except in the testimony of faith, where 'No' must be said."*

A Persian poet rendered this meaning as:

*"From his hand and tongue, no 'No' was ever heard,*
*Except 'La ilaha illallah' — in that, 'No' is right."*

That is, the Prophet never said "No" to any petitioner; he always said "Yes." But in the Kalima of Shahada, the word "No" (La) necessarily came from his blessed tongue. If there were no need for "No" in the Shahada, he would have said "Yes" there as well.

The Prophet's generosity was not limited to responding to petitioners; he would give wealth to people so abundantly without being asked that such examples are rare in the history of generosity. When Safwan bin Umayya (the son of the Prophet's great enemy Umayya bin Khalaf) came to the court at the place "Zi'eerana", the Prophet gave him such a large herd of camels and goats that the valley between two mountains was filled. Safwan went to Makka and shouted to his people: "O people! Enter the fold of Islam! Muhammad gives so generously that there is no fear of poverty!" After this, Safwan himself also became Muslim. (Zurqani, Vol. 4, p. 295)

In short, the accounts of the Prophet's munificence and generosity are so unparalleled and numerous that if they were all recorded, many volumes could be filled. What we have written in these pages is sufficient to understand the Prophet's generosity. May Allah Almighty grant us all the ability to act upon the blessed way of the Holy Prophet ﷺ as much as possible. (Ameen)

### The Blessed Names (Asma-e-Mubaraka)

It is a famous Arab saying: "The abundance of names for something is evidence that that thing is honored and revered." Since the Creator of the Universe (may His glory be exalted) has honored the Holy Prophet ﷺ with such immense grace and dignity that he is the Imam of the Prophets, the Chief of the Messengers, and the Beloved of the Lord of the Worlds, his blessed names and titles are indeed very numerous.

Sayyidina Jubair bin Mut'im (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) narrates that the Prophet ﷺ said: "I have five names: I am Muhammad, I am Ahmad, I am Mahi (by whom Allah eliminates disbelief), I is Hashir (before whom people will be gathered), and I am Aaqib (the last Prophet)."

In the Holy Quran, the Prophet's titles and names are mentioned in great number. Some scholars have said that just as Allah has ninety-nine names, the Prophet also has ninety-nine names. Allama Ibn Dahiyya wrote in his book that if all the names of the Prophet mentioned in the Quran, hadith, and previous scriptures are counted, their number reaches 300. Some Sufis say that Allah has 1,000 names and the Prophet's names also number 1,000. (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 118)

Among all the blessed names, two are most famous: "Muhammad" and "Ahmad" ﷺ. His grandfather Abdul Muttalib named him "Muhammad" and performed his Aqiqa under this name. When people asked: "O Abdul Muttalib! Why did you name your grandson Muhammad, when none of your ancestors had this name?" He replied: "I named this child Muhammad with the intention and hope that all the people of the earth would praise him." According to another narration, he said: "I named him Muhammad hoping that Allah would praise him in the heavens and all His creatures would praise him on earth."

The reason for Abdul Muttalib's intention was a dream he had seen: a silver chain emerged from his back, one end of which was in the earth and the other touching the sky, and all the people of the East and West were clinging to that chain. When Abdul Muttalib asked the soothsayers of Quraish to interpret this dream, they said: "O Abdul Muttalib! A son will soon be born from your lineage, whom all the people of the East and West will follow, and all the inhabitants of the heavens and earth will praise him." (Zurqani, Vol. 3, pp. 114-115)

Others say that when the Prophet was in his noble mother's womb, she heard an angel saying: "O Aamina! The Chief of the entire world is in your womb. When he is born, name him Muhammad." (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 115)

There is no contradiction between these narrations — both Abdul Muttalib and Sayyida Aamina (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anha), due to their respective dreams, mutually consulted and named the Prophet Muhammad.

Allah Almighty has mentioned him as "Muhammad" in several places in the Holy Quran, while Hazrat Eisa (Alaihissalam) proclaimed his coming by the name "Ahmad" throughout his life. The Holy Quran states: "And remember when Eisa, son of Maryam, said: 'O Children of Israel! I am a messenger of Allah to you, confirming the Torah before me, and giving glad tidings of a Messenger who will come after me, whose name is Ahmad.'"

### His Patronymic (Kunyat)

The Prophet's famous patronymic is "Abul Qasim", mentioned in many hadith. Sayyidina Anas (Radi Allahu Ta'ala Anhu) also narrated that the Prophet had the patronymic "Abu Ibrahim". Sayyidina Jibreel (peace be upon him) greeted him with the words: "O father of Ibrahim! Peace be upon you." (Zurqani, Vol. 3, p. 151)
