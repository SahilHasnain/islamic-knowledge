## Chapter 12: The Seventh Year of Hijra — 7 AH

### Ghazwa-e-Zaatul Qarad

Near Madina, there was a grazing ground named Zaatul Qarad where the Prophet's milch camels used to graze. Abdurrahman bin Uyaina Fazari of the Ghatfan tribe, along with some men, suddenly raided this pasture and made off with 20 camels. The renowned archer Companion, Hazrat Salama bin Akwa, was the first to learn of this. He raised the alarm with a loud cry of "Ya Sabahah!" and pursued the raiders alone. Shooting arrows at them, he recovered all the camels, also capturing the sheets they had thrown away while fleeing.

Then the Beloved ﷺ arrived with an army. Hazrat Salama submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! I have not let these raiders drink water yet. They are all thirsty. Send the army after them and they will all be captured." The Beloved said: "You have become the master of your camels. Now deal gently with these people." Then he seated Hazrat Salama behind him on his camel and returned to Madina.

Imam Bukhari records that this Ghazwa took place only three days before setting out for the Battle of Khaibar. (Bukhari)

### The Battle of Khaibar

#### The Significance of Khaibar

Khaibar is a town eight stages from Madina. An English traveller has written that Khaibar is 320 kilometres from Madina. It was a highly fertile region producing excellent dates in abundance. This was the greatest centre of the Jews in Arabia. The Jews of Khaibar were the wealthiest and most warlike in Arabia, and they took great pride in their financial and military strength. They were the worst enemies of Islam and its founder. The Jews had built many strong fortresses here, some of whose ruins exist to this day. Among them, eight forts are very famous:

(1) Qutaiba (2) Naaim (3) Shaq (4) Qamoos (5) Natara (6) Sa'ab (7) Sateekh (8) Salalam

These eight forts were like eight quarters, and the entire area of these eight forts was called Khaibar.

#### When Did the Battle of Khaibar Occur?

All historians agree that the Battle of Khaibar took place in the month of Muharram, but there is disagreement about the year — whether it was 6 AH or 7 AH. This difference likely arises because some start the Hijri year from Muharram (so Muharram is in 7 AH) while others start from Rabiul Awwal (so it is still in 6 AH).

#### The Cause of the Battle

It has been mentioned before that the Jews of Khaibar were among those Arab disbelievers who attacked Madina during the Battle of Khandaq. Indeed, they were the real instigators. When the Banu Nazeer were exiled from Madina, their leaders who settled in Khaibar — among them Huyay bin Akhtab and Abu Raafe Salam bin Abdul Huqaiq — went to Makka and roused the Quraish to attack Madina. They toured all the tribes, inciting the Arab disbelievers, generously funding the attackers with money poured like water, and brought all the Jews of Khaibar with them. Both these Jewish leaders were party to the attacking force. Huyay bin Akhtab was killed in the Battle of Quraiza, and Abu Raafe was killed in 6 AH by Hazrat Abdullah bin Ateeq Ansari.

But even after these events, the Jews of Khaibar did not sit idle. The fire of revenge burned even more fiercely in their hearts. They began preparing for another attack on Madina and enlisted the powerful Ghatfan tribe for this purpose. The Ghatfan tribe was one of the strongest and most warlike Arab tribes, whose settlements were adjacent to Khaibar. The Jews of Khaibar themselves were the greatest capitalists of Arabia, extremely warlike and skilled swordsmen. Together they formed a large army and planned to attack Madina and crush the Muslims completely.

#### The Muslims March to Khaibar

When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ learned that the Jews of Khaibar, together with the Ghatfan tribe, were about to attack Madina, he took 1,600 Companions and marched to Khaibar to prevent their advance. He appointed Hazrat Sabaa bin Urufta as his deputy in Madina. Three banners were prepared: one for Hazrat Hubab bin Munzir, one for Hazrat Saad bin Obada, and the sacred banner of prophethood was given into the blessed hand of Hazrat Ali. Among the Mothers of the Believers, he took Hazrat Bibi Umme Salama with him.

The Beloved ﷺ reached the borders of Khaibar with his victorious army at night. After the Fajr prayer, when he entered the town, the Jews of Khaibar were coming out of their forts with their sickles and baskets to work in the fields and gardens. When they saw the Beloved, they raised a cry: "By Allah's oath! Muhammad has come with an army!" At that moment, the Beloved said: "Khaibar is destroyed. Whenever we alight in the field of a people, evil is the morning of those who are warned." (Bukhari)

Hazrat Abu Moosa Ash'ari relates: When the Beloved turned towards Khaibar, the Companions began raising the Takbeer with very loud voices. The Beloved said: "Be gentle with yourselves. You are not calling a deaf or absent one. You are calling Allah, Who is All-Hearing and Near." Hazrat Abu Moosa was reciting "La Hawla Wala Quwwata..." behind the Prophet's mount. When the Beloved heard this, he said: "Shall I tell you of a word that is a treasure from the treasures of Paradise? It is `La Hawla Wala Quwwata Illa Billah`." (Bukhari)

#### The Jews' Preparations

The Jews had sent their women and children to a secure fort and gathered their food supplies in Fort Naaim. Their armies were concentrated in the forts of Nataat and Qamoos. Among these, Fort Qamoos was the strongest and safest. The Jewish champion Marhab — considered equal to a thousand horsemen among Arab warriors — was chief of this fort. Though Salam bin Mashkam was ill, he too had brought his forces to Fort Nataat. The Jews had approximately 20,000 soldiers deployed in various forts.

#### Mahmood bin Muslima is Martyred

The first battle took place at Fort Naaim with great ferocity. Hazrat Mahmood bin Muslima fought with great bravery and devotion, but due to extreme heat and scorching winds, he was overcome by thirst. He fell asleep under the wall of Fort Naaim. The Jew Kinana bin Abil Huqaiq saw him and dropped a massive stone from the roof, crushing his head. He was martyred. Fifty Muslims were wounded in taking this fort, but it was conquered.

#### The Martyrdom of Aswad Raai

Hazrat Aswad Raai was also martyred in this battle. He was an Abyssinian slave who used to graze goats for a Jew of Khaibar. When the Jews were preparing for battle, he asked them: "Who are you preparing to fight?" They replied: "Today, we will fight the man who claims prophethood." Hearing this, a desire to meet the Beloved arose in his heart.

He brought the goats to the court of prophethood and asked: "What do you invite to?" The Beloved presented Islam to him. He asked: "If I become Muslim, what reward will I receive from Allah?" The Beloved replied: "You will receive Paradise and its bounties." He immediately recited the Kalima and accepted Islam. Then he submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! These goats are a trust with me. What should I do with them?" The Beloved said: "Drive the goats towards the fort and throw pebbles at them. They will all return to their owner's home by themselves." By the miracle of the Beloved, when he threw pebbles at them, the goats all went to their owner's home.

Then this fortunate Abyssinian donned armour, stood in the ranks of the Muslim warriors, and fought with tremendous zeal and fervour until he was martyred. When the Beloved learned of this, he said: "This man did very few deeds but was given immense reward." He ordered his body brought to the tent, stood by his head, and gave the glad news: "Allah has made this dark face beautiful, made his body fragrant, and given him two maidens of Paradise. This man performed no good deed except faith and jihad — no prayer, no fast, no Hajj or Zakat — yet through faith and jihad, Allah granted him this lofty station."

#### The Muslim Army's Headquarters

The Beloved already knew that the Ghatfan tribe would certainly come to aid the people of Khaibar. Therefore, he established his army's headquarters at Rajee, between Khaibar and Ghatfan. The tents, equipment, and women were kept here, and from here the Muslims would sally forth to attack the Jewish forts.

#### The Siege of Fort Qamoos

After Fort Naaim, the other forts were conquered easily and quickly. But Fort Qamoos was extremely strong and well-fortified, with a large Jewish force inside, and the great Jewish hero Marhab himself defended it. For several days, the campaign did not succeed.

On the first day, the Beloved sent the Muslim army under the command of Hazrat Abu Bakr to attack this fort. He launched a brave assault, but the Jews rained down such intense arrows and stones from the ramparts that the Muslims could not even reach the gate. Night fell.

The next day, Hazrat Umar launched a powerful assault. The Muslims fought with great fervour all day, but the fort could not be conquered. And how could it be? The honour of "Conqueror of Khaibar" was destined for Haidar-e-Karrar, Ali.

The Beloved ﷺ then declared: "Tomorrow I will give the banner to a man at whose hand Allah will grant victory. He loves Allah and His Messenger, and Allah and His Messenger love him."

The narrator says: The people spent that night in great anxiety, wondering who would receive the banner in the morning.

Morning came, and the Companions presented themselves at the sacred court with eager hopes, each desiring this honour, for three glad tidings were attached to it:
(1) He is the beloved of Allah and His Messenger.
(2) He is loved by Allah and His Messenger.
(3) Khaibar will be conquered at his hand.

Hazrat Umar says: That day I desired nothing more than to receive the banner. He further says: Apart from this occasion, I never desired army command.

Hazrat Saad's narration also indicates that other noble Companions yearned for this great blessing.

But suddenly, in the morning, a voice was heard: "Where is Ali?" The Companions submitted: "His eyes are sore." The Beloved sent for him, applied his blessed saliva to his inflamed eyes, and prayed. Immediately he was cured as if he had never felt any pain. Then the Chief of the Worlds placed his sacred banner — made from the black sheet of Hazrat Umme Ayesha — into Hazrat Ali's hand.

He said: "Proceed with calmness. Invite the Jews to Islam. Tell them that after accepting Islam, such-and-such rights of Allah become obligatory upon them. By Allah's oath! If even a single man accepts Islam through you, this is better for you than red camels." (Bukhari)

#### The Duel of Hazrat Ali and Marhab

Hazrat Ali reached Fort Qamoos and invited the Jews to Islam. Their response was bricks, stones, arrows, and swords. The chief of the fort, Marhab himself, came out with great pomp, wearing a Yemeni yellow sheet on his head and a stone-studded armour. Reciting Rajaz verses, he advanced for battle:

"Khaibar knows well that I am Marhab, armoured, extremely brave and experienced."

Hazrat Ali replied in Rajaz:

"I am the one my mother named Haidar (Lion). I am terrible like a lion of the forest."

Marhab struck at Hazrat Ali with his sword with great force, but Hazrat Ali dodged, and Marhab's blow missed. Then Hazrat Ali dealt a blow to his head with such force that the helmet was cut through, the armour was cut through, and Zulfiqar-e-Haidari sliced through the head down to the teeth. The sound of the blow reached the entire army, and Marhab collapsed to the ground. (Muslim)

Seeing Marhab's body writhing on the ground, the entire Jewish army fell upon Hazrat Ali. But Zulfiqar-e-Haidari flashed like lightning, mowing down ranks. The renowned Jewish champions Marhab, Haris, Aseer, Aamira, and others fell in this fierce battle. During the melee, Hazrat Ali's shield was cut and fell. He then rushed forward, wrenched off the gate of Fort Qamoos, and used it as a shield against the enemy's swords. This gate was so large and heavy that even forty men could not lift it afterward.

#### The Conquest of Khaibar

Hazrat Ali, with his valour, conquered Khaibar, and the promise of the Truthful Prophet was fulfilled: "Tomorrow I will give the banner to a man at whose hand Allah will grant victory. He loves Allah and His Messenger, and Allah and His Messenger love him."

Indeed, the Master of the Universe is the beloved of Allah and His Messenger, and undoubtedly Allah granted the conquest of Khaibar at his hand. Allah honoured him with the exalted title of "Conqueror of Khaibar" for all time. This was the great conquest that laid to rest the military power of the Jews throughout the Arabian Peninsula. Before the conquest of Khaibar, Islam was in a precarious state due to the alliance of Jews and polytheists. But after Khaibar, Islam emerged from this dangerous situation, and the doors of future Islamic conquests opened. Soon after this, Makka itself was conquered. It is an established fact that the entire chain of Islamic conquests is connected to the personality of the Conqueror of Khaibar.

Fort Qamoos was conquered after a twenty-day siege and intense fighting. In these battles, 93 Jews were killed and 15 Muslims attained martyrdom. (`Zurqani`)

#### The Administration of Khaibar

After its conquest, the land of Khaibar came under Muslim control. The Beloved intended to exile the people of Khaibar as he had done with Banu Nazeer. But the Jews requested: "Do not expel us from Khaibar. Let the land remain in our possession. We will continue to give half of the produce to you." The Beloved accepted their request. Whenever the dates ripened and the grain was ready, he would send Hazrat Abdullah bin Rawaha to Khaibar. He would divide the dates and grain into two equal portions and tell the Jews: "Take whichever portion you prefer." The Jews, astonished at this justice, would say: "The heavens and the earth are established on such justice." (`Futoohul Baldan`)

Hazrat Abdullah bin Umar relates: After the conquest of Khaibar, the Beloved made a treaty with the Jews on these terms: The Jews would hand over all their gold, silver, and weapons to the Muslims, but they could keep whatever was loaded on their animals — provided they concealed nothing.

Despite accepting this condition, the Jews concealed the leather bag of Huyay bin Akhtab, which was filled with gold and silver brought from Banu Nazeer at the time of their exile. When questioned, they lied, claiming all the wealth had been spent in battles. But Allah revealed the truth to His Messenger through divine revelation, and the Muslims recovered the bag.

Since Kinana bin Abil Huqaiq had killed Hazrat Mahmood bin Muslima by dropping a stone from the roof, the Beloved had him executed in retribution, and his women were taken as captives. (`Madarijun Nubuwwah`)

#### The Marriage of Hazrat Safiyya

Among the captives was Hazrat Bibi Safiyya, the daughter of Huyay bin Akhtab (chief of Banu Nazeer). Her husband, Kinana bin Abil Huqaiq, was also a chief of Banu Nazeer. When all captives were gathered, Hazrat Dihya Qalbi requested: "O Messenger of Allah! Grant me a slave girl from among them." The Beloved gave him permission to take his pick, and Hazrat Dihya chose Safiyya.

Some Companions submitted: "O Messenger of Allah! You have given Safiyya to Dihya Qalbi. She is the chieftainess of Quraiza and Banu Nazeer. She is worthy of none but you." (Abu Dawood)

Hearing this, the Beloved called Hazrat Dihya and Safiyya and told Hazrat Dihya: "Take another slave girl instead." Then he freed Safiyya and married her. For three days at Manzile Sahba, he kept her in his tent. He invited the Companions to a Walima feast of dates, ghee, and cottage cheese mixed into a paste. (Bukhari)

#### The Beloved is Poisoned

The Beloved stayed in Khaibar for several days after the conquest, granting complete peace and security to the Jews and bestowing various favours upon them. But such were the evil natures of these people that Zainab, the wife of Salam bin Mashkam, invited the Beloved to a meal and mixed poison into the meat. By Allah's command, a piece of the meat informed the Beloved of the poison. After taking one morsel, he withdrew his hand. But one Companion, Hazrat Bishr bin Bara, ate his fill and died of the poison. The Beloved suffered from the effects of this poisoned morsel for the rest of his life.

When the Beloved questioned the Jews about this, those criminals confessed their crime, saying: "We did this with the intention that if you are a true prophet, the poison will not affect you; otherwise, we will be rid of you." The Beloved never took revenge for himself personally. So he said nothing to Zainab. But when Hazrat Bishr bin Bara died from the poison, she was executed in retribution. (Bukhari)

### Hazrat Ja'far Returns from Abyssinia

The Beloved had just finished the conquest of Khaibar when Hazrat Ja'far — the brother of Hazrat Ali, who had migrated from Makka to Abyssinia — arrived from Abyssinia with his companions. In extreme love, the Beloved kissed his forehead and said: "I cannot say whether the conquest of Khaibar gives me greater joy or Ja'far's arrival." (`Zurqani`)

The Beloved gave these Companions the title of "Sahibul Hijratain" (Masters of Two Migrations) — for they had migrated from Makka to Abyssinia and then from Abyssinia to Madina. Although they could not participate in the Battle of Khaibar, the Beloved gave them an equal share of the spoils.

### Rulings Announced at Khaibar

On the occasion of the Battle of Khaibar, the Beloved announced the following rulings:

(1) Birds of prey with talons were declared forbidden.
(2) All predatory animals were declared forbidden.
(3) Donkeys and mules were declared forbidden.
(4) The sale of silver for silver and gold for gold was decreed permissible only when equal in weight and exchanged hand-to-hand; any increase constituted interest (riba), which is forbidden.
(5) Until then, it had been permitted to have intercourse with a slave girl immediately upon acquisition. Now, the rule of Istibra was made mandatory — meaning if she was pregnant, until she gave birth; otherwise, one month must pass. Mut'a marriage was also declared forbidden during this Ghazwa. (`Zurqani`)

### The Battle of Wadiyul Qura

After the Battle of Khaibar, the Chief Prophet proceeded to Wadiyul Qura, a valley between Teema and Fidak. Several Jewish settlements were located here. The Beloved had not come with the intention of fighting, but the Jews here were ready for battle and began showering arrows at the Beloved. A slave of the Beloved named Hazrat Madam was struck by an arrow while unloading a camel and was martyred.

The Beloved invited these Jews to Islam, but those ill-fated ones responded with arrows and swords, lining up in battle formation. The Muslims were compelled to fight. For four days, the Chief Prophet besieged them, continuously inviting them to Islam, but they persisted in fighting. Finally, ten Jews were killed, and the Muslims achieved a manifest victory. Following this, the people of Wadiyul Qura made peace on the same terms as the people of Khaibar — they would send half of their agricultural produce to Madina.

When the fate of Khaibar and Wadiyul Qura became known, the Jews of Teema also made peace by paying Jizya. The Beloved stayed four days in Wadiyul Qura.

### The Peace of Fidak

When the Jews of Fidak learned of the outcomes at Khaibar and Wadiyul Qura, they did not fight. Instead, they sent a messenger to the court of prophethood requesting peace on the same terms as Khaibar and Wadiyul Qura. The Beloved accepted their request. Since no army was sent here, the mujahideen received no share of this settlement; rather, it became the personal property of the Beloved. The lands of Khaibar and Wadiyul Qura, however, became the property of all the mujahideen.

### Umratul Qaza (The Fulfilled Umrah)

The Treaty of Hudaibiya stipulated that the following year the Beloved could come to Makka for Umrah and stay for three days. Accordingly, in Zulqada, 7 AH, the Beloved resolved to go to Makka for Umrah and announced that everyone who had been present at Hudaibiya the previous year should accompany him. Except for those who had been martyred or had passed away, all obtained this blessing.

Since the Beloved did not trust the Quraish to honour their covenant, he set out fully prepared for battle. He appointed Hazrat Abu Reham Ghifari as governor of Madina. With 2,000 Muslims — 100 of them mounted on horses — and 60 sacrificial camels, he set out for Makka.

When the Quraish learned that the Beloved was coming with weapons and war equipment, they were alarmed and sent men to Marruzzahran to investigate. Hazrat Muhammad bin Maslama, commander of the cavalry, met the Quraish's messengers and reassured them: "The Prophet will enter Makka without weapons, as per the treaty."

At Yajaj, eight miles from Makka, the Beloved deposited all weapons under the guard of Hazrat Basheer bin Saad and some Companions. He carried no weapon except a sword. With the congregation reciting "Labbaik," he proceeded towards the Haram.

As they entered Makka, Hazrat Abdullah bin Rawaha held the reins of the Prophet's camel and recited Rajaz verses with great fervour:

"O sons of the disbelievers! Get out of the way. Today, if you try to stop us, we will strike with our swords.
"We will strike such a blow that will separate the head from its resting place and make a friend forget his friend."

Hazrat Umar objected: "O Abdullah bin Rawaha! In the presence of the Messenger of Allah and in the sacred sanctuary of Allah, you recite verses?" The Beloved said: "O Umar! Leave him. These verses are more effective against the disbelievers than arrows." (Shamail-e-Tirmizi)

When the Chief Prophet entered the sacred precincts of the Kaaba, some Quraish, unable to bear the sight in their jealousy, went to the mountains. Others stood near Darunnadawa watching the Muslims perform Tawaf with wide eyes, remarking: "What Tawaf can these people perform? They have been crushed by hunger and Madina's fever."

The Beloved entered Masjid-ul-Haram and performed Iztiba'a — wrapping the sheet so that his right shoulder and arm were exposed. He said: "May Allah have mercy on him who shows his strength before the disbelievers." He then performed the first three circuits of Tawaf walking with vigorous strides, shaking his shoulders. This is called "Ramal" in Arabic, and this Sunnah remains to this day.

#### Hazrat Hamza's Daughter

After three days, some Quraish chiefs came to Hazrat Ali and said: "The term is complete. You should leave Makka now." Hazrat Ali conveyed their message to the Beloved, who immediately departed.

As they were leaving, a little daughter of Hazrat Hamza — named Umama — came running after the Beloved, calling "Chacha! Chacha!" (Uncle!). Hazrat Hamza had been martyred in the Battle of Uhud, and this orphan girl had remained in Makka. Seeing the memory of his martyred uncle, the Beloved was moved.

When the girl came near, Hazrat Ali picked her up, but now three claimants stepped forward for her guardianship. Hazrat Ali said: "O Messenger of Allah! She is my cousin, and I picked her up first. I have the right to raise her." Hazrat Ja'far said: "She is my cousin too, and her aunt is my wife, so I am more deserving." Hazrat Zaid bin Harisa said: "She is the daughter of my brother (in faith), so I will raise her."

After hearing all three, the Beloved ruled: "The aunt is like a mother." Therefore, the girl remained in Ja'far's care. To console all three, the Compassionate of the Worlds said: "O Ali! You are from me and I am from you. O Ja'far! You resemble me in character and appearance. O Zaid! You are my brother and my freed slave."

#### The Marriage to Hazrat Maimuna

During this journey of Umratul Qaza, the Beloved married Hazrat Bibi Maimuna — the sister of Hazrat Abbas's wife, Umme Fazl. On the return from Umratul Qaza, when the Beloved reached Saraf, he kept her in his tent and honoured her with his company. By a remarkable coincidence, 44 years later, at this very place of Saraf, Hazrat Bibi Maimuna passed away, and her blessed grave lies at this very spot. The correct report is that her year of death is 51 AH. A detailed account will appear in the section on the Mothers of the Believers.
