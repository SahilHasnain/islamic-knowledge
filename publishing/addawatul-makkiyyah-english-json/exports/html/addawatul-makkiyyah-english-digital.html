Opening Discussion and First View

The Leader of the Prophets ﷺ beholds the Being and Attributes of Allah Almighty in such a manner that neither crookedness nor shortcoming enters his blessed sight. Today people doubt and argue about what his sight had already witnessed. Allah Almighty revealed His Quran to him, and the Quran explains everything clearly.

The Noble Prophet ﷺ knew what has happened and what is yet to happen. His gaze encompassed all knowledge of the past and the future. He knew kinds of knowledge that have neither limit nor number, and no one besides him attained access to those kinds of knowledge.

If the knowledge of Sayyiduna Adam عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام, all worldly knowledge, and the knowledge of the Preserved Tablet were gathered and set beside the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, they would appear like a drop of water beside an ocean.

In the same way, all the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is like a splash—or a handful of water—beside the ocean of Allah Almighty’s infinite knowledge. The Noble Prophet ﷺ seeks help from his Lord, while the whole universe seeks help through him ﷺ. All the knowledge possessed by the learned people of the world is a gift from the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ and has reached them through him. All this knowledge came from his blessed court and was received from his door.

During the days when I was staying in Makkah Mukarramah, some people from India presented me with a questionnaire concerning the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ. It was Monday, the afternoon of 25 Dhu al-Hijjah 1323 AH.

As soon as I saw it, I suspected that it had been put together by those Wahhabis who had begun a campaign of insulting speech against Allah and His Messenger in India and had written books on this subject. Makkah Mukarramah is a peaceful and secure city, and it is home to countless religious scholars. If a Sunni encounters a religious question here, he can ask them.

The scholars of Makkah Mukarramah are boundless oceans of religious knowledge; people do not leave them to seek out narrow streams.

The noble scholars of Makkah Mukarramah are our leaders. They give comprehensive answers to the objections raised by the Wahhabis about the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ and other matters.

On one or two occasions, they explained such questions so clearly that all the faithful became reassured: the rust was removed from their hearts, their minds were illuminated, and their defects were corrected. These explanations brought the Wahhabis into a state of despair.

By the grace and generosity of my Lord, I, this weak servant—Ahmad Raza Khan Barelvi—also continue to follow the radiant Sunnah of my forefathers and to bring the Wahhabiyyah to ruin. I have authored more than two hundred books and have repeatedly invited the leading Wahhabis to debate—not merely once or twice, but many times—yet they have been unable even to answer and have remained bewildered.

Those who sit in India and insult the Noble Prophet ﷺ, persist in abuse and slander, and attribute lies and falsehood to Allah Almighty flee from the field of debate. They run away and disappear, then cannot face it again for the rest of their lives. Most of them have already departed from this world; those who remain will soon leave it in the same disgrace. Their end will be marked by bewilderment and confusion.

They learned that I was staying in Makkah Mukarramah for only a few days. I had no reference books with me. I was occupied with visiting the House of Allah and was preparing to travel to the city of my Master and Patron, Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. At such a time, they contrived a questionnaire and put it before me.

They hoped that, without books, and while preparing for Madinah al-Munawwarah, I would be unable to answer. Then they could rejoice and say, “Ahmad Raza Khan could not answer,” and take revenge for their humiliation.

At first I remained silent, although I had silenced their leaders many times before. But what did they know? I was under the protection of the firm religion. The one who supports and aids the religion is himself granted victory and protection. Allah Almighty has power: when He says to a thing, “Be,” it comes to be. By His grace, Allah Almighty also gave me strength.

Then it occurred to me to prepare two kinds of answers to this questionnaire: one for the people of truth and for circulation in treatises, so that readers might find the path of guidance, and another for those stubborn attackers. So I took up my pen and prepared for each of them an answer suited to what they deserved.

The First Discussion

The Foundation of Religion

Remember that religion rests upon whatever brings salvation in the Hereafter. Belief in the entire Holy Quran is essential. In the world there are many misguided people who believe in some verses of Allah Almighty but disregard others. The Qadariyyah sect is well known among them. They considered themselves the creators of their own actions—that is, their deeds. They believed in this noble verse:

“We did not wrong them; rather, they themselves used to wrong their own souls.” (An-Nahl: 118)

But they rejected this noble verse:

“Allah has created you and what you do.” (As-Saffat: 96)

There is also the Jabariyyah sect. They regarded a human being as wholly compelled, like a stone. They believed in this noble verse:

“You cannot will unless Allah, the Lord of all the worlds, wills.”

The Jabariyyah reject this verse:

“We repaid them for their rebellion; and We certainly speak the truth.” (Al-An'am: 146)

The Kharijites call even a person who commits a major sin an unbeliever. They believe in this noble verse:

“Indeed, the wicked will surely be in Hell; they will enter it on the Day of Judgement.” (Al-Infitar: 14–15)

Then they reject this noble verse:

“Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating partners with Him, but He forgives whatever is lesser than that for whomever He wills.” (An-Nisa: 116)

The belief of the Murji'ah sect is that no sin can harm a Muslim, whatever sin he may commit. They believe in this noble verse:

“Do not despair of Allah’s mercy. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins; He is the Most Forgiving, the Most Merciful.” (Az-Zumar: 53)

But they reject this noble verse:

“Whoever does evil will be requited for it.”

There are thousands of examples of this kind that can be presented concerning different religions and sects. Books of Islamic theology contain many such proofs.

The First Discussion — Knowledge of the Unseen and the Classification of Knowledge

Knowledge of the Unseen in the Light of Quranic Verses

The decisive text of the Mighty Quran states:

“No one in the heavens and the earth knows the unseen except Allah.” (An-Naml: 65)

At another place, He states:

“Allah does not disclose His unseen to anyone, except to those Messengers of His whom He has chosen.” (Al-Jinn: 26–27)

Then He states:

“The Beloved Prophet ﷺ is not withholding knowledge of the unseen.” (At-Takwir: 24)

He further states:

“O Prophet! Allah has taught you what you did not know, and Allah has bestowed great favour upon you.” (An-Nisa: 113)

In another noble verse, He states:

“These are reports of the unseen that We reveal to you. You were not present with them when his brothers plotted.” (Yusuf: 102)

At another place, He states:

“These are reports of the unseen that We reveal to you.” (Hud: 49)

Besides the Quranic verses mentioned above, many other verses shed light on knowledge of the unseen. These verses provide both kinds of proof: negation and affirmation. Both are part of faith, and denying them is disbelief.

A Muslim believes in all these verses and does not follow conflicting paths. Negation and affirmation cannot both lead to one and the same conclusion; we must seek the distinct conclusions to which each leads.

By the grace and power of my Lord, I now enter the field of investigation. Whoever deceives or acts fraudulently in this field, I shall strike back at him.

Classification of Knowledge

Knowledge is classified in one way according to its source—that is, where it comes from. Another classification considers its subject, that is, what the knowledge concerns. This gives rise to a further distinction: we must examine the nature of the relation. According to this distinction, knowledge is either intrinsic (zaati), issuing from the very essence of the knower, or bestowed (atai), granted by another.

Intrinsic knowledge belongs exclusively to the Divine Being. No one besides Allah has any share in it, and such knowledge cannot be established for anyone in the world. Whoever affirms even less than a particle of intrinsic knowledge for anyone will certainly become an associator of partners with Allah and be ruined.

The second kind of knowledge—bestowed knowledge—is granted to Allah’s servants and is specific to the servant. It cannot be ascribed to Allah.

Anyone who ascribes bestowed knowledge to Allah will certainly be an unbeliever and will commit major shirk. Shirk is to regard another as equal to Allah; here, however, such a person has placed another above Allah. Or he is so ignorant that he has attributed his own knowledge to someone other than God—Allah forbid!

From another perspective, knowledge is divided into two kinds. One is mutlaqul ilm, an expression used in the technical terminology of the principles of jurisprudence. For this knowledge to be established, the existence of a single individual is necessary. But negating it negates all its individuals. The Seal of the Verifiers, my revered father—may his secret be sanctified—explained this discussion and investigation beautifully in الرشاد لقمع مبانی الفساد, in his discussion of the affirmative proposition.

The second kind is mutlaq ilm, by which I mean knowledge that entails generality and true exhaustive comprehensiveness. This kind is not established until all individuals are included; denying even one individual negates it. Here, the proposition is universally affirmative and particularly negative. This knowledge has two levels: general and detailed. At the detailed level, each kind of knowledge, each detail, and each meaning is distinct from the others. That is, whatever knowledge a knower possesses—whether particular or universal, all or some—is distinguished from the rest.

This second kind also has four categories. One is exclusive to Allah Almighty; it is called ilme mutlaq tafseeli (absolute, detailed knowledge), and the following noble verse points to it:

“Allah is the Knower of everything.” (Al-Ahzab: 40)

Our Generous Lord, through His Noble Being and infinite Attributes, knows every event that exists now, existed before, or will continue to occur until eternity. He knows every possibility that has never existed and will never exist, as well as every impossibility. Among all meanings, there is nothing outside the scope of Divine knowledge. He knows every one of these things in complete detail; from pre-eternity to eternity, everything is within His knowledge.

The Being of Allah Almighty is infinite, and so are His Attributes; each Attribute is infinite in number. The days of eternity and every moment within them are in His knowledge: every blessing of Paradise, every punishment of Hell, the breaths and blinks of the people of Paradise and Hell, their slightest movements, and everything besides these. Indeed, Allah Almighty’s knowledge concerning every particle is infinite. He knows every particle’s relation to every other particle—what has passed, what will come, and what could exist, including each relation of nearness, distance, and direction. He knows how these relations change through times and places and what effects those changes produce, from the beginning until the limitless future. Thus Allah Almighty’s knowledge is infinite and beyond limit.

In the terminology of mathematicians, this is the third power, called a cube. When a number is multiplied by itself, it becomes a square; when that square is multiplied by the same number, it becomes a cube. These matters are clear not only to mathematicians but also to the noble scholars who know religion. It is indisputable that no created being’s knowledge can, at a single moment, encompass every other individual in all the complete details of actual infinity. Distinctions can be made only by considering each individual specifically; the infinite cannot be comprehended in one instant.

However vast and extensive the knowledge of creation may be—even if it reaches from the Throne to the earth, from beginning to end, and across countless degrees—it remains finite. The Throne and the earth are two directions and two boundaries; the beginning and the end are likewise two limits. A thing enclosed between two things is finite, not infinite. Yet something without a boundary may be infinite. Finitude, in the true sense, is impossible for Allah’s knowledge, because His Attributes and His knowledge transcend origination. It is therefore established that actual infinity belongs exclusively to Allah’s knowledge, while finite knowledge belongs to His servants.

These are philosophical ideas and logical inferences. Apart from them, the Quran’s declaration concerning Allah Almighty’s infinite knowledge is sufficient:

“Allah encompasses everything.” (An-Nisa: 126)

The Divine Being is not limited, and no created being can be unlimited. Allah Almighty is as He is, in all perfection. He cannot be known completely. It is correct to say, “I have attained knowledge of Allah,” but it is incorrect to say, “I have attained so much knowledge of Allah that nothing more remains.” That would make the Being of Allah limited and place Him within the bounds of human knowledge and reason, whereas He is exalted above that. Nothing can encompass Him; rather, He encompasses all things.

The Prophets, Awliya, righteous people, and believers attain knowledge of Allah according to their respective ranks and degrees, and their ranks differ accordingly. Their knowledge of Allah continues to increase throughout eternity. Even so, they will never be capable of attaining complete knowledge of all the perfections of Allah’s knowledge; rather, they will continue to receive a finite measure. This proves that it is impossible, both rationally and according to the Sacred Law, for any created being to encompass all Divine knowledge. Even if all the knowledge of every earlier and later person were gathered, it would bear no comparison to Divine knowledge.

To help us understand, we may say that if one million oceans of Divine knowledge were imagined, the combined knowledge of all creation would be less than a single drop beside them. The rivers of created knowledge, however abundant, are finite. A finite thing can be compared with another finite thing: we may compare a drop with one million oceans. But those oceans would eventually perish, because even one million oceans are finite. However many examples one offers, they will remain finite and will never reach the infinite. The infinite will remain forever, and our faith is that finite knowledge has no status or comparison before Allah’s infinite Attributes.

The Knowledge of Sayyiduna Khidr and Sayyiduna Musa عَلَيْهِمَا السَّلَام

Sayyiduna Khidr عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام referred Sayyiduna Musa عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام to what had been said and explained that a bird had taken a beakful of water from the sea. This kind of infinite knowledge belongs exclusively to Allah Almighty.

We shall now discuss three kinds of knowledge, one of which has already been discussed above:

  1. Ilme mutlaq ijmali — general absolute knowledge.
  2. Mutlaq ilm ijmali — absolute general knowledge.
  3. Mutlaq ilm tafseeli — absolute detailed knowledge.

These kinds of knowledge are not exclusive to the Being of Allah; rather, subject to the stated qualification, they are specific to His servants. General absolute knowledge is attained by servants. This is rationally established and is part of the essential beliefs of religion.

Just as we believe that Allah knows everything, by saying “everything” we have taken all Divine knowledge into account and known it collectively. Whoever does not know this for himself denies his faith and admits his disbelief—Allah forbid!

“Indeed, Allah knows everything.” (Al-Baqarah: 231)

Once absolute knowledge has been established for servants, general absolute knowledge is established by that very fact. Likewise, absolute detailed knowledge is also specific to servants. We include belief in the Resurrection, Paradise, Hell, the Being of Allah, and His Attributes among the foundations of faith. Although all these matters are unseen, we recognize each of them distinctly. This proves that, in this sense, every Muslim possesses absolute detailed knowledge of the unseen—while the rank of the Prophets is, of course, far higher.

Belief in the Unseen

Allah Almighty has commanded us to believe in the unseen. Faith is affirmation, and affirmation is knowledge. How can one affirm the unseen without knowing it? And how can one believe in it without affirming it? It follows that the knowledge exclusive to Allah is intrinsic knowledge. When verses deny knowledge to anyone besides Allah, they refer to intrinsic, absolute, detailed knowledge that truly encompasses all Divine knowledge. But when verses state that Allah has granted knowledge to His servants and mention such knowledge, they refer to bestowed knowledge—whether general absolute knowledge or absolute detailed knowledge. Allah Almighty praises His servants for these kinds of knowledge and distinguishes them on account of this bestowed knowledge.

  1. “The angels gave glad tidings of a knowledgeable boy.” (Adh-Dhariyat: 28)
  2. “Indeed, Sayyiduna Yaqub was certainly a person of knowledge because We had granted him knowledge.” (Yusuf: 68)
  3. “We granted Sayyiduna Khidr knowledge from Our presence.” (Al-Kahf: 65)
  4. “O Prophet of Allah! Allah granted you the knowledge that you did not know.” (An-Nisa: 113)

Countless other verses also explain in detail how Allah Almighty grants knowledge to His servants. In every such verse, what is meant is bestowed knowledge. Where verses state that knowledge of the unseen is given to servants, this is bestowed knowledge of the unseen. These are such true meanings of the Quranic verses that no one has room to deny them, nor can any other meanings be offered in their place.

This belief is among the essentials of faith. Whoever denies it commits disbelief and leaves the fold of Islam. These are the meanings by which the scholars of Islam reconciled the verses of negation and affirmation. Imam Zakariyya—may Allah Almighty have mercy upon him—presented this same explanation in his legal rulings. Imam Ibn Hajar Makki presented it in his Fatawa Hadithiyyah, and other reliable scholars of Ahl-e-Sunnat wa Jama'at also set out this same view in their books.

By negating knowledge of the unseen from anyone besides Allah, what is meant is intrinsic knowledge of the unseen. It is a reality that no created being can encompass all Divine knowledge. This is as clear as daylight and as radiant as the shining sun: whoever denies the knowledge of the unseen that Allah Almighty bestowed upon the Noble Prophet ﷺ has left the fold of faith. In our country, the Wahhabis have become so insolent that they openly claim that the Noble Prophet ﷺ did not even know the circumstances of his own end, nor did he know the end of his Ummah.

First View: Conclusion and Second View

In 1318 AH, these people sent me a similar questionnaire in Delhi. In response, I wrote a book entitled:

"انباء المصطفى بحال سر و اخفى"

That book brought a calamity upon the Wahhabis. They were denying what Allah Almighty had stated in the Quran, even though their claims negated their own faith and stood as clear testimony to their loss. Because of these blasphemous statements, they became unbelievers and apostates.

Then the Wahhabis committed an enormous act of disbelief concerning the Leader of the Prophets ﷺ. They said that the Prophet ﷺ had not known the circumstances of his own end, nor the end of his Ummah. This, too, is a denial of verses that are perfectly clear:

  1. “Indeed, your Hereafter is better for you than your worldly life.” (Ad-Duha: 4)
  2. “Indeed, soon your Lord will give you so much that you will be pleased.” (Ad-Duha: 5)
  3. “On that Day, Allah will not disgrace the Prophet or the believers who are with him. Their light will run before them and behind them.” (At-Tahrim: 8)
  4. “Soon your Lord will grant you the Praiseworthy Station.” (Bani Isra'il: 79)
  5. “Allah only intends to remove impurity from you, O people of the Prophet’s household, and to purify you thoroughly.” (Al-Ahzab: 33)
  6. “So that Allah may admit the believing men and believing women into Gardens beneath which rivers flow, where they will remain forever; He will remove their sins from them. That is a tremendous attainment with Allah.” (Al-Fath: 5)
  7. “Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory, so that He may forgive your earlier and later sins through you, complete His favour upon you, and guide you to a straight path. Allah is your Helper and the Bestower of honour.” (Al-Fath: 1)
  8. “Blessed is He Who, if He wills, can give you something better than that: Gardens beneath which rivers flow, and He can make lofty palaces for you.” (Al-Furqan: 10)

Countless Hadith, transmitted successively with a single meaning, have come concerning the interpretation and explanation of these verses. They form an ocean without shore, and it would be difficult to enumerate them. But after the Word of Allah and the Hadith of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, what speech is there upon which one should place faith?

The Second View

The Difference Between Intrinsic and Bestowed Knowledge

After studying the preceding pages, even a person with a short-sighted view will have his eyes illuminated by the light of these verses: the combined knowledge of all creation cannot even raise the suspicion that it equals the knowledge of our Lord, the Lord of all the worlds.

There cannot be even a particle of doubt in a Muslim’s heart that the knowledge of Allah Almighty and the knowledge of His creation could be compared or considered equal.

These people do not even understand the following distinctions: Allah’s knowledge is intrinsic, while the knowledge of creation is bestowed; Allah’s knowledge is necessary by virtue of His Being, while the knowledge of creation is originated, since all creation is originated. An attribute cannot precede the one it describes. Allah’s knowledge is not created, whereas the knowledge of creation is created. Allah’s knowledge is not under anyone’s power or command. Divine knowledge is always necessary and enduring, while created knowledge is originated and temporary. Divine knowledge cannot change in any way, whereas the knowledge of creation is subject to change.

Despite these differences and distinctions, could anyone imagine equality? Only those wretched people upon whom Allah’s curse has fallen would become entangled in such confusion. They have become deaf to the truth, and their eyes have been deprived of light.

With complete conviction and faith, we can say that even to imagine that the knowledge of the Beloved ﷺ encompasses Allah’s knowledge is a false notion. Equality with Divine knowledge is impossible. Given these clear and vast differences, how could there be equality between Divine knowledge, which is intrinsic, and the knowledge of Mustafa ﷺ, which is bestowed?

Between the knowledge of Allah Almighty and that of the accepted Messenger ﷺ, there is no shared quality except the letters “I-L-M” (the letters forming the word ilm, “knowledge”).

We have established through decisive proofs that the notion that Divine knowledge is limited is false, both rationally and according to the Sacred Law.

When the Wahhabis read the books of the Imams of religion and their followers, study the Prophet’s ﷺ knowledge of the unseen in the light of the Quran and Hadith, and come to the conclusion that the Beloved ﷺ knows all events from the first day to the last—past and future—they begin issuing rulings of disbelief and shirk against those scholars. They accuse them of equating Divine knowledge with the knowledge of the Prophet ﷺ.

Those who make such judgements are extremely misguided and mistaken. They themselves fall into pits of disbelief and shirk. They have made limited, finite knowledge equal to Allah’s unlimited, infinite knowledge. They bear witness—Allah forbid—that the knowledge of Allah and the knowledge of creation are equal.

If, according to their own understanding, knowledge were truly infinite and immeasurable, how could they consider the knowledge of the Beloved ﷺ—which is finite and bestowed—to be equal to it? They would not fall into equating the knowledge of the Creator with that of creation.

When they display their intellect steeped in ignorance, they mock Allah’s infinite knowledge and describe it as deficient. May Allah Almighty drown such people and protect us from their tribulations.

Opening of the Third View

They have made finite knowledge equal to Allah Almighty’s knowledge, which is unlimited and infinite. By doing so, they testify to disbelief, claiming that the knowledge of Allah and the knowledge of creation are the same—Allah forbid!

If, in their view, knowledge were infinite or beyond measure, how could they declare equal to it the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, which is finite and bestowed? They would not fall into equating the knowledge of the Creator with that of creation. When they display an intellect steeped in ignorance, they mock Allah Almighty’s infinite knowledge and describe it as deficient. May Allah Almighty drown such people and protect us from their tribulations.

The Third View

O Allah, have mercy! Darkness has spread, shadows have grown exceedingly dense, and many people are entering the blackness of misguidance. In the preceding pages, we discussed in detail Allah Almighty’s intrinsic and all-encompassing knowledge. This knowledge belongs exclusively to Allah’s Being; no servant shares in it. However, every Muslim has a share in absolute bestowed knowledge—let alone the noble Prophets عَلَيْهِمُ السَّلَام. If bestowed knowledge is not accepted, faith cannot remain sound.

If some confused person were to imagine, “Then what distinction is there between us and our Prophet ﷺ? Whatever knowledge the Noble Prophet ﷺ has, all the Prophets have the same; and we, too, have that same knowledge. Whatever we do not know, neither do they; therefore, you and we are equal”—such a thought could not enter even the mind or imagination of a scholar, let alone an ignorant person. Yet it is no surprise from the Wahhabis, for they are an irrational people and a crooked sect. Among them is not even one scholar who has the capacity to acknowledge the perfections of the Noble Prophet ﷺ.

A Crooked-Minded Indian

It so happened that, during those days, an old man from this sect—crooked-minded and obsessed with calling himself a Sufi—kept making loud claims. He was an arrogant, conceited, stubborn Indian. Recently, he had written a short treatise. Its wording was so base and insolent that it seemed as if the seven heavens would split apart. He had named his treatise Hifz-ul-Iman (“Safeguarding Faith”), although its wording was Khifz-ul-Iman (“destroying faith”). He writes:

“The claim that knowledge of the unseen belongs to your sacred person—if, according to Zaid, it is correct—raises the question: by ‘the unseen,’ is some of the unseen meant, or all of it? If some knowledge of the unseen is meant, then what distinction is there for the Noble Prophet? Such knowledge of the unseen is available to Zaid and Amr, indeed to every child and madman, and even to all animals and beasts. But if all knowledge of the unseen is meant, so that not a single individual is excluded, then its impossibility is established by rational and transmitted proofs.”

The Claim That Bestowed Knowledge Is Specific to the Prophets

This stubborn old man does not even know that absolute bestowed knowledge is, in essence, specific to the Prophets عَلَيْهِمُ السَّلَام. For this reason, our Generous Lord has stated:

“Allah is the Knower of the unseen, and He does not disclose His unseen to anyone, except to whichever of His chosen Messengers He wills.” (Al-Jinn: 26–27)

Therefore, whoever besides Allah receives knowledge of the unseen receives it through His gift and grace, and through the guidance He grants. How, then, could there be equality?

The World Is Illuminated Before Me

Those who are not Prophets receive of the knowledge of the noble Prophets only as much as the Prophets teach them. The oceans of the Prophets’ knowledge surge before them; what standing can the knowledge of others have beside theirs? The Prophets know—and indeed behold—all knowledge of maa kaana wa maa yakoon (what has happened and what will happen), from the beginning to the end. The whole universe lies open before their witnessed sight.

Allah Almighty states:

“And thus We show Ibrahim the entire kingdom of the heavens and the earth.” (Al-An'am: 75)

At-Tabarani in Al-Mu'jam al-Kabir, Nu'aym ibn Hammad in Kitab al-Fitan, and Abu Nu'aym in Hilyat al-Awliya narrate from Abdullah ibn Amr رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُمَا that the Noble Prophet ﷺ said:

The Mu'tazilah’s Interpretation

The Mu'tazilah also use these verses as proof to deny miracles of the Awliya. In Sharh al-Maqasid, the Allama answers the Mu'tazilah that “the unseen” here is not general but absolute or specific—that is, the time of the Day of Judgement—and there is a contextual indicator in the noble verse, for it mentions the Hour. It is not impossible that some angels, some people, and especially some noble Prophets عَلَيْهِمُ السَّلَام be granted knowledge of it.

It is established that Messengers are given knowledge of the unseen, though opinions may differ concerning the Awliya. Imam Qastalani states in Irshad al-Bari, his commentary on Sahih Bukhari, that no one besides Allah Almighty knows when the Hour will come; however, Allah opens the doors of the unseen to whichever chosen Messenger He is pleased with. A Wali follows a Messenger and receives knowledge through him.

Shah Abdul Aziz Dehlawi’s revered father, Shah Waliullah قُدِّسَ سِرُّهُ, recorded his own experience in Tafhimat-e-Ilahiyyah: in a particular spiritual event, he was shown the time when the Day of Judgement would be established and the heavens would split.

Further Testimony

When he returned to the world of wakefulness, the event felt like a dream. If such conditions are disclosed to Awliya, then—Glory be to Allah!—where does the Noble Prophet ﷺ stand, and where does his knowledge stand?

Hazrat Imam Nawawi رَحْمَةُ اللّٰهِ تَعَالَى عَلَيْهِ writes in his commentary on Futuhat Ilahiyyah, and again in the marginal notes to his other commentary, Fath-ul-Mubin, that the Noble Prophet ﷺ has knowledge of the Day of Judgement. The true position is that one group of scholars said Allah Almighty called our Prophet ﷺ into His presence only after disclosing hidden matters to him. Yes, the Noble Prophet ﷺ was also given the command and choice whether to disclose them or not.

Usmawi, in Salat, and Sayyidi Ahmad Kabir Badri, in his commentary, judged this statement sound. These are all rays of the Divine declaration: “We have sent down the Quran to you, explaining everything clearly.” The truth shone forth through the light of the Quran as clouds part from before the sun.

We therefore need not discuss the details of the fifth unseen matter, which are also disclosed to the noble Awliya. This is an ocean whose shore and depth are unknown; if we tried to count it, we would never reach its shore. If the Quranic verses cannot heal a person, where will his illnesses ever end?
