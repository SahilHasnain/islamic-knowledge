# Bahjat-ul-Asrar and Its Author

## Bahjat-ul-Asrar and Its Author

The eminent Imam, the most excellent knower of Allah, the perfected Wali, Shaykh of the reciters, support of the scholars, and choice of the *Urafa* (knowers of Allah), Sayyiduna Imam Abdul Hasan Ali ibn Yusuf ibn Jarir al-Misri قُدِّسَ سِرُّهُ, had the honour of being a student of the eminent Imam Abul Khair Shamsuddin Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Jariri, author of *Hisn-ul-Hasin*.

Shams-ul-Ulama al-Dhahabi, an Imam in the science of Hadith transmitters and author of *Mizan-ul-I'tidal*, attended his gathering, mentioned him in *Tabaqat al-Qurra*, and offered praise for his person. The eminent Imam and knower of Allah, Sayyiduna Abdullah ibn As'ad al-Yafi'i al-Shafi'i رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ, remembered him in his famous book *Mir'at-ul-Jinan* with lofty titles and honorifics. Imam Jalaluddin Suyuti رَحْمَةُ اللّٰهِ تَعَالَى عَلَيْهِ called him “the Unique Imam.”

In his book *Bahjat-ul-Asrar*, which illuminates hearts and minds, he writes that Shaykh Umar ibn Wahab al-Farazi al-Halabi said:

> “I read this book and found nothing in it that my heart did not wish to act upon, or that did not make me ready to put it into practice. This book contains excerpts from many great books.”

In *Kashf-uz-Zunun*, the qualities of the knower of Allah, the eminent and generous Makarim-un-Nahar al-Khalisi قُدِّسَ سِرُّهُ—one of the eminent deputies of Sayyid Ali ibn Bayhaqi—are said to need no introduction. In another place in his book *Bahja*, he writes:

> “I myself was honoured with a visit from the Supreme and Magnificent Ghaus-ul-Azam رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ. My eyes have not seen a spiritual guide like Hazrat Muhiyuddin Abdul Qadir Jilani رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ.”

### Hazrat Ghaus-ul-Azam and Reports of the Unseen

Shaykh ibn Shaykh Abil Majad Mubarak ibn Ahmad al-Baghdadi al-Harimi al-Hanbali told us that his father had related to him—and his father had heard it from his grandfather, Abul Majad رَحْمَةُ اللّٰهِ تَعَالَى عَلَيْهِ—this account:

“One day I was in the presence of Shaykh Makarim. His house stood beside the Khalis canal. A thought entered my father’s heart: ‘If only I could witness a wonder of the Supreme Ghaus-ul-Azam.’ His Eminence smiled, turned his attention toward me, and said:

> ‘Soon five people will come to us. One will be a fair man with a reddish complexion and a mole on his right cheek. He has only nine months left to live. A lion will tear him apart at Bataij, and Allah Almighty will take his life there.
>
> ‘The second will be an Iraqi, reddish-white in complexion, but one-eyed and lame. He will remain with us for treatment for one month, but he will not recover.
>
> ‘The third will be an Egyptian of wheat-coloured complexion. He will have six fingers on his left hand and an old spear wound on his left thigh, inflicted thirty years ago. He will set out for India to trade and will pass away there thirty years later.
>
> ‘The fourth will be a Syrian of wheat-coloured complexion, with thick calluses on his fingers. He will pass away in the Haram, in front of your house, seven years, three months, and seven days from now.
>
> ‘The fifth will be a fair-skinned Yemeni Christian. He will wear a *zunnar* (a distinctive girdle) beneath his clothing. He left his country three years ago and does not disclose his religion to anyone. He is spying to learn about the condition of the Muslims.’”

## The Five Visitors Arrive

It was only a short time before these people came into the service of Hazrat Ghaus-ul-Azam. The non-Arab asked for roasted meat; the Iraqi asked for *chawak* (a game bird) and duck meat; the Syrian asked for Syrian apples; and the Yemeni asked for a soft-boiled egg. None of them had told the others what he wanted. While we watched, the foods they desired were brought to them, and they began eating.

Abul Majad said:

> “By Allah, I watched closely, and there was not the slightest difference between them and the descriptions he had given. I asked the Egyptian about his old wound. He was astonished and said, ‘I received this wound thirty years ago.’”

After they had eaten the food each had desired, they fainted. When they regained consciousness, the Yemeni said, “Hazrat, what can be said about the rank of the one who knows the secrets hidden in the hearts of the Sufis?” His Eminence replied, “I know that you are a Christian, and that beneath your clothing is the sign of the cross.”

As soon as he heard this, the man leapt up with a cry and accepted Islam. His Eminence said, “My son, various Shaykhs saw you. They knew that you were a Christian and wore the cross, but they also knew that you would come to me and accept Islam, so they remained silent.”

The man passed away exactly as the Shaykh had foretold—not a moment earlier or later. The Iraqi remained ill for a month and died there, in a corner of one of the Shaykh’s lodges; I myself performed his funeral prayer. The Syrian passed away in my presence in the Haram. His death occurred precisely seven years, three months, and seven days later, just as the Shaykh had said.

## The Ghaus’s Foreknowledge of Seventy-Two Matters

This is an account that no sensible person can deny. Hazrat Ghaus-ul-Azam رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ is a devoted servant among the servants of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, a servant of his ﷺ servants’ servants. He disclosed seventy-two matters of the unseen concerning one man: secrets hidden in hearts, the place and time of death, its causes, what a person would do the next day, and many other unseen matters. There is no doubt or uncertainty about this.

Abul Majad was informed of the thought in his heart, and then told about the people who were going to arrive: five men—a non-Arab, an Iraqi, an Egyptian, a Syrian, and a Yemeni. These were eight unseen matters of which he was informed.

Eleven unseen matters were disclosed concerning the non-Arab: he would be fair with a reddish face; he would have a mole on his cheek, specifically the right cheek; he would desire meat and eat it roasted; he would die after nine months; a lion would tear him apart; his death would occur at Bataij; he would be buried there and would not be moved from that place; and his resurrection would be from there.

Likewise, eleven unseen matters were disclosed concerning the Iraqi: he would be fair, with a reddish hue; he would have a defect in one eye; he would be lame in his foot; he would desire duck and eat it with rice; he would become ill; remain ill for a month; die of that illness; die there; and die one month later.

## Further Details of the Predictions

Fifteen unseen matters were disclosed concerning the Egyptian: he would be wheat-coloured; have a squint; have six fingers on his left hand; have a wound that had not fully healed, an old wound from thirty years earlier; desire honey—not merely honey, but honey mixed with ghee; engage in trade; conduct his business in India; continue trading there for twenty years; die in India; and pass away after twenty years.

Nine unseen matters were disclosed concerning the Syrian: he would be wheat-coloured; desire apples; have thick calluses on his fingers; ask for Syrian apples; die in the sacred precinct; pass away at Abul Majad’s door; and live seven years, three months, and seven days.

Eight unseen matters were disclosed concerning the Yemeni: he would be fair and wheat-coloured; be a Christian; have a cross beneath his clothing; have left his country to test the Muslims; have spent three years in this work; concealed his intention from both his family and his townspeople; desire an egg; and ask for it soft-boiled.

These were the sixty-two unseen matters that Hazrat Ghaus-ul-Azam رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ disclosed before these people arrived. In addition, he disclosed five more unseen matters: none of them had told the others what he desired or intended, yet each of the five wished to receive a particular item. These seventy-two matters of the unseen were made manifest through the words of Hazrat Ghaus Pak رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ.
