## Printed Page 42 — Reports of the Events of the World

Sahih Bukhari relates from Sayyiduna Umar Faruq رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ:

> “Once the Noble Prophet ﷺ stood to deliver a sermon and informed us of all conditions from beginning to end, from pre-eternity until the Day of Judgement—so much so that he told us when the people of Paradise would enter Paradise and the people of Hell would enter Hell.”

There is a Hadith in Sahih Muslim that the Noble Prophet ﷺ addressed the people from morning until sunset. These words are especially noteworthy:

> “He informed us of everything that is to happen in the world until the Day of Judgement. The one among us who remembered the most received the greatest share of that knowledge.”

In a Hadith reported in both Bukhari and Muslim, Sayyiduna Hudhayfah رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ narrates that once the Noble Prophet ﷺ stood to deliver a sermon and told us all the circumstances from the time he stood until the Day of Judgement—everything that would happen—leaving nothing out.

Sayyiduna Mu'adh ibn Jabal رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ narrates in a Hadith of Tirmidhi that the Noble Prophet ﷺ said:

> “I saw my Majestic Lord. He placed His hand of power between my shoulders, and I felt its coolness in my chest. Everything became illuminated for me, and I recognized everything.”

### Knowledge of the Heavens and the Earth

> “I came to know everything that is in the heavens and the earth.”

At another place, he ﷺ said:

> “I came to know everything from the east to the west.”

It is also reported from Sayyiduna Abu Darda رَضِیَ اللّٰہُ تَعَالٰی عَنْہُ in Abu Ya'la, Ibn Muni', and At-Tabarani that:

## Printed Page 49 — Generality and Definitive Proof

This is not hidden from people of insight and faith: this is what is meant by *maa kaana wa maa yakoon* (what has happened and what will happen). In the principles of jurisprudence, it is made clear that an indefinite noun in a negative context is general. It is therefore impermissible to say that Allah Almighty left anything out of His speech. The word “all” is even more explicit in its generality than any other text, so it is not permissible that anything remain absent from a clear statement and detailed explanation.

### Definitive Theological Proof and Definitive Legal Proof

The principles of jurisprudence distinguish between *qatiyyat-e-kalami* (theological definitiveness) and *qatiyyat-e-usooli* (definitiveness in legal principles). General definitiveness in legal principles is subject to juristic reasoning; before theological definitiveness, it carries little weight.[^2]
