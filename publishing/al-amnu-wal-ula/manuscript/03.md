## Call Upon Huzoor with Humility and Submission

In the translation of his second Qasida-e-Naatia, he writes:

Huzoor is the refuge from every affliction.

When the one praising Huzoor <sup>عليه السلام</sup> realizes his own inadequacy, he should call upon Huzoor with extreme humility and sincerity, and cry out in supplication — and should seek Huzoor's refuge in this manner: "O Messenger of Allah! ## The Souls of the Awliya Go Wherever They Wish

Qazi Sanaullah Panipatti, in "Tazkiratul Mauta" — a work of his righteous student Mirza Sahib Mausoof's murid — writes regarding the souls of the Awliya:

"Their souls go to the heavens, the earth, and Paradise — wherever they wish. They help their friends and devoted ones in this world and the Hereafter, and they destroy their enemies." (pg. 41)

And what is the name of the one who wards off calamities?

In the Malfoozat of Mirza Sahib:

"My special relationship with Hazrat Ali <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> is for a specific reason — the faqeer has a particular need from him. Whenever a physical ailment or illness occurs, I turn my attention toward him, and it becomes a means of healing." (*Malfoozate Mirza Azhar, pg. 78*)

Pay special attention to this "need."

The same Da'iye Sunnate Nabawiyyah states:

"Huzoor Ghausus Saqalain pays attention to the conditions of all his deputies. There is no murid of his upon whom he does not turn his attention." (*Malfoozat*)

Look at the tone of this statement, and also keep the title "Ghausus Saqalain" in mind — these are the very meanings, are they not? That he is the one whose arrival reaches all — humans and jinn alike.

And listen to what the same Nafs-e-Zakiyyah says:

"Similarly, Hazrat Khwaja Naqshband is always occupied with the conditions of his devoted ones. Shepherds and travelers in the jungle or at the time of sleep entrust their belongings and livestock — horses, etc. — to Hazrat Khwaja Naqshband, and unseen support comes to them." (*Malfoozat*)

Now the water of shirk has become overflowing from the head. Say with faith — upon your faith, how great a shirk is this! Upon which unseen help descends, and this matter is counted among the praises of Hazrat Khwaja <sup>قدس سرّه</sup>. May Allah protect you — at this moment, perhaps the hadees comes to your mind:

أعوذ بعظیم ھذا الوادی

(*In this hadees, there is mention of seeking refuge from the jinn*)

"And there were men from among men who sought refuge in men from among the jinn." (`al-Jinn: 6`)
