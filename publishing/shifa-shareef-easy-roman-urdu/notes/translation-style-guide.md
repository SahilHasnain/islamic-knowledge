# Easy Roman Urdu Style Guide

## Core Method

Rewrite the Roman Urdu source in the easiest natural Roman Urdu possible. Preserve every idea, example, quotation, reference, and paragraph. Do not summarize, omit, or add explanation.

## Arabic Preservation

- Preserve Quranic Arabic, Arabic duas, Arabic hadith quotations, Arabic honorifics, and `ﷺ` exactly as they appear in the source.
- Do not romanize Arabic text that is already written in Arabic script.
- Keep respectful forms for the Prophet ﷺ and the Ambiya, Sahaba, Ahl-e-Bait, and scholars.

## Simplification

- Prefer short sentences over long, difficult sentences.
- Replace difficult Urdu vocabulary with familiar Urdu where the meaning remains exact.
- Do not use words such as `iktifa`, `roop`, `asatiza`, or `musawwadat` without replacing them with easy wording or adding a clear bracket explanation.
- Preferred forms: `iktifa` -> `sirf itna karna`, `roop` -> `kitab` or `andaaz`, `asatiza` -> `teachers (ustaad)`, `musawwadat` -> `likhe hue papers (drafts)`.
- Preferred forms: `aib` -> `kharabi (aib)`, `mashghale` -> `kaam`.
- Preferred forms: `mushahida` -> `dekhne ka mauqa`, `takreem` -> `khaas izzat` or `adab`, and `mansookh` -> `pehli shariaton ki jagah nayi shariat jaari ki (mansookh kiya)`.
- Preferred form: `taeed` -> `khaas madad se mazboot kiya`.
- Preferred form: `farakhi` -> `aasani aur khushhaali` when the context refers to comfort and abundance in worldly life.
- Preferred spelling: use `unhone` instead of `unhon ne`, and `inhone` instead of `inhon ne`.
- Use the easy word first and put the difficult source word in brackets only when it helps the reader: `kharabi (aib)`.
- Replace difficult action words with clear everyday wording: `shaqq ho jaata` -> `phat jaata`, when the meaning remains accurate.
- For the source phrase `apni jaan par khel jaaoge`, preserve the clearer respectful wording instead of using `apni jaan khatam kar doge`.
- Prefer `marte dam tak` over `maut aane tak` when expressing the same source meaning.
- Preserve important source phrases even when they are not ordinary modern Urdu. For example, keep `haibate kalaam`, `sharaite muhabbat`, `lutf-o-karam`, and similar adab or theological expressions.
- Do not replace a source phrase merely because it sounds formal. First check whether it carries a specific religious, respectful, or technical meaning.
- Keep the original sentence's subject and point of view. Do not change `Aap par` into `mujh par`, or otherwise shift who is being described.
- When simplifying a quotation or explanation, compare it against the literal Roman Urdu source before approving the wording.
- Keep established Islamic terms when replacing them would reduce accuracy.
- Explain a difficult word briefly in parentheses only when the source already gives an explanation or the meaning would otherwise be unclear.
- Do not turn the work into English or modern casual speech.

## Adab

- Preserve ﷺ wherever it appears.
- Never use diminishing or irreverent wording for the Prophet ﷺ.
- Use respectful forms such as Huzoor ﷺ, Aap ﷺ, Nabi-e-Kareem ﷺ, and appropriate honorifics.

## References

Preserve Quran and hadith references exactly, including surah names, ayat numbers, and source names.

## Review Checks

- Compare every paragraph with `content/books/shifa-shareef.json`.
- Confirm no content was summarized or omitted.
- Confirm Arabic text and `ﷺ` were preserved.
- Confirm the prose is simpler without changing the meaning.
- Confirm no accidental English translation was introduced.
