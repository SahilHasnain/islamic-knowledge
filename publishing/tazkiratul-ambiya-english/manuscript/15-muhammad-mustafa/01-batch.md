### The First Creation — Noor-e-Muhammadi <sup>ﷺ</sup>

Abdurrazzaq <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> narrated with his chain from Hazrat Jabir bin Abdullah <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup>: "I said: 'O Messenger of Allah, may my parents be sacrificed for you — tell me what Allah created before all other things.' The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: 'O Jabir, indeed Allah created the light of your Prophet from His light before all other things. Then that light journeyed through Allah's power wherever He willed. At that time, there was no Tablet, no Pen, no Paradise, no Hell, no angels, no heavens, no earth, no sun, no moon, no jinn, and no human.'"

It should be noted that Maulvi Ashraf Ali Thanwi Deobandi has also narrated this hadith in his book.

It is narrated from Hazrat Arbaaz bin Saarya <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> that the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: "Indeed, I was the Seal of the Prophets in the sight of Allah when Adam was still in his clay."

Maulvi Ashraf Ali Thanwi Deobandi mentions in his book *Nasr Tayyib* that Ahmad, Baihaqi, and Hakim have also narrated this, and in Mishkat this hadith is recorded from Sharhus Sunnah.

Elaborating on this blessed hadith, Hazrat Allama Abul Hasnaat Muhammad Ashraf Siyalwi <sup>مد ظله</sup> states:

"This blessed hadith proves that the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> was indeed a prophet before Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, and that he was honored with the station of the Seal of the Prophets, and that his reality is light — because the father of humanity was being created later, while his reality already existed before, already established, and qualified with these perfect attributes."

At this point, let us also consider the removal of a doubt mentioned by Maulvi Ashraf Ali Thanwi:

"If someone has the misconception that perhaps the intended meaning is only that 'my being the Seal of the Prophets was decreed' — so his existence before Adam is not proven — the answer is: if this were the intended meaning, what distinction would there be? The pre-decreed destiny of all created things precedes their existence. Therefore, this very distinction itself proves that it is not the pre-determination that is meant, but rather the proof of this attribute itself. And obviously, the proof of an attribute is secondary to the proof of the one who possesses it. Hence, the precedence of his existence is established. And since the station of the body was not yet actualized, the station of light and spirit is determined."

This question and answer have made it clear that the Prophet's prophethood was not merely in Allah's knowledge — rather, externally and in reality, his most luminous light, his most holy spirit, and his Muhammadan reality were qualified with this perfection. And this is our view and belief: that in terms of human appearance, he is a child of Adam, but in terms of reality, he is the origin of all existent things, and Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and all the prophets have him as their foundation.

Thanwi Sahib, while answering another question, states: "The question is: when the prophets did not yet exist, how could their seal exist? How did he become the Seal of the Prophets when even the prophets, their parents, and their origins did not exist?"

Let us see Thanwi Sahib's question and answer in his own words:

"If someone doubts: what was the meaning of the proof of the seal of prophethood, indeed of prophethood itself, at that time — since prophethood was given to him at the age of forty, and because he was sent after all the prophets, this attribute itself necessitates delay? The answer is: this delay is in the time of manifestation, not in the station of prophethood. When someone is appointed to a post in a treasury, the salary begins from that day, but the actual appearance will be after he is sent to a particular treasury. That is, just as people learn that this person is their treasury officer when he goes to the treasury and takes charge — although he has been the treasury officer in the government's view since his appointment — similarly, the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> had been honored with the station of the Seal of the Prophets in Allah's sight when Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was still in the world of clay and water, although people came to know this when he appeared. In short, although his appearance came later, his existence was earlier. And this is our belief: that in terms of his light-reality, he is the origin of all existent things and the foundation of Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, although in terms of worldly appearance and development, he is a descendant of Adam."

Hazrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> narrates that the Companions asked: "O Messenger of Allah, when was prophethood established for you?" The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> replied: "When Adam was between spirit and body — when the soul had not yet entered his body — I was a prophet from that time."

Tirmizi narrated this and declared it *hasan*. Similar words are narrated by Maisara Zabi. Imam Ahmad narrated it in his Musnad, Imam Bukhari in his Tarikh, Abu Nuaim in Hilya, and Hakim authenticated it.

From the Companions' asking "Since when have you been a prophet?" — even though he was born in their homes and spent forty years without announcing prophethood — it becomes clear that their faith testified that the Noble Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>, although he announced his prophethood after forty years, had been a prophet before that. They did not ask: "When did you announce your prophethood?" They asked: "When was prophethood established for you?"

And the Prophet's reply — "I have been a prophet since the time when your father Adam's soul had not yet been breathed into his body" — is a seal of affirmation upon the Companions' understanding: "You have understood correctly. Indeed, I did not become a prophet after spending forty years. Rather, I was honored with this station when the father of humanity, Adam, had not yet received life."

This narration is recorded in Tirmizi Shareef, and Tirmizi Shareef is that book of hadith regarding which the scholars have said: "One in whose home this book is present should consider that the Prophet of Allah is present and residing in his home." Imam Tirmizi did not call this hadith fabricated or defective, nor did he declare it weak — rather, he called it *hasan*. In the principles of hadith, it is clear that a *hasan* hadith can serve as proof and evidence.

And then Ashraf Ali Thanwi has stated that similar words come from two Companions, so now a total of four Companions (Hazrat Jabir bin Abdullah Ansari, Hazrat Arbaaz bin Saarya, Hazrat Abu Huraira, and Hazrat Maisara Zabi <sup>رضي الله عنهم</sup>) have testified that the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is light and was honored with prophethood, messengership, and the station of the Seal of the Prophets before the creation and emergence of Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.

In addition, Imam Ahmad — who is the fourth Imam of Ahlus Sunnah after Imam Abu Hanifa, Imam Maalik, and Imam Shafi'i — narrated this in his Musnad. Then Imam Bukhari narrated it in his Tarikh, and Imam Bukhari's teacher Abu Nuaim narrated it in Hilya, and the hadith scholar Hakim authenticated it — the same Hakim who compiled authentic hadiths that Bukhari and Muslim did not include, naming his book *Mustadrak*.

### The Prophet's Light and Human Appearance

Qaazi Iyaaz <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> presents evidence on this matter: the Prophet's outward is human, and his inward is light. "Just as the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: 'My eyes sleep, but my heart does not sleep.' This blessed statement indicates that his inward is angelic (luminous like the angels) and his outward is human."

Qaazi Shahaab Khafaaji <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> states: "Similarly, the eyes of other prophets sleep while their hearts remain awake, as explicitly mentioned in a hadith in Bukhari. That is, all prophets possess this honor — their outward is human and their inward is light. However, the Prophet's <sup>ﷺ</sup> light is *noorum min noorillah* (a light from Allah's light) and serves as the origin and foundation for the entire universe — that is, his light is also the origin of the luminosity of all prophets."

The Prophet's blessed statement — "my eyes sleep, my heart remains awake" — is evidence that his outward is human and his inward is angelic and luminous. For this reason, the noble hadith scholars and great jurists have unanimously stated that his sleep does not break his wudu. No one from his Ummah — not the Companions, not the saints, not any individual — can be compared to the Prophet's sleep. If someone claims that a Companion or saint's sleep did not break his wudu, it would be his delusion, a false notion — such a person's statement should not be accepted.

It should be noted that Allama Shaami <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> has clarified that the sleep of all prophets does not break their wudu.

The Prophet's performing wudu after waking was not obligatory; rather, he would do it as a commendable act or to teach the Ummah, or because he knew that something contrary to wudu might have occurred during sleep, so he would sometimes perform wudu due to the breaking of wudu according to his knowledge.

### Proof of the Prophet's Luminosity from the Holy Quran

*"Indeed, there has come to you from Allah a light and a clear Book."*

Regarding this noble verse, three opinions are found:

1. The general exegetes — "light" means the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> and "clear Book" means the Holy Quran.
2. The Mu'tazila — both "light" and "clear Book" refer to the Quran.
3. The research scholars, such as Allama Aalusi and Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمة الله عليهما</sup> — both "light" and "clear Book" refer to the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>.

Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> states: "By 'light' is meant the great light which is the light of all lights — that is, the origin of all lights — and that is the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>."

This is also the statement of Qataada, and Zijaaj preferred this view. Abu Ali Jabaai said that "light" also means the Quran, because the Quran makes the paths of guidance and certainty apparent and discloses them — therefore it is called "light." Zamakhshari also restricted himself to this view. There was an apparent objection to this view: the conjunction particle (*atf*) indicates difference, but here both the conjoined and the conjoined-to would refer to the same thing — the Quran. However, the difference found is in the level of the names themselves, not in the essence.

The first view is more apparent: "light" means the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> and "clear Book" means the Quran.

Allama Taibi <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> says: "Taking 'light' to mean the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is more appropriate, because earlier *'qad ja'akum rasooluna'* (there has come to you Our Messenger) is mentioned, and then, without a conjunction particle, *'qad ja'akum min Allahi noorun'* (there has come to you from Allah a light) is mentioned. This indicates that both refer to the same being — hence no conjunction particle is used, as it would indicate difference."

Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> says: "In my view, there is no obstacle to both 'light' and 'clear Book' referring to the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>. If someone objects that the conjunction particle indicates difference — how can both the conjoined and the conjoined-to refer to one being? — then we give the same answer that Jabaai gave: the difference in the titles themselves can be considered as difference at that level, and the conjunction can be considered correct. There is no doubt that both 'light' and 'clear Book' are applied to the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>."

How both can be correctly applied to the Prophet is clarified by Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup>: "What prevents both 'light' and 'clear Book' from being attributes of the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>? There is no obstacle — because he is the great light, so his light is more apparent than all lights, and he is a 'clear Book' because he is a book encompassing all secrets, and he is *mubeen* (clear) because he makes all rulings, states, and reports manifest."

**Note:** From the above discussion, it becomes clear that Jabaai and Zamakhshari — who are chiefs of the Mu'tazila — took "light" to mean the Quran. The general scholars hold that "light" means the Prophet and "clear Book" means the Quran, while the research scholars take both to refer to the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>.

**Objection:** The first thing created was the Pen, not the Prophet's light — because the hadith "The first thing Allah created was the Pen" is authentic. The narration about the first creation of the Prophet's light is weak. How can a weak narration be accepted against an authentic one?

**Answer:** First of all, we do not accept the principle that a weak narration is absolutely rejected. The rule is: when certain and speculative evidence conflict, an attempt should be made to reconcile them. If reconciliation is achieved, that is better. Otherwise, the speculative evidence is set aside. 

The Quran says: *"So recite whatever is easy from the Quran."* This verse establishes the obligation of reciting the Quran in prayer in absolute terms (one may recite any surah). But there is a hadith whose apparent meaning is: "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite Surah Fatiha." The Quranic verse is certain and the hadith — being a single-narrator report — is speculative. However, the view that the hadith should be absolutely abandoned is false. Rather, the scholars have reconciled: the Quranic verse establishes the obligation of recitation in prayer, and the hadith establishes the obligation of reciting Surah Fatiha specifically. The correct translation of the hadith is: "There is no complete prayer for the one who does not recite Surah Fatiha" — the negation is of perfection, not of existence.

After this preamble, the summary of the answer is: the priority of the Pen's creation is relative, while the priority in the creation of the Prophet's light is absolute. This is the view of the scholars, research scholars, the pious, and those of complete faith. Yes, those whose faith is weak keep rejecting the hadiths that indicate the Prophet's dignity by declaring them weak.

When Allah places a seal upon hearts and puts the necklace of misguidance around their necks, who can guide them? Let us see the statements of the scholars:

The Chief of the Universe <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: "Allah had written the destinies of creation fifty thousand years before the creation of the heavens and the earth, when His Throne was upon water."

This hadith makes clear that the Throne existed earlier. Moreover, the Pen was created and commanded: "Write!" It asked: "What shall I write?" Allah said: "Write the destiny." So it wrote whatever had happened and whatever will happen until the Day of Judgment. This clearly shows that creation existed before the Pen — expressed as *maa kaan* (what has been). When priority here itself is relative, what does it mean to deny the absolute priority of the Prophet's most holy light on the basis of this hadith?

Allama Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> states: "It is apparent that the meaning of the Pen being the first creation is that after the Throne, water, and air, it is the first creation — because the Prophet's <sup>ﷺ</sup> blessed statement is: 'Allah had the destinies of creation written fifty thousand years before the creation of the heavens and the earth, and His Throne was upon water.'" 

Hazrat Abdullah bin Abbas <sup>رضي الله عنهما</sup> was asked: "Allah says: 'My Throne was upon water' — tell us, what was the water upon?" He replied: "Upon the back of the wind."

This makes clear that *awwalu ma khalaqallahul qalam* (the first thing Allah created was the Pen) is not an absolute priority but a relative one. In that case, how can this hadith prevent the absolute priority in the hadith of the light? This is the research of the leading scholars and those who have preceded.

Allama Ali Qaari <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> said: "The light of Muhammad is the first creation in an absolute sense, as I have researched in a treatise, while the priority of the Pen is relative."

Allama Ali Qaari further said: "Ibn Hajar <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> said: 'Which thing is the first creation? The narrations differ. But the way of reconciliation is what I mentioned in Shamail Tirmizi: the very first thing is the light from which the Prophet was created, then water, then the Throne.'"

Allama Qastalaani <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup>, discussing this issue of the Pen being the first creation, said: "The narrations about priority differ. The reconciliation and agreement among all of them is this: the Pen's being the first creation is relative to things other than the Muhammadan light, water, and the Throne. Another explanation given is that everything has priority within its own kind — that is, among lights, the Muhammadan light was created first; among pens, that pen which wrote the destinies; and among things called 'Throne,' the greatest Throne was created first."

**Note:** The hadith about the absolute priority of the Prophet's light has only been declared weak by those of weak faith. Imam Qastalaani, Allama Ibn Hajar Haitami, Mulla Ali Qaari, Allama Yusuf bin Ismail Nab'haani <sup>رحمة الله عليهم</sup> — none of them declared it weak.

As has been explained, the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is human in his outward and light in his inward. But the common people — who are unaware of the perfect meaning of "human" — should add other words along with "human" that indicate reverence. Explaining this issue, Sayyidul Auliya Hazrat Peer Mehar Ali Shaah <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> states:

"There is no doubt that for the people of faith, mentioning the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> with reverence and honor is obligatory and necessary. Now, we must see whether the word *bashar* (human), according to Arabic lexicology, contains greatness and perfection or lowliness. In my humble opinion, the word *bashar*, both in its concept and its application, encompasses complete perfection. However, since not everyone — except the people of research and gnosis — has access to this perfection, the application of the word *bashar* has a different ruling for the elite and the common. It is permissible for the elite but not permissible for the common without adding words of reverence."

Explanation: Why was Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> called *bashar*? The reason is that Adam was honored with the honor of direct contact (*mubaasharat*). *"What prevented you from prostrating to the one I created with My own hands?"* Since the angels were unaware of Adam's station — and similarly Iblees (he said what he said) — the difference is that the angels understood after being informed and admitted their fault, while Iblees, in addition to the sin of ignorance, also had arrogance. So Satan refused and became arrogant, becoming the embodiment of pride.

*Bashar* was made the manifestation of perfect divine manifestation, and the angels, due to their lack of manifestative capacity, were deprived of this perfection. The manifestations and mirrors of the perfections of divine manifestation are the group of prophets, and their leader is Abul Qasim <sup>ﷺ</sup>.

"It is upon the footprints of the Prophet of perfect perfection that I walk."

Sayyidna Abdul Qaadir Jilani and those like him <sup>رضي الله عنهم</sup> became the inheritors of the most perfect manifestation. It is for *bashar* that the final descent occurred, and for this reason such great care was taken that the collective form down to the physical appearance of the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> was brought into focus, and servants were created so that "whoever has seen me has seen Allah."

"If you wish to see Allah, look at my face — I am His mirror, He is not separate from me."

From this discourse it is established that for the gnostic, calling the Prophet "human" is a form of praise, unlike the non-gnostic — for him, uttering merely the word "human" without accompanying words is not permissible. Thus, in the noble verse, after *bashar* comes *yooha ilayya* (revelation comes to me), and in tashahhud, after *abduhoo* comes *wa rasooluhu*. The same is found in the speech of the people of gnosis: "Our limited knowledge is that indeed the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is human, and indeed he is better than all of Allah's creation."

**Summary of the discourse:** The scholars who know the meaning of *bashar* and the perfections contained in it can call him *bashar*. But the common people should mention additional words along with *bashar* so that they too may know of his greatness — for example, *sayyidul kainaat* (chief of the universes), *afdalul Ambiya* (most excellent of prophets), *habeebullah* (beloved of Allah), etc., should be added.

In reality, *bashar* possesses that perfection which even the angels have not attained, because Allah has made only *bashar* the manifestation of His essence, attributes, and names — the angels are deprived of this perfection. Then this attribute of perfection was given to all prophets and the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> without any intermediary, while the saints were given this perfection through his mediation. Some prophets were made the manifestation of some attributes and some noble names, but the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is the manifestation of all of Allah's attributes except eight.

When one understands that the great being of this stature is called *bashar*, and that in this perfection the Prophet has no equal, then one can understand that *bashar* is a great attribute of his. But the common person understands *bashar* to mean — Allah forbid — "he too was like us." To think this is itself to depart from religion. Such a person should be prevented from using the word *bashar* alone.

### Incomparability (*Imtenaye Nazeer*)

*Imtenaye nazeer* means that producing an equal to the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is beyond Allah's power. Things that are inherently impossible — if they are excluded from Allah's power — do not imply any deficiency in Allah's majesty and power, because such things are not capable of coming within Allah's power. Explaining this issue, Sayyidul Auliya Hazrat Allama Peer Sayyad Mehar Ali Shaah <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> states:

**Premises:**

1) The exclusion of inherent impossibilities from the power of the Holy Being does not stain His essential perfection. Rather, the deficiency lies in the recipient — the inherent impossibility is not capable of reception.

2) The transformation of existing realities — whether they be countable things (like human, cow, sheep, goat) or numerical grades (like first, second, third, fourth) or mixed — is such that a thing is inherently impossible in terms of its number. For example, Zaid, who is the first-born, is inherently impossible to be born after his siblings in terms of age.

3) The "likeness" (*nazeer*) of something is that which, in addition to sharing its type, equals it in its complete distinguishing qualities.

4) The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is the first creation — the explicit statements of the research scholars among the people of unveiling and witnessing testify to this. The Prophet's blessed statement: "The first thing Allah created was my light," or the statement "The first thing Allah created was the intellect" — both have the same meaning. Shaikh Akbar <sup>قدس سره</sup> has clarified that this light which was created first and is called the Muhammadan Reality is also called "intellect," and it is the beginning of the entire universe. Its existence precedes all worlds, and this light came into existence as the locus of manifestation of the Divine Light.

Just as the Prophet is qualified with the attribute of being first, he is also qualified with the attribute of being last — he is the Seal of the Prophets. Allah's command is: *"But he is the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets."*

The people of insight, upon deeply examining these premises, find it established that an equal to the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is inherently impossible in the sense that the Creator has made him such and has adorned him with such complete and distinguishing qualities that if an equal were to exist, a change in reality would follow. Because if an equal existed after him, it would inevitably be a limited being to which a secondary grade and number would accrue, and for it to be called "equal," it would have to share the distinguishing complete attribute of being the first creation and the Seal of Prophethood. But the secondary grade would accrue to the primary grade.

Similarly, in terms of finality, if we suppose him to be, say, at the sixth level, his equal would be at the seventh level and would thus be equal to the sixth. (This is contrary to reality.)

There is no doubt that a clear difference exists between the first two types and the third type of inherent impossibilities. In short, the Creator has shown unique perfection in the mirror of Ahmad — that is, He created him in such a way that an equal to him is impossible.

Creating the Prophet in this manner is in reality Allah's own perfection. Just as this dignity and beauty are exclusive to the Prophet, they are also from Allah's gift. Pure is the Being who created him with this unique dignity, made him the most beautiful, the most splendid, and the most perfect.

**Summary:** Allah brought the Prophet into existence as the first of all creation and made him the Seal of the Prophets. If an equal to him could be created, that equal too would possess these two attributes. But "first" is unique — if another is called "seal" after him, the first being's being "seal" would become false. Both situations are inherently impossible. Inherent impossibilities are excluded from Allah's power — they do not have the capacity to enter Allah's power, and no difference occurs in Allah's power.

### What Is the Muhammadan Reality?

Since it has been established that the Muhammadan reality is present and flowing in all existents of the universe, we must now see what the Muhammadan reality is. Let us consider the statement of Allama Jalaluddin Muhaqqiq Dawaani <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> in his famous book *Akhlaaqe Jalaali*:

"The research on this topic is that the people of insight and proof and the masters of witnessing and vision agree that through the power of the Peerless Being and His will, the first thing to emerge from the hidden ocean of the unseen onto the shore of manifestation, through the command of *'Kun fayakun'*, is that simple substance which is called the 'First Intellect' in philosophical terminology and the 'Supreme Pen' in some hadiths. The great leaders and the people of spiritual retreat and research have called this the Muhammadan Reality."

Sayyidul Auliya Peer Mehar Ali Shaah <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup>, discussing the matter of the Prophet's presence and observation, states:

"In my view, the manifestation and emergence of the Ahmad reality in every level and every particle is established according to the research scholars. They call it the 'Reality of Realities' and write: 'That is his light.'"

Firstly, the manifestation in the form of pure and holy physical body — the appearance of the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> in a symbolic noble form in every place and time is established by authentic hadiths. Acknowledging this is in reality acknowledging the Prophet, and denying it is considered denying him.

This refers to the hadith regarding the questioning by Munkar and Nakir — every deceased person is asked: "What do you say about this person — meaning Muhammad <sup>ﷺ</sup>?" Since the word *haaza* (this) is used in this hadith, which indicates the physically present and visible — and when several people are buried at the same time — it becomes certain that he is present in symbolic form in multiple places simultaneously. To believe this is faith, and to deny it is disbelief. The people of experience repeatedly encounter this symbolic manifestation. Certainly, finding the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is possible for some people of vision, and considering the event of Mi'raj and his special qualities and attributes, this is not far-fetched.

When Allah intended to bring creation into existence and decree its provision, He first manifested the Muhammadan reality. In *Zarqaani*, the explanation given of this statement is: "The meaning of the author of *Mawaahib*'s statement is that his being was manifested first with all attributes, as mentioned in the *Tauqeef*. And in *Lataaif Kashi*, it is mentioned that the research scholars take the Muhammadan reality to mean that reality which is called the 'Reality of Realities.' This reality is present and flowing in all realities just as a whole is present and flowing in its parts. It has been stated that the Muhammadan reality is the form of the Reality of Realities, which serves as a barzakh and intermediary between Allah and His creation. At that stage, his name and attribute 'Muhammad' were not dominant. This very intermediary — the Muhammadan reality — is the Ahmad light to which the Prophet's own blessed statement points: 'The first thing Allah created was my light.' For this reason, Mustafa <sup>ﷺ</sup> is called *Noorul Anwaar* (Light of Lights) and *Abul Arwaah* (Father of Spirits)."

In short: the Prophet's light was created first; all of creation was created from his light; all prophets, despite being original prophets in their own prophethoods, are his followers. The Muhammadan reality is present and flowing in the entire universe. The Muhammadan reality is called the First Intellect, the Supreme Pen, the Simple Substance, the Luminous, the Light of Lights — that is, the Spirit of Spirits. For more clarity, read my treatise *Aqeeda Haazir wa Naazir*.

### The Prophet Came Through Pure Lineages

Allama Imam Ibn Jauzi narrated in *Kitabul Wafa* from Ka'b bin Ahbaar <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup>: "When Allah intended to create Muhammad <sup>ﷺ</sup>, He commanded Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. He took a fistful of white soil from the place of the Prophet's blessed grave. It was kneaded with the water of the Tasneem stream of Paradise, then immersed in the rivers of Paradise and taken around the heavens. Thus the angels recognized Muhammad <sup>ﷺ</sup> before they recognized Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Then the Muhammadan light was placed on Adam's forehead, which made his forehead shine brightly. Adam was told: 'O Adam, this one will be from your descendants and will be the chief of all messengers.' 

"When Hawwa <sup>رضي الله عنها</sup> became pregnant with Shees, that light was transferred from Adam's forehead to Hawwa <sup>رضي الله عنها</sup>. Although Hawwa used to give birth to two children at a time, Shees was born alone — to preserve the Muhammadan light from being divided. In this was the honor of the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>. Then his light continued to be transferred from pure being to pure being until his mother Aamina <sup>رضي الله عنها</sup> gave birth to him from Abdullah bin Abdul Muttalib."

It should be noted that "coming through pure lineages" means that his ancestors were pure from fornication and disbelief. To restrict purity only to being free from fornication is false — the greater objective is purity from disbelief. The details have been explained in the section on Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.

### The Prophet's Birth in This World

The majority of scholars hold that the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> came into this world in the year that Abraha mounted elephants to destroy the Kaaba and was destroyed himself. That year is called *Aamul Feel* (the Year of the Elephant). He was born fifty days after this event. It was the month of Rabiul Awwal, the 12th day, a Thursday, at the time of true dawn.

### The Significance of Thursday for the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>

His birth was on Thursday; prophethood was granted on Thursday; he migrated from Makkah to Madina on Thursday; he arrived in Madina on Thursday; to eliminate the discord of Quraish, he placed the Black Stone in his blessed cloak and commanded the chiefs of all tribes to lift it — and he himself placed the Black Stone in the wall of the Kaaba — this too was Thursday; the conquest of Makkah was on Thursday; Surah Maa'idah was revealed on Thursday.

### The Prophet's Light Continued to Have Effect Even After Being Transferred

Fifty days before the Prophet's birth, the incident of Abraha occurred. At that time, the light had already been transferred from Abdul Muttalib to the mother of Abdullah, then to Abdullah, and then to Aamina. Yet its effects were still present on Abdul Muttalib's forehead.

Hazrat Abdul Muttalib, mounted and accompanied by a group of Quraish, climbed a mountain to assess the condition of Abraha's army. The Prophet's light appeared on his forehead like a full moon, and its rays fell upon the Kaaba like a lamp. When Abdul Muttalib saw this, he said: "O group of Quraish, turn back — this is sufficient for you. By the oath of Allah, this light that has shone from my forehead is proof of our success."

### The Light Illuminated the Towns of Syria

Hazrat Arbaaz bin Saarya <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> narrates that the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: "I was written as the Seal of the Prophets in the sight of Allah when Adam was still in clay — when his dough was being prepared. I inform you of my earliest matter: I am the supplication of Ibrahim, the glad tidings of Eisa, and the dream of my mother that she saw when I was presented before her — a light emerged from her that illuminated the towns of Syria."

The supplication of Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> refers to what he prayed after building the Kaaba: *"Our Lord, raise up among them a messenger from among themselves."* The glad tidings of Eisa refer to what he said: *"He gave glad tidings of a messenger who will come after me, whose name will be Ahmad."*

Regarding the dream (*rooya*) — whether it means a dream in sleep or sight with open eyes — Shaikh Abdul Haq Muhaddis Dehalwi <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> writes: "Outwardly, it seems that seeing the light that illuminated the towns of Syria was an event of a dream. However, in the hadiths, this same event is also mentioned as occurring while awake." There is no contradiction — during pregnancy, such states often occur in dreams. But the meaning of the hadith is not affected by this.

The incident of the splitting of Aamina's cloak and the naming of the child "Muhammad" — these are well-known matters.

### The Prophet's Lineage

His paternal lineage: Muhammad bin Abdullah bin Abdul Muttalib bin Hashim bin Abd Manaf bin Qusay bin Kilab bin Murra bin Ka'ab bin Lu'ay bin Ghalib bin Fihr bin Maalik bin Nazr bin Kinana bin Khuzaima bin Mudrika bin Ilyaas bin Muzar bin Nizaar bin Ma'add bin Adnan.

From Adnan to Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — the exact number of generations is not confirmed. The authentic view is eighteen generations. The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: "When I reached the lineage of Adnan, stop."

### Maternal Lineage

His mother is Aamina bint Wahb bin Abd Manaf bin Zuhra bin Kilab. Her lineage joins the Prophet's paternal lineage at Kilab bin Murra. She was from the noblest family of Quraish in lineage and the most honorable in status.

### The Prophet Was an Only Child

The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> was an only child — he had no siblings. His paternal uncles and aunts were from Abdul Muttalib's other children. The wisdom behind this was that the light of prophethood was not meant to be divided among siblings.

### The Graves of His Parents in One Place

The grave of Hazrat Aamina is in Abwa (a place between Makkah and Madina), and the grave of Hazrat Abdullah is in Makkah or Madina. Some narrations mention that both graves are at the same place. However, the correct view is that they are not in one place.

### The Prophet's Paternal Uncles

The Prophet had many paternal uncles. According to one statement, there were twelve: Harith, Qutham, Mughira, Zubayr, Abu Taalib, Hamza, Abbas, Abu Lahab, Ghaidaq, Maqwam, Safar, and Abdullah (who is the same as Abdul Kaaba). According to Ibn Abbas, Harith was the eldest, then Qutham, then Zubayr, then Abdullah, then Abu Taalib, then Abbas, then Hamza, then Ghaidaq, then Abu Lahab.

### Only Two Uncles Accepted Islam

Only Hamza and Abbas <sup>رضي الله عنهما</sup> accepted Islam. Hamza <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> was martyred in the Battle of Uhud. Abbas <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> accepted Islam before the conquest of Makkah. The others died in disbelief — among them Abu Taalib, who supported the Prophet greatly but did not accept faith. He was the Prophet's protector from the Quraish until his death. 
Hazrat Hamza was a foster brother of the Prophet as well. 
Hazrat Abbas is the one whose descendants continue as the Abbasid caliphs.

### The Prophet's Paternal Aunts

His paternal aunts were: Safiyya (the mother of Zubayr <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup>), Umm Hakeem, Baiza, Barra, Aatika, Arwa, Umaima, and Umm Habib Dara. 
Among them, Safiyya and Aatika <sup>رضي الله عنهما</sup> accepted Islam. The others remained in disbelief.

### The Prophet's Grandmothers (Paternal)

His paternal grandmother was Fatima bint Amr bin Aa'iz bin Imraan bin Makhzoom. She was a woman of noble lineage, and the Prophet would honor her greatly.
Another grandmother (Abdul Muttalib's mother) was Salma bint Amr bin Zaid from the Khazraj tribe of Madina.

### The Prophet's Grandmothers (Maternal)

His maternal grandmother was Barra bint Abdul Uzza bin Uthman bin Abdudar bin Qusay.

### Foster Mothers

Thuwayba — the freed slave of Abu Lahab — nursed the Prophet for a few days. She had also nursed Hazrat Hamza and Hazrat Abu Sufyan bin Harith, making them the Prophet's foster brothers.

Then Halima Sa'diyya <sup>رضي الله عنها</sup> took him into her care. She was from the tribe of Bani Sa'd. The well-known incident of the splitting of his chest occurred while he was with her. He stayed with her for about four to five years.

### Foster Siblings

The Prophet's foster siblings were:
1. Hazrat Hamza (also his uncle)
2. Abu Sufyan bin Harith
3. Abdullah bin Harith
4. Anisa bint Harith
5. Hudhaafa bint Harith (also known as Shayma)
These were the children of Halima Sa'diyya.
6. From Thuwayba: Hazrat Hamza (again) and Abu Sufyan bin Harith.

### The Prophet's Pure Wives (Ummahat al-Mu'mineen)

The Prophet's wives <sup>رضي الله عنهن</sup> are:

1. **Khadija bint Khuwaylid** — the first and most beloved. She was the first to believe. All the Prophet's children except Ibrahim were from her. She died three years before Hijrah.
2. **Sawda bint Zam'a** — the Prophet married her after Khadija's death.
3. **Aayesha bint Abi Bakr** — the daughter of Siddiq Akbar. She was the most knowledgeable of women in Fiqh and hadith.
4. **Hafsa bint Umar** — daughter of Farooq Azam.
5. **Zaynab bint Khuzayma** — known as "Umm al-Masaakeen" (Mother of the Poor).
6. **Umm Salama** — Hind bint Abi Umayya.
7. **Zaynab bint Jahsh** — the Prophet's cousin.
8. **Juwayriyya bint Harith** — from the tribe of Bani Mustaliq.
9. **Umm Habiba** — Ramla bint Abi Sufyan.
10. **Safiyya bint Huyayy** — from the tribe of Bani Nadeer, from the lineage of Haroon <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.
11. **Maymuna bint Harith**.
12. **Maria al-Qibtiyya** — gifted to the Prophet by the ruler of Egypt. She was the mother of Ibrahim.

### The Prophet's Pure Children

All the Prophet's children were from Khadija <sup>رضي الله عنها</sup> except Ibrahim, who was from Maria al-Qibtiyya. His children were:

1. **Qasim** — the eldest, after whom the Prophet was named Abul Qasim.
2. **Zaynab** — married to Abul Aas bin Rabi'.
3. **Ruqayya** — married to Uthman <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup>.
4. **Umm Kulthum** — also married to Uthman after Ruqayya's death.
5. **Fatima** — the leader of the women of Paradise, married to Ali <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup>. Through her, the Prophet's lineage continues.
6. **Abdullah** — also called Tayyib and Tahir.
7. **Ibrahim** — born in Madina, died in infancy.

All the Prophet's sons died in infancy. His only surviving child was Fatima <sup>رضي الله عنها</sup>.
