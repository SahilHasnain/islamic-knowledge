## Hazrat Muhammad Mustafa ﷺ — Virtues and Excellence

### Superior to All Prophets

Allah says: *"These messengers — We favored some over others."* In the commentary of this noble verse, Allama Raazi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> states that the Ummah is unanimous that some prophets are superior to others, and Muhammad ﷺ is the most superior of all. His superiority is established through many proofs:

**1.** Allah says concerning him: *"And We did not send you but as a mercy to all the worlds."* Since he is a mercy to all the worlds, he is necessarily superior to all the worlds — that is, he is the most excellent of all creation. After Allah, his station is the highest and most exalted. This is the gist of the matter.

**2.** The Lord said: *"And We exalted for you your remembrance."* The Master of the kingdom joined the remembrance of the Noble Prophet ﷺ with His own remembrance — in the kalimah of shahadat, in the adhan, in the tashahhud, and elsewhere. The remembrance of all other prophets is not joined in this manner. From this it is proven that he is the most superior of the prophets.

**3.** Allah joined obedience to him with obedience to Himself and said: *"Whoever obeys the Messenger has indeed obeyed Allah."* Your bay'ah the Lord made as His own bay'ah: *"Indeed those who pledge allegiance to you are only pledging allegiance to Allah; the hand of Allah is above their hands."* And your honor He mentioned alongside His own honor: *"And honor belongs to Allah and His Messenger and the believers."* And your pleasure He mentioned alongside His own pleasure: *"And it is more fitting that they should please Allah and His Messenger."* The Lord mentioned responding to your call alongside responding to His call: *"O you who believe, respond to Allah and His Messenger when he calls you."* This greatness is granted only to the Noble Prophet ﷺ, not to other prophets.

**4.** Allah commanded you to challenge with a single short surah of the Holy Quran — who can bring a surah like it? *"Then bring a surah like it."* The shortest surah is Surah al-Kausar, which has three verses. So through you, Allah challenged with every three verses, but they were unable to meet the challenge. Since the Holy Quran contains thousands of verses, every three verses are a miracle, so the Quran alone contains thousands of miracles, besides other miracles. When Moosa <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> was given nine miracles, it is clear that the Noble Prophet ﷺ possesses superiority over all prophets due to the abundance of miracles.

**5.** The miracle of the Noble Prophet ﷺ — that is, the Holy Quran — is superior to the miracles of all prophets. Therefore, our Noble Prophet's superiority over all prophets is established. The Holy Quran has precedence over all speech, just as Adam <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> has precedence over all humans. And this is clear: when the garment is excellent, the wearer acquires honor. So why should it not be that when the miracle is excellent, the possessor of the miracle is also excellent?

**6.** The miracles of all other prophets were temporary. When the prophets left this world, their miracles ended with them. But the miracle of the Prophet ﷺ — the Holy Quran — remains forever. It is certain that what remains is superior to what perishes. Therefore, the one who received a lasting miracle must be considered of elevated rank.

**7.** The individual excellences that were granted separately to various prophets were all gathered together in the Noble Prophet ﷺ. Therefore, he is superior to all prophets. After mentioning the conditions of the prophets, Allah said: *"These are those whom Allah guided, so follow their guidance."* In this verse, the Noble Prophet ﷺ is commanded to follow the earlier prophets. Now we must consider what this means. If it is said that he was commanded to follow the earlier prophets in matters of fundamental belief (usool), this is not correct because that is taqleed, and there is no taqleed in fundamentals. If it is said that he was commanded to follow them in branches of religion (furoo), this is also not correct because his shari'ah abrogates the earlier shari'ahs. There is no other meaning except that what is intended is good character and excellences — as if the Lord said: "We acquaint you with the conditions and habits of the prophets; adopt their good and beautiful character and habits, and follow them in these habits." From this discussion it becomes clear that all the good habits that were given separately to various prophets were gathered together in him. Therefore, he is superior to all prophets.

**8.** The Noble Prophet ﷺ was sent as a messenger to all of creation. The Lord said: *"And O beloved, We did not send you but as a bringer of glad tidings and a warner to all people."* The more followers one has, the greater the hardship on the prophet. In righteous deeds, the more hardship one endures, the higher the ranks. Especially when one has no wealth, no friends or helpers, and then says to all people "O disbelievers" — upon hearing this, people become enemies. What a fearful station this is, which is a cause of great hardship. And consider: when Moosa <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> was sent with prophethood, his enemies were only Firaun and his people; Bani Israel were with him. But all of the Noble Prophet's ﷺ people were initially opposed to him. This is why Allah gave him superiority over all prophets.

And note that the Noble Prophet ﷺ was commanded to convey Allah's commands to humans and jinn day and night throughout his entire life, especially in conditions where it was clear that they would become his enemies, cause him suffering, and consider him insignificant. Despite these circumstances, the Noble Prophet ﷺ did not delay in obeying Allah's command. He promptly conveyed Allah's commands and endured tremendous hardships. Enduring great hardship is a cause of superiority, because Allah declared the superiority of those companions who believed before the conquest of Makkah over those who came later. The reason is that they endured hardships, and for this reason they are superior. The command of Allah is: *"Not equal among you are those who spent and fought before the conquest of Makkah."* When the companions who endured more hardships are more superior than others, then certainly that Prophet who bore more hardships than all other prophets is the possessor of greater excellence than all prophets.

**9.** The religion of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is superior to all religions, so his superiority over all prophets is necessary. Since Allah made his religion abrogate all other religions, the religion that abrogates others is superior. The superiority of his religion is also proven from his saying: "Whoever introduces a good practice in Islam, he will have its reward and the reward of all who act upon it until the Day of Judgment." Since there is more reward in his religion, and especially one who introduces a good practice receives great reward — which is not found in other religions — it follows that Muhammad ﷺ has superiority over all prophets.

**10.** The Ummah of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is superior to all nations, so he too must have superiority over all prophets. The Lord expressed the honor of his Ummah in these blessed words: *"You are the best nation brought forth for mankind."* Why is his Ummah superior? Because they follow the Noble Prophet ﷺ, and it is due to their following him that they are superior to other nations and beloved to Allah. The Lord said: *"Say: If you love Allah, then follow me, Allah will love you."* When his Ummah gained superiority and Allah's love and friendship due to following him, then his own superiority over all prophets is proven.

Furthermore, the Noble Prophet ﷺ came as a prophet to all jinn and humans, so he received greater reward. The elevation of a person's ranks depends on the abundance of reward. The more people who accept his call, the higher his ranks. This honor is not granted to other prophets.

**Benefit:** Hadhrat Ibn Mas'ood <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "The person closest to me on the Day of Judgment among people will be the one who sends the most durood upon me." In the commentary of this hadith, Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> states that "Ula" (أولى) means closest. One meaning is that the one who sends more durood upon you will have a station closer to you in Paradise than others. Another meaning is that such a person will be more deserving of your special intercession. Only the one in whose heart your reverence is found will send abundant durood upon you. And the one who has a sense of your greatness will also follow you. One follows you perfectly only when he has complete love. The one who has complete love for the Noble Prophet ﷺ is, in reality, the beloved of Allah.

Allah said: *"Say: If you love Allah, then follow me, Allah will love you and forgive your sins."* SubhanAllah! How clear the result is: without love for the Messenger of Allah, love for Allah is not possible, nor will Allah love him. Merely calling oneself a monotheist by name means nothing. If you want your end to be good, become a slave of the Leader of the Prophets. Yet the one who says "the Prophet did not know the unseen, he did not know what was behind the wall, his rank was like that of our elder brother, he did not possess any authority, he was a human like us" — uttering such frivolous nonsense from his tongue, and then claims "we also love the Prophet" — who will believe such a claim?

'Today seek his refuge, today seek help from him,
Tomorrow in Qiyamah you will not be helped if you are not helped today.'

**11.** The Noble Prophet ﷺ is Khatam an-Nabiyyin (the Seal of the Prophets). Since the chain of prophethood ended with him — that is, the coming of prophets is abrogated by his advent — it is a certain thing that only the most excellent one can abrogate the coming of others. It is against reason that one of lower rank should abrogate the coming of one of higher rank.

**12.** Some prophets have superiority over others due to their miracles. Abundance of miracles indicates their truthfulness and greatness. Since the Noble Prophet ﷺ has more miracles than all prophets, he has greater excellence than all. As stated earlier, even one external miracle — the Holy Quran — contains over 3,000 miracles. Moreover, he was granted miracles that indicate his power, such as feeding a large multitude with little food and quenching many with little water. And some miracles relate to his knowledge, such as reports of the unseen. Kaash! If only the dear ignorant ones of my community would also understand that giving news of the unseen is a miracle of the Prophet ﷺ. Even the disbelievers could not deny his miracles.

Some of his miracles relate to his person: among all Arab nobles, he was the highest in lineage and nobility. In courage, noble character, forbearance, faithfulness to promises, eloquence and rhetoric, and generosity — in all these qualities, the Noble Prophet ﷺ had no equal.

**13.** The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "Adam <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> and all others will be under my banner on the Day of Judgment." From this it is clear that he has superiority over Adam and his children. The Prophet ﷺ said: "I am the leader of the children of Adam, and I say this without pride." And another noble saying of the Prophet: "Not one of the prophets will enter Paradise until I enter, and not one of the nations will enter until my Ummah enters."

**14.** Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "People will be raised on the Day of Judgment. I will be the first to come out of the grave. When everyone comes, I will be the first to address them. When people become hopeless, I will give them glad tidings. The Liwa al-Hamd (the banner of praise) will be in my hand. I will be the most honored among all of Adam's children in the sight of my Lord, and I say this without pride."

Hadhrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہما</sup> narrates that some companions were sitting and discussing. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ heard their conversation. Some said with wonder: "Indeed Allah made Ibrahim <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> Khaleel." Others said: "Moosa <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> is even more wondrous — Allah made him Kaleem." Some said: "Eisa <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> is Kalimatullah and Roohullah." Others said: "Adam is Safiullah." At that moment, the Noble Prophet ﷺ arrived and said: "I heard your words and your arguments. Indeed it is true that Ibrahim is Khaleelullah, Moosa is Kalimullah, Eisa is Roohullah, Adam is Safiullah. But beware! I am the Habib of Allah, and I say this without pride. On the Day of Judgment, I will be the one to raise the Liwa al-Hamd — I say this without pride. On the Day of Judgment, I will be the first to intercede and the first whose intercession will be accepted — I say this without pride. On the Day of Judgment, I will be the first to knock at the door of Paradise, the door will be opened for me, I will enter, and with me will be the poor Muslims — I say this without pride. I will be more honored than all the earlier and later people — I say this without pride." All these ahadith that have been mentioned point to the superiority of the Noble Prophet ﷺ like the bright day.

**15.** Baihaqi, in his discussion of the virtues of the companions, mentions that once Hadhrat Ali <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> was seen from a distance. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "This person is the leader of the poor." Hadhrat Aayesha <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> asked: "Are you not the leader of the poor?" He said: "I am the leader of all the worlds; he is the leader of the poor." From this it is clear that "all the worlds" includes the prophets. Therefore, he possesses leadership, excellence, and superiority over the prophets.

**16.** Muhammad bin Eisa Hakeem Tirmizi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> illustrated the superiority of the Noble Prophet ﷺ through an example: Every ruler must bear hardship according to the number of his subjects. If a person is the ruler of a town, he must bear hardship according to the residents of that town and consider their needs. If a person is made ruler of the entire earth, east to west, he will need more wealth and resources than the ruler of a single town or region because he must fulfill the needs of far more people and manage their affairs.

Similarly, if a messenger is sent to one nation, he is given treasures of tawheed and gems of ma'rifat according to the measure of his message — that is, the number of his followers. If a messenger is sent to a region, he is given according to his followers. But if a person is made the messenger of all the people of the east and west and all jinn and humans, then certainly, considering the breadth of his message, he is given more extensive spiritual treasures — gems of ma'rifat and treasures of tawheed — so that it may match the number of followers.

All prophets were not granted as extensive prophethood as the Noble Prophet ﷺ was granted, because every prophet was made a prophet for one nation or region. But the beloved Prophet was made a prophet for the entire universe. When it is established that the prophethood of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is more extensive, then it also becomes clear that he was granted treasures of wisdom and knowledge that were not granted to anyone before him.

Allah said: *"He revealed to His servant what He revealed."* Allama Ismail Haqqi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup>, the author of Ruh al-Bayaan, states that Hadhrat Jafar Saadiq <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> said: Allah revealed to His servant what He revealed — this revelation was without any intermediary because there was no intermediary between Allah and His Beloved. This refers to that revelation which was a secret between Allah and His Messenger. No one else was informed of these secrets. Allah kept this secret hidden from all creation. No one was told what the revelation was, because these were the secrets of the Lover and the Beloved. The Lover and Beloved do not inform others of the secrets between them — that is, these were secrets between Allah and His Beloved, upon which even the noble recording angels were not informed.

In eloquence too, the Noble Prophet ﷺ had no equal. Mentioning his special gifts, the Prophet said: "I have been given Jawami' al-Kalim" — that is, concise speech that encompasses many meanings. This is called Jawami' al-Kalim, and it is a special gift of the Noble Prophet ﷺ that was not given to anyone else. The Book (the Noble Quran) that was given to the Prophet is superior to all books, and his Ummah is superior to all nations. Considering all these reasons, the superiority of the Prophet ﷺ over all prophets is manifest and clear.

**17.** Muhammad bin Hakeem Tirmizi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> mentions in Kitab al-Nawadir that Hadhrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates from the Noble Prophet ﷺ that he said: "Indeed Allah made Ibrahim <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> Khaleel and Moosa <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> Kaleem, and He made me His Habib and Najiyy. Then the Lord said: 'I swear by My honor and My majesty, I am giving My Habib preference over My Khaleel and My Najiyy.'" When the Lord took an oath and declared the superiority of His Habib over His Khaleel and Najiyy, it is no longer difficult to understand that he is the most superior of the prophets.

**18.** Bukhari and Muslim record from Amaama bin Munabbih <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup>, who narrates from Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "My example and the example of the prophets before me is like a man who built a house, very beautiful and perfect, but left the space of one brick in a corner. People began to walk around it, marveling at its construction, saying: 'It is so beautiful and perfect — why did you not put this brick so the house would be complete?'" The Prophet ﷺ said: "That brick is me." That is, Allah built the palace of prophethood, splendid and beautiful, but left the place for one prophet to come. People were waiting for the Seal of the Prophets to come so that the remaining place in the palace of prophethood would be completed. Thus the palace of prophethood was completed when I came. Now it is clear that the person without whom the palace of prophethood was incomplete, and who came and completed it — that person is the most superior. That is Muhammad Mustafa ﷺ, who is Sayyid al-Anbiya.

**19.** Among the prophets, when Allah addressed some, He called them by their personal names, such as: "O Adam, stay!" "We called him: O Ibrahim!" "O Moosa, indeed I am your Lord!" But our Noble Prophet ﷺ was never addressed by his personal name — that is, "Muhammad" was not said. Rather, he was addressed by attributive names such as "O Prophet" and "O Messenger," which are clear proofs of his superiority.

It should be noted that this humble writer, without any Shar'i necessity, is not in favor of "Ya Muhammad" because the respect found in "Ya RasoolAllah," "Ya HabibAllah," and similar blessed phrases is not present in it. Therefore, instead of "Ya Allah Ya Muhammad," one should use "Ya Allah Ya RasoolAllah."

**20.** According to this humble writer, the twentieth reason for the superiority of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is the type of companions he was given: Abu Bakr Siddiq, Umar Farooq, Usman, Ali Murtada <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہم</sup>. Such companions were not given to any other prophet. All his companions were ready to sacrifice their lives for him — such was not the case with any other prophet. In this respect too, he is superior to all prophets.

### The Noble Prophet Distributes the Treasures of Allah

Hadhrat Muawiya <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "When Allah intends good for a person, He grants him understanding of the religion. I am the distributor, and Allah is the Giver." Shaikh Abdul Haq Muhaddis Dehalwi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> writes in the commentary of this hadith in Ashat al-Lam'aat: "I am nothing but a distributor, and Allah gives. To whomever Allah gives, He gives what He wills — whether it is knowledge of fiqh and understanding of religious sciences, or anything else. Whatever the Lord gives, I distribute."

### The Noble Prophet and All Prophets are Protected from the Narrowness of the Grave

No one is protected from the ordinary squeezing of the grave, no matter how pious and God-fearing, except for the prophets. That is, Allah has granted this distinctive honor to the prophets that they are protected from the narrowness of the grave.

### Faith is Impossible Without Love for the Noble Prophet

Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "None of you can be a believer until I am more beloved to him than his parents, his children, and all people." In the hadith, "father" is mentioned, but what is meant is "possessor of children" — this meaning includes both mother and father. "Child" means son or daughter — that is, all children. The meaning is: one should consider me more beloved than parents, children, and all people.

By love, natural love is not meant — the love that is acquired without choice — because Allah said: "Allah does not burden any soul beyond its capacity." What is meant is intellectual love, which a person chooses through his intellect, even if it is against his nature. Just as a patient prefers bitter medicine even though his nature does not desire it, his intellect chooses to use the bitter medicine.

From this it is clear that if the Prophet ﷺ commands a person to kill his disbelieving parents or disbelieving children, it becomes obligatory for him to give precedence to the Prophet ﷺ, even if his nature does not desire it. Similarly, when he said that one who is killed fighting against the disbelievers is a martyr, the intellect will prefer this even if nature does not desire it. Loving the Prophet means having faith-based love, and this love is acquired through reverence, honor, gratitude, and mercy. The requirement of this love is that one gives precedence to the beloved's objectives over all others — whether they be close relatives or one's own self — and considers the beloved superior to everyone.

All the qualities of belovedness are found in the Noble Prophet ﷺ: beautiful character, beautiful appearance, perfection of virtue, and perfection of kindness. Especially when he is also the beloved of the Master of the Kingdom, why would a person not consider him beloved?

Among those who acquired high-level love was Hadhrat Umar Farooq <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup>. When he heard this hadith, according to the requirement of his nature, he said: "O Messenger of Allah, you are more beloved to me than everything except my own life." The Prophet ﷺ said: "Faith is not complete. By the One in whose hand is my life, (you will not believe) until I am more beloved to you than your own life." Hadhrat Umar said: "O beloved, by Allah, now you are more beloved to me than my own life." The Prophet ﷺ said: "O Umar, now your faith is complete."

Why did Hadhrat Umar say initially that he loved his own life more? There are two possibilities. One is that he understood according to the Prophet's statement that the love being referred to is natural love, and natural love for one's own life is greater. Later he understood that what is meant by this love is faith-based and intellectual love, which is opposite to natural love. Faith and intellect require giving preference to the Prophet over natural demands. After understanding this, he stated that now the Prophet is more beloved to him than his own life.

The second possibility is that Allah granted Hadhrat Umar such a high station of faith through the blessed attention of the Noble Prophet ﷺ that the love of the Prophet became ingrained in his heart — to the extent that the Prophet's love became his life and intellect — that is, it became part of his life and intellect.

Allama Qurtubi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: The one whose faith is perfect will give precedence to the love of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, even if he is immersed in abundant desires or veils of heedlessness cover him most of the time.

We often see that those who possess love for the Messenger of Allah, even during his apparent lifetime, preferred seeing him over their children. Even when their own life was in great danger of destruction, the intoxicated one of love for the Messenger of Allah would say: "If only once in my life I could be blessed with the visitation of the Prophet's ﷺ holy rawdah, then death may come whenever it comes, for the purpose of life has been achieved." This station is attained only after knowing the worth and status of the Noble Prophet ﷺ.

There is no doubt that this station was granted more to the companions <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہم</sup>. They had the highest level of faith because the highest standard of love for him is attained after knowing his worth and status, and this honor was possessed most by the companions — because they knew best the ranks and status of the Prophet ﷺ.

Allama Nawawi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: The one who gives precedence to the side of the tranquil soul (nafs mutma'innah) — that is, does not give precedence to carnal desires over the religion of Mustafa ﷺ — has greater love for the Prophet. The one who gives precedence to the side of the commanding soul (nafs ammara) — that is, prefers his carnal desires — his love is deficient. The blaming soul (nafs lawwama) is between these two. Note that committing evils and boasting about them is the work of nafs ammara. If one commits an evil by mistake and then regrets it and blames himself, that is nafs ammara (actually nafs lawwama). And refraining from evil is nafs mutma'innah.

### The Noble Prophet is Protected from Satan

Hadhrat Ibn Mas'ood <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There is not a single one of you except that he has a jinn companion (qareen) appointed over him and an angel companion appointed over him." The companions asked: "Even with you, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: "Yes, even with me, but Allah has helped me against him, so he has become obedient to me — he commands me only to do good."

In the hadith, the word "فأسلم" is used. This is the form of the past tense third person masculine singular. Its apparent meaning is that my companion has accepted Islam. Allama Toorbashi took this meaning: Allah is Powerful, so it is possible that Allah, considering the special qualities and excellence of the Prophet, blessed his qareen shaytan with the wealth of Islam. However, other scholars interpreted it as "he has become obedient to me." Some have said it is the form of the first person passive, in which case the meaning would be "I have been protected from him." In Jaami' Tirmizi, it is mentioned that Ibn Einiya said: "فأسلم" has a dammah on the hamzah, meaning "I am protected from him," because a shaytan does not become Muslim.

Note that some have taken it as the form of the ism tafzeel (comparative) with an omitted mubtada. According to them, the original phrase is "I am more protected from Satan than you." But according to this meaning, the Prophet's absolute protection from Satan is not proven; rather, only greater protection relative to others is proven. This meaning is not correct, which is why Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> rejected it. He said: To claim that the Prophet sometimes experienced whisperings from Satan is wrong, because even if he ever experienced any whisper, it was only due to his own self — that is, due to lack of attention — Satan had no involvement in it. The first meaning is more well-known, and I have used it in translation.

### The Prophet's Eyes Sleep, His Heart Remains Awake

The Prophet ﷺ said: "My eyes sleep, and my ears hear, and my heart understands." From this hadith it is clear that just as sleep has no effect on the hearts of the prophets, similarly it has no effect on their ears. The reason is that the sleep of the prophets relates only to their apparent bodies. In other people, sleep affects both the apparent and the inner — that is, their eyes, hearts, and ears are all affected.

### The Prophet Was Born Circumcised

Narrations mention that Adam, Shees, Nooh, Hood, Saaleh, Looth, Shoaib, Yusuf, Moosa, Sulemaan, Zakariya, Eisa <sup>علیہم السلام</sup>, Hadhrat Hanzala bin Safwaan <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> (who was the prophet of the People of Rass), and Hadhrat Muhammad ﷺ were born circumcised.

### The Noble Prophet Will Be Given the Strength of Four Thousand Men

Tirmizi narrated a hadith, which he called Sahih Gharib: "In Paradise, every person will be given the strength of one hundred men, and the Prophet ﷺ will be given the strength of four thousand men." The Prophet's food was very small in quantity, yet he had the strength of many men in this world and will have the strength of many in the Hereafter. This is, in reality, a miracle that breaks the norm, because the condition of ordinary people is that one who eats little has little strength.

### Healing in the Water that Dripped from His Wudu

Hadhrat Saa'ib bin Yazeed <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that his aunt took him to the Noble Prophet ﷺ and said: "O Messenger of Allah, indeed my sister's son is in pain." The Prophet ﷺ passed his hand over my head and made a prayer of blessing for me. Then he performed wudu, and I drank the water that fell from his limbs during wudu.

Hadhrat Ibn Hajar <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: Passing the hand over Saa'ib's head — the likelihood is that he had a headache, so the Prophet passed his blessed hand over his head to become a means of cure for him. And so it happened: through the blessing of his blessed hand, he was cured.

The blessing of the Prophet's blessed hand was that Hadhrat Saa'ib lived to nearly 100 years of age, but not a single hair on his head turned white, nor did any of his teeth fall out.

Hadhrat Saa'ib's statement could mean one of two things: either the Prophet performed wudu and he drank the remaining water in the vessel, or the water that had been used (mustamal) — that is, the water that touched his limbs and fell — he drank. The second meaning is more appropriate because the purpose of seeking blessing (tabarruk) is better achieved in this way.

Note that this is a special quality of the Noble Prophet ﷺ. It is not permissible for an ordinary person to drink the used water of even a great saint's wudu for tabarruk. Allama Ibn Hajar Asqalaani <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: The water that touched the blessed limbs of the Noble Prophet ﷺ did not become impure. For this reason, many of our scholars held that the excretions of the Noble Prophet ﷺ (urine, stool) were pure.

### The Wisdom Behind the Prophet's Prayer Becoming Qada

Hadhrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was returning from Ghazwa Khaybar. They traveled by night until he felt drowsy. He stopped at a place to rest. He told Hadhrat Bilal <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> to guard them for the night — that is, wake them for the Fajr prayer. Hadhrat Bilal performed some nafl prayers according to his strength. The Prophet ﷺ and the companions slept. When Fajr time approached, Hadhrat Bilal leaned against his saddle, facing east, waiting for Fajr. Sleep overcame him. Neither the Prophet ﷺ nor any other companion nor Bilal himself woke up until the sun had risen.

The Noble Prophet ﷺ was the first to wake up. He was distressed (because the Fajr prayer had been missed). He said: "O Bilal, why did you not wake us?" Hadhrat Bilal said: "The same thing overcame me that overcame you." The Prophet said: "Prepare your mounts." They prepared their mounts and moved on. After going a short distance, the Prophet performed wudu, commanded Bilal to give the adhan, then Bilal gave iqamah, and the Prophet led the companions in prayer. After praying, he said: "Whoever forgets a prayer should perform it when he remembers, for indeed the Lord said: 'Establish prayer for My remembrance.'"

In this blessed hadith, two things are noteworthy. First: upon waking, the Messenger of Allah performed wudu. In another hadith: "The Noble Prophet lay down and slept until his breathing became heavy (the condition of deep sleep), then Bilal gave the adhan, and he prayed without performing wudu." The fuqaha have written: "The sleep of the prophets does not break wudu." The people of knowledge used this as evidence: the Prophet said, "My eyes sleep, but my heart remains awake."

The point to consider is: if his wudu is not broken by sleep, then why did he perform wudu here? One answer is that it is possible that when the Prophet rested, he was not in a state of wudu. When he went to sleep without wudu, performing wudu upon waking was necessary. But usually the Prophet slept with wudu, so a better answer is that he performed wudu for renewal (tajdeed), because even when one has wudu, performing a new wudu for the next prayer is mustahab.

**Objection:** If someone objects that how could the Prophet not know the time for prayer and sleep, while his own statement is "my heart does not sleep" — meaning his heart is awake, so he should have known about dawn?

**Answer:** The answer is that there is no contradiction between his noble statement and not knowing the time of dawn, because the heart's function is to perceive inner matters (spiritual things) like pleasure, pain, etc. The knowledge of external sensory things is not the heart's function. Knowing the time of dawn is not the heart's function — these things are seen by the eyes. Since his statement is "my eyes sleep," his eyes were sleeping and did not see the appearance of dawn, while his heart was awake, focused on perceiving the manifestations of the Lord.

Another point to consider: why did the Prophet's prayer become qada? Allama Taibi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> explained the reason: The delay in prayer was only in apparent obedience; otherwise, even while sleeping, he was focused towards Allah. So internal obedience was still attained even while sleeping. The wisdom behind this apparent delay was so that it would become an established sunnah for the Ummah.

Imam Shafi'i <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> holds that a practical proof (daleel fe'li) is stronger than a verbal proof (daleel qauli). Therefore, according to him, the Prophet's action itself is proof. Imam Abu Hanifa <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> holds that verbal proof is stronger than practical proof. Thus he says: the Prophet missing a prayer, then performing it, and then saying "whoever forgets a prayer" — meaning whoever could not perform it on time should perform it when he remembers — this is proof for the Ummah regarding making up missed prayers. It became clear: if the Prophet's prayer had not become qada, how could it be established for the Ummah?

Ibn Arabi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: The Noble Prophet ﷺ, in whatever state — whether asleep or awake — is upon the truth. Every state of his is based on reality. He walks on every difficult and narrow path with the near angels. If he forgets, he is engaged towards the One who caused the forgetfulness. Even if he sleeps, he is focused towards Allah with his heart and soul. That is, he does not forget — he is made to forget. His forgetting does not involve deviation from the path of truth; his focus towards the Lord never turns away.

For this reason, the companions said that when the Prophet slept, they did not wake him until he himself woke up, because they did not know what state he was in. His sleeping during prayer time and his prayer becoming qada, or his forgetting, were not due to any defect. His transition from one state to another occurred so that it would become a sunnah for us.

### Performing Prayer in Masjid Nabawi

Hadhrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Prayer in my mosque (Masjid Nabawi) is better than prayers elsewhere except Masjid al-Haram." This hadith mentions that prayer in Masjid Nabawi is better than one thousand prayers elsewhere, except Masjid al-Haram. But how much better than one thousand — the limit is not mentioned.

In another narration, the limit is given. Hadhrat Anas bin Maalik <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "If a person prays in his home, he gets the reward of one prayer. If he prays in the tribal mosque, he gets the reward of 25 prayers. If he prays in the central mosque (Jaami' Masjid), he gets the reward of 500 prayers. If he prays in Masjid al-Aqsa, he gets the reward of 50,000 prayers. If he prays in my mosque (Masjid Nabawi), he also gets 50,000 prayers. And if he prays in Masjid al-Haram, he gets 100,000 prayers."

**Benefit:** In terms of quantity, praying in Masjid al-Haram gives the reward of 100,000 prayers, while in Masjid Nabawi the reward is 50,000. However, in terms of rank, due to proximity to the Noble Prophet, 50,000 has superiority over 100,000. Superiority is not confined to abundance of quantity. Performing five prayers in Mina is better than performing them in Masjid al-Haram, even though in terms of quantity there is no superiority — that is, one prayer in Mina gives the reward of one prayer, while that same prayer in Masjid al-Haram gives 100,000 — yet Mina is more virtuous because the real virtue lies in following the Noble Prophet ﷺ.

For this reason, Hadhrat Umar <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> said: In Makkah, prayer in Masjid al-Haram gives more numerical reward, but praying in Madinah gives superiority in reward.

### The Station of the Prophet's Illuminated Grave is Higher Than the Lofty Throne

There is a difference of opinion among scholars about whether Makkah or Madinah is superior. Hadhrat Umar <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup>, some companions, most people of Madinah, and Imam Malik <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> hold that Madinah is superior. However, there is no difference of opinion about the superiority of the station of the Noble Prophet's illuminated grave.

Qaazi Iyaaz <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> and other scholars have reported that there is consensus of the Ummah that the part of the earth that is connected to the blessed limbs of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is superior to the Kaaba. The difference of opinion is regarding other places — whether Makkah or Madinah is superior.

Faqhaani <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> explicitly stated that this station has superiority over all the heavens. He said: The apparent matter is that it has superiority over all the heavens because the Noble Prophet ﷺ is present on earth. Most scholars have based their argument for the superiority of the earth on this. Some have said that all prophets were created from earth and are buried in the earth, so in this respect too, the earth has superiority over the heavens.

Allama Nawawi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said that the majority of scholars hold that the heavens have superiority over the earth, except for the place connected to the blessed limbs of the Prophet ﷺ. Similarly, the place of disagreement is other than the Kaaba, because aside from the Prophet's illuminated grave, the Kaaba has superiority over the rest of Madinah.

### The Prophet's Beautiful and Far-Carrying Voice

Ibn Asaakir narrated that the Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah never sent a prophet except that he was sent with a beautiful face and a beautiful voice. So much so, when He sent your Prophet, He sent him with a beautiful face and a beautiful voice." In one hadith it is said: "Indeed the voice of the Noble Prophet ﷺ reached where no one else's voice could reach."

### All the Treasures of the Lord are with the Noble Prophet

Hadhrat Rabee' bin Ka'b <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: I used to spend the night with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. I would bring water for his wudu and his other needs (miswak, prayer mat, etc.). He said to me: "Ask for something." I said: "I desire your companionship in Paradise." He said: "Nothing else besides this?" I said: "Only this." He said: "Then help me (in achieving this) for yourself by performing abundant prostrations."

The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "When you need something, ask from me." Ibn Hajar <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said that his purpose was: "I want to give you a gift in return for your service, because this is the way of noble people, and no one is more noble than the Noble Prophet ﷺ."

The Prophet's statement is general — "ask" — he did not say "ask for such-and-such thing and don't ask for such-and-such." From this it is clear that Allah has granted him the power to give whatever he wishes from Allah's treasures. For this reason, our noble imams have counted this among his special qualities: Allah has authorized him to give whatever he wishes to whomever he wishes.

Accepting the sole testimony of Hadhrat Khuzaima bin Thaabit <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> as equivalent to two testimonies indicates his extensive authority. And permitting Hadhrat Bara' <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> to sacrifice a six-month-old goat, while stating "this is permissible for you but not for others" — from this too it is clear that Allah granted him extensive authorities. Indeed, Allah placed Paradise under his authority — he may give whatever share he wishes to whomever he wishes.

When the Prophet asked Hadhrat Rabee'ah, "Nothing else besides this?" — this was actually a test to see if he would remain firm on this high request or not, because this was a request for something that had no equal. Remaining firm in seeking high stations is a sign of perfect excellence.

Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup>, commenting on Hadhrat Rabee'ah's answer, says: SubhanAllah! What an elevated rank that person possesses who is honored with the beautiful service of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, and what high aspiration — he asked for the highest request and remained firm upon it! The Prophet commanded him to perform abundant prostrations, indicating that companionship in Paradise comes from high ranks. A high rank is attained only when one performs righteous deeds against the desires of his soul. The meaning is: help me in reforming your soul through abundant prostrations. This indicated that this high and exalted station will not be attained without prostrations. You must perform many prostrations against the desires of your soul, but this station in Paradise will be granted only when my prayers are also joined with your worship.

### The Prophet Responds to Salutations

Hadhrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "There is no one who sends salam upon me except that Allah directs my attention towards him so that I may respond to his salam." I say: "And upon you be peace." Regarding what is mentioned in the hadith, Ibn al-Malik explained: The returning of the soul is not used in a literal sense but metaphorically. It indicates that Allah informs you that such-and-such person is sending durood upon you, and you respond.

Allama Suyooti <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> explained the meaning of this hadith in several ways. Among them, he stated that one meaning is very strong (قوی جدا). He explained: The returning of the soul does not mean that the soul returns to the body after separation. Rather, the Prophet is engaged in the conditions of the malakut in barzakh, immersed in the contemplation of the Lord's manifestations — just as in this world he was in the state of receiving revelation, and at other times (without revelation) he would attend to the people of the world. Similarly, after immersion in the contemplation of the Lord's manifestations, when he turns his attention towards those who send salam, this state is expressed as "return of the soul."

### Sending Durood is Also Dhikr of Allah

Hadhrat Ubayy bin Ka'b <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> says: I said, "O Messenger of Allah, I want to send abundant durood upon you — how much time should I set aside for your durood?" He said: "As much as you wish." I said: "Shall I set aside one-fourth of my time?" He said: "As much as you wish; if you do more, it is better for you." I said: "Half?" He said: "As much as you wish; more is better." I said: "Two-thirds?" He said: "As much as you wish; more is better." I said: "Should I set aside all my time?" He said: "If you do so, it will be sufficient for all your affairs and will erase your sins."

If you spend all your time in sending durood upon me, it will suffice for all matters that occur for you — meaning, whatever events (religious or worldly) cause you grief, durood will be sufficient for all of them.

Why did the Prophet consider it better to spend all the time of supplications, litanies, and dhikr on sending durood? Because sending durood upon the Prophet includes the remembrance of Allah as well as the reverence of the Messenger of Allah. Durood encompasses both fulfilling the rights of the Prophet and, compared to individual supplications, involves giving precedence to a great act — an act that is pure grace.

That is, durood is even better than supplication (dua) because it pleases both the Lord and the Lord's Messenger. When the Lord knows one's need, He gives it through His pleasure and mercy without one having to ask. Here, the objection of the ignorant people is also refuted — those who say that the Ahl al-Sunnat just keep reciting durood and do not remember Allah. Kaash! Allah would grant them knowledge and they would understand that durood is, in essence, also the remembrance of Allah.

Here there is a famous objection: It is generally established that when something is likened to another, the likened thing is of a lower rank than that to which it is likened. In this durood, a comparison is made of the Messenger of Allah with Ibrahim <sup>علیہ السلام</sup>: "O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and his family as You sent blessings upon Ibrahim and his family." This would suggest that Ibrahim is superior to the Prophet, whereas he is the most superior of the prophets — so how is this comparison correct?

Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> gave several answers to this question. I quote three:

1) Here the comparison is in the type, not in the quantity. That is, the meaning is simply: "O Allah, send blessings upon the Noble Prophet as You sent blessings upon Ibrahim." It does not mean "in the same manner and same quantity." Just as the Lord said: "Fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you" — here the comparison is only in the prescription, not in the number of fasts. Similarly, the Lord said: "Indeed We revealed to you as We revealed to Nooh" — this only establishes that revelation was given to you and to Nooh, not that the same amount was given. And He said: "Do good as Allah has done good to you" — here too the meaning is not that you do as much good as Allah has done to you, but simply to do good.

2) In durood, the letter "kaf" (of comparison) is not used for comparison but for cause and reason. According to this answer, the meaning of durood becomes: "O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and his family because You have sent blessings upon Ibrahim and his family."

3) The comparison is of the group to the group. That is, from the family of Ibrahim, many prophets have come, and the Prophet is also among them. So the meaning is: "O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and his family in as abundant a quantity as You sent blessings upon Ibrahim and the many prophets from his family."

### The Wisdom in the Prophet's Forgetting

Hadhrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Noble Prophet ﷺ proceeded for prayer. When he said the takbeer, he turned back and gestured to the people to stay where they were. He left the mosque, performed ghusl, then returned with water dripping from his head, and led the prayer. When he finished, he said: "Indeed I was in a state of janabah; I had forgotten to perform ghusl."

In his commentary of this hadith, Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> writes: The Prophet forgot so that it would become a sunnah for the Ummah, and so that if an imam faces such a situation, he should not feel ashamed. The reason for his forgetting was only so that his action would become a sunnah for the Ummah; otherwise, the Prophet was aware even of the janabah of others.

Hadhrat Imam Yaafai <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> narrated an incident: Indeed, Imam al-Haramain Abu al-Ma'ali Ibn Imam Abu Muhammad Juwaini was sitting one day after Fajr prayer teaching. A shaikh from the sufiya passed by with his companions, going to some invitation. Imam al-Haramain thought to himself: "These people have no work besides eating and dancing." When the shaikh returned from the invitation, he said: "O faqeeh, what do you say about a person who prays Fajr in a state of janabah, then sits in the mosque teaching, and backbites people?" Upon hearing the shaikh's words, Imam al-Haramain remembered that ghusl was obligatory upon him but he had forgotten. After this, a good belief about the mashaa'ikh was established in his heart — that is, he came to believe that they possess kashf.

The purpose of relating this incident is to show that when the mashaa'ikh can be aware of the janabah of others, then the Prophet's awareness of his own state was certainly necessary. However, the Lord made him forget in order to establish a sunnah for his Ummah.

### The Prophet Receives Full Reward for Praying While Sitting

Hadhrat Aayesha <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> narrates that when the Messenger of Allah became advanced in age, he mostly performed nafl prayers while sitting. Hadhrat Ibn Hajar Asqalaani <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: This is a special quality of the Prophet ﷺ — he would perform nafl prayers sitting, but his reward would be the same as if he had performed them standing.

Hadhrat Abdullah bin Amr <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: I was told a hadith that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "One who prays sitting gets half the reward of one who prays standing." He says: I came to the Prophet and found him praying sitting. I placed my hand on his blessed head. He said: "O Abdullah bin Amr, what is the matter with you?" I said: "I was told that you said one who prays sitting gets half the reward, but I see you praying sitting." He said: "Yes, that is true. But I am not like any one of you." That is, it is one of my special qualities that however I pray, there is no reduction in my reward. Whatever state I pray in, it is my manifestation. This is the grace of Allah — He gives to whom He wills.

Note: It may seem that Abdullah bin Amr placing his hand on the Prophet's blessed head was contrary to etiquette. He should have observed the reverence of the Prophet. Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> gave two answers: First, perhaps this action occurred unintentionally, and actions done unintentionally are not blameworthy. Second, since he was surprised that the Prophet's statement and action differed, he did this to fully focus the Prophet's attention on investigating the matter. That is why the Prophet addressed him by name and his father's name, saying: "What is the matter with you, O Abdullah bin Amr? You are a man of knowledge — what are you doing?"

Then Mulla Ali Qaari quotes Ibn Hajar's answer, which is more appropriate: The custom of the Arabs was that when they were amazed by something, they would do such things. He too acted according to this custom. Similarly, some Arabs would touch another's beard. Mulla Ali Qaari said: In our time, we have observed that some people touch the beard of the sharif of Makkah and say, "O Hasan, I sacrifice myself for you," even though they are holding shoes in their hands at that time.

### The Prophet's Character Was the Quran

Hadhrat Sa'd bin Hishaam <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> says: I came to Hadhrat Aayesha <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> and said: "O Mother of the Believers! Tell me about the character of the Messenger of Allah." She said: "Have you not read the Quran?" I said: "Yes, I have read the Quran." She said: "The character of the Noble Prophet was the Quran."

**Why was the Quran called his character?** Hadhrat Aayesha called the Quran the Prophet's character because all the noble traits of character mentioned in the Holy Quran were found in him. If the Quran said: "Indeed Allah commands justice and excellence," look throughout the universe — you will not find anyone more just and excellent than him. If the Holy Lord said: "Bear patiently what afflicts you," the brightest example of acting upon this command was set by him. If the Creator of the universe said: "Forgive them and overlook," then the great station of forgiveness and forbearance that was granted to him could not be attained by anyone else. In short, every command of the Holy Quran was manifested in him, and he completely refrained from what the Quran commanded to avoid. Therefore, his character was the Holy Quran.

Another reason: The Holy Quran described his character: "Indeed you are upon an exalted standard of character." When the Quran has called something "great," how can a person express its worth?

### The Prophet Possesses Life

The prophets have complete life in their pure graves. Their bodies are preserved; rather, they even enjoy the pleasure of prayer and recitation of the Quran. Not only the prophets, but the great awliya and scholars also have life in their graves. The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "Indeed Allah has forbidden the earth to consume the bodies of the prophets." All their parts are preserved. There is no difference between their apparent life and their life in the grave. For this reason it has been said: "The friends of Allah do not die; rather, they are transferred from one world to another."

The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "The prophets of Allah are alive; they are given sustenance." The word "prophet" here is generic, not specific to any particular prophet, because the Prophet saw Moosa <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> praying in his grave, and similarly he saw Ibrahim. And in a sahih hadith: "The prophets are alive in their graves, performing prayer."

Baihaqi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: It is rationally permissible for prophets to be in multiple places at different times, as is mentioned in many ahadith. The prophets of Allah remain alive; they are given sustenance. This sustenance could be spiritual or physical. What immediately comes to mind is that this is physical sustenance. Spiritual sustenance is also given, because Allah said concerning the martyrs of the Prophet's Ummah: "Rather, they are alive, and they are given sustenance from their Lord."

When the martyrs of the Prophet's Ummah are alive and given sustenance, then what about their leader and chief — especially when he also attained the station of martyrdom? (Though his passing without apparent martyrdom is also a cause of his life.) Because a Jewish woman gave him a poisoned goat in Khaybar. He consumed a small portion of it, and later the meat itself informed him that it had been poisoned. He stopped consuming it, but at the time of his passing, the effect of that poison returned, and so he attained martyrdom.

However, he was protected from apparent martyrdom. One reason was that the splendor, dignity, and majesty of his blessed form were intended to be preserved. The second reason was to demonstrate perfect power — how Allah protected a single person from numerous evil enemies. The third reason was to fulfill the promise that Allah made with him: "And Allah will protect you from the people."

One purpose of giving sustenance is that just as sustenance sustains a person's life, similarly, without providing food and drink, the Lord gives the prophets and martyrs such strength that they remain alive. This sustenance is spiritual — when someone calls them, they hear and respond. Another meaning could be that they are actually given food and drink — this is the more apparent meaning.

In Tirmizi, Ka'b bin Maalik <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the souls of martyrs are placed in green birds that feed on the fruits of Paradise. In another narration, the souls of martyrs are in the crops of green birds in Paradise; they roam wherever they wish, eat the fruits of Paradise, and then hang from chandeliers beneath the Throne.

For a detailed study of the life of prophets, martyrs, and saints, one should consult Al-Hawi lil Fataawa, Sharh al-Sudoor, Baihaqi, Fath al-Baari, and the treatise "Jila' al-Sudoor" by our respected teacher Allama Abul Hasnaat Muhammad Ashraf Siyaalwi. Here, only a brief mention of the Noble Prophet is being made, which is based on extreme brevity.

**Note:** Indeed, all deceased persons hear greetings and speech, and on certain days, the deeds of their close relatives are presented to them. However, the life of the prophets is high and exalted. Similarly, the life of the martyrs is higher than that of ordinary people.

### Charity is Forbidden for the Prophet and His Family

Hadhrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that Hadhrat Hasan ibn Ali took a date from the dates of sadaqah and put it in his mouth. The Noble Prophet ﷺ said "kakh kakh" in disapproval, so that he would spit it out. Then he said: "Do you not know that we do not eat sadaqah?"

"Kakh kakh" — the Arabs used this word to scold and rebuke someone when stopping them from something undesirable, similar to "hoon hoon" in our language. This word conveys the purpose more strongly than saying "la taf'al" (don't do). Usually, this word is used for children who do not have discretion. The Prophet's purpose in rebuking him in this way was to make the attendees aware of the ruling regarding sadaqah for the family of the Prophet.

From this it becomes clear that it is obligatory for fathers and forefathers to prevent their children from un-Islamic actions. For this reason, our scholars have said that it is forbidden for parents to dress children in silk clothing or adorn them with gold and silver jewelry.

Hadhrat Ibn Hajar Asqalaani <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: Obligatory and voluntary sadaqah were forbidden for the Prophet himself, but for his family, only obligatory sadaqah is forbidden; voluntary sadaqah is not forbidden. Ibn al-Malik said: Sadaqah is absolutely forbidden for the Prophet — whether obligatory or voluntary. As for his family (close relatives), only obligatory sadaqah is forbidden; voluntary is permissible. In Nihayah, it is mentioned that voluntary sadaqah is absolutely permissible for the family of the Prophet.

**Why the difference between obligatory and voluntary sadaqah?** Spending voluntary sadaqah and waqf wealth on the family of the Prophet is permissible; obligatory is not. Because the one giving obligatory sadaqah purifies himself by fulfilling a duty incumbent upon him. The wealth given as sadaqah becomes like used water (mustamal), which has lost its ability to purify. But voluntary sadaqah is given by the donor willingly without it being obligatory on him; therefore, it does not have the quality of impurity. Just as a person with wudu who uses water for cooling — even if there is no apparent impurity on his body — the water remains in its original state and retains its purifying ability. Similarly, this wealth is pure and clean, free from impurity, and worthy of the dignity of the Prophet's family. Similarly, it is permissible for a wealthy person to eat voluntary sadaqah.

The author of Hidayah stated: Once sadaqah is given, taking it back is not correct because the purpose of obtaining reward has already been achieved. Similarly, if one gives sadaqah to a wealthy person, the wealth should not be taken back, because the wealthy person is also given the wealth with the intention of reward.

**Change of Ownership Changes the Ruling**

One day the Noble Prophet came home and asked: "Is there any food in the house?" Some dates were presented to him. He said: "Is there any share for us from the meat cooking in the pot?" Bareera <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> said: "This meat was given to me as sadaqah. Since you do not eat sadaqah, it was not presented to you." The Prophet said: "For you it is sadaqah, and for us it is a gift." That is, if sadaqah wealth is given to a deserving person, and he, from his side, gives it to someone undeserving (such as the family of the Prophet or a wealthy person), it is permissible because the change of ownership changes the ruling.

### A Glimpse of the Prophet's Generosity

Hadhrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہما</sup> narrates: When Ramadan entered, the Messenger of Allah would free prisoners and give to every person who asked. That is, in Ramadan, he would give more than usual to every asker. Otherwise, he never said "no" on any occasion. In Muslim: Whenever someone asked the Noble Prophet for something, he never refused. A person came to him (accepted Islam, for winning his heart) and he gave him all the goats grazing between two mountains. He returned to his people and said: "O my people, accept Islam! Indeed, Muhammad gives so much that there is no fear of poverty."

In Bukhari, Hadhrat Jabir <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: There was nothing asked of the Prophet that he answered with "no."

Note: If he did not have anything to give, he would express an excuse. That was not really a refusal, but a statement of fact.

### Who Among You Can Be Like Me?

Hadhrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah prohibited fasting wisal (continuous fasting day and night without breaking). Someone said: "O Messenger of Allah, you fast wisal." He said: "Who among you can be like me? I spend the night in such a state that my Lord feeds me and gives me drink."

The hadith is not to be taken literally; actual feeding and drinking is not meant. Because if he were actually eating and drinking, then wisal would not apply. The meaning is: you cannot have the strength for continuous day-and-night fasting because I have spiritual strength from Allah. I am fed the food of love and given the drink of love — that is, through witnessing the manifestations of the Lord, I do not need food and drink.

### The Prophets are Protected from Wet Dreams

Indeed, the prophets are protected from wet dreams, because in sleep, this is a sign of Satan's approach. Allama Ibn Hajar Asqalaani <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: When Hadhrat Aayesha <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> said that the Noble Prophet would wake up in Ramadan at Fajr time in a state of janabah without a wet dream, perform ghusl, and fast — from this hadith, the purpose became clear: the prophets would not have the kind of wet dream involving intercourse in a dream, because this state occurs to a person with whom Satan plays during sleep, and the prophets are protected from this. However, if by wet dream only the emission of fluid is meant without seeing intercourse during sleep, this is not impossible for the prophets, because this is a matter of natural and habitual occurrence in which Satan has no involvement. In this, the prophets and others are equal.

### The Reason for the Prophet's Istighfar

Hadhrat Aghar Mazni <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Indeed, when my heart is covered (with a veil), I seek forgiveness from Allah one hundred times a day."

In this hadith, the word "لَیغان" is used. Its explanation is that the letter "ya" has a dammah; it means covering, enveloping, and a veil falling. What veil is meant? It means: "When I intend to turn towards my Lord, that which becomes an obstacle — for its removal, I seek forgiveness." This attention refers to a special attention, which is mentioned in another narration: "I have a time with Allah in which no near angel nor any sent prophet can share."

What are the things that act as an obstacle in this state, which he called a veil? One is the necessities according to human requirements — eating, drinking, marriage, etc. When these human necessities stood between him and the high assembly, he would seek forgiveness to polish the heart and remove the obstacle — even though these were not sins. This state was merely somewhat less compared to being fully focused on the manifestations of the Lord, and he would seek forgiveness to attain higher rank.

Qaazi Iyaaz <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said that what is meant is everything that becomes an obstacle in the remembrance of Allah, because he wished to remember Allah at all times, but other responsibilities sometimes became a veil. Some scholars said that when he learned of the condition of his Ummah, a state of grief would come over him, and he would seek forgiveness for its removal. Some said that he would turn towards his Ummah for their betterment, engaging with enemies and new Muslims, winning their hearts. Due to such engagements, he sometimes felt that these moments were away from his special engagement with Allah, so he would seek forgiveness.

### Hajj Before and After Hijrah

Ibn Aseer stated that the Prophet performed Hajj every year before Hijrah. Ibn Jawzi's statement also agrees, as he mentioned that he performed several Hajjs — the number is not known. Hakim narrated from Soori with a sahih chain that he performed several Hajjs before Hijrah. Tirmizi narrates from Hadhrat Jabir that the Prophet performed two Hajjs before Hijrah, and Ibn Maaja and Hakim mention three in one narration. These narrators spoke according to their knowledge; those who mentioned more did so according to their knowledge, so there is no contradiction among them.

### The Prophet's Expression of Humility

Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that a man came to the Prophet and said: "O best of all creation!" The Messenger of Allah replied: "That is Ibrahim." This was said out of humility and modesty, and out of respect for Ibrahim <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> being Khaleelullah and his noble grandfather. Otherwise, our Noble Prophet is the best of all creation. His own noble statement is: "I am the leader of the children of Adam, and I say this without pride." That is, his words sometimes indicate humility, and at other times state the reality.

Hadhrat Umar <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah said: "Do not exaggerate in praising me as the Christians exaggerated in praising the son of Mary. Indeed, I am His servant, so say: the servant of Allah and His Messenger." The Prophet clarified the matter through a comparison: do not exaggerate in praise like the Christians. In the commentary of this hadith, Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: Indeed, the Christians greatly exaggerated in praising Eisa, and they did so falsely, calling him the son of Allah. The Prophet forbade his companions from such false praise. Note that the Jews exaggerated in condemning Eisa, while the Christians exaggerated in praising him. The Lord addressed both groups: "O People of the Book, do not go to extremes in your religion." That is, speak the truth, and truth is justice. Do not praise so much that you make Eisa a god or son of God, and do not condemn so much that you accuse him of illegitimacy.

When the Prophet stated about himself: "Call me the servant of Allah and His Messenger," it implicitly became understood that Eisa <sup>علیہ السلام</sup> is also a servant and messenger of Allah — not a god or son of God. The Prophet is not to be worshipped. Whatever praise is given besides this, it is still insufficient. The truth is that doing justice to praising him is beyond human capacity.

Allama Busairi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: Forget what the Christians claimed about their prophet (being son of God) — praise your Prophet as much as you wish, and command others to do so.

### Servitude is Superior to Messengership and Prophethood

The meaning of the Prophet's statement "I am a servant" is that he is a servant of Allah in a specific station. In reality, calling the Noble Prophet a "servant" ('abd) is his complete praise. As some virtuous and perfect person presented in the form of the Prophet's statement: "Call me only by 'Abdullah,' for indeed this name is superior to all my names." Allah too, in His Book, mentioned this high attribute and excellent quality in the incident of Mi'raj: *"Pure is He who took His special servant on a journey."* While mentioning the revelation of the Quran, the Lord also mentioned this attribute: *"Blessed is He who sent down the Quran to His special servant."* *"All praise is for Allah who sent down the Book to His special servant."*

In these verses, mentioning his attribute of 'abd contains a subtle indication and high-level glad tidings: the high favor and great kindness that was granted to him was because he possessed the highest degree of servitude. Indeed, servitude is superior to messengership because in servitude, attention is directed away from creation towards the Lord; through this, connection with the Lord is achieved. In messengership, attention is towards creation, because conveying divine commands to creation is necessary. Therefore, in this state, complete attention towards the Lord is not maintained, which apparently results in a station of separation.

**Warning:** This discussion is only about the servitude of the prophets — that their servitude is superior to their prophethood and messengership. To say that an ordinary person's servitude is superior to a prophet's prophethood is absolutely impermissible; rather, saying this with intent would necessitate disbelief.

### The Prophet's Intercession for Sinners

Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah said: "I will be the first to intercede in Paradise. No prophet has had as many people testifying to his truth as I have. Some prophets had only one person testifying to them. I will be the first to intercede for the sinful members of my Ummah to enter Paradise." Some scholars interpreted this to mean that he will be the first to intercede for raising people's ranks in Paradise.

Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> also narrates: "On the Day of Judgment, my followers will be more numerous than those of all other prophets. Two-thirds of all the people of Paradise will be from my Ummah, and the remaining one-third will be from all the other prophets and their nations." From this it is clear that the more followers one has, the greater the excellence of the one followed.

From this too it is clear that among all the imams, Imam Abu Hanifa's station is very high and exalted, because the majority of the people of Islam follow him.

### The Keys to All Treasures Were Given to the Prophet

Hadhrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah said: "I was sent with Jawami' al-Kalim, and I was granted awe (rohb). While sleeping, I saw that I was given the keys to the treasures of the earth, which I held in my hand."

Jawami' al-Kalim means: I have been given the power to speak very concisely, with numerous meanings, yet the speech remains clear — it is not that someone cannot understand it. The Prophet said: "The one who is consulted is entrusted." These are just two words, but many meanings are understood: when someone seeks advice about what to do — whether it is about business, education, buying, selling, marriage, etc. (thousands of examples can be made) — the adviser should give honest advice according to his understanding, in which the person's benefit lies. If he knows what is good for him but advises him against it, it is as if a trustworthy person has betrayed the trust.

Regarding awe: In another hadith, "I was granted awe from a distance of one month's journey." That is, Allah granted the Prophet this station: awe was placed in the hearts of his enemies. When there was a month's distance between him and his enemies, awe would overcome them, and they would begin to fear.

The Prophet said: "I was given the keys to the treasures of the earth in my dream" — this is reality, because the dream of a prophet is true. Here, it is explained that Allah made it easy for him and his Ummah to conquer various cities, and their treasures were granted to him.

### The East and West of the Earth Were Shown to Him

Hadhrat Sowban <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah said: "Indeed Allah folded the earth for me, and I saw its east and west. My Ummah will reach where the earth was folded for me."

"Zawi" means to gather or bring together — to make something distant appear near. That is, the entire earth was gathered and placed before him, so that he could see it as a person sees his palm before his eyes. Therefore, he said: "I saw its east and west."

In its commentary, Shaikh Abdul Haq Muhaddis Dehalwi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: That is, my Ummah will reach all countries — Islam will reach every country. How brilliantly does the noble statement of my beloved Prophet shine today! There is no country in the world where there is not someone who takes the name of my master and protector ﷺ.

### The Prophet is the Completer of Morals

Hadhrat Jabir <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that the Noble Prophet said: "Indeed, Allah sent me to complete noble character and good actions."

"Makaarim" means those habits due to which a person is called noble. "Akhlaq" means states — that is, all those states due to which a person becomes noble. The Lord completed them through me. I was sent to complete all apparent actions and words — that is, his shari'ah encompasses all good actions, and his way (tariqah) encompasses all perfect states.

Although the noble character of the Prophet is countless and beyond enumeration, the good character granted to his Ummah through his noble means are ten. These are qualities that are sometimes found in a son but not in his father, and sometimes in a father but not in his son. They are:

1) Truthfulness
2) True hope
3) Giving to the asker
4) Rewarding good deeds with good
5) Fulfilling trusts
6) Silah rahmi — good treatment of relatives
7) Good treatment of one's wife and friends
8) Hospitality
9) Good treatment of neighbors
10) Above all of these: having modesty (haya)

All these qualities were found in the Noble Prophet, and he possessed every kind of good action and word in a way that no one else could attain.

### The Prophet's Hands Were Softer Than Silk and More Fragrant Than Perfume

Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: I have never touched any silk cloth that was softer than the blessed hand of the Messenger of Allah.

SubhanAllah! How compassionate and merciful my Beloved was! He showed such affectionate love to children, patting each one's cheeks with his generous hands. The children too would run towards him like mad, eager to obtain blessings and love from him, and shake hands with him.

### His Sweat Was Fragrant and Blessed

Hadhrat Umm Sulaim <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> narrates that the Noble Prophet would frequently visit her home and take a midday nap (qailulah). She would spread a leather mat for him on which he would sleep. The Prophet would perspire a lot. She would collect his sweat and mix it with her perfume. The Prophet asked: "O Umm Sulaim, what is this?" She said: "This is your sweat, which I mix with my perfume because it is superior to all fragrances." In another narration, she said: "O Messenger of Allah, we hope to obtain blessings for our children through it." The Prophet said: "It is good; blessings will be obtained through it."

The Prophet perspired a lot because he had great modesty (haya), and a person with great modesty perspires more. No perfume, musk, or fragrant substance had a fragrance like that of my Beloved's blessed sweat. Those who consider my master and protector as an ordinary person have never considered: does their sweat have a bad odor? Does no one like to sit near them? But the Prophet's sweat was used to enhance the fragrance of other perfumes, and blessings were obtained from it. When Umm Sulaim said they would use his sweat for obtaining blessings for their children, he did not forbid it but said: "You have done the right thing; indeed, there is blessing in my sweat."

From this hadith, we learn that seeking blessings from the effects of righteous people and seeking closeness to them is mustahab. When Umm Sulaim's son, Hadhrat Anas bin Maalik's death approached, he made a will: "After my death, apply perfume to me, and mix in it the perfume that my mother prepared by mixing the Prophet's sweat." SubhanAllah! What firm faith the companion of the Prophet had — that the Prophet's sweat would be a source of blessing even in the grave!

Note: Umm Sulaim and Umm Haraam were sisters, daughters of Milhaan. Umm Sulaim was the mother of the fortunate companion Hadhrat Anas, who served the Prophet for ten years. She was first married to Maalik, with whom Hadhrat Anas was born. Later she married Hadhrat Talha. Allama Nawawi stated that Umm Sulaim and Umm Haraam were the Prophet's aunts — whether through fosterage or lineage. Therefore, he would frequently visit their homes; they were his mahram, and seclusion with a mahram is permissible. He never secluded himself with a non-mahram woman. Both were from Bani Najjar, and Hadhrat Abdul Muttalib also married a woman from Bani Najjar.

### The Fragrance of His Sweat Indicated Where He Had Passed

Hadhrat Jabir <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates that whenever the Noble Prophet walked on a path, after he left, if anyone walked on that path, he could tell from the fragrance of his sweat that the Prophet had passed there. The fragrance mentioned here is the natural fragrance found in his blessed sweat — not the perfume that people use to eliminate the odor of sweat. Having natural fragrance in one's sweat is a special quality of the Prophet; no other prophet was granted this honor.

### Obtaining Blessing and Cure from the Prophet's Blessed Hand

Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: When the Messenger of Allah finished the Fajr prayer, all the servants (children) of Madinah would bring vessels of water to him. Whoever brought a vessel, he would dip his blessed hand into it. Sometimes they brought very cold water, and he would dip his hand into that too. Why did they bring their vessels to him? They sought blessing from him, sought increase in sustenance, sought well-being, and sought cure.

Allama Taibi <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: The Prophet dipped his hand in cold water because he wanted that even if he was discomforted, the people's hearts would be happy and they would be comforted. He especially took care of servants and weak people, because they brought their vessels precisely to obtain blessing from his blessed hand. From this it is clear that he set a high example of humility before the poor.

Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> says: The Prophet was more merciful to children than anyone else, and also more merciful to family members. If a servant came to him, he would ask: "Do you have any need?"

### The Prophet's Humility and Modesty

Hadhrat Aayesha <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> says: The Messenger of Allah was neither obscene nor used obscene speech, nor did he shout in the markets, nor did he repay evil with evil. Rather, he would forgive and overlook. He did not use foul language, nor was any of his actions based on obscenity. He was not vulgar — that is, speaking and acting in a vulgar manner, laughing loudly, mocking passersby, annoying noble people — these are all signs of vulgarity, and he was completely free from such actions.

Speaking loudly in markets, calling someone loudly without need, raising one's voice at someone — these were not his ways. Such actions are found in worthless people. According to the Lord's command — "Forgive them and overlook; indeed Allah loves those who do good" — he acted perfectly. He did not repay anyone's bad behavior with bad behavior. If someone treated him badly, he would treat them well. He would forgive and overlook personal wrongs.

Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> says about the Noble Prophet: He would visit the sick, follow funerals, accept the invitation of a slave, and ride a donkey. I saw him on the day of Khaybar riding a donkey whose reins were made of palm leaves.

All these things indicate his complete humility, because there was no trace of pride in him. Ibn al-Malik said: Riding a donkey is sunnah.

Hadhrat Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمتہ اللہ علیہ</sup> said: I say: A person who feels ashamed to ride a donkey — as arrogant people do, and as the ignorant people of Hindustan do — is more despicable than a donkey. Hadhrat Anas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: Whenever the Messenger of Allah shook hands with someone, he never withdrew his blessed hand until the other person withdrew his. He never turned his face away from someone until the other person turned away. He was never seen stretching his knees forward in front of his sitting companions. This hadith also mentions his humility: he never adopted the way of the arrogant. Rather, he set the highest standard of good character.

All this was to teach the Ummah: do not withdraw your hand hastily when shaking hands, do not turn your face away from someone when speaking, do not sit ahead of someone considering them inferior, and do not stretch out your legs.

### He Chose Servitude Over Kingship

Hadhrat Aayesha <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah said: "O Aayesha, if I wished, mountains of gold would move with me. An angel came to me whose garment hem reaches the Kaaba. He said: 'Your Lord sends salam to you and says: If you wish, you may be a prophet-servant, and if you wish, you may be a prophet-king.' I looked towards Jibreel, and he gestured to me to humble myself."

In a narration from Hadhrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہما</sup>, the Messenger of Allah looked towards Jibreel as if seeking his counsel. Jibreel gestured with his hand to choose humility. So the Prophet said: "I will be a prophet-servant."

Hadhrat Aayesha says: After this, the Prophet never ate reclining. He said: "I eat as a servant eats, and I sit as a servant sits."

The angel was sent with such a tall stature that his waist reached the roof of the Kaaba, and the rest of his body was above it. Why was the angel given such height? Perhaps the reason was that the message he was sent with was so great that its greatness needed to be manifest.

The angel said: "Allah sends salam to you." The author of Nihayah used this as evidence that if someone tells another to convey his salam to a third person, he should convey it, and the recipient, upon hearing it, should respond.

Allah gave the Prophet the choice: with prophethood, he could choose either servitude or kingship. He consulted Jibreel, who advised him to choose servitude. This indicates that kingship and perfect servitude cannot be combined in one person. Since the Prophet is the most superior of the prophets, he possesses the highest degree of servitude — although, absolutely speaking, the attainment of kingship is not contradictory to servitude, because some prophets were granted both — kingship and servitude. Sometimes, spending wealth in good deeds is also a virtue, as some scholars have mentioned: The wealth of a righteous person is good wealth; it becomes a means of conquering cities, providing comfort to Allah's servants, and being spent in righteous deeds.

It is clear that when the Leader of the Prophets chose servitude, the outcome of servitude is also exalted. Its rank is higher than all ranks. Those who attain the pleasure of the Lord experience the highest type of pleasure in this, because true kingship belongs only to Allah, who is One and Subduer. The Lord said: *"And I did not create jinn and humans except to worship Me"* — that is, they should manifest that they are My servants and I am their God and Lord. As it comes in a hadith qudsi: "I was a hidden treasure, and I wished to be known, so I created creation so that I may be known."

In this is clear evidence that a patient poor person is superior to a grateful wealthy person. Sometimes, abundance of wealth makes a person rebellious and he forgets God. This becomes a cause of arrogance, and he becomes ungrateful for the Lord's blessings, resulting in deprivation from the Lord's mercy. In most cases, this is the situation. The Lord's noble command is:

*"If Allah were to bestow abundant provision on all His servants, they would surely rebel in the earth, but He sends down in measure as He wills. Indeed, He is Aware and Seeing of His servants."*

In Tafseer Ibn Kaseer, a statement of Hadhrat Qataadah is quoted: "The best life is that which neither makes you heedless nor makes you rebellious." For this reason, Allah has given less wealth to most prophets, awliya, scholars, and righteous people, so that they may remember Him.
