## Hazrat Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — Miracles, Hawareen, and Ascension

### Knowledge of the Unseen

Allah granted Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> knowledge of the unseen — he would tell people what they ate and what they stored in their homes. This was a sign that his knowledge was not ordinary but from Allah.

### The Miracle of the Bird

Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> would take clay, shape it into the form of a bird, blow into it, and by Allah's permission it would become a real bird.

The Jews specifically asked him to create a bat (chamgadhad), because the bat combines features of birds, mammals, and humans — it has teeth and molars, experiences menstruation, gives birth (does not lay eggs), has ears and breasts, produces milk, flies on fleshy wings (not feathers), laughs like humans, and can only see at twilight (dawn and dusk). Creating such a complex creature was considered the ultimate challenge.

Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> made a bat from clay, blew into it, and it flew away. When it disappeared from sight, it fell down dead — this was so that Allah's creation and a human's creation would be distinguished.

### Healing the Blind and Leper

Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> would pray for the blind from birth and those afflicted with leprosy (bars), and Allah would cure them. This was a specific miracle suited to his time — medicine was at its peak, with great physicians. To demonstrate prophethood, he was given cures that the doctors had given up on, just as Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was given miracles against sorcery when sorcery was at its peak.

Fifty thousand people sought treatment from him. Those who could come, came; those who could not, he would visit them. He would first ask them to accept faith, then pray for their healing.

### Raising the Dead

Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> raised four people from the dead by Allah's command:

**Aazir**: Eisa's friend. When he died, his sister sent word to Eisa. It was a three-day journey. Eisa came to his grave, prayed, and Allah brought him back to life. He lived for some time afterward and even had children.

**The Old Woman's Son**: His funeral bier was passing near Eisa. Eisa prayed, and the dead son sat up on the bier, removed his shroud, put on clothes, lifted the bier onto his own shoulders, and walked home. He lived for some time and had children.

**Aashir's Daughter**: A tax collector's daughter died. The next day, Eisa prayed, and she was revived. She too lived on and had children.

**Saam bin Nooh**: Several centuries after Nooh's son Saam had died, Eisa came to his grave and prayed. Saam emerged from the grave — his hair half-white from the fear of Qiyamah (though people did not go grey in that time; the first to have grey hair was Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>).

Saam asked: "Has Qiyamah come?" Eisa said: "No, I prayed and Allah revived you." Eisa said: "Now you will die again." Saam said: "Agreed, but spare me the agony of death." Eisa prayed, and Allah took his soul without the pangs of death.

The reason for reviving Saam bin Nooh: The people doubted that the recently dead were truly dead — perhaps they had only fainted. So Eisa revived someone from 4,000 years earlier, who testified: "Believe in him — he is Allah's prophet."

### The Hawareen (Disciples)

Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> called people to Allah, and some believed, becoming his disciples (Hawareen). They were his close companions who supported him in the cause of Allah. They said: "We believe in Allah, and bear witness that we are Muslims."

The Hawareen asked Eisa: "Can your Lord send down to us a table (Maa'ida) from the sky?" Eisa warned: "Fear Allah, if you are believers." But they insisted: "We wish to eat from it, so our hearts may be reassured and we may know that you have spoken the truth to us."

### The Heavenly Table (Maa'ida)

Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> prayed: "O Allah, our Lord, send down to us a table from the sky, to be for us a festival — for the first of us and the last of us — and a sign from You. Provide for us, for You are the Best of Providers."

Allah replied: "I will send it down to you. But whoever disbelieves after that — I will punish him with a punishment unlike any in the worlds."

The table descended with food. Some say it was fish, some say bread and meat, some say fruits of Paradise. Those who ate from it were satisfied, and those who disbelieved after witnessing this were turned into pigs and apes.

### The Jewish Plot

The Jews plotted against Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. They conspired to kill him. Allah informed Eisa of their plot.

### Eisa's Ascension

Eisa asked his disciples: "Who will be my helpers in the cause of Allah?" The Hawareen said: "We are the helpers of Allah."

The Jews surrounded the house. Allah raised Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> to the heavens — alive. He did not die. The Jews seized a man who resembled Eisa — he was crucified, but it was made to appear to them as Eisa. Allah says: **"They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them."**

### The Life of Eisa

Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is alive in the heavens, body and soul. He will descend at the end of time, near the Day of Judgment. He will:

- Descend near the white minaret in Damascus
- Break the cross
- Kill the swine
- Abolish the jizyah
- Kill the Dajjaal
- Perform Hajj
- Be buried beside the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

### Proofs of Eisa's Life

Multiple evidences are cited:

1. **Quranic**: "They did not kill him, nor crucify him" — negation of death
2. **Quranic**: "I will raise you to Myself" — elevation, not death
3. **Hadith**: The Prophet ﷺ said Eisa will descend to judge justly
4. **Hadith**: Eisa will perform Hajj from a place called Rauha
5. **Consensus of Ahlus Sunnah**: Eisa is alive
6. **Arguments from Imam Raazi, Imam Suyooti** and others on the necessity of his life

### Summary of Correct Belief

The correct Islamic belief about Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>:

- He is a servant of Allah and His messenger
- He is the word of Allah (created by "Be")
- He is a spirit from Allah (honored, not a part of Allah)
- He has no father, created without a male
- He is not the son of God, nor God, nor part of a trinity
- He was not crucified nor killed — Allah raised him to Himself
- He is alive in the heavens
- He will descend before Qiyamah
- He will rule by the shariah of Muhammad ﷺ
- He will die after his descent and be buried
