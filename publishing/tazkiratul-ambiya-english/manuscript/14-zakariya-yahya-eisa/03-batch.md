## Hazrat Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — Refutation of Objections and Correct Belief

### Objections and Answers Regarding Eisa's Miracles

The author addresses objections raised primarily by Qadianis (followers of Mirza Ghulam Ahmad Qadiani), who denied Eisa's miracles and his physical ascension.

#### Objection 1: Creating is Allah's attribute alone — if Eisa made birds from clay, he would be God.

**Answer**: "Creation" (khalq) in the specific sense of bringing something from nothing is Allah's alone. But shaping and forming (tasweer) is given to human beings. Eisa shaped clay into a bird's form, but Allah gave it life. The Quran explicitly says: *"You would breathe into it, and it would become a bird by My permission."* The phrase "by My permission" makes clear that the life came from Allah, not Eisa. This was a miracle (mu'jiza) proving prophethood, not an act of divinity.

#### Objection 2: The dead returning to life is against the natural law — martyrs wish to return but cannot.

**Answer**: The inability of martyrs to return is a matter of Allah's wisdom, not incapability. Allah can do all things. The raising of the dead by Eisa was a temporary miracle for a specific purpose — not a permanent change to the natural order. Those revived lived for a time, had children, and then died again.

#### Objection 3: Eisa's physical ascension is impossible.

**Answer**: Whatever Allah wills is possible. The Mi'raj (ascension) of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was physical. The ascension of Idrees <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is known. Allah's power is not limited by human understanding.

#### Objection 4-16 (Summary of Remaining Objections)

All objections are systematically refuted with Quranic verses, hadith, and logical arguments. The consistent theme is that denying Eisa's miracles and ascension leads to denying the Quran itself, which explicitly affirms these events.

### The Correct Belief About Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

The Quran and Sunnah establish these points:

1. **Eisa is a servant of Allah and His messenger** — not God, nor son of God
2. **He was created without a father** — by Allah's word "Be"
3. **He is Kalimatullah** — created by Allah's word, not the word itself
4. **He is Roohullah** — a spirit honored by Allah, not a part of Allah
5. **He spoke in the cradle** — a miracle proving Maryam's purity
6. **He was given the Injeel** and wisdom
7. **He healed the blind, leper, raised the dead** — by Allah's permission
8. **He was not killed nor crucified** — it was made to appear so
9. **Allah raised him to the heavens** — alive, body and soul
10. **He will descend before Qiyamah** — to break the cross, kill the swine, kill Dajjaal, rule by the shariah of Muhammad ﷺ
11. **He will die after descending** and be buried beside the Prophet ﷺ

### Refutation of the Trinity and Other False Sects

**Trinitarians (Taslees)**: Those who believe in Father, Son, and Holy Spirit as three persons of one God. The Quran refutes: *"They have disbelieved who say Allah is the third of three — there is no god but One God."*

**Malakaniyya (Melkites)**: They say Eisa has two natures — divine and human — united in one person, but also use icons and images in worship.

**Nestorians (Nastoor)**: They say Eisa had two separate natures — the divine nature dwelt in the human Eisa like light in a lamp, and Maryam gave birth only to the human. They emphasize Christ's humanity.

**Jacobites (Yaqoobiyya)**: They say Eisa has one composite nature — divine and human mixed together. They deny icons.

**Sir Sayyad Ahmad Khan's view**: He denied Eisa's miracles and ascension, claiming they were allegorical. This is rejected as contrary to the clear Quran.

All these sects are refuted by the Quran's clear statements. The correct Islamic belief is the middle path — Eisa is a great prophet, not God, not an ordinary man. He was created miraculously without a father, given miracles by Allah, raised alive to the heavens, and will return before the end.

### Eisa on the Day of Judgment

Allah will ask Eisa on the Day of Judgment:

> **"Did you say to the people, 'Take me and my mother as deities besides Allah?'"**

Eisa will reply:

> **"Glory be to You! It was not for me to say what I had no right to say. If I had said it, You would have known it — You know what is within me, and I do not know what is within You. Indeed, You are the Knower of the unseen. I did not tell them except what You commanded me: 'Worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord.'"**

### Summary

The chapter concludes with a powerful call to adhere to the correct Islamic belief about Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — neither exaggerating his status as Christians do, nor diminishing it as the Qadianis do. He was a mighty messenger of Allah, alive in the heavens, who will return before the end of time to restore justice and then die a natural death.
