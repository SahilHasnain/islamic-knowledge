## Hazrat Ayyub <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

### Lineage and Description

Hazrat Ayyub <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was a descendant of Hazrat Is'haaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — his lineage reaches Is'haaq through his son A'eis. His mother was the daughter of Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. There is a difference of opinion, but one narration states his age was 140 years, and he lived 80 years after his trial.

**Blessed appearance:** He had a handsome face, broad shoulders, a long beard, and a wheatish complexion.

**Qualities:** He was the most patient of people, extremely grateful, and abundant in charity. He never let a beggar leave disappointed. He was humble in heart, soft in speech, generous in hand, and constantly engaged in the remembrance of Allah.

### Abundance of Wealth and Children

Allah ta'ala had blessed him with immense wealth: all kinds of livestock — cattle, goats, sheep, horses, donkeys, and camels. He had vast lands for cultivation, numerous servants, and great prosperity.

He also had many sons and daughters, with the most authentic narration stating he had seven sons and seven daughters.

### The Angels Spoke of His High Station

The angels in the heavens discussed Hazrat Ayyub <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Iblees heard them and said: "He is grateful and obedient only because of the blessings he has been given. Remove his blessings and you will see how ungrateful he becomes."

Allah ta'ala permitted Iblees to test Ayyub, and thus his trial began.

### The Days of Trial

Iblees destroyed all of Ayyub's wealth, killed his children, and inflicted severe illness upon his body. His body was covered with such affliction that no sound limb remained except his tongue (for Allah's remembrance) and his heart (filled with faith). He was abandoned by everyone except his wife, who served him despite her own noble lineage. She would work as a servant for others and bring food to feed him.

### A Partner in Difficulty

When asked, "How much time passed in comfort and ease?" his wife replied, "Eighty years." Ayyub <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "I feel ashamed before Allah to pray for relief when my time of trial has not even equaled my time of ease."

### Ayyub's Unmatched Patience

His state was described in the Quran:

> "Indeed, We found him patient. An excellent servant! Indeed, he was one who repeatedly turned back to Allah."

### Displeasure with His Wife's Mistake

Once, his wife was late when he called her. It is possible the illness affected his temperament. However, the greater reason was this: Iblees came to his wife in the form of a physician and said, "If you wish, I can give medicine to cure your husband. In return, he should only say, 'You cured me.'" She considered this a small matter and told Ayyub.

When she related the story, Ayyub understood that Iblees wanted to make him fail in his trial. He became angry and said: "If I recover, I will strike you a hundred lashes. Until then, I will not eat anything from your hands."

This is why Ayyub <sup>عليه السلام</sup> supplicated: "Indeed, Satan has afflicted me with hardship and torment."

### The Time of Trial Comes to an End

> "And remember Our servant Ayyub when he called to his Lord: 'Indeed, Satan has touched me with hardship and torment.' So We responded to him and removed what afflicted him. And We gave him his family and the like of them with them, as a mercy from Us and a reminder for the worshippers."

Ayyub <sup>عليه السلام</sup> mentioned his difficulty in a beautiful, gentle manner. He spoke of Allah's boundless mercy but did not explicitly ask, "O Master of the universe, remove my hardship." What patience! What a beautiful way of pleading before the Lord!

### The Spring of Healing

> "Strike with your foot; this is a cool spring for washing and drinking."

He was commanded to strike the ground with his foot. A spring gushed forth. He drank from it and bathed. Washing healed all his external illnesses, and drinking healed all his internal ailments.

### Wealth and Children Were Restored

The majority of scholars say Allah resurrected all his children who had died, healed the sick, and gathered all who had scattered. Another narration says he was granted youth again and given new children like before, along with abundant wealth. One narration says Allah rained down golden locusts upon him — he kept gathering them into his garment, spreading a sheet to collect more. Allah revealed: "O Ayyub, are you not satisfied?" He replied: "O Master of the universe, who can be satisfied with Your grace?"

He had spent 18 years in illness and affliction, and then all his splendor was restored.

### His Wife's Difficulty Made Easy

Since he had taken an oath for a legitimate reason and for Allah's pleasure — "When I recover, I will strike you one hundred lashes" — fulfilling the oath was necessary after recovery. Allah ta'ala, in His mercy, saved the wife of His prophet from the lashes, because the prophet took the oath for Allah's pleasure, and his wife served him for Allah's pleasure when everyone else had abandoned him.

Allah ta'ala said: "Take in your hand a bunch of thin twigs (a broom) and strike with it, and do not break your oath."

He was commanded to take a broom of one hundred thin twigs and strike her once, thus fulfilling his oath. This was Allah's grace — no ordinary person could fulfill such an oath in this way. They would have to break the oath and pay expiation.

Note: When a concession comes from Allah or His Messenger ﷺ in a Shariah matter, it is His mercy. But for a person to devise tricks to evade Shariah rulings — such as avoiding zakah or postponing fasts to winter without valid reason — is impermissible.

May Allah grant every Muslim the ability to thank Him in times of ease and be patient in times of trial.

### Note Regarding Worms

Allama Qurtubi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> narrates from Ibn Arabi that the incident of worms appearing on Ayyub's blessed body needs a valid chain of transmission. However, Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> confirmed it with an authentic chain: Ahmad, Abu Nuaim, and Ibn Asaakir narrate from Hasan Basri that there was no place on Ayyub's body except his eyes, heart, and tongue where worms had not appeared. When a worm fell off, he would pick it up and put it back, saying: "Eat from Allah's provision which He has given you."

## Hazrat Zulkifl <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Hazrat Yasa'a <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

> "And remember Ismail, Yasa'a, and Zulkifl — and all are among the best."

### Hazrat Zulkifl <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

His name is Bashar or Sharaf. He is the son of Hazrat Ayyub <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Although there are various opinions, this view is more accepted.

Allah ta'ala sent him as a prophet after his father, commanding him to call people to believe in His oneness. He remained in the region of Syria throughout his life, conveying Allah's commands. He passed away at the age of 75. He advised his son "Abdaan" to remain firm on good deeds after his death and encourage others to faith and righteous works.

He showed mercy to orphans, the needy, the poor, and widows, caring for their needs. Due to his taking responsibility for the needy, he was named Zulkifl (the one who takes responsibility/kafalah).

### Hazrat Yasa'a <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

Allah ta'ala granted him prophethood and kingship. He would fast during the day and stand in prayer at night. He never became angry, especially in matters concerning his community — he would judge with great deliberation without haste or anger.

When his death approached, the elders of Israel came and asked him to appoint a successor for the kingdom. He said: "I will entrust the reins of governance to whoever gives me a guarantee in three matters." No one agreed except a young man who said, "I give my guarantee." The three matters were:

1. Spend the entire night in worship — no sleeping.
2. Fast every day.
3. Never judge while angry.

When the young man accepted these three conditions, Yasa'a handed the governance to him. Note that prophethood cannot be delegated — Allah chooses whom He wills as a prophet.

## Hazrat Ilyaas <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

> "And indeed, Ilyaas was among the messengers. When he said to his people, 'Will you not fear Allah? Do you call upon Ba'al and leave the best of creators — Allah, your Lord and the Lord of your forefathers?' And they denied him, so they will surely be brought forth. But the chosen servants of Allah, except. And We left for him among the later generations: 'Peace be upon Ilyaas and upon those who believe with him.' Indeed, We thus reward the doers of good. Indeed, he was among Our believing servants."

Hazrat Ilyaas <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is a prophet of Allah and a descendant of Hazrat Haroon <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, the brother of Musa. His lineage: Ilyaas alaihissalam bin Yaseen bin Qehhas bin al-Izaar bin Haroon.

### Annual Meetings Between Ilyaas and Khizr

Hazrat Ilyaas <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was appointed over land, and Hazrat Khizr over seas and islands. They meet every year at the time of Hajj.

### The Prophet ﷺ Meets Ilyaas

During a journey, Ilyaas met the Prophet ﷺ. Allah sent down food from the heavens containing bread and fish. Both ate together, then prayed Asr together.

### Ilyaas' Call to His People

He told them: "Why do you not fear Allah? Follow Allah's commands and avoid what He has forbidden. You worship idols — you seek your needs from idols instead of Allah, abandoning the One who is the best of creators."

Note: Allah alone is the Creator. The verse says "the best of creators" (أحسن الخالقين) addressing their false belief that other creators existed alongside Allah — saying, "Of all those you consider creators, Allah is the best."

### The Idol of Ilyaas' People

Their idol was named "Ba'al." In the Yemeni language, "ba'al" means "lord" or "master." They would ask, "Who is the master of this house?" This is also why a husband is called "ba'al" in Arabic.

The idol was 30 feet tall, made of gold, with four faces. They greatly revered it and appointed 400 servants for its worship. Since these servants attended to their deity, some commentators wrote "Ambiya" instead of "abnaihim" — meaning they considered these servants to be the prophets of their god.

### The City of Ba'albak

The city was named "Ba'albak" because the ruler at that time was named "Bak" and his deity was "Ba'al." He founded a city and combined both names. In grammar books, this is how it is recorded. Some say "Bak" was the name of the place where the idol was installed.

**Note:** Some have written that Satan would speak from within the Ba'al idol and issue commands. Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> refutes this: "It is very difficult to accept that Satan entered the idol and spoke. If that is accepted, then many miracles would be undermined. When the Prophet's ﷺ miracles include a wolf speaking, a camel speaking, and a palm trunk weeping, one could claim Satan entered them too — and thus trust in miracles would be destroyed." This shows that the view of Satan speaking through the idol is baseless.

**Note on punishment:** In the verse "they denied him, so they will surely be brought forth," the reference is to punishment in the Hereafter. The exception "except the chosen servants of Allah" is also from the Hereafter punishment. The destruction of Ilyaas' people in this world and the salvation of the believers could not be found in reliable commentaries.

## Hazrat Yunus <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

> "Then why was there not a city that believed so its faith could benefit it, except the people of Yunus? When they believed, We removed from them the punishment of disgrace in worldly life and gave them enjoyment for a time."

### The People of Yunus

Hazrat Yunus <sup>عليه السلام</sup>'s people lived in "Nenwa" in the region of "Mosil." They were immersed in disbelief, polytheism, and idol worship. Allah sent Yunus to them. He commanded them to believe and abandon idol worship, but they denied him. He warned them of Allah's punishment.

When he became angry with them and left the city, the people became terrified at his absence, saying: "Now the punishment will surely come."

He had given them a specific deadline. According to Tafseer Kabeer, when 35 days had passed, thick black clouds covered the sky, emitting thick smoke that reached the city and enveloped the houses. They realized the promised punishment was imminent. They became so terrified that they fled the city to the wilderness, separated their women and children, and even separated the animals from their young. The animals began crying out in anguish. The men, women, and children expressed their humility before Allah, weeping and praying:

"O Allah, we believe in You and in Your prophet Yunus. We seek forgiveness for our sins. O Master of the universe, forgive us and protect us from the coming punishment."

They forgave one another's transgressions, returned usurped rights, and even removed stones from foundations that had been placed without permission. When they truly believed and repented, Allah had mercy on them and removed the punishment.

### The Day of Acceptance

The day was Ashura — the 10th of Muharram — which was also a Friday. They had gone to a learned elder asking what to do. He advised them to make these supplications:

"O He who is alive when none else lives. O Ever-Living. O Reviver of the dead. O Ever-Living. There is no deity but You. O Allah, our sins are great and have exceeded all bounds. You are Almighty and Exalted. Treat us as befits Your majesty — for You are Merciful and Generous — and do not treat us as we deserve."

**Objection:** Pharaoh also believed when he saw the punishment and repented, but his faith was not accepted. Why was the faith of Yunus' people accepted?

**Answer:** Pharaoh repented upon witnessing the actual punishment as he was drowning. But Yunus' people believed and repented after seeing only the signs of punishment, before witnessing it directly. The distinction is clear: Pharaoh's faith came at the sight of punishment itself, theirs came before.

### Yunus in the Belly of the Fish

When Yunus left his people in anger (and they later repented in his absence), he boarded a ship to cross a river. The ship became caught in a whirlpool. According to their custom, they believed that if a slave fleeing from his master was on board, the ship would not reach shore until the slave was thrown off. They cast lots — Yunus' name came up three times. He said: "I am the slave fleeing from my Master." He jumped into the river so that the others could reach shore.

Allah inspired a fish: "Swallow Yunus, but know that his belly is a prison for him, not food. Do not scratch him or harm him in the slightest." Thus Yunus entered the fish's belly — a trial and a loving reprimand from the Beloved.

### Explanation of Quranic Words

He is called *Dhun-Nun* and *Sahib al-Hoot*, both meaning "the companion of the fish." Allah said:

> "And remember Dhun-Nun when he went away in anger and thought that We would not straiten him."

This translation is by A'la Hazrat Maulana Ahmad Raza Khan <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>. The correct meaning is "thought that We would not straiten him" — not "thought We would not have power over him," as some faulty translations suggest. Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> refutes those who use this verse to claim that prophets commit sins.
