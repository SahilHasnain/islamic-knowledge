### The Quarrel of the Two Sons of Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

By the time of Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> death, his children and grandchildren numbered more than 40,000. In *Tafseer Saawi* and *Jumal* etc., there is mention of them reaching one hundred thousand.

Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> direct sons were Qabil and Habil. Qabil was the elder and Habil was the younger. Qabil used to farm and Habil used to graze goats. The girl born with Qabil was named Aqleema, who was very beautiful and handsome, and the girl born with Habil was Libwa, who was somewhat less in beauty. According to the law of Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> shariah, the girl born with Qabil was to be married to Habil, and the girl born with Habil was to be married to Qabil. But Qabil refused to do this. He said, "The girl born with me will be married to me alone."

### Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Advised Both to Present an Offering

When Qabil began to persist and be stubborn, Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said, "Both of you present something in the path of Allah. The one who is truthful — his offering and charity will be accepted, and the one who is false — his charity will not be accepted." At that time, the sign of acceptance was that a naturally occurring fire would consume the charity of the one whose offering was accepted, and would not consume the one whose offering was not accepted.

Qabil presented a heap of wheat and Habil presented a goat or a sheep in the path of Allah ta'ala. Both presented their offerings saying, "O Allah, accept the sacrifice of the one who is more deserving of Aqleema."

The heavenly fire consumed Habil's charity, granting acceptance, and did not consume Qabil's charity, rejecting it. Jealousy, malice, and obstinacy flared up in Qabil's heart. He threatened to kill Habil. Allah ta'ala has mentioned this incident in the following manner:

> "And recite to them the true story of the two sons of Adam when each presented an offering. One was accepted. He said, 'I swear I will kill you.' Habil said, 'Allah accepts only from those who fear Him. If you stretch your hand toward me to kill me, I will not stretch my hand toward you to kill you. I fear Allah, the Lord of all the worlds. I want you to bear my sin and your sin, so you become an inhabitant of Hell. That is the reward of the unjust.'"

The purpose of mentioning this incident was to make the evil of jealousy known, so that those who envied the Noble Prophet ﷺ may learn a lesson, and so that people may consider jealousy evil until the Day of Judgment.

Habil was upon the truth. Due to his piety, his offering was accepted. He said, "I want my sin and your sin to fall upon you." His meaning was: you disobeyed your father — that sin is upon you, and if you want to kill me, then the sin of my murder will also be upon you.

### Eventually Qabil Killed Habil

> "Then his soul made the murder of his brother seem appealing to him, so he killed him and thus became among the losers."

Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had gone to Makka. Qabil killed his brother behind his back. This was the first murder on the face of the earth. Therefore, Qabil did not know how to kill his brother. Satan placed a bird's head on a stone before him and struck it with another stone, crushing it. Qabil understood that this is how to kill. Since Habil used to graze goats, he was sleeping under a tree. Qabil struck a stone on his head and killed him. At that time, Habil was 20 years old.

### Qabil's Disgrace in the World After the Murder

It is narrated from Abdurrahman bin Fazaala that when Qabil killed Habil, his intellect left him, the ability to understand in his heart was destroyed. He remained insane until he died.

Before the murder, his complexion was fair, and after the murder, his entire body turned black. When Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> returned from the land of Makka Mukarrama, he asked Qabil, "Where is your brother?" He said, "I was not responsible for him." He said, "You have killed him, that is why your body has turned black."

### Qabil's Punishment in the Hereafter

It is narrated from Hazrat Ibn Mas'ud <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> that the Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever is killed unjustly, the punishment for his murder will also be upon the son of Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, because he was the first to initiate murder." That is, just as the murderer will be punished for the murder, the one who initiated murder will also continue to receive punishment.

### Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Grief Over His Son's Murder

Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was so grieved over the murder of his son that he did not smile for 100 years. After 100 years, he was given the glad tidings of a son — that is, he was told:

> "May Allah keep you alive. Allah ta'ala is going to grant you another son."

So he smiled.

However, Hazrat Allama Muhi'us Sunnah <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> has mentioned that Hazrat Shees <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was granted to Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> 50 years after the murder of Habil, and he became Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> successor.

It should be noted that in *Tafseer Kashshaaf*, it is mentioned that after his son's murder, Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> composed verses as an elegy. But in *Tafseer Kabeer*, Allama Razi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> and in *Ruhul Ma'ani*, Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> rejected this, saying it is false and incorrect — all the noble prophets did not compose verses.

### Qabil's Distress After the Murder

When Qabil killed Habil, he did not know what to do. There was the danger that if he left him like this, wild beasts would eat him. So he put his brother's body in a sack and kept carrying it around until the body became foul-smelling. He could not find any way to hide it. He was very distressed. It should be noted that in the Holy Quran at this point, the word *saw'ah* (سَوءَة) is used for the body, whose original meaning is "private parts" — that part of the body which is not exposed. Because he too was carrying the body after the murder, it is called *saw'ah*.

### The Crow's Assistance in Hiding the Body

The divine command is:

> "Then Allah sent a crow that scratched the earth to show him how to hide his brother's body. He said, 'Woe to me! Could I not even be like this crow to hide my brother's body?' So he became regretful."

How generous Allah ta'ala is! Even though Qabil was a criminal, when he was trapped in the distress of what to do with his brother's body — this was the first death in the world, he was unaware of digging a grave and burying — so Allah ta'ala made a crow his helper to solve his difficulty.

### How Did the Crow Assist?

Allama Razi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> has mentioned three ways at this point, although the first is the most famous.

1) Allah ta'ala sent two crows. They fought each other, and one killed the other. Then with its beak and claws, it scratched the earth, placed the dead crow's body in that ditch, and covered it with soil. Qabil realized that he had to do the same. He became very remorseful that he was more helpless than that crow — he could not even do that much.

2) Qabil, becoming fed up, threw Habil's body away. Allah ta'ala sent crows that dug soil and threw it over the body, hiding it. Seeing this, Qabil felt great sorrow — that Allah had granted Habil so much honor, and he had become so degraded.

3) The common habit of a crow is that if it has some food, it buries it in the ground for another time. When it hid something in the ground, Qabil realized that he had to hide his brother's body in the same way. At the same time, he became very remorseful due to having less knowledge than the crow — that even a crow is better than him, as it knows how to hide things.

**Note:** Qabil's regret was not due to fear of Allah ta'ala, nor was he repenting. Rather, he regretted that he kept carrying his brother's body and was less intelligent than a crow — he could not bury it. He was also remorseful because even after killing his brother, he had not succeeded in his objectives, since his parents, sister, and brother were all displeased with him. Another reason was that when he saw a crow burying another's body with great care, he regretted that he could not feel such compassion for his brother as the crow had. It is clear that he did not repent — his regret was only over his own foolishness.

### The Death of Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

When Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> final moments came, he desired to eat fruits of Paradise. He said to his sons, "Go to the Ka'aba Muazzama and pray there that Allah ta'ala fulfills this wish of mine." Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> sons went there upon receiving this command. There they met Hazrat Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and other angels. They mentioned Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> request to them. The angels said, "Come with us — we have brought fruits of Paradise with us."

So they all reached Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Hazrat Hawwa <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> became frightened upon seeing those angels and wanted to hide in Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> cloak. He said, "Hawwa, now stay away from me — do not become an obstruction between me and the messengers of my Lord." In this manner, the angels seized the soul of Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.

### Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Shrouding and Burial Was Performed by Angels

The angels said to Hazrat Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> sons, "In the same way that we shroud and bury your father, you too should shroud and bury those who pass away."

Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had brought from Paradise a compound fragrance, a shroud of heavenly garments, and some leaves of the heavenly berry tree. He himself gave him a bath, enshrouded him, applied fragrance, and the angels brought his blessed body to the Ka'aba. All the angels performed his funeral prayer, in which Hazrat Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was the imam and all the angels were the followers. In that prayer, four takbeers were said, just as they are today. Then they took him to a place three miles from Makka Muazzama — the station of Mina, where the pilgrims perform sacrifice, and where Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> sacrificed Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. There, near Masjid Khaif, they dug a side grave, buried him, and made his grave slightly raised like the hump of a camel.

Hazrat Hawwa's <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> grave is in Jeddah. According to some narrations, both their graves are in the Haram, in the place of tawaf.
