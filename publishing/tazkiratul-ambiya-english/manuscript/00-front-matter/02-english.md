### The Number of Noble Prophets

Although the well-known tradition states that the number of noble prophets is 124,000, another tradition mentions 224,000, and yet another mentions 8,000. Therefore, it is better to hold the belief that all the noble prophets who came from Allah ta'ala are true and we have faith in all of them, without specifying a particular number. This is because it is possible that one believes in a lesser number while the actual number is greater, or one believes in a greater number while the actual number is lesser.

In the first case, faith would not be complete in many noble prophets, and in the second case, believing someone to be a prophet who is not a prophet would become necessary. Both situations are problematic. Hence the better approach is to hold this belief: "O Allah, I have faith in all the noble prophets sent by You, and they are true."

**Note:** We do not have certain knowledge of the exact number of noble prophets — the traditions vary. This does not imply that the Noble Prophet ﷺ also lacked this knowledge. Similarly, the fact that the details of the events of the noble prophets were not mentioned does not mean that the conditions of many noble prophets were not revealed to him through revelation. If he had been informed through revelation, we too would have gained that knowledge — but this reasoning is incorrect, for the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is of such a station:

> "Indeed, the Noble Prophet ﷺ did not depart from this world until ALLAH TA'ALA had granted him all the hidden knowledge of this world and the Hereafter. However, he was commanded to conceal certain things."

## The Number of Messengers and Heavenly Books

Among all the noble prophets, some were of higher rank, called messengers. The number of those messengers is 313. The total number of heavenly books is 104. Four of them have established names — the Torah, the Gospel, the Psalms, and the Noble Quran — while 100 are called scrolls, without specific names.

### Who Is Called a Prophet?

The word "Nabi" is either derived from "Nabaawah," meaning high status, or from "Naba" (with a sukoon on 'ba'), meaning to inform or make known, or from "Nabaah" (with sukoon on 'ba' and an extra 'ta'), meaning a hidden voice.

According to the first meaning, a prophet is called "Nabi" because he holds the highest rank among all creation. According to the second meaning, because he manifests the truth and conveys news of the unseen. According to the third meaning, because he hears revelation — a voice hidden from others. Another possibility is that this word is originally "Nabeeya," meaning path. In this case, the prophet is called "Nabi" because he is the intermediary between ALLAH TA'ALA and creation — just as a path is a means to reach a destination, so the noble prophets are the means and intermediary to attain closeness to Allah ta'ala and reach the intended goal.

These were the lexical meanings of the word "Nabi," all of which are united simultaneously in a prophet.

### The Technical Definition of a Prophet

"A prophet is a human being from the children of Adam — male, free — to whom revelation comes, who conveys Allah's commandments to people, gives glad tidings of Paradise to the righteous, warns the disbelievers of Hell, and whose prophethood is supported by miracles."

A messenger means one who delivers a message, one who is sent. But technically, a messenger is one who is also given a book or who is commanded to renew a previous law that has been abrogated. Every messenger is necessarily a prophet, but it is not necessary that every prophet be a messenger.

All messengers and noble prophets are strengthened with miracles. Now let us see what a miracle is.

### Miracle (Mojiza)

An action contrary to normal custom, occurring without the means of instruments, from a claimant of prophethood after the proclamation of prophethood, is called a miracle. An action that conforms to normal custom is not called a miracle — for example, outrunning others by sprinting fast, or a sharp-sighted person seeing something from a distance that an ordinary person cannot see. Such actions are not miracles.

An action contrary to custom performed through instruments is also not a miracle — for example, speaking over a distance via telephone, or seeing someone's face through television. Such things are not miracles.

A miracle is only an action contrary to normal custom performed by a prophet. If a non-prophet performs an astonishing feat, calling it a miracle is ignorance and foolishness. In the present age, it has become common among many so-called educated ignoramuses to call ordinary things miracles (mojizat), which is utterly false.

## Who Cannot Be a Prophet?

A female cannot be made a prophet because conveying the religion is not possible for her. A prophet must go outside the home, face crowds of men, and deliver divine commandments in gatherings — tasks that a woman cannot perform.

A slave cannot be a prophet because a slave is considered lowly in the eyes of others and cannot do anything without the master's permission. Hence the delivery of religious commandments is not possible for him.

Jinn and angels have not been made prophets. Although benefit can be derived from one's own kind, deriving benefit from a different kind is difficult. Therefore, for benefiting mankind, it is necessary that a prophet be human. Hence ALLAH TA'ALA said:

## Prophets Are Free from Sins

The only way to benefit from the teaching of a prophet is that the prophet be sent in human form so that people can derive benefit. If an angel were made a prophet, humans would not have the capacity to see him in his true form. Even if an angel were made a prophet, he would have to come in human form so that people could benefit from him.

Imam Qazi Iyaz <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> states: A group of verifying scholars among the jurists and theologians hold that the noble prophets <sup>عليهم السلام</sup> are free from both major and minor sins, just as they are free from major sins before and after prophethood.

### The Noble Prophets Possess Grand Character

ALLAH TA'ALA grants the noble prophets <sup>عليهم السلام</sup> such lofty and pure character even before the proclamation of prophethood that people cannot raise any objection about their past, present, or future. These pure qualities remain with them at all times — courage, forbearance, gracious speech — they possess all kinds of good morals and are free from low and base actions.

### All Prophets Are Equal in the Essence of Prophethood

All noble prophets <sup>عليهم السلام</sup> are equal in the essence of prophethood — that is, by virtue of being prophets. It cannot be said that one prophet's prophethood is original and another's is temporary. Rather, the prophethood of all noble prophets <sup>عليهم السلام</sup> is original; no prophet's prophethood is temporary. However, in terms of rank, some noble prophets <sup>عليهم السلام</sup> hold superiority over others, and the most superior of all noble prophets <sup>عليهم السلام</sup> is our Noble Prophet ﷺ.

In terms of coming into this world, the first prophet is Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and the last to come is Hazrat Muhammad ﷺ.
