### Hazrat Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — Lineage and People

Hazrat Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> belonged to the tribe of Aad, also called "Aad Uula" (the first Aad). The second Aad — the people of Hazrat Saaleh <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — are known as the Samood.

Aad was a son of Aaus bin Iram bin Saam bin Nooh. The lineage of Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is: Hood bin Abdullah bin Ribaah bin Khulood bin Aad.

This is why Allah ta'ala said: "We sent to the people of Aad their fellow-tribesman Hood." Some translators have rendered the word *اخاهم* as "their brother," which is entirely incorrect. The entire people were not his real brothers, and the Prophet of Allah cannot be a religious brother of disbelievers. He was merely a member of their tribe. A'la Hazrat Maulana Ahmad Raza Khan Barelvi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> translated it as "their fellow-tribesman," and this is also the research of Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>.

By saying Hood was the "fellow-tribesman" of Aad and Saaleh the "fellow-tribesman" of Samood, Allah ta'ala refuted the disbelievers of Makkah who said, "How can Muhammad become a prophet from our own tribe?" Allah replied: Hood was from the tribe of Aad, yet he was their prophet. Saaleh was from the tribe of Samood, yet he was their prophet.

### Hood's Arrival and Exalted Station

Hazrat Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came 800 years after Hazrat Nooh <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. He lived 464 years in this world before departing for the eternal life.

### Hood's Call to His People

> "He said, 'O my people, worship Allah. You have no deity other than Him. Then will you not fear Him?'"

He said, "Will you not fear?" — meaning he considered their lack of fear as strange. He essentially told them: "You are completely without fear. The punishment of Nooh's people has already been inflicted, which you know about. If you had any fear of Allah's punishment, you would certainly believe in Allah and not commit the foolishness of idol worship."

> "He said, 'O my people, worship Allah. You have no deity other than Him. You are only fabricators. O my people, I do not ask you for any reward for it. My reward is only upon the One who created me. Then will you not reason? And O my people, ask forgiveness of your Lord and then repent to Him. He will send upon you abundant rain from the sky and increase you in strength beyond your strength. And do not turn away as criminals.'"

Hazrat Hood's people were idol worshippers. They had three chief idols: Saam, Samood, and Haba, which they considered their supreme deities. Their land was spread between Yemen and Hadramawt — a desert valley called "Ahqaaf."

### The People of Aad — Their Strength and Deeds

The people of Aad were exceedingly proud of their strength. They would say, "No one is more powerful than us."

> "As for Aad, they were arrogant in the land without right and said, 'Who is greater than us in strength?' Did they not see that Allah, who created them, was greater than them in strength? But they were rejecting Our signs."

The shorter ones among them were 60 cubits (90 feet) tall, and the taller ones 100 cubits (150 feet). Due to their massive bodies and strength, they would boast: "No one is stronger than us. We lift huge stones and boulders from mountains and carry them from one place to another. If punishment comes before us, we will stop it with our own hands."

Allah ta'ala said: "You boast of your strength. Have you ever considered My power? No one has any ability against My power."

### Their Mockery — Building High Landmarks

> "Do you build on every high place a landmark, mocking the wayfarers?"

Various interpretations of this verse:

1. They built high palaces so that passersby would be aware of their status — a useless act, condemned in our Shariah as well. The Prophet ﷺ disapproved of such needlessly tall buildings.

2. They built high structures to guide travelers — but this too was pointless, as guidance comes from the stars and sun.

3. They built high bridges to play with pigeons — spending extravagantly on pigeon racing.

4. They built houses along mountain passes to collect tolls.

5. They built high structures on elevated places to mock travelers and make fun of them. A'la Hazrat Maulana Imam Ahmad Raza Khan <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> adopted this last interpretation.

### Fortified Palaces for Eternal Stay

> "And do you take for yourselves palaces, hoping to live forever?"

Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> told them: "Your ways show that you think you will remain in this world forever, though this world is perishable. Do not attach your hearts to it. You build fortified palaces and reservoirs in the earth, all with the notion that you will live forever in this world."

### Their Oppression of Others

> "And when you seize someone, you seize him brutally."

When they seized someone, they would whip him, strike him with swords, or set cruel oppressors upon him who had no mercy. They had no intention of teaching discipline or seeking a good outcome. Hazrat Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> condemned these evil deeds.

**Benefit:** A teacher's mild hitting or reprimanding a student for the purpose of discipline and good outcome is permissible. Sheikh Sa'di <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> said: "A teacher's strike is better than a parent's love." However, the teacher should consider others' children as his own — would he grab his own child's ear for hours, make him stand like a rooster, strike him severely with sticks until wounded, or abuse him with foul language? Such acts are beneath a teacher's dignity. They only drive students away from schools and make them averse to the religion.

### Hitting with a Stick Is Not Permissible

When children reach ten years of age, if they do not pray, hitting them with the hand is necessary, but hitting with a stick or whip is not permissible. The Prophet ﷺ said: "When your children reach seven years, command them to pray. When they reach ten years, hit them (if they do not pray)."

Allama Shaami <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> says this hitting is by hand, not exceeding two or three slaps. The same rule applies to teachers. The Prophet ﷺ told a male teacher who taught children: "Keep yourself from hitting more than three slaps. If you hit more than three times, Allah will take retribution from you."

Sheikh Sa'di <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> said: "It is best to combine strictness and gentleness. The one who applies leeches both wounds and applies ointment. The wise person is neither too harsh that his respect diminishes, nor too soft that he is humiliated. Neither be a cannibal nor so soft that you degrade yourself. For a teacher, this is the supreme lesson: warn, threaten, reprimand, make them feel, and also show compassion."

### The Response of Hood's People

> "The disbelieving chiefs of his people said, 'Indeed, we see you in foolishness, and indeed, we think you are among the liars.'"

**Note:** Regarding Nooh's people, it is said: "The chiefs of his people said, 'Indeed, we see you in clear error.'" All the chiefs of Nooh's people were disbelievers, so the word *كَفَرُوْا* was not used. But among Hood's people, some chiefs had secretly believed, so *كَفَرُوْا* was used — i.e., the disbelieving chiefs said this.

Just as Nooh's people, seeing him building the ark, called it "clear error" (no water, no mud here, yet building a ship), likewise Hood's people called his condemnation of idol worship "foolishness."

Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> refuted them: "O my people, what foolishness have I to do with? I am a messenger of the Lord of the worlds. I convey to you the messages of my Lord, and I am your sincere advisor."

The noble prophets were called misguided and foolish by their people, but they did not respond in kind. Instead, they responded with forbearance and good character, trying to bring them to the right path — for it befits the noble prophets not to answer foolishness with foolishness, even though reciprocating insults is permissible.

### Hood's Challenge to His People

> "They said, 'O Hood, you have not brought us clear proof, and we are not going to leave our gods on your say-so, nor are we believers in you. We only say that some of our gods have afflicted you with evil.'"

The people lied, saying he had brought no miracles. It is certainly known that Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> did show miracles, but due to their ignorance, they denied them. They also contradicted themselves — on one hand they said their idols could neither benefit nor harm, yet on the other they claimed the idols had afflicted Hood with evil.

> "He said, 'Indeed, I call Allah to witness, and bear witness that I am free from whatever you associate with Allah. So plot against me, all of you, and give me no respite. Indeed, I have relied upon Allah, my Lord and your Lord. There is no creature but He holds it by its forelock. Indeed, my Lord is on a straight path.'"

This is a great miracle of Hazrat Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup>: one man challenging an entire nation — "All of you, together, leave no stone unturned in your enmity toward me, try your utmost to harm me, give me no respite. I have no fear of you. I rely completely on my Lord. He alone is my Protector. All creation is in His grip. No one has the power to oppose Him."

### Hood Warns of Punishment

After exhausting every effort to bring his people to the right path while they remained stubborn in idol worship, Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "O my people, now await the punishment of Allah."

> "Then if you turn away, I have conveyed to you what I was sent with. My Lord will replace you with another people, and you will not harm Him at all. Indeed, my Lord is Guardian over all things."

He said: "I have delivered Allah's messages to you and shown you the path of success. But you persisted in your stubbornness and remained on idol worship. Now the Lord's punishment will come and destroy you. Even if you try to resist it, you cannot. Your destruction does not affect His sovereignty in the least. He can create a new creation in your place that will obey Him."

> "Indeed, I fear for you the punishment of a great Day."

A severe punishment will come upon you in this world, and in the Hereafter you will be afflicted with intense torment. Just as gratitude for blessings increases them, ingratitude for Allah's blessings is a cause of severe punishment.

### The People's Response to the Warning

> "They said, 'It is all the same to us whether you advise or are not among the advisors. This is nothing but the custom of the former peoples, and we are not to be punished.'"

They said: "We care nothing for your warnings. We will not benefit from your preaching. You are just like the previous prophets, threatening us with punishment — this is an old custom. We are people of great strength — punishment cannot harm us."

### They Mistook the Punishment for Mercy

> "Then when they saw it as a cloud approaching their valleys, they said, 'This is a cloud bringing us rain.' Rather, it is that which you were impatient for — a wind containing a painful punishment, destroying everything by the command of its Lord. And they became such that nothing was seen except their dwellings. Thus do We recompense the criminal people."

As mentioned earlier, rain had been withheld from the people of Aad for three years due to their denial of Hood. When the time for punishment came, Allah ta'ala sent black clouds that appeared from their valleys. Such clouds are normally called *mughees* (rain-bringing). The people saw clouds rising from the valleys and rejoiced: "Rain clouds have come! The three-year drought is ending!"

Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> told them: "This is what you were impatient for." For the people used to say, "Bring the punishment if you are truthful."

### What Punishment Came Upon Aad?

> "And as for Aad, they were destroyed by a screaming, violent wind which He imposed upon them for seven nights and eight days, successively. So you see the people lying therein as if they were hollow trunks of palm trees. Then do you see any of them remaining?"

For seven nights and eight days, a fierce windstorm raged continuously — thunder without rain. The very people who rejoiced at the clouds and boasted they would stop the punishment with their strength saw the wind hurling animals and birds through the air. They took refuge in their homes, but all their boasts were in vain.

Allah ta'ala commanded the angel in charge of winds to release upon the people of Aad an amount of wind equal to the size of a ring from His treasuries. Outwardly insignificant, it became a horrific storm. A woman was the first to see it: "I see sparks of fire in the wind." The wind knocked down the doors of their houses.

> "It entered their nostrils and came out from their posteriors. The wind threw them down, their necks breaking. Sometimes it lifted them from the earth and then hurled them down."

They became buried under sand dunes. This continued for seven nights and eight days without pause. They were all destroyed. Those who were tall and proud of their strength — when caught in the Lord's grip, they perished so completely that their dead bodies looked like fallen palm trunks.

Consider Allah's great power: where the disbelievers were destroyed, Hood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and the believers were saved. Hood drew a line around himself and his followers. That fierce wind felt to them like the gentle, cool breezes of spring. The disbelievers were falling and perishing, their necks breaking, buried under sand — while the people of Allah, those who believed in the prophet, were enjoying the same wind.

SubhanAllah! How can man fathom the power of the Master of the universe? He alone knows His wisdom.

> "And when Our command came, We saved Hood and those who believed with him, by mercy from Us, and We saved them from a severe punishment."

The disbelievers were afflicted with severe punishment in this world, and on Judgment Day they will be afflicted with intense torment. The believers were protected by Allah in this world and will be protected in the Hereafter. Denying a prophet and showing disrespect to him is synonymous with inviting destructive punishment. May Allah grant us the ability to remain firm on faith and protect us from disrespect toward the noble prophets.
