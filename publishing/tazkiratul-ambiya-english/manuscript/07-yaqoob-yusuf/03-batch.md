### The Effects of Yusuf's Rule

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> established his government on the foundations of justice and fairness. His rule grew stronger day by day, and love for him was planted in the hearts of all men and women.

He implemented the interpretation of the king's dream: during the seven years of abundance, he built vast storehouses and gathered immense reserves of grain. When the years of abundance passed and the famine arrived, he rationed food — the king and his servants received only one meal per day.

One day at noon, the king complained to him of hunger. Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said, "This is only the beginning of the famine years." He himself never ate to fullness throughout the entire famine. When those close to him said, "You own such vast treasures, yet you remain hungry?" he replied, "I fear that if I become full, I might forget hunger."

SubhanAllah! What pure character! Because of this, even the king accepted the ration without objection.

All the people of Egypt began buying grain from Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. In the first year, all their gold and silver came to him. In the second year, they bought grain with their jewelry and gems. In the third year, they gave their livestock. In the fourth year, their slaves and maidservants. In the fifth year, their estates and lands. By the sixth year, they had nothing left but their own lives and children. In the seventh year, the people themselves entered into his servitude.

There was no man or woman in Egypt who had not entered his servitude. Everyone's tongue proclaimed: "No king has attained the greatness and majesty that Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> has."

Then Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said to the king: "You have seen Allah's favor upon me. Now what is your opinion regarding them?" The king replied, "Whatever you decide — we are your followers." Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> announced: "I make Allah my witness, and you my witness — I free all the people of Egypt and return all their property and estates to them."

### The Wisdom Behind This

Allah ta'ala's wisdom in having all the people become his servants was so that no one could say, "Yusuf came as a slave, bought by a man of Egypt." Now the entire Egyptian people were his purchased and freed servants. It was to demonstrate that a free person who enters servitude due to circumstances does not truly become a slave.

### Zulaikha's Wish Fulfilled

The king had dismissed the former Aziz of Egypt (Qatfeer), who soon died. His wife — who had tried to seduce Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> but failed — had already confessed her error after his release from prison and testified to his truthfulness. After her husband's death, the king married her to Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Upon meeting her, Yusuf said, "Is this not better than what you were demanding?"

Note that since the first Aziz was impotent, she was still a virgin. Her love was only for Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — she was not an immoral woman. From her, Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had two sons: Afra'im and Meesha.

**Note:** The wife of Aziz was named Zulaikha, but the name is not mentioned explicitly in the Quran. It is not proper to unnecessarily mention a woman's name; modesty is more appropriate. This teaches that a man should not call his wife by name but address her in terms that do not explicitly state her name. In the Holy Quran, no woman's name is mentioned except that of Hazrat Maryam <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup>, and her name was mentioned only because people called her (God forbid) the wife of Allah, so Allah ta'ala mentioned her name to refute them.

### The Brothers Go for Grain

> "And the brothers of Yusuf came and entered upon him, and he recognized them, but they did not recognize him. And when he had furnished them with their provisions, he said, 'Bring me your brother from your father. Do you not see that I give full measure and that I am the best of hosts? But if you do not bring him to me, no measure will be for you with me, nor should you come near me.'"

Canaan was also affected by the famine. Like others, the sons of Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> loaded their pack animals and set out for Egypt. The distribution of grain was entirely supervised by Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, so they came to his court and presented their request.

Although many years had passed since they last met, Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> recognized his brothers immediately — but they could not recognize him. How could they? It could never enter their imagination that this man in royal dress, adorned with gold, seated on a throne with hundreds of thousands of servants standing with folded hands — this was the Yusuf they had thrown into a dark well and sold to a caravan for a mere twenty silver coins.

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> did not let his emotions get the better of him. As a stranger, he inquired about their family circumstances. From their own words, he learned that they had left another brother at home.

He gave them their portion, then said: "Next time, bring your brother with you. Do you not see how generous and just I am — I fill the measure to overflowing — and how great a host I am? But know this: if you do not bring him, you will receive no grain."

He also ordered his servants to secretly place their money back into their packs.

### Why Yusuf Returned Their Money

**First reason:** When they open their belongings and find the money, they would return it — because they are the sons of a prophet, men of faith, and would consider it their responsibility to return.

**Second reason:** When they find the money in their packs, they would think, "Perhaps he put it there by mistake." They would return to investigate the matter.

**Third reason:** Having learned of the family's circumstances, it was beneath Yusuf's dignity to accept money from his father and brothers for grain.

### The Request to Bring the Younger Brother Before the Father

> "And when they opened their baggage, they found their money returned to them. They said, 'O our father, what more could we desire? This is our money returned to us. We will get provisions for our family, protect our brother, and receive an extra camel-load — that is easy for the king.' He said, 'I will never send him with you until you give me a solemn pledge by Allah that you will surely bring him back to me, unless you are surrounded.' And when they gave him their pledge, he said, 'Allah is witness over what we say.'"

Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "You undertook responsibility for Yusuf before, and look what happened." Yet he ultimately decided to send Binyamin. Why?

They had grown older and inclined toward righteousness. They bore no envy toward Binyamin as they had toward Yusuf. The famine compelled them to obtain grain, and Yusuf had refused to give grain without Binyamin. But the greatest reason was that Allah ta'ala informed him through revelation: "Send him. I will protect him and return him to you." This is why he said, "Allah is the best guardian, and He is the most merciful of the merciful."

Some scholars say his words meant: "I trusted your word before regarding Yusuf's protection. Now I place no reliance on your promises. But I send him with you, entrusting him to Allah's protection."

### Yaqoob's Precautions When Sending His Sons

> "And he said, 'O my sons, do not enter by one gate, but enter by separate gates. And I cannot avail you against Allah at all. The decision is only Allah's. Upon Him I rely, and upon Him let the reliant rely.' And when they entered as their father had instructed them, it did not avail them against Allah at all, but it fulfilled a need in Yaqoob's soul."

Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> instructed them to enter through different gates of the city so as not to attract the evil eye — for a large group entering together might draw attention.

### Binyamin's Meeting with Yusuf

> "And when they entered upon Yusuf, he took his brother to himself and said, 'Indeed, I am your brother. Do not grieve over what they used to do.'"

When Binyamin stood before Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, the vizier's heart was moved. He took Binyamin aside privately, revealed his identity, and comforted him: "Do not grieve over what they did to me in the past."

### How Was Theft Alleged Against Yusuf?

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> needed a way to keep Binyamin with him. He arranged the famous incident of the king's cup.

> "And when he had furnished them with their provisions, he placed the drinking cup in his brother's bag. Then an announcer called out, 'O people of the caravan, indeed you are thieves.' They said, while approaching them, 'What is it you are missing?' They said, 'We are missing the king's cup. Whoever produces it will receive a camel-load, and I am responsible for it.'"

Some narrations say an angel called out. Others say it was Yusuf's men who made the announcement according to his plan.

### The Accusation of Theft

The brothers protested: "We have not come to spread corruption in the land, nor are we thieves."

Yusuf's men asked, "What should be the punishment if you are found lying?"

They replied, "According to our father's religion, the punishment for theft is that the thief himself becomes the property of the one from whom he stole."

### Binyamin Is Detained

> "So he began with their bags before the bag of his brother. Then he extracted it from the bag of his brother. Thus did We plan for Yusuf. He could not have taken his brother by the law of the king except that Allah willed. We raise in degrees whom We will, and above every man of knowledge is the All-Knowing."

When the cup was found in Binyamin's bag, the brothers were astonished. Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had not taken Binyamin through the king's law (which would have been imprisonment or flogging) but through the religious law of Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, which made the thief a slave to the victim.

### The Brothers' Plea

The brothers said: "If he has stolen, a brother of his stole before." They alluded to Yusuf. Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> controlled himself and said nothing aloud, but thought: "You are worse in position, and Allah knows best what you describe."

They pleaded: "O Aziz, he has an aged father — take one of us in his place." But Yusuf refused: "Allah forbid that we take anyone but him with whom we found our property."

### The Eldest Brother Stays Behind

> "And when they despaired of him, they conferred privately. The eldest of them said, 'Do you not know that your father has taken from you a solemn pledge by Allah, and before that you failed in your duty regarding Yusuf? So I will never leave this land until my father permits me or Allah judges for me — and He is the best of judges.'"

The eldest brother (some say Reuben, others say Judah or Simeon) refused to return without Binyamin. He remained in Egypt while the others went back to Canaan.

### Yaqoob's Grief

> "And they returned to their father and said, 'O our father, the measure has been denied us. Send our brother with us so we may obtain the measure, and we will surely protect him.' He said, 'Should I entrust you with him as I entrusted you with his brother before? Allah is the best guardian, and He is the most merciful of the merciful.'"

Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup>'s grief intensified. He was already blind from weeping for Yusuf, and now Binyamin was also gone.

> "And he turned away from them and said, 'Oh, my sorrow for Yusuf!' And his eyes became white from grief, and he was suppressing his anger."

### The Strange Wisdom of Yaqoob's Weeping

Allah ta'ala gave Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> the appearance of Yusuf — he would see him in his sleep. This external appearance had the same glow as Yusuf's own face. Each time Yaqoob looked at the vision, he would weep out of longing for the actual meeting. This is why his eyes weakened — not from ordinary weeping, but from the intensity of looking at a spiritual light.

### The Sons Speak to Their Father

Seeing his distress, the sons said: "By Allah, you will not stop remembering Yusuf until you are destroyed or die."

Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> replied: "I only complain of my anguish and grief to Allah, and I know from Allah what you do not know."

He told them: "Go, seek news of Yusuf and his brother. Do not despair of Allah's mercy. None despairs of Allah's mercy except the disbelieving people."

### The Sons Return to Egypt

They went back to Egypt and appealed to Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>: "O Aziz, adversity has afflicted us and our family. We have brought but meager goods. Give us full measure and be charitable to us. Indeed, Allah rewards the charitable."

At this moment, Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> felt the time had come. He asked them: "Do you know what you did with Yusuf and his brother when you were ignorant?"

They froze. They stared at him and asked, "Are you indeed Yusuf?"

> "They said, 'Are you indeed Yusuf?' He said, 'I am Yusuf, and this is my brother. Allah has certainly been gracious to us. Indeed, whoever fears Allah and is patient — then indeed, Allah does not allow the reward of the doers of good to be lost.'"

### Yusuf Reveals Himself

When Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> revealed himself, the brothers were filled with shame and regret. They said, "By Allah, Allah has preferred you over us, and we were indeed sinners."

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> responded with immense magnanimity: "No blame upon you today. May Allah forgive you, and He is the most merciful of the merciful."

### The Shirt and Yaqoob's Sight

> "Go with this shirt of mine and cast it upon my father's face; he will recover his sight. And bring me your family, all together."

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> gave his shirt to the brothers and said: "Throw this over my father's face — his sight will return. Then bring all your family to me."

### Which Shirt Was It?

Some narrations say it was the same shirt Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had brought for Yusuf from Paradise when he was in the well — the shirt of Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> that was woven in Paradise, the very shirt that was placed on Ibrahim when he was thrown into the fire.

As the caravan departed Egypt, Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said from afar: "I sense the fragrance of Yusuf, if you do not think me senile."

Those present said, "By Allah, you are still in your old confusion."

When the bearer of good news arrived, he cast the shirt over Yaqoob's face, and his sight returned immediately. Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "Did I not tell you that I know from Allah what you do not know?"

According to Hazrat Qatada <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>, what those present had meant by "you are in your old confusion" was: "You still cannot forget your old love for Yusuf — he has not left your heart even after all these years."

The one who brought the shirt was the son Yahuda. Hazrat Mujahid <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates: Yahuda said to his brothers, "You know that I was the one who brought the blood-stained shirt to our father that grieved him. Now let me be the one to bring the shirt that brings him joy." The brothers agreed.

When Yahuda placed the shirt on his father's eyes, his sight was fully restored.

### The Sons Seek Forgiveness

> "They said, 'O our father, ask forgiveness for our sins. Indeed, we were sinners.' He said, 'I will ask my Lord for forgiveness for you. Indeed, He is the Forgiving, the Merciful.'"

The sons addressed him as "our father" — you are our father, compassionate to us. We are sinners. If you do not pray for us, who will have mercy on us?

Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> promised to seek forgiveness for them. He delayed it until the night before Friday, as that time has the greatest acceptance. He did not pray immediately because he was waiting for the time when prayers are quickly answered.

This is a point of reflection for those who consider praying for the deceased on Thursday evening (the night of Friday) to be an innovation. Would that they knew: the increased likelihood of acceptance on the night of Friday has come down from the noble prophets themselves.

### The Family Migrates to Egypt

> "Then when they entered upon Yusuf, he embraced his parents and said, 'Enter Egypt, if Allah wills, in security.' And he raised his parents upon the throne, and they fell down before him in prostration. And he said, 'O my father, this is the interpretation of my dream of old. My Lord has made it come true. And He was gracious to me — He brought me out of prison and brought you from the countryside after Satan had sown discord between me and my brothers. Indeed, my Lord is subtle in what He wills. Indeed, He is the All-Knowing, the Wise.'"

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> sent 200 mounts with abundant provisions for his father, family, and household. Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> set out for Egypt with 72 or 73 men and women.

When Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> drew near to Egypt, Yusuf informed the great king of Egypt of his father's arrival. He rode out with 4,000 soldiers and many Egyptian horsemen to welcome his revered father. Silk canopies fluttered in the air, and banners were tied.

Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was leaning on his son Yahuda when he saw the great army approaching. He asked, "O Yahuda, is that the Pharaoh of Egypt whose army approaches with such splendor?" Yahuda replied, "No, it is your son Yusuf coming to welcome you."

Note: At that time Yusuf was the Aziz of Egypt — the grand vizier. The commentators have used the word *مَلك* (malik, king) for him, but the distinction is: Yusuf was the deputy king, while Walid bin Riyaan was the supreme monarch.

Hazrat Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> saw Yaqoob's amazement and said: "Look toward the sky — angels have gathered for your joy, who wept for your grief for ages." The angels' tasbeeh and the horses' neighing created a wondrous atmosphere. This was the 10th of Muharram.

When the two prophets drew near, Yusuf intended to greet first, but Jibreel said: "Wait — let your father greet first, to show that Yaqoob is more honored in Allah's presence than Yusuf." So Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "O Remover of grief and sorrow, peace be upon you."

They embraced and Yaqoob kissed Yusuf.

### Benefit: Embracing and Kissing on Occasions of Joy

Embracing and kissing one's beloved on occasions of joy is the sunnah of the prophets. Those who call embracing on Eid an innovation are ignorant of the religion. When embracing is proven permissible on one joyful occasion, it is established for all such occasions.

Numerous hadith establish this: The Prophet ﷺ embraced Ja'far when he returned from Abyssinia. He kissed Hasan bin Ali. The Companions kissed the Prophet's hands and feet. Aisha <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> said she never saw anyone more resembling the Prophet ﷺ than Fatima <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> in conduct, and when Fatima entered upon the Prophet, he would stand, hold her hand, kiss her, and seat her in his place, and she would do likewise.

### Yusuf's Childhood Dream Fulfilled

When Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and his family arrived in Egypt, Yusuf seated them on his throne. His parents and eleven brothers prostrated to him. This was the fulfillment of his childhood dream: the sun, moon, and eleven stars prostrating to him.

Some scholars say: "Allah ta'ala resurrected Yusuf's mother from the grave so that she too could prostrate to Yusuf and complete the fulfillment of the dream."

**Objection:** Although Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is a prophet, Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is greater in rank. How could a father prostrate to his son?

**Answer:** According to a narration from Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنهما</sup>, the meaning of the verse is: "Due to meeting Yusuf, all prostrated to Allah" — it was actually prostration to Allah in gratitude for the reunion. If it had been prostration to Yusuf, it would have been done before he seated them on the throne. Alternatively, Yaqoob prostrated to Yusuf to set an example so that the brothers would also prostrate without hesitation, or the prostration was Allah's command, and only He knows the full wisdom — just as He commanded the angels to prostrate to Adam.

### How Much Yusuf Cared for His Brothers

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> did not say, "My brothers oppressed me, they wronged me." Instead, he said: "The discord between me and my brothers was created by Satan." He carried no rancor in his heart. He considered the reunion a favor from Allah.

SubhanAllah! Such magnanimity can only be attained by a prophet. A sinner like us cannot even imagine such generosity and forgiveness.

### Yusuf Shows His Father the Royal Treasuries

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> took his father by the hand and showed him all the treasuries: the gold treasury, the silver treasury, the jewelry treasury, the clothing treasury, and the weapons treasury. Finally, when he showed his father the treasury of documents and correspondence, Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "O my dear son, with all these documents, you could not write me even one letter?"

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> replied: "Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> forbade me."

Yaqoob asked: "Why did he forbid you?"

Yusuf said: "You are more closely connected to Jibreel — ask him yourself."

Yaqoob asked Jibreel, who replied: "You yourself said, 'I fear that a wolf may eat him.' Your own words were used by your sons. But above all, it was a trial. The noble prophets have such exalted stations that their trials match their rank."

### Yaqoob's Death and Grave

Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> remained in Egypt for 24 years. When his death drew near, he instructed his sons: "Bury me in Syria, beside my father Is'haq <sup>عليه السلام</sup>."

When he passed away, Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> himself carried his father's pure body to Syria and buried him beside his grandfather Is'haq <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Yaqoob's twin brother "Aa'is" had died on the same day both were born, at age 145. Both were buried together beside their father.

Yaqoob's coffin was made of a special wood and carried from Egypt to Syria.

### Yusuf's Death

After Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> lived another 23 years. When his time of departure approached, he became increasingly focused on the Hereafter, longing for the eternal abode. He supplicated:

"O my Lord, You have granted me a kingdom and taught me the interpretation of events. O Creator of the heavens and earth, You are my Protector in this world and the Hereafter. Cause me to die a Muslim and join me with the righteous."

This is not a request for death, which is impermissible to wish for. Rather, he knew through revelation that his time was near, so he prayed to be joined with Allah's close ones after death.

### Yusuf's Grave

After his death, a dispute arose among the Egyptians. Each faction wanted him buried in their district so they could receive blessings from him. It nearly sparked fighting. Eventually, some people decided to place his pure body in a marble coffin and bury it in the River Nile, so that all the city's people could benefit equally from the water.

Hazrat Akrama <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates: First, he was buried on the right side of the Nile. That side became lush and green, while the other side remained dry. Then the coffin was moved to the left side — prosperity shifted there. Finally, he was buried in the middle of the Nile, and both sides became equally fertile.

His pure body remained in the Nile for 400 years. When Musa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> led the Children of Israel out of Egypt, he took Yusuf's body with him and buried it with his forefathers in Syria.

SubhanAllah! What pure beliefs those people had — they knew that just as blessings are obtained from a prophet during his lifetime, blessings are also obtained after his departure from this world.

### Yusuf's Children

At the time of his death, Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> left behind two sons and one daughter. The sons were named Afra'im and Meesha. Afra'im's son was Noon, and Noon's son was Yoosha'a bin Noon, who lived until the time of Musa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and accompanied him on the sea journey — Musa took him along when going to meet Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Yusuf's daughter was named Rehma, who married Hazrat Ayyub <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.

After Yusuf's departure from this world, the rule of Egypt came into the hands of the Amalqa (Amalekites). The Children of Israel remained under their subjugation until Musa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came and delivered them.

These were the unseen tidings that Allah granted to Hazrat Muhammad ﷺ and said: "These are some of the news of the unseen which We reveal to you."
