### The Seduction of Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — The Clear Proof

> "And she, in whose house he was, sought to seduce him and closed the doors firmly and said, 'Come to me!'" He said, "I seek refuge in Allah. Indeed, he is my master who has given me honorable residence. Indeed, the wrongdoers do not prosper." And she certainly desired him, and he would have desired her if he had not seen the clear proof of his Lord. Thus it was, that We might turn away from him evil and immorality. Indeed, he was of Our chosen servants.

Although Zulaikha wanted to trap him in sin, the prophets of Allah are pure from all sins, minor and major, both before and after prophethood. Therefore, Allah ta'ala showed him a clear proof and kept him pure and clean.

**What proof did he see?** One, he saw that the woman was closing the doors and covering her idol — the one she had made her deity, adorned with pearls and rubies. He asked her, "Why are you covering it?" She said, "I feel ashamed before my deity that it should see me engaged in evil." He said, "Your deity has no power — you feel ashamed before it, but should I not feel ashamed before the True Deity who sees every action of every person? Do not pin your hopes on me. You can never succeed in your desire with me."

The greatest clear proof was this: Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates that the image of Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was shown to Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, and he struck him on the chest.

Hazrat Qatada <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates: "We have been told that Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> appeared to Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, biting his fingers in distress and saying, 'O Yusuf! Beware — such deeds are done by fools. Your name has already been written among the noble prophets.'"

SubhanAllah! What power Allah ta'ala has granted His noble prophets, and how they assist their close ones and the members of their nation! After knowing of Yusuf's vindication and Yaqoob's assistance, if anyone still persists in denying the assistance of the noble prophets out of stubbornness, it is his own misfortune. By Allah, do not ruin your end!

### Clarification Regarding Translations

At this point, one should not be misled by certain translations that show disrespect toward the Prophet's station. Some have written: "He intended," others wrote: "He also began to feel some inclination toward that woman," still others wrote: "And he also had some thought of her." Such translations yield nothing but misguidance. Look instead at translations that strengthen faith. The translation I have quoted is from *Zia ul Quran* by Peer Karam Shah <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>, and also the translation of A'la Hazrat Maulana Ahmad Raza Khan Barelvi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>: "Indeed, the woman intended him, and he too would have intended her if he had not seen the proof of his Lord."

These are the correct translations. I have detailed this in my book *Taskeenul Jinaan Fi Mahaasine Kanzul Imaan*, where I have also written the Arabic texts of the commentaries. Here I present a brief summary from *Tafseer Kabeer* in Urdu.

### Allama Raazi's Decisive Statement

Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> says: If such a sin (i.e., intending zinā) were attributed to even the greatest of sinners among Allah's creation, and to one who stays far from all good deeds, even he would feel shame. So how can such a sin be attributed to a magnificent Messenger who has been granted tremendous miracles?

After this, he elaborates further, stating that those connected to this incident are: Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, the wife of Aziz of Egypt, Aziz himself, the women of Egypt, the witness, and Allah Rabbul Aalameen. All of them testified that he was free from sin — even Satan testified to his innocence. When so many testimonies exist for his innocence, what reason does a Muslim have to hesitate?

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> himself mentioned his purity: "Did she not desire that I should not protect myself?" Similarly, he said, "O my Lord, prison is dearer to me than that (evil) to which they invite me."

These statements of Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> clearly establish his purity.

### The Testimony of the Woman (Zulaikha)

Even the woman who accused him admitted to Yusuf's innocence. Before the women of Egypt, she confessed: "I tried to seduce him, but he protected himself."

Similarly, she said, "Now the truth has come out — I tried to seduce him, and indeed he is truthful." After this testimony, it became clear that she too had declared Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> innocent.

### The Testimony of Her Husband (Aziz)

Her husband, Aziz of Egypt, said: "This is the guile of you women. Indeed, the guile of women is great. O Yusuf, disregard this. And O woman, seek forgiveness for your sin."

This is the testimony of her own husband regarding Yusuf's innocence.

### The Testimony of the Witness

The witness's testimony establishes Yusuf's innocence from any false act or intention of sin, for it was the testimony of a suckling infant:

> "And a witness from her household testified: If his shirt is torn from the front, then she is truthful and he is lying. But if his shirt is torn from the back, then he is truthful and she is lying."

Yusuf's shirt was torn from the back, so the testimony of his innocence was established. The witness was from the woman's own household and was still a suckling infant. For this reason, the woman's husband called women deceitful.

### The Testimony of Allah ta'ala

Allah ta'ala bore witness to Yusuf's innocence, saying: "Thus it was, that We might turn away from him evil and immorality. Indeed, he was of Our chosen servants." The testimony that Allah ta'ala has given in this verse is fourfold:

**First:** Allah ta'ala said *لِنَصْرِفَ عَنْهُ السُّوءَ* — the letter *laam* here is for emphasis and intensification, making it certain that evil is kept away from him.

**Second:** Allah ta'ala mentioned *وَالْفَحْشَاءَ* — "and immorality" — i.e., *كَذٰلِكَ لِنَصْرِفَ عَنْهُ السُّوءَ وَالْفَحْشَاءَ*. When Allah ta'ala has taken it upon Himself to keep immorality away from him, then committing evil or even thinking of it becomes impossible.

**Third:** Allah ta'ala said *إِنَّهُ مِنْ عِبَادِنَا الْمُخْلَصِينَ* — "Indeed, he was of Our chosen servants." And Allah ta'ala has described His chosen servants thus: "The servants of the Merciful are those who walk on the earth gently, and when the ignorant address them, they say, 'Peace.'" This shows that one whom the Lord has called His chosen servant cannot commit evil.

**Fourth:** The word *الْمُخْلَصِينَ* has two possible readings — as an active participle or a passive participle. If active, it means he is one who acts with sincerity in obedience and closeness to Allah. If passive, it means Allah has made him pure for Himself and chosen him in His presence. Either way, his purity from intending sin is clear.

### The Confession of Iblees (Satan)

Iblees also confessed to Yusuf's purity. When he sought respite from Allah ta'ala and was granted it until the Day of Judgment, he said, "O Allah, by Your glory, I will mislead all except Your chosen servants." His confession makes clear that it is impossible for Satan to lead Allah's chosen servants astray. And since Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is among the *مخلصين* (chosen servants) — as Allah ta'ala has testified — if one does not accept the Lord's testimony, whose testimony will he accept?

### Allama Raazi's Conclusive Statement

Those ignorant ones who attribute evil (or the intention of evil) to Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>: if they are followers of Allah's religion, let them accept Allah's testimony of his purity. And if they are followers of Satan or his army, let them accept Satan's testimony of his purity.

### Zulaikha's Accusation

> "And they both raced toward the door, and she tore his shirt from the back, and they found her husband at the door. She said, 'What is the punishment for one who intended evil toward your wife — except imprisonment or a painful punishment?'"

When the woman confined him inside, Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> began running toward the door to open it and escape. The woman ran after him to catch him. She could not succeed in catching him, but she grabbed his shirt from behind. Since he was running, the shirt tore from the back due to her grabbing it. At that moment, the woman's husband arrived at the door. Fearing accusation, she quickly tried to impute her fault to Yusuf: "He intended evil toward your wife, so send him to prison or give him a severe punishment."

Since the woman had extreme love for Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, although she imputed the fault to him to save herself, she still showed leniency toward him — she mentioned prison first and severe punishment later. A lover does not wish to cause pain to the beloved, so she did not say "choose whichever of the two you wish." Rather, she tried to save him as much as possible: "Send him to prison. Yes, if there is no other option but punishment, then punish."

She did not mention any words suggesting life imprisonment or a long term. She only said "send him to prison," meaning perhaps release him after a day or two.

### Zulaikha Did Not Explicitly Attribute Evil to Him

The woman said, "One who intended evil toward your wife" — she did not say "he intended evil toward me," nor did she say "he assaulted me" or "he committed evil." She knew that someone who remains pure despite being in the prime of youth with full strength and desire — how could he be slandered? In her heart, his purity had become deeply rooted. She felt ashamed to explicitly declare him guilty of evil.

Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> says: That woman felt ashamed to declare him guilty of evil, but those who came thousands of years later feel no shame in saying "he intended evil" or "he had begun to feel some inclination." Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> initially remained silent, but when he saw evil being attributed to him, he said, "She herself tried to seduce me."

### Evidence Pointing to Yusuf's Innocence

1. He was apparently her slave. A slave can never dare to forcibly commit evil with his master's wife.

2. Aziz of Egypt and others in the house saw Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> running desperately toward the door to escape. They understood that one who desires evil never runs away.

3. They also saw that this woman had adorned herself, while Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was in ordinary clothes. They understood who was trying to seduce whom.

4. They had observed Yusuf's character over a long period. His habits and conduct were not hidden from them. They themselves understood that Yusuf could never intend evil.

5. The woman did not explicitly attribute evil to him; she said "if someone does such a thing." But Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> explicitly attributed it to her. This shows he was truthful, because a liar fears attributing such accusations to others.

6. Aziz of Egypt was impotent — the woman's sexual desires were not being fulfilled by him at all. Given these circumstances, it was clear that the inclination was from her side.

### The Testimony of the Infant

> "He said, 'She tried to seduce me, that I should protect myself.' And a witness from her household testified: 'If his shirt is torn from the front, then she is truthful and he is lying. But if his shirt is torn from the back, then she is lying and he is truthful.' When he (Aziz) saw that his shirt was torn from the back, he said, 'Indeed, this is the guile of you women. Indeed, your guile is great. O Yusuf, disregard this. And O woman, seek forgiveness for your sin. Indeed, you are among the sinful.'"

The one who testified was a young boy from the woman's family, only three months old — still a suckling infant. Allah ta'ala gave him the power of speech and made him testify, thus declaring Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> innocent of the accusation. The fact that a child of that age could speak was enough to prove his innocence, but Allah also gave the child such a wise answer that it became a strong proof in itself.

A shirt torn from the front would mean that Yusuf had intended evil and she had resisted, grabbing his collar and pushing him back, causing the front to tear. If that had been the case, the woman would have been proven truthful. But the tear from the back made it clear that Yusuf had been forcibly confined in the room, that he ran to free himself, and that she tried to grab him from behind, causing the shirt to tear from the back. On this very proof, Aziz of Egypt told him, "You are truthful — do not grieve," and told his wife, "This is your guile. Seek forgiveness for your sin."

### The Women of Egypt Reproach Aziz's Wife

> "And women in the city said, 'The wife of Aziz seeks to seduce her young slave. Indeed, love has penetrated her heart. We see her clearly in manifest error.'"

This incident became famous throughout the city. Many women began saying, "How mistaken is the wife of Aziz, loving her household slave so intensely that she sees nothing else. Love has surrounded her heart like a sheath envelops its contents. That love has become a veil between her heart and everything else — she is aware of nothing but that young man. We see her clearly astray in loving this young man."

### Aziz's Wife Defends Herself in a Strange Manner

> "So when she heard of their scheming, she sent for them and prepared couches for them, and gave each of them a knife. Then she said (to Yusuf), 'Come out before them.' When they saw him, they exalted him and cut their hands and said, 'Holy is Allah! This is not a human being — this is none other than a noble angel.' She said, 'This is the one about whom you reproached me. I did indeed try to seduce him, but he protected himself. And if he does not do what I command him, he shall surely be imprisoned and will be among the humiliated.'"

There are three reasons why the wife of Aziz called the women's talk "scheming" (مَكْر):

**First:** When the women heard of this incident, they too began to desire seeing Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. They also knew that when Zulaikha would mention them, she would certainly present Yusuf before them to defend herself. So, by working against her, they devised a pretext to see Yusuf — this was called "scheming."

**Second:** Aziz's wife had mentioned her love to these women and had also asked them to keep this secret. But when the women made it public, her deception was called "scheming."

**Third:** They had backbitten her, and backbiting resembles scheming.

### The Women Cut Their Hands Upon Seeing Yusuf

Aziz's wife invited the women who had been speaking about her. They were the wives of great chieftains, forty in number. Among them were five women who had spoken the most:

1. The wife of the water-bearer of Aziz
2. The woman who baked bread — who had not seen Yusuf in all that time
3. The wife of the chief jailer
4. The wife of the keeper of Aziz's animals
5. The wife of the gatekeeper

In the gathering, seating was arranged for each. Cushions were laid out, various foods were placed on the tablecloth, and fruits were set out. Knives were also placed for cutting fruits — especially lemons — and meat. One knife was placed before each seat. When the women arrived and took their places, Aziz's wife commanded Yusuf to come out of his room and pass before the gathering of women so they could catch a glimpse of him.

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had already fallen victim to one scheme of that woman; Allah had vindicated him. Now the same fear arose in his heart — that disobeying her command might bring some new calamity. Compelled, he had to come out and pass before the women.

When Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> passed before them, "They were so stunned upon seeing him that they began cutting the fruits with knives, but they did not even realize whether they were cutting fruits or their own hands. They cut and wounded their own hands." They were so mesmerized by his appearance that they did not know whether they were holding the knives correctly or reversed — they turned the sharp side toward their hands and the blunt side toward the fruit, or in their intent to cut fruit they cut their own hands.

It is also possible that some women turned the knives the wrong way and cut their hands, while others cut through the fruit and continued cutting into their hands.

### The Cause of Their Wounds Was Being Enraptured by Yusuf's Beauty

"There is consensus among most scholars that these women held Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> in highest esteem due to his superb beauty and perfect comeliness. This was the reason they cut their hands."

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was granted such excellence in beauty over others as the full moon of the fourteenth night excels the stars. The Prophet ﷺ said: "On the night of Mi'raj, when I was taken through the heavens, I passed near Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. I asked, 'Who is this?' Jibreel said, 'This is Yusuf.' He was asked, 'O Messenger of Allah, how did you find him?' He replied, 'Like the full moon of the fourteenth night.'"

When Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> walked through the streets of Egypt, due to the radiance of his face, the walls would become illuminated just as light spreads from the sun's rays.

The same beauty that was granted to Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> on the day he was created by divine power was granted to Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.

The greatest reason was that these women saw the light of prophethood and messengership in him, and they observed the effects of humility and devotion. They witnessed the awe and majesty of prophethood. When they saw that this person was angelic in nature — he did not look at food, he did not look at young women, he did not care for them at all — both his outward form and inner character were magnificent. Seeing this, they were astonished, enraptured, unable to maintain their senses. They concluded that this was no ordinary human — this was a near-station angel. And why should it not be so, when the station of a prophet is higher than that of angels?

### Our Prophet ﷺ Is More Beautiful Than the Moon

Hazrat Jabir <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> says: "I saw the Prophet ﷺ on a moonlit night. Sometimes I looked at the Prophet ﷺ and sometimes at the moon, while he was wearing a garment with red stripes. He appeared more beautiful to me than the moon."

### Yusuf's Supplication in Prison and Its Acceptance

> "He said, 'O my Lord, prison is dearer to me than that to which they invite me. And if You do not turn away their guile from me, I will incline toward them and be among the ignorant.' So his Lord responded to him and turned away their guile from him. Indeed, He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing. Then, after they had seen all the signs, it seemed proper to them that he should be imprisoned for a time."

When the wife of Aziz said before the women of Egypt, "If he does not obey me, he will certainly be imprisoned and humiliated," all the women who had been invited collectively began to persuade Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. They said, "It is better for you not to disobey her command, because disobedience will lead to imprisonment and humiliation."

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> now faced several difficulties:

1. Aziz's wife was extremely beautiful and graceful.
2. She possessed wealth and riches, and she was determined to sacrifice all her wealth to achieve her goal with Yusuf.
3. All the women were collectively tempting him while also instilling fear. In such circumstances, the guile of women is very great.
4. He also feared that opposing her would make it very difficult to escape her harm — she might even have him killed.

Considering these circumstances, he deemed it best: "If they send me to prison, that would be better for me."

For a person to save himself in such circumstances through his own human power and strength is very difficult. Therefore, he prayed: "O Allah, only You can save me from the guile of these women. If Your grace is not with me, a person falls into such sins and becomes ignorant. O Allah, compared to what they are inviting me to do, prison is better for me."

Note that Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> chose the difficult path. Allah ta'ala revealed to him: "O Yusuf, you have chosen a difficult path for yourself. If you had said, 'I prefer well-being (over what they invite me to),' you would have been granted well-being."

For this reason, the Prophet ﷺ rejected the person who was praying for patience during calamities. In Tirmidhi, Hazrat Mu'az bin Jabal <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates that the Prophet ﷺ heard a man saying, "O Allah, I ask You for patience" — i.e., grant me the ability to bear calamities and afflictions. The Prophet ﷺ said, "You have asked Allah for calamity. Ask Allah for well-being."

### Yusuf Was Sent to Prison

Aziz of Egypt and all the other high-ranking officials had become convinced of Yusuf's innocence. "The signs pointing to his purity and freedom from faults had been seen by everyone." However, considering outward expediency, they decided to send him to prison. Apart from Aziz's wife, other women had also fallen in love with him. House to house, Yusuf's beauty was being discussed. People had failed to restrain their women. They saw sending him to prison as a solution — but in reality, it was the effect of his own supplication.

"Hazrat Ali ibn Husain, i.e., Hazrat Zainul Abideen <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنهما</sup> narrates that every woman who had been invited to the gathering had secretly sent him messages demanding a meeting."

Similarly, Aziz's wife told her husband: "This Hebrew slave is defaming me, saying that I tried to seduce him but he escaped. Since he can go outside while I am confined to the house, either give me permission to go to the markets and answer him, or imprison him." Thus, Aziz and the other ministers had him imprisoned.

### Two Prisoners Ask Yusuf to Interpret Their Dreams

> "And two young men entered the prison with him. One of them said, 'I saw myself pressing wine.' The other said, 'I saw myself carrying bread on my head from which birds were eating.' (They said), 'Inform us of its interpretation. Indeed, we see you among the doers of good.'"

The two young men who saw the dreams were slaves of King Riyaan bin Waleed. One was in charge of serving drinks and the other of baking bread. They were accused of attempting to poison the king. The name of the cupbearer was "Abrooha" or "Yoona," and the baker's name was "Ghaalib" or "Makhlib." Both were sent to prison on this charge.

One described his dream: "I saw a beautiful grapevine with three branches, and clusters of grapes on them. I was squeezing them and serving drinks."

Note that wine is also made from squeezed grapes, so he interpreted squeezing grapes as wine.

The other described his dream: "I saw myself coming out of the king's kitchen with three baskets of bread on my head, and birds were eating from above."

When Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> entered the prison, he had mentioned that he could interpret dreams. He had already told many people their dream interpretations that came true. So these two also asked him for interpretation, knowing that he gave true interpretations. He was a man of knowledge and a very good person. All the prisoners were aware of his good conduct — he was punctual in prayer and fasting, and doing every kind of good deed was his noble habit. His excellent character was hidden from no one. He visited the sick and comforted the grieved.

When Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> went to prison, he saw that the prisoners had lost all hope and were afflicted with severe calamities and prolonged grief. He consoled them: "Be happy — have patience. Allah ta'ala certainly grants the reward of patience." The people said, "O young man, how beautiful your face is! How lovely your appearance! How excellent your character! Since you told us that patience brings reward, calamity expiates sins, and afflictions are a means of purification from sins, we have attained blessing from your company and advice." They asked, "O young man, who are you?" He replied, "I am Yusuf, son of Yaqoob, son of Ibrahim." The chief jailer said, "O young man, if it were in my power, I would set you free. But it is not in my control. However, I will treat the prisoners in your neighborhood well."

Seeing his virtues and perfections, the two prisoners either asked him the interpretation of dreams they had actually seen, or they fabricated dreams merely to test him.

One narration says that when Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> mentioned in prison that he knew the interpretation of dreams, the two young men said to each other, "Let us test this Hebrew slave. We will make up dreams ourselves and present them to him." They did this — they had not seen any dream but fabricated one and asked.

Hazrat Ibn Mas'ood <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> said they had not seen any dreams; they had fabricated dreams to test his knowledge.

### Yusuf Interprets the Dreams

> "O two companions of the prison! As for one of you, he will serve his king wine. As for the other, he will be crucified, and birds will eat from his head. The matter is decreed concerning which you inquire."

When he gave the interpretation, they said, "We did not see any dream." He replied, "The matter is decreed concerning which you inquire."

"Indeed, the interpretation he gave was bound to come true." Because before interpreting, he had said, "I will inform you of its interpretation before your food comes to you — my Lord has taught me." He made it very clear that he does not give dream interpretations by conjecture or guesswork — this is knowledge given by my Lord, on the basis of which I speak.

"He told them, 'Allah ta'ala has granted me abundant knowledge. What you have heard is but a drop of rain, one bud from a garden of flowers.'"

### Yusuf's Request to Be Mentioned to the King

> "And he said to the one he knew would be saved, 'Mention me to your king.' But Satan caused him to forget to mention him to the king, so he remained in prison for several years."

A'la Hazrat Maulana Ahmad Raza Barelvi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> interpreted the word "ظَنَّ" at this point as "deemed" or "assumed," not translated as "thought," because scholars hold that its subject is Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and a prophet's knowledge is not conjectural but certain. Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> says that one scholarly opinion is that the word "ظَنَّ" should be taken in the sense of knowledge and certainty, because Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> gave the interpretation of the dream through revelation (wahi), and in many places in the Holy Quran, "ظَنَّ" is used in the sense of certainty.

### The Laws for the Near Ones Are Different

Scholars have stated a principle: Sometimes the good deeds of ordinary righteous people are not appropriate for the special near ones to perform — rather, for them there is a station higher than this. Therefore, their deeds should also be of a higher station.

Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> says there is no difficulty in this matter: "There is no harm in seeking help from Allah's servants in difficulties," because the Holy Lord Himself has stated, "Help one another in righteousness and piety."

For ordinary people, it is also a good deed to seek help from Allah's servants in their difficulties. But the station of the noble prophets is different. For this reason, Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> did not even ask Allah ta'ala for deliverance from the fire — "When He is aware of my condition, what need is there for me to ask?"

When Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> told the one who would be freed to mention him to the king, Allah ta'ala affectionately reminded him: "O My beloved, think! Who saved you from being killed by your brothers? Who brought you out of the well? Who saved you from the slander of women?" He submitted, "Master of the universe, these were Your favors alone." The Lord said, "Then what was the benefit of mentioning this to a human?" Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> apologized: "O my Master! It just came to my tongue."

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had already spent five years in prison before interpreting the two prisoners' dreams, and he spent another seven years after that — meaning Allah's prophet spent 12 years in prison for the sake of his purity. The noble prophets were given very difficult trials.

### The King's Dream

> "And the king said, 'I saw seven fat cows being eaten by seven lean ones, and seven green ears of grain and seven others dry. O chiefs! Explain to me my dream, if you can interpret dreams.' They said, 'Jumbled dreams — and we are not knowledgeable of the interpretation of dreams.'"

Allah ta'ala's law of nature is that when He intends something, He creates the means for it. When He intended to release Yusuf from prison, He created this means: The king of Egypt, Riyaan, saw a dream: "Seven fat, healthy cows came out of a dry river, and seven lean cows ate the fat ones. And he saw seven ears of grain full of grain and green, and seven others dry, which wrapped around the green ones and overcame them."

The king gathered his courtiers, soothsayers, and astrologers and asked them the interpretation of the dream. They said, "If the dream had some sequence that indicated events, we would give its interpretation. But this dream has no sequence — confusion and disarray are found in it. Therefore, these are scattered thoughts — no interpretation can be given." But the king was very distressed, because the weak overcoming the strong and the dry overcoming the green was certainly a sign of some danger.

Seeing the king's distress, the former prisoner (the cupbearer) remembered — the one to whom Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had said, "You will be saved." He said, "Send me to the prison. There is a learned man there who will interpret this dream. I will return and tell you." He went to Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and asked him the interpretation of the king's dream.

According to another narration, it was a different person who had heard of Yusuf's reputation. Upon seeing the king's distress after he narrated his dream, he remembered after some time to go to that person in prison about whom it was famous that he correctly interprets dreams.

SubhanAllah! How exalted is the station of a prophet! When the questioner asked for the interpretation, he did not say, "After seven years, you remembered me?" Nor did he say, "First tell the king that I have been imprisoned unjustly — what is the reason? First release me from prison, then I will tell the interpretation." No, no — the patient, grateful prophet did not set any condition, nor did he reproach the man.

### Hazrat Yusuf Interprets the King's Dream

> "He said, 'You will sow for seven consecutive years. Then what you harvest, leave it in its ears, except a little from which you will eat. Then after that will come seven severe (years) which will consume what you have stored for them, except a little which you will preserve. Then after that will come a year in which the people will have abundant rain and in which they will press (grapes).'"

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> gave the interpretation of the dream: For seven years, grain will be plentiful as usual. Then there will be seven years of famine — there will be no rain. These famine years will consume the grain stored in the previous years. After that, there will be a year of prosperity — abundant rains, resulting in plentiful produce of grapes, olives, sugarcane, sesame, etc., from which you will extract juices and oils.

Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> not only gave the interpretation but also suggested precautionary measures: Do not use the grain produced in the first seven years extravagantly. Only separate enough grain (husk and kernels) to meet your needs. Leave the rest in the ears. This will economize expenditure and also protect the grain from insects.

### The King Summons Yusuf — He Refuses Until Vindicated

> "And the king said, 'Bring him to me.' But when the messenger came to him, he said, 'Return to your king and ask him: What is the case of the women who cut their hands? Indeed, my Lord knows their guile.' The king said, 'O women, what was your affair when you sought to seduce Yusuf?' They said, 'Holy is Allah! We know no evil of him.' The wife of Aziz said, 'Now the truth has come to light. I sought to seduce him, and indeed he is truthful.' He (Yusuf) said, 'That is so that he (Aziz) may know that I did not betray him in secret, and that Allah does not guide the guile of the treacherous.'"

Observe the patience of Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>: despite spending 12 years in prison, when the message of release comes, instead of rejoicing and rushing out, he refuses, saying: "First investigate the matter thoroughly, then I will come out of prison." If he had come out immediately, it was possible that the king's heart might have retained some doubt about the accusation being true. After the investigation, the king became certain, and the women declared his innocence clearly.

The wife of Aziz, who had not yet confessed her fault, now also admitted her error, calling him pure and truthful. Thus, the truth became manifest before the king.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "If I had remained in prison as long as Yusuf, I would have accepted the invitation of the one inviting me."

Remember that the Prophet ﷺ praised Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, but this does not prove that if the Prophet ﷺ had been in that position, he would not (God forbid) have been patient. Rather, he would have immediately come out. This is a figurative expression — in humble speech, reality is not stated.

Hazrat Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> writes: "Ibn Malik said: Know that in this noble hadith, the Prophet ﷺ did not mention any distress or lack of patience on his part. Rather, he praised Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> for not preferring to leave quickly, so that the king's heart would be cleared of the false accusation against him — i.e., the king should know that a false accusation had been made against him and should not look at him with suspicion."

**Benefit:** The king said, "Bring him to me." The reason was that when he learned that Yusuf was a man of great knowledge, he said, "How can such a learned man remain in prison?" He recognized the excellence of knowledge — that Allah ta'ala made his knowledge a means of deliverance from worldly calamities. How then can religious knowledge be a means of deliverance from the difficulties of the Hereafter?

### From Prison to Treasurer and Grand Vizier

> "And the king said, 'Bring him to me that I may appoint him exclusively for myself.' Then when he spoke with him, he said, 'Indeed, today you are with us established and trusted.' He said, 'Appoint me over the treasuries of the land. Indeed, I am a knowledgeable guardian.' And thus We gave Yusuf authority in the land, to settle therein wherever he willed. We extend Our mercy to whom We will, and We do not allow to be lost the reward of the doers of good. And the reward of the Hereafter is better for those who believe and are righteous."

The king sent a group of dignitaries with the finest mounts, royal equipment, and luxurious garments to the prison, so that Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> could be brought to the royal court with the utmost honor and respect. They presented the king's message to him. He accepted. As he left the prison, he made a supplication for the prisoners. When he came out, written on the prison gate was: "This is the house of calamities — a grave for the living, a place of enemies' slander and even their delight, and a place of trial for the truthful."

He then performed a bath (ghusl), donned fine garments, and proceeded toward the royal palaces. When he reached near the fort, he said, "My Lord is sufficient. His protection is great and His praise is sublime. There is no deity other than Him." Then he entered the fort. Upon reaching the king, he prayed: "O my Lord, by Your grace I seek his goodness, and from his and others' evil I seek Your refuge."

When his gaze met the king's, he greeted in Arabic. The king asked, "Which language is this?" He replied, "This is the language of my uncle, Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>." Then he made a supplication in Hebrew. The king again asked, "Which language is this?" He replied, "This is the language of my father, Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup>." The king could not understand either language, even though he knew seventy languages. Then, whatever language the king spoke with him, he answered in that same language. At that time, his age was 30 years. Seeing such vast knowledge at this age, the king was amazed.

He was appointed as the treasurer and the grand vizier of Egypt. His fame spread throughout the land. The famine set in just as he had interpreted, and due to his wise planning, the people were saved from starvation. The king accepted faith at his hands.
