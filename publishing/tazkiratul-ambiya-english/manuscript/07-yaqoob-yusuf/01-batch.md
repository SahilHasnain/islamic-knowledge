### Chapter 7: Hazrat Yaqoob and Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليهم السلام</sup>

Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was the son of Hazrat Ishaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and the grandson of Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was the son of Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Hazrat Ibn Umar <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "The noble son of the noble son of the noble son of the noble son — Yusuf bin Yaqoob bin Ishaq bin Ibrahim."

### The Sons of Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had 12 sons, meaning Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had 11 brothers. Their names are: Yahooda, Rubeel, Sham'oon, Laadi, Riyaloon, Yashjar, and Deenah — all these boys were from his wife Liya bint Liyaan bin Faahir, who was the daughter of Yaqoob's maternal aunt. Daan, Yaftaali, Jaad, and Aashriya were from Zalqeh and Balhatah. Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Buniyaamin (Benjamin) were from Raheel (Rachel). Raheel passed away shortly after Buniyaamin's birth. He married Raheel after Liya's death; Raheel was Liya's sister.

Note that the names listed excluding Yusuf total 12, though it is well-known that Yusuf had 11 brothers. For this reason, most scholars did not include Deenah's name, considering her a sister (female), not a brother.

### Yusuf's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Dream

Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> told his father: "O my father! Indeed, I saw eleven stars and the sun and the moon — I saw them prostrating to me." The names of the stars that Yusuf saw were: Jirbaan, Taariq, Ziyaal, Qaalbis, Umoodaan, Feelaq, Faza'a, Wisaab, Zulkaftain, Zarooj, and Misbah (in one narration, Misbah is replaced with Natah — but the first narration is followed by most scholars).

A Jewish man named San'an came to the Master of the Universe ﷺ and said, "Tell me about the stars that Yusuf saw." The Prophet was silent until Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came and told him the names. He said to the Jewish man, "If I tell you their names, will you believe?" He said, "Yes." He told him the names, and he said, "By Allah, these are indeed their names."

### Interpretation of the Dream

By the eleven stars were meant his brothers, and by the sun and moon were meant his parents. Note that his mother had already passed away before he saw this dream. The prostration meant prostration of respect, which was permissible in earlier nations but is not permissible in our shari'ah.

**Benefit:** Seeing the sun in a dream indicates kingship, gold, or a beautiful wife. Seeing the moon indicates kingship, ministry, closeness to the king, leadership, nobility, a slave, rank, authority, visiting an elder, parents, spouse, or honor. Sometimes it may also indicate discord or falsehood — all of this depends on the one seeing the dream.

When a person sees a good dream, it is from Allah ta'ala — he should praise Allah and may relate it if he wishes. If he sees a bad dream, it is from Satan — he should spit to his left three times and recite *أَعُوْذُ بِاللّٰهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطٰنِ الرَّجِيْمِ وَشَرِّ الرُّؤْيَا*, and should not relate it to anyone; no harm will come to him.

### Yaqoob's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Prohibition from Relating the Dream

> "He said, 'O my dear son, do not relate your dream to your brothers, lest they devise a plot against you. Indeed, Satan is a clear enemy to man.'"

Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> saw this dream at the age of 12. Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> knew that Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> would attain the station of prophethood, so the brothers would envy him. Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> loved Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> greatly, and the brothers envied him for this. Yusuf himself was aware of this. Therefore, he forbade him from relating the dream to his brothers, lest their envy increase and they fall into Satan's trap in harming him.

### The Brothers' Envy of Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

> "When they said, 'Surely Yusuf and his brother are dearer to our father than we are, while we are a group. Indeed, our father is clearly mistaken.'"

Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> loved Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> the most, followed by Buniyaamin. The other brothers became jealous. They said, "We are a group — we can serve our father more and benefit him more. These two are younger and cannot serve our father like we can. Why does he love them more than us?"

**Objection:** How could Yaqoob's sons attribute "clear error" to their father? This is extreme reproach and criticism. One who speaks excessively against a messenger of Allah is a disbeliever (yet they were believers). How could children be so critical?

**Answer:** The meaning is not that their father was astray from the truth. Rather, they meant: "Our father is not considering worldly expediency. We are a group — we can benefit him more and serve him more, yet he loves those two more."

### Why Was Yaqoob's Love Greater for Two Sons?

Love is a matter of the heart over which a person has no control. This is why the Noble Prophet ﷺ, despite being extremely careful in maintaining justice among his pure wives, used to pray: "O Allah, regarding what I control, I have done justice. Do not hold me accountable for what I do not control."

That is, greater heartfelt love for someone is beyond human control. These two brothers were the youngest, and greater love for younger children is natural and instinctive.

The real reason for loving Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> the most was that he had learned that if any of his children was to attain prophethood, it was this son. Therefore, he loved him the most, and after him, Buniyaamin, as he was the youngest. Because of this greater love for Yusuf, the brothers were most opposed to him — they were not as opposed to Buniyaamin, as the matter of the dream had also reached them somehow.

### The Brothers Resolved to Remove Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

> "Kill Yusuf or cast him out to some land, so that your father's attention will be solely toward you, and after that you can become righteous."

They consulted: "If you want your father to have pure love only for you, removing Yusuf from the path is necessary. Without this, your father's complete love cannot be attained." They were Muslims, not disbelievers. They understood that this would be a great crime, but the fire of jealousy had blinded them. They had firmly resolved to implement their plan, though they thought they would repent afterward and become righteous.

### Only One Brother Forbade Killing Him

> "One of them said, 'Do not kill Yusuf, but throw him into the bottom of a well so that some passing caravan may take him — if you must do something.'"

The one who prevented them was the eldest brother, named Yahooda. He said, "Killing is a great crime — your intention is not right. Instead, throw him into a well in the jungle. Perhaps someone passing by will take him. This way your purpose will also be served, and Yusuf will also be saved from death. If you must act upon your intention, do this."

### The Brothers' Pretext for Taking Yusuf to the Jungle

> "They said, 'O our father, why do you not trust us regarding Yusuf, while we are surely his well-wishers? Send him with us tomorrow to enjoy himself and play. And indeed, we will be his protectors.' He said, 'Indeed, it grieves me that you should take him, and I fear that a wolf may eat him while you are heedless of him.' They said, 'If a wolf eats him while we are a group, then we would surely be losers.'"

Their purpose in seeking permission to go outside the city and play was: "We will prepare for combat with the enemy, compete in racing, and gain skill in archery." If they had sought permission merely for entertainment and play, Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> would never have allowed them. Since Yusuf was young, they said, "O our father, allow our younger brother to come with us. He will be pleased to see our military training. We will pick wild fruits for him to eat, as we will also practice grazing and feeding the camels and gathering fruit from wild trees."

Since Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had already seen a dream that a wolf had attacked Yusuf, and that land was also known for wolves, he said, "I fear that a wolf may eat him while you are heedless." Perhaps Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> interpreted the dream this way — the wolf referring to an enemy. The brothers later used their father's own words as an excuse, as this gave them the idea to use the wolf story.

Hazrat Ibn Umar <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not say something before someone that gives them an opening to lie." The brothers did not know that wolves could eat humans until their father mentioned it, which gave them the opportunity to lie: "How can it be that a group of strong brothers would let a wolf eat their brother? If that happened, we would be worthless."

### The Brothers Persuaded Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

When Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was not agreeing to send Yusuf with them, they said to Yusuf, "Will you come to the jungle with us where we will race and compete with camels and other military exercises?" Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said, "Yes, I will come." They said, "Then tell your father." He gathered all the brothers and they came before their father. In his presence, they submitted: "Yusuf wants to come with us — please permit him." He turned to Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and asked, "What do you think?" He replied, "My brothers want to take me with love and affection, so I must go with them." Thus, despite not wanting to send Yusuf with them, Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> gave his consent — no plan avails against destiny.

### The Brothers' Oppression

When they took Yusuf with them, as long as Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was watching, they carried him on their shoulders. He stood watching until they disappeared from sight. When they reached the jungle and were out of their father's sight, they threw Yusuf to the ground and began displaying their enmity — sometimes verbally abusing him, sometimes hitting him. He would run from one brother to another, hoping for sympathy and compassion, but the one he ran to would start beating him as well. When he understood their intentions and what they wanted, he cried out: "O my father! If only you could see how your sons are oppressing me, how grieved you would be! If you could see the injustices they are inflicting upon me, you would surely weep. O my father! How quickly they forgot their promise to you! How quickly they forgot your advice!"

Saying this, Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> wept bitterly. In this state, Rubeel threw him to the ground and sat on his chest, intending to kill him. Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said, "O my brother, stop! Do not kill me. Killing is a great crime."

SubhanAllah! How exalted is the rank of a prophet! Even before the proclamation of prophethood, he was advising his brothers and preventing them from the great crime of murder. Rubeel said, "You used to have such grand dreams — now call upon your dreams to save you from my hands." He twisted his neck and was about to kill him when Yusuf called out to his eldest brother Yahooda: "O my brother, fear Allah! Stand between me and the one who wants to kill me!" At his words, some brotherly feeling stirred in him, and his heart softened. He said, "O my brothers! Did you not promise me that you would not kill him? Even now, do something easy — do not kill him." In their anger, they had forgotten their earlier promise. They asked, "What should we do then?" Yahooda said, "There is a well nearby — throw him into it. Either he will die on his own, or some caravan will pass by, take him out, and take him along."

There was a well that was open at the bottom and narrow at the top. When they tried to throw him in, he clung to the edge. They bound his hands and feet and removed his shirt, intending to stain it with blood as an excuse before their father. Yusuf cried out, "O my brothers! Return my shirt so that I am not left naked in the well!" But they did not heed him. As they were about to throw him in, he said, "O my brothers, will you leave me alone?" They replied, "Now call upon the moon, the sun, and the stars — they will help you." They lowered him in a bucket halfway and then let go, thinking the hard fall would kill him. But he fell into the water and sat on a stone at one side.

### Yusuf's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> State in the Well

After he was thrown into the well, he wept. They called out from above. He thought perhaps they had taken pity on him and answered them. Then, realizing he was alive, they tried to kill him by dropping stones, but Yahooda again stopped them.

Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> possessed a shirt that had been brought from Paradise and given to his grandfather Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> when he was thrown into the fire. When Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> sent Yusuf with his brothers, he had put that shirt around his neck as a talisman. When the brothers threw him into the well, they removed his shirt, but an angel came, removed the talisman from his neck, made a shirt from it, and dressed him, and the well shone with light.

Hazrat Hasan <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates that when he was thrown into the well, the water became sweet (though it was previously salty), and it gained nourishing properties — giving both food and drink. Jibreel came to him in the well so that he would not feel lonely. At evening, when Jibreel rose to leave, he said, "I will feel lonely alone." Jibreel taught him a supplication to recite whenever he was in need:

> *يَا صَخِيَ الْمُسْتَصْخِيْنَ وَيَا غَوْثَ الْمُسْتَغِيْثِيْنَ وَيَا مُفَرِّجَ كُرْبِ الْمَكْرُوْبِيْنَ*

Upon reciting this, he would find Jibreel with him, knowing his state and affairs.

Hazrat Ibn Umar <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> narrates that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When Yusuf was thrown into the well, Jibreel came to him and asked, 'O boy, who threw you into the well?' He said, 'My brothers.' He asked, 'Why did they throw you?' He said, 'My father loves me, and they envied me.' Jibreel asked, 'Do you want to get out of here?' He said, 'This request is only to the God of Yaqoob.' Jibreel said, 'Then pray to Allah:'"

> *اللّٰهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ بِاسْمِكَ الْمَكْنُونِ الْمَخْزُونِ يَا بَدِيعَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ يَا ذَا الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ أَنْ تَغْفِرَ لِي وَتَرْحَمَنِي وَأَنْ تَجْعَلَ أَمْرِي فَرَجًا وَمَخْرَجًا وَأَنْ تَرْزُقَنِي مِنْ حَيْثُ أَحْتَسِبُ وَمِنْ حَيْثُ لَا أَحْتَسِبُ*

When he recited this, Allah made his affairs easy — brought him out of the well and granted him the kingdom of Egypt, which was beyond his imagination. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Keep reciting this supplication, for it is the supplication of Allah's chosen righteous servants."

### Yaqoob's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Sons Returned Weeping

> "And they came to their father at night, weeping. They said, 'O our father, we went racing ahead and left Yusuf with our belongings, and a wolf ate him. And you will not believe us, even if we are truthful.' And they brought his shirt with false blood upon it. He said, 'Rather, your souls have enticed you to something. So patience is best. And Allah is the one from whom help is sought against what you describe.'"

They slaughtered a deer, stained Yusuf's shirt with its blood, and brought it to their father, pretending he had been eaten by a wolf. Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> took the shirt, put it to his face, and wept until his face was stained with the blood. He said, "I have never seen such a wise wolf — it ate my son but did not tear the shirt." Saying this, he wept again until he lost consciousness. His sons sprinkled water on him, but he did not regain consciousness — there was no movement in his body. They called out to him, but he gave no response. Yahooda placed his hand over his nose and mouth but could feel no breath, nor any pulse. Yahooda said, "On the Day of Judgment, we will receive only punishment from the Master of the Kingdom. We have lost our brother and killed our father." He spent the entire night unconscious, regaining consciousness only at dawn.

**Benefit:** Three signs were found in Yusuf's shirt: first, Yaqoob recognized from the shirt that a wolf had not eaten him; second, Yaqoob's lost eyesight was restored by the same shirt when Yusuf sent it from Egypt; third, Yusuf was proven innocent of Zulaikha's accusation through the torn shirt.

**Objection:** Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> knew that Yusuf was alive, because he had told him, "Your Lord will choose you, teach you the interpretation of dreams, and make you a prophet." This was communicated through *wahi* (revelation). Yet despite knowing this, he did not search for him but spent his time weeping — why?

**Answer:** "Indeed, Allah ta'ala had forbidden him from searching, so that he might bear severe hardship and this matter might become difficult for him" — so that he might receive the reward of patience. Another reason was that if he searched too much, the brothers might go and kill Yusuf. Yaqoob's weeping was only due to separation, not ignorance. He was patient and grateful for Allah's decree, but distressed by his sons' actions — that being the progeny of a prophet, they turned out so envious — and this was worth weeping over.

**Note:** Hazrat A'mash <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> said: "After the false weeping of Yaqoob's sons, no one's weeping can be considered genuine." Ibn Manzar narrates that Qazi Shuraih had a woman come to him with her case, weeping. People said, "O Abu Umayya, do you not see her weeping?" He said, "Yusuf's brothers also came to their father at night, weeping — yet they were oppressors and liars. Therefore, no one has the right to pass judgment without investigation."

### Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Was Brought Out of the Well

Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> says that a caravan set out from Madyan toward Egypt. They lost their way and were wandering in search of the path when they saw the well in which Yusuf was confined, though it was in such a barren jungle that no one visited it except shepherds. The caravan sent a man, Malik bin Za'ar Khazai, to fetch water. When he lowered his bucket into the well, Yusuf clung to it, and thus he came out. Seeing his beauty, Malik said joyfully, "What a delightful find — it's a boy!" — what a beautiful boy has come into our hands who will be great capital for us.

### The Beauty of Yusuf

Yusuf's face was extremely beautiful. His hair was curly, his eyes large and lovely, his limbs perfectly proportioned, his skin wheatish with a reddish tint, his wrists and calves full, his belly small, his navel small, and when he smiled, rays of light shone from his teeth. No one possessed these features at that time. His beauty radiated like the light of day.

Seeing such a beautiful boy, how could the one who drew him out not be astonished? Even the walls and stones of the well wept at being deprived of his beauty and blessings.

### The Brothers Sold Yusuf for a Paltry Price

The men who took him out of the well considered him valuable capital and hid him, intending to sell him in Egypt for a large sum. However, after three days, his brothers came to check on him — to see if he was alive or dead. At the well, they found he was not there. Looking around, they spotted the caravan. Upon inquiry, they learned that a boy had been taken out. The brothers said, "This is our runaway slave. If you want to buy him, we will sell him cheap. Take him to some distant land so he won't try to escape."

Yusuf remained silent out of fear of his brothers and did not tell them that he was their brother, not a slave. In the end, he was sold for 20 or 22 silver coins. Allah ta'ala says: "And they sold him for a paltry price — a few silver coins."

### Yusuf's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Farewell to His Brothers

When his brothers sold him for a few silver coins, they told the merchant, "He is a thief and runs away." The merchant imprisoned him and appointed an Abyssinian slave to guard him. When the caravan prepared to depart, he began to weep. The merchant asked, "Why are you crying?" He replied, "I want to bid farewell to those who sold me — a farewell of one who will never return." The merchant told his slave, "Take him to his masters so he may bid them farewell, then rejoin the caravan. I have never seen such an obedient slave who loves his masters so much, and I have never seen masters as cruel as his."

The slave brought him to his brothers — all were sleeping, while one was awake guarding the sheep and goats. Yusuf, with chains on his feet, staggering, approached him weeping. He asked, "Why have you come?" He said, "I have come to bid you farewell — a farewell after which you will never see me again. Alas, what a farewell this is!" (Perhaps this was Yahooda, who had some softness toward him.) Everyone woke up, and Yusuf went to each brother, bowing his head with love, kissing them, and embracing them. SubhanAllah! Look at their cruelty and look at Yusuf's love. As he left, he said: "May Allah protect you, even though you have destroyed me. May Allah keep you established in your homes, even though you have expelled me from my home. May Allah have mercy on you, even though you have shown no mercy to me."

The effect of his farewell, his heart-touching words, and his painful cry was so intense that even the sheep and goats miscarried from the horrific scene.

### Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Wept at His Mother's Grave

After bidding farewell to his brothers, the slave seized him and took him along to rejoin the caravan. He was put on a mount with chains and taken away. When they passed by the cemetery of Kan'aan and he saw the grave of his mother Raheel, he could not control his emotions. He dismounted, clung to the grave, and wept: "O my mother! Lift your head from the grave and see your son bound in chains. O my mother, my brothers threw me into a well, separated me from my father, and sold me for a few coins. My young age did not soften their hearts, nor did they feel any compassion for me. I ask Allah to gather me and my father in the station of mercy — He is the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate."

### The Slave Slapped Yusuf and the Divine Wrath

The slave looked back and did not find Yusuf. He returned and found him weeping by a grave. He said, "Those who sold you spoke the truth when they said you were a runaway!" Saying this, he gave him a powerful slap that knocked him unconscious. When he regained consciousness, he said, "Do not say anything — this is my mother's grave. I had dismounted to bid her farewell. I will not do anything you dislike again." His face was bloodied and covered with dust from the fall. Trembling, he submitted before his Lord: "O Allah, if I have erred, forgive me by the honor of my forefathers Ibrahim, Ishaq, and Yaqoob <sup>عليهم السلام</sup>."

Seeing his state, the angels of the heavens cried out and pleaded for him before Allah ta'ala. The Lord said: "O angels, this is My prophet and the son of My prophets. He is pleading to Me and seeking help from Me. I am the One who responds to his cry — I alone reach every supplicant's plea." He said, "O Jibreel, go and help My servant."

Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came and said, "O friend of Allah, your Lord sends you salam and says: Stop weeping — you have made the angels of the seven heavens weep. Do you want the heavens and the earth to become one?" He said, "No, no. Allah has granted me forbearance. He is not hasty, so I too do not act hastily."

Jibreel struck his wing, and a red wind began to blow. The sun's light vanished, darkness spread from the red storm, and the caravan members could not see each other. The merchant said, "O caravan! Dismount from your rides and save yourselves! I have been passing this route for many years and have never seen a day like this. Seek forgiveness for your sins — this calamity is surely the result of one of our sins."

At that moment, Yusuf's Abyssinian guard said, "I struck Yusuf. When I struck him, he raised his head toward the heavens and moved his lips." The merchant said, "Alas for your destruction — you have destroyed us and yourself!" The merchant came to Yusuf and said, "O boy, we have wronged you by striking you. If you want revenge, take it — we are ready." He replied: "We do not take revenge from oppressors. I belong to a household that forgives those who wrong them and prays for their forgiveness. I forgive you — may Allah also forgive you."

As soon as he forgave them, the darkness lifted, the storm ceased, the sun shone again, and light spread from east to west. Thus, the caravan arrived in Egypt safely. SubhanAllah! What is the rank of a prophet? How does the one who oppresses a prophet fall into disgrace, while the prophet remains patient and prays for the oppressors.

### Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Was Sold in the Market of Egypt

Yusuf was brought to Egypt and put up for sale as a slave in the market. Because of his beauty and countenance, his price kept rising until it reached the point where musk, silver, and silk equal to his weight were offered. Such a high price could not be afforded by ordinary people, so he was bought by the treasurer of Egypt, whose name was Qatfeer or Atfeer, with the title of *Aziz of Egypt*. At that time, the king of Egypt was Al-Riyaan bin Al-Waleed, from the Amaliqa tribe. He believed in Yusuf and appointed him as his treasurer. After him, Qaboos bin Mus'ab became king, whom he invited to faith but he refused.

Note that the kings of Egypt were called Fir'aun (Pharaoh). The king of Yusuf's time was also called Fir'aun, but the famous Pharaoh of Musa's time was much later.

When Aziz of Egypt bought him, he was 17 years old. He stayed in his house for 13 years. When Riyaan bin Waleed appointed him as minister, he was 30 years old. At age 33, Allah granted him dominion and wisdom. He passed away at 120 years of age.

### Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> in Comfort

> "And the one who bought him said to his wife, from among the people of Egypt, 'Treat him honorably; perhaps he will benefit us, or we may adopt him as a son.' And thus We established Yusuf in the land, and that We might teach him the interpretation of dreams. And Allah has full power over His matters, but most people do not know."

Aziz of Egypt saw the signs of felicity and nobility on Yusuf's forehead. He brought him home with great love and said to his wife, "I have found a very dear boy. Take care of his comfort at all times — do not let him be distressed in any way. His face and appearance indicate a splendid future. Perhaps one day he will prove useful to us, or we may adopt him as a son." The wife's name was Raa'il or Zulaikha — the second name is more famous.

In a land where no one had any knowledge of Yusuf's great family, where he was brought in chains of slavery, where those who sold him considered him a runaway slave, he was brought to the market like an ordinary slave and sold — and then to be provided with such honor and comfort, for a great Egyptian chief to show paternal affection, even devotion, toward him — this could only be the perfect wisdom of Allah ta'ala.

### The Insight of Three Personalities

Three respected personalities displayed great insight:

1) **Aziz of Egypt** — when he told his wife to treat Yusuf honorably.
2) **The daughter of Hazrat Shoaib <sup>عليه السلام</sup>** — who, through her insight, recognized Musa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> as strong and trustworthy and advised her father: "O my father, hire him. Indeed, the best to hire is the strong and trustworthy."
3) **Abu Bakr Siddique <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>** — who, through his insight, chose Hazrat Umar <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> as his successor.
