## Hazrat Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

### Heir to Dawood

> "And Sulaiman succeeded Dawood."

Hazrat Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام</sup> lived 59 years and came 1700 years before the Prophet ﷺ. He was the youngest of Dawood's 19 sons.

"Sulaiman succeeded Dawood" — A'la Hazrat Maulana Ahmad Raza Khan <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> translated *warisa* as "janasheen" (successor/vicegerent). Sulaiman inherited his father's kingship and khilafat, not wealth, because the Prophet ﷺ said: "The prophets do not leave wealth as inheritance — what they leave is charity."

The Prophet ﷺ also said: "Indeed the scholars are the heirs of the prophets. The prophets do not leave dinars and dirhams as inheritance — they leave knowledge. Whoever takes hold of it has taken a great portion."

### Knowledge of Bird Languages

> "He said: 'O people, we have been taught the language of birds and have been given from all things. Indeed, this is clear favor.'"

We observe daily that birds have their own languages. Different species have different calls; the same species varies its calls in different situations — fighting, loving, danger. This shows their speech carries meaning. Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام> could understand what each bird was saying.

He used the plural form ("we") when speaking to his people — this was in accordance with royal protocol, not pride.

Some scholars say both Dawood and Sulaiman knew bird languages, hence the plural. But this is not confirmed for Dawood.

**Specific bird calls interpreted by Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام>:**

- **Peacock**: "As you do, so shall you reap."
- **Hoopoe**: "O sinners, seek forgiveness from Allah."
- **Khattaaf** (a long-armed black bird): "Do good deeds so you may receive their reward."
- **Ring dove**: Glorifying "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la."
- **Eagle**: "Everything besides the Lord is bound to perish."
- **Partridge**: "Who remained silent remained safe."
- **Rooster**: "O heedless ones, remember Allah."
- **Vulture**: "O man, live as long as you wish — death will surely come."
- **Falcon**: "There is companionship in staying away from people."
- **Frog**: Glorifying "Subhana Rabbiyal Quddus."

Note: Birds do not make these calls constantly — they vary their speech at different times.

Sulaiman's statement "we have been given from all things" was a declaration of gratitude, not pride — just as the Prophet ﷺ said "I am the leader of the children of Adam, and I say this not out of pride."

### Wind Subjugated

> "And We made the wind subservient to Sulaiman: its morning journey was one month, and its evening journey was one month."

The wind was subjugated to him like a mount. From morning to noon he would travel as far as a normal traveler covers in one month, and from noon to evening another month's journey. He would be in Baitul Muqaddas at dawn, reach Astghar by midday, and reach the fort of Khurasan by evening.

According to Allama Raazi, this was not ordinary wind but a special type of wind.

### The Spring of Copper

> "And We made a spring of molten copper flow for him."

One purpose was that Allah made copper soft for him just as iron was softened for his father. Craftsmen would bring cold copper, and he would shape it without fire or hammering as needed. Another meaning is that Allah provided copper like a flowing spring — as much as needed was taken from it.

### The Army of Sulaiman

> "And gathered for Sulaiman were his soldiers — jinn, humans, and birds — and they were kept in order."

His army consisted of three divisions: jinn, humans, and birds.

**Refutation of modernist interpretation:** Some modernists claim "jinn" here means mountain tribes, "birds" means fast horses. But if both "jinn" and "tuyoor" refer to humans, what is the point of mentioning "al-ins" (humans) separately? The conjunction particle *atfa* indicates these are three distinct categories. Taking metaphorical meanings without necessity is ignorant audacity.

The word *yooza'oon* comes from *waza'a*, meaning to restrain — despite the vast army, there was no disorder. Every unit observed strict military discipline.

### Unreliable Stories About the Throne

Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> states: Most incidents narrated about Sulaiman's throne are unreliable. Only those proven by Quran and authentic hadith should be accepted. Exaggerated stories provide an opportunity for the irreligious to mock religion. Many such tales may have been fabricated by the irreligious themselves.

**Unreliable story (1):** 300,000 chairs were arranged for him — believing humans sat closest, then believing jinn. He commanded birds to provide shade, then commanded the wind to carry them.

**Unreliable story (2):** His army spread across 100 farsakh — 50 farsakh humans, 25 jinn, 25 wild animals, 25 birds. He had 1000 houses of glass on wood, 300 wives, 700 concubines. First, strong wind would lift his houses, then gentle wind would carry them where he wished.

**Unreliable story (3):** While traveling through the air, revelation came: "I am increasing your kingdom — all of creation's speech will reach your ears through the wind."

**Unreliable story (4):** Jinn made a carpet of gold and silk one farsakh square. He placed his golden pulpit in the center. 600,000 gold and silver chairs surrounded him — prophets on gold, scholars on silver. Birds shaded them, and the east wind would carry the carpet a month's distance.

### The Ant Story

> "Until when they came to the valley of the ants, an ant said: 'O ants, enter your dwellings lest Sulaiman and his armies crush you while they perceive not.' So Sulaiman smiled, laughing at her speech, and said: 'My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents, and to do righteous deeds that please You, and admit me by Your mercy among Your righteous servants.'"

Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and his army were traveling in the normal manner — some walking, some riding. The ant colony was in Taif or Shaam. The ant who gave the warning was their queen, who was lame and named Taakhiya or Manzarah.

Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام</sup> heard the ant's voice from three miles away and stopped his army so the ants could enter their homes — a miracle of Allah's prophet.

The Prophet ﷺ himself heard a lizard speak and testify to his prophethood in Khaibar. A poisoned goat's foreleg also spoke to him. Hearing voices from other creatures is a miracle of Allah's Beloved.

**Why Sulaiman smiled:** The ant's precautionary measures amazed him, and he also felt joy and gratitude that Allah had enabled him to hear the ant's voice.

**Types of laughter:** *Tabassum* — smiling without sound; *Zihak* — light laughter showing teeth; *Qehqaha* — loud laughter. The Prophet ﷺ mostly smiled; loud laughter is not authentically reported from him. Loud laughter in streets is unbecoming.

### Wisdom in the Ant's Speech

One reason was clear — to protect her colony from being trampled. Another reason according to Allama Raazi: she feared her people might see Sulaiman's splendor and become ungrateful for their own lesser blessings. So she ordered them indoors to avoid envy.

**Lesson:** One should frequent the company of the worldly less, lest the heart covet their wealth.

### The Ant's Intelligence vs. Human Ignorance

The ant queen said: "lest Sulaiman and his armies crush you while they perceive not" — she explicitly acknowledged that Allah's prophets are sinless, that they would never intentionally kill creatures unjustly, but that it might happen inadvertently. This contains a great lesson: belief in the *ismat* (infallibility) of prophets is obligatory. Those who claim prophets commit sins are less intelligent than an ant.

### Ants' Practical Wisdom

Ants store provisions for winter, cut grains in half to prevent sprouting, and cut coriander and lentils into four pieces because they still sprout even when halved. Allama Aalusi says this proves animals possess rational understanding.

### The Hoopoe — Missing from the Army

> "He inspected the birds and said: 'Why do I not see the hoopoe? Or is he among the absent? I will surely punish him with a severe punishment or slaughter him unless he brings me clear authority.'"

Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام</sup> personally inspected his subjects, especially the weak. When he checked his army, the hoopoe, whose name was Yafoor, was absent without permission. Sulaiman expressed anger: either the hoopoe must present a valid excuse, or face severe punishment — plucking its feathers or slaughtering it.

The hoopoe also served a practical purpose: it would locate underground water by placing its beak on the ground, and jinn would dig there. It also had the duty of shading Sulaiman.

**Lesson for rulers:** A ruler must inspect the condition of his subjects, lest the powerful oppress the weak. Hazrat Umar <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> was always aware of his subjects' conditions — he once said: "If a wolf takes a lamb from the banks of the Euphrates, Umar will be questioned about it."

### The Hoopoe Returns

The hoopoe returned quickly, out of fear of Sulaiman. It came dragging its wings and tail in humility. Sulaiman pulled its head in anger. The hoopoe said: "O prophet of Allah, remember that one day you and your people will stand before Allah." Hearing this, Sulaiman trembled and forgave it.

Hazrat Akrama <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> says the hoopoe was also forgiven because it served its elderly parents — it would bring food and feed them.

### The Hoopoe's News

> "It said: 'I have seen what you have not seen, and I bring you certain news from Saba. I found a woman ruling over them, and she has been given of all things, and she has a great throne."'

The hoopoe explained its delay: it had gone to the land of Saba and found a queen named Bilqis bint Sharaheel ruling there. She possessed all kinds of wealth, and her throne was immense — 80 cubits long, 40 wide, 30 high, made of gold and silver, decorated with pearls, rubies, and emeralds, locked in seven chambers.

**Why was this hidden from Sulaiman?** Although Saba (Yemen) was only three days' journey from Shaam, Allah concealed Bilqis's situation from him for wisdom and purpose — so that he would turn to Allah in gratitude and supplication, and so that Allah's power would be manifest.

### The Land of Saba

Saba, with its capital Ma'arib (three days from Sana'a), was founded by Saba bin Yashhab bin Ya'arib bin Qahtan. It was a fortified, prosperous city with pure air, sweet water, abundant gardens, and diverse animals. It was so clean that flies and mosquitoes were unknown. Two mountains were joined by a great dam to collect rainwater, with channels distributing water through canals.

Prosperity led to luxury, immorality, and disobedience. When their transgression exceeded limits, divine punishment came as a flood — the dam burst and the entire region was destroyed. The Quran mentions this in several places.

### Bilqis and Her People's Religion

They worshipped the sun. Some worshipped fire, others were atheists. Satan had adorned their evil deeds for them.

**Note on female leadership:** The Quran and tafsir make clear that they were disbelievers. Their practices provide no evidence for the permissibility of a female ruler in Islam. The Prophet ﷺ said: "A people who entrust their affairs to a woman will never succeed." If irreligious people in a Muslim land appoint a female ruler, their action is rebellion against Allah and His Messenger.

### Sulaiman's Letter to Bilqis

> "He said: 'Go with this letter of mine and deliver it to them, then turn away and see what they respond.' She said: 'O chiefs, indeed a noble letter has been delivered to me. Indeed it is from Sulaiman, and indeed it begins: In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Do not be arrogant toward me, and come to me in submission.'"

Sulaiman wrote the letter, perfumed it with musk, sealed it, and gave it to the hoopoe. The hoopoe found Bilqis sleeping in her seventh chamber with all doors locked and keys under her head. It entered through a skylight and dropped the letter on her chest. Another narration says the hoopoe fluttered until she looked up, then dropped the letter in her lap.

**Why Bilqis called the letter *kareem* (noble):** 1) Its content was beautiful. 2) It came from a noble king. 3) It was sealed — sealed letters were called noble.

The prophets keep their letters brief and to the point. Sulaiman's letter contained complete guidance in a condensed form: knowledge (Bismillah teaches that the Creator exists, is All-Knowing, All-Powerful, Ever-Living...) and action (do not be arrogant, come in submission).

**Objection:** Why did Sulaiman start with his own name before Bismillah?

**Answer:** He did not. Bilqis, when reporting to her chiefs, first said "it is from Sulaiman," then read the letter which began with Bismillah. The Quran reports in that order. Sulaiman began with Bismillah.

### Bilqis Consults Her Chiefs

> "She said: 'O chiefs, advise me in this matter. I do not decide a matter until you are present with me.' They said: 'We are men of strength and great might, and the command is yours — see what you command.'"

Bilqis sought counsel to win her chiefs' hearts and ease her difficulty. Her chiefs said they had military strength, weapons, and steadfastness — but ultimately the decision was hers.

### Bilqis Chooses Diplomacy

> "She said: 'Indeed kings, when they enter a city, ruin it and humiliate its honorable people — and thus they do. And I will send them a gift and see with what the envoys return.'"

Bilqis argued that kings destroy cities, kill people, take prisoners, exile people. Victory is not guaranteed despite numbers and strength, so the path of peace should be chosen.

She intended the gift as a test: if Sulaiman is a king, he will accept the gift. If he is a prophet, he will reject it, and she must accept his faith.

### Sulaiman Rejects the Gift

> "When he came to Sulaiman, he said: 'Do you provide me with wealth? What Allah has given me is better than what He has given you. Rather you rejoice in your gift. Return to them, for we will surely come to them with armies they cannot withstand, and we will drive them out in humiliation.'"

Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام> said: "Allah has given me prophethood, knowledge, and eternal success. He has also given me enough worldly wealth that I need no increase. I am not a person to be won over by gifts. This wealth means nothing to me. Keep your gifts and rejoice in them yourselves."

To the envoy he said: "Return to your people and tell your queen: if you do not abandon your polytheistic beliefs and accept the faith I bring, I will come with my army, defeat you, expel you humiliated from your lands."

### The Throne Brought Before Bilqis Arrives

> "He said: 'O chiefs, which of you will bring me her throne before they come to me in submission?'"

When Bilqis prepared to come in submission, Sulaiman wanted to demonstrate Allah's power and his own prophethood before her arrival, to incline her heart toward faith. He also wanted to test her intelligence by changing the throne's appearance.

**An Ifreet (rebellious jinn) said:** "I will bring it before you rise from your assembly. I am strong and trustworthy."

> "The one who had knowledge of the Book said: 'I will bring it to you in the blink of an eye.'"

Sulaiman's servant, widely believed to be Aasif bin Barkhiya, brought the throne from Saba (1500 miles away) in an instant.

**Who was this person?** Various opinions: Hazrat Khizr, Hazrat Jibreel, or Aasif bin Barkhiya. The Quran describes him only by his attribute — "the one with knowledge of the Book" — indicating this knowledge was instrumental in performing this miracle.

**Proof of miracles of saints:** If an ordinary member of Sulaiman's community could do this through knowledge of the Book, what is impossible for a saint of the Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ community, who has knowledge not just of the Book but of *Kitabul Mubeen* and its mysteries? Those who deny the miracles of saints should reflect deeply on this verse.

### Sulaiman's Gratitude

When the throne appeared before him, Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام> said:

> "This is from the favor of my Lord, to test me — whether I will be grateful or ungrateful. And whoever is grateful, his gratitude is for his own good. And whoever is ungrateful — indeed my Lord is Free of need, Generous."

He did not feel pride but humbly acknowledged his Lord's favor. His servant's ability to perform this feat was Allah's grace, which was a test: would he be grateful or ungrateful?

**Tests come through both hardship and ease.** We easily recognize hardship as a test. But periods of comfort and abundant blessings are also a test — and a more severe one. Success in hardship requires patience; success in ease requires gratitude. True gratitude is not just verbal thanks but using the blessing in a way that pleases Allah.

### Bilqis in Sulaiman's Court

> "When she arrived, it was said: 'Is your throne like this?' She said: 'As if it is the same.' And we were given knowledge before her, and we had submitted."

Sulaiman had the throne altered before her arrival to test her perception. He asked: "Is your throne like this?" — not "Is this your throne?" (which would have given away the test). She answered wisely: "As if it is the same" — neither affirming it definitively (since it was altered) nor denying it (since it was indeed hers). She also indicated she had already recognized his prophethood before coming.

Then she was invited into the glass palace.

### The Glass Palace

> "It was said to her: 'Enter the palace.' But when she saw it, she thought it was deep water and uncovered her shins. He said: 'Indeed it is a palace made smooth of glass.' She said: 'My Lord, indeed I have wronged myself, and I submit with Sulaiman to Allah, Lord of the worlds.'"

Sulaiman had constructed a palace of clear glass over water containing fish. When Bilqis was told to enter, she thought the floor was water and lifted her garment. Sulaiman said: "It is glass, not water." She immediately realized her error and, overwhelmed by Sulaiman's majesty and prophethood, declared: "I have wronged myself. I submit to Allah, Lord of the worlds."

**Refutation of a false story:** The popular story that Sulaiman was told Bilqis had hairy legs like a donkey's and built the glass palace to see them is false. Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> states the glass palace was built to display the majesty of his prophethood and kingship. Bilqis uncovering her shins was her own mistake — she thought it was water. A prophet would never scheme to see the legs of a non-mahram woman. Just as the Prophet ﷺ laughed at Sa'ad's enemy being killed, not at his nakedness, Sulaiman had no such intention.

### Unreliable Incidents About Bilqis's Test

The following stories are not reliably established. Allama Aalusi says: "These reports — whether authentic or false, we do not know. It is possible some are fabricated."

1. Bilqis sent male slaves dressed as women (with bangles and earrings) and female servants dressed as men, asking Sulaiman to distinguish them. He had them wash their hands — those who washed both hands together were male slaves; those who poured water on one hand first were female servants.

2. She sent a pearl with no hole, asking him to pierce it. A termite said it would do it, took a hair in its mouth, and bored through. Sulaiman told the termite: "Your sustenance is in trees."

3. She sent a pearl with a crooked hole, asking him to thread it. A white worm threaded it and was told: "Your sustenance is in fruits."

4. A messenger was told: "If he speaks harshly, he is a king — do not fear him. If he speaks gently, he is a prophet — listen carefully." Sulaiman received the messenger with kindness.

### Sulaiman and the Horses

> "When there were presented before him in the afternoon the swift horses standing on three legs, he said: 'Indeed I have loved the love of good things over the remembrance of my Lord until the sun disappeared.' He said: 'Return them to me.' And he began to pass his hands over their shins and necks."

**Correct interpretation (Allama Raazi):** Sulaiman ordered horses to be presented before him so he could inspect their condition. The horses were described as *saafnaat* (standing calmly on three legs) and *jiyaad* (swift yet smooth). He said: "I love these horses for the remembrance of my Lord" — his love was for Allah's sake, for jihad, and in obedience to divine command.

When the horses passed and disappeared from sight, he ordered them brought back and lovingly passed his hands over their shins and necks — checking their health, feeling for ailments, and teaching his community that a ruler should personally attend to matters. He also had knowledge of veterinary medicine.

**Refutation of the false story:** Some narrate that Sulaiman was so absorbed in watching the horses that he missed Asr prayer, and in anger he cut off their legs and necks. Allama Raazi refutes this decisively:

1. It contradicts "I love these horses for the remembrance of my Lord" — if love was for Allah, prayer could never be forgotten.
2. The verse uses *mashan* (passing hands over), not *qata'a* (cutting). If *masah* means "cut," then "wipe your heads" in wudu would mean "cut your heads" — absurd.
3. If he had missed prayer, he should have repented — not butchered horses.
4. Could a prophet who forgot prayer then command his Lord to return the sun? This is insolence toward Allah.
5. Allah told the Prophet ﷺ: "Remember Our servant Dawood" as consolation — showing patience. If Sulaiman was sinful, what consolation would that be?

Allama Raazi concludes in amazement: "How have people accepted such weak reasoning, contrary to both intellect and shariah?"

### Forgetting to Say "Insha'Allah"

> "And indeed We tried Sulaiman and placed on his throne a lifeless body, then he turned back."

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Sulaiman said, 'Tonight I will sleep with 70 women, and each will give birth to a son who will be a great horseman fighting in Allah's way.' He did not say 'Insha'Allah.' He slept with them, but only one became pregnant, and she gave birth to a stillborn child."

In Muslim's narration: 70 women; in Bukhari's: 40. Angels told him to say "Insha'Allah" but he did not — whether due to forgetfulness or otherwise, it was not a sin. The angels' command was not obligatory on him. At most, this is *tark al-awla* (abandoning what is better), which may be called *zanb* (slip) in prophetic terminology.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "By Him in whose hand is my soul, if Sulaiman had said 'Insha'Allah,' every woman would have given birth to a horseman fighting in Allah's way."

**Alternative interpretation (Allama Raazi):** The "lifeless body" refers to Sulaiman himself during a severe illness that left him like a skeleton on his throne. When he prayed and was healed, he became energetic again — as if a dead body had been replaced by a living one.

### Jewish Fabrication Refuted

The Jews fabricated a story that Sulaiman married a woman named Jaraadah, daughter of a killed king. She mourned her father, so Sulaiman had Satan make an idol of her father for her to worship. When his vizier Aasif informed him, he broke the idol, punished the woman, and went into the wilderness to repent.

Another fabrication: Sulaiman would leave his signet ring (source of his power) with a concubine named Ameena. A Satan took his form, obtained the ring, and ruled for 40 days while Sulaiman wandered unrecognized, begging, and finally working for fishermen.

Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> refutes these thoroughly: "Know with certainty that this is false and baseless according to verified scholars." Satan cannot even overpower ordinary scholars, let alone prophets. It contradicts Allah's wisdom to give Satan authority over a prophet's wives.

### Sulaiman's Supplication

> "He said: 'My Lord, forgive me and grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me. Indeed You are the Bestower.'"

He first sought forgiveness, then asked for kingdom. Everyone asks according to their capacity and the One they ask from. Sulaiman asked from Rabbul Aalameen, the Most Generous.

Allama Paanipati clarifies: This does not mean Sulaiman's rank exceeds the Prophet's ﷺ — the Prophet ﷺ chose to be a *Nabi-e-Abd* (servant-prophet) over a *Nabi-e-Mulk* (king-prophet). Nevertheless, the Prophet's ﷺ command is effective over all jinn and humans. He once said: "Last night a rebellious jinn tried to corrupt my prayer, but Allah gave me power over him. I intended to tie him to a pillar of the mosque for all to see, but I remembered my brother Sulaiman's dua: 'Grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me.'" This is a clear indication that the Prophet ﷺ also has authority over jinn but did not manifest it.

### Sulaiman's Judgments

**The crop case:**

> "And remember Dawood and Sulaiman when they judged concerning the crop when the sheep of a people strayed into it by night. And We were witness to their judgment."

A man's sheep strayed at night into another's field and ate the crop. Dawood ruled that the sheep should be given to the crop owner, as their value was equal. Sulaiman, aged 11, suggested a better solution: the crop owner should keep the sheep temporarily, benefiting from their milk and wool, while the sheep owner should work to restore the field. When the field was restored, each would receive their property back. Dawood accepted this judgment.

Both judgments were *ijtihadi* (based on juristic reasoning), consistent with their shariah. In our shariah, if animals stray on their own, the owner is not liable.

**The two women and the child:**

The Prophet ﷺ narrated: Two women each had a child. A wolf took one child. Each claimed the surviving child was hers. They came to Dawood, who judged in favor of the elder woman. When they passed Sulaiman, he said: "Bring me a knife — I will cut the child in two." The younger woman cried: "Do not! Let her have him!" The older woman was silent. Sulaiman ruled in favor of the younger — her love revealed she was the true mother.

**Why did Dawood rule for the elder?** Perhaps the child resembled the elder, or their shariah gave priority to the possessor. When Sulaiman proposed dividing the child, the elder (false mother) was silent because she knew she was lying; the younger (true mother) could not bear the child's death and relinquished her claim.

**How did Sulaiman overturn his father's judgment?**

1. Dawood had not issued a final ruling — only a preliminary opinion based on possession.
2. Dawood had issued a *fatwa* (legal opinion), not a *qada* (binding judgment). A fatwa is not binding.
3. Perhaps their shariah allowed one mujtahid to overturn another.
4. Perhaps Sulaiman devised a stratagem to reveal the truth, and Dawood himself withdrew his judgment once truth was clear.

### Jinn Subjugated

> "And the devils — every builder and diver — subjugated to him. And others bound in chains."

Jinn skilled in construction built great structures rapidly. Others dived into the seas to extract pearls, rubies, and jewels. Sulaiman was the first to initiate pearl diving.

> "They made for him what he willed — lofty chambers, statues, bowls like reservoirs, and fixed cauldrons."

They built fortified palaces (*mahaareeb* — places requiring defense), statues (permitted in Sulaiman's shariah), large bowls feeding a thousand men, and massive fixed cauldrons on stands.

### The Death of Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

> "Then when We decreed death upon him, nothing informed them of his death except a termite eating his staff. When he fell, the jinn realized that if they had known the unseen, they would not have remained in humiliating punishment."

The jinn claimed knowledge of the unseen, intimidating humans. Allah's protective jealousy exposed their lie. Sulaiman <sup>عليه السلام</sup> died while leaning on his staff, standing in worship. His body remained upright, supported by the staff. The jinn, engaged in grueling labor under his authority, dared not stop, seeing him standing.

A full year passed. By divine command, a termite gradually ate through the staff from below. When it became hollow and could bear no more, it broke, and Sulaiman fell. Only then did the jinn realize their leader had been dead all along — proving their claim to knowledge of the unseen was utterly false.

**A miracle within this:** An ordinary person leaning on a staff would lose balance if drowsy, let alone dead. Yet Sulaiman remained standing for a full year. His face remained fresh like a flower, his body fresh and pure — no decay, no change. The heat of summer and cold of winter did not affect him. Allah showed the people the majesty of prophethood even after death.
