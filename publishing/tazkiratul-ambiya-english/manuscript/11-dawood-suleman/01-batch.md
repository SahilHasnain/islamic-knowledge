## Hazrat Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

### Lineage, Age, and Qualities

Hazrat Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> lived 100 years. He came 599 years after Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> (other narrations also exist). He was a descendant of Yahooda bin Yaqoob.

> "And remember Our servant Dawood, the possessor of strength. Indeed, he was one who repeatedly turned back to Allah."

The word *Zal Ayd* means both "possessor of strength" and "possessor of blessings." Allah ta'ala gave him strength to worship and abstain from sin. When Allah praised his strength, it refers to praiseworthy strength — the strength to act on worship and stay away from sin. Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> turned back to Allah in all his affairs.

### His Worship

He fasted every alternate day — one day fasting, one day breaking fast. This was a jihad against his nafs, because the nafs is like a child: feeding it one day and denying it the next is very difficult. He made his nafs submit in a way that ordinary people could not bear.

He would stand in worship before Allah for half the night, sleep for a third of the night, and then remain awake for the remaining sixth part engaged in worship.

### Dawood and Sulaiman Granted Knowledge

> "And indeed We gave knowledge to Dawood and Sulaiman, and they both said: 'All praise belongs to Allah, who has favored us above many of His believing servants.'"

The knowledge here refers to judgment between people, understanding the speech of birds, and other sciences. The favor granted was prophethood and the subjugation of jinn and devils. Knowledge is the means by which a person attains virtue. One should give thanks upon receiving blessings. Mentioning blessings as gratitude is commendable; mentioning them out of pride is impermissible. It is the sunnah of the noble prophets.

### Dawood Given Kingship

> "O Dawood, indeed We have made you a vicegerent on earth, so judge between people with truth and do not follow desire, lest it lead you astray from the way of Allah. Indeed, those who go astray from the way of Allah will have a severe punishment because they forgot the Day of Reckoning."

Hazrat Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was given both prophethood and kingship.

**The command "do not follow desire":** This was to teach the ummah. If a sinless prophet was given this command, then others are certainly bound by it.

**A lesson:** A caliph asked Hazrat Umar Bin Abdul Azeez <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup>: "Have you heard that a caliph's deeds are not written down and no sin is recorded against him?" Hazrat Umar Bin Abdul Azeez replied: "Are caliphs more virtuous than the prophets?" Then he recited this same verse, meaning that Allah commanded His prophet to not follow desire — so what is a caliph compared to that?

**The necessity of a ruler:** In any society, people engage in different professions — farming, grinding, baking, weaving, sewing. When all people live in one land with different occupations, differences and disputes naturally arise. Someone with authority and power is needed to resolve conflicts and enforce judgment. Only a king whose command is binding can do this.

If a king rules by his own desires, pursues worldly gains, oppresses people, sacrifices his subjects to preserve his rule, the world is ruined. Bloodshed and plunder become widespread, and eventually the king himself is destroyed. But if a king rules by shariah, the system of the world is set right, and doors of goodness open.

### Mountains and Birds Subjugated

> "Indeed, We subjected the mountains to exalt with him at evening and sunrise, and the birds gathered — all were obedient to him."

Allah ta'ala subjected the mountains to Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — wherever he walked, the mountains would move with him. This miracle demonstrates Allah's perfect power and wisdom.

His voice was exceptionally beautiful, with awe and authority. When he would recite the Zaboor beautifully, the mountains would echo with beautiful glorifications.

This shows Allah's power: He creates life in mountains, grants them consciousness, gives them the ability to speak and glorify Allah. Similarly, in the Quran:

> "When his Lord manifested Himself to the mountain, He made it crumble to dust" — meaning Allah created understanding in the mountain, then gave it capacity. But when He showed His attributive light, the mountain could not bear it.

When Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> recited the Zaboor, birds would gather near him, listen attentively, draw so close that he would take them by the neck and express affection toward them. Some scholars say Allah placed such an effect in his voice that when he recited Zaboor, flowing water would stop, trees would seem to join in glorification, and their leaves would begin to fall.

**Benefit:** Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> says: Since mountains moved with him and birds gathered to him — the gathering of birds is like a resurrection, and the One who gathers them is Allah. This shows Allah's power to resurrect — if He can give speech to the insentient and life to the lifeless, then surely He can restore life on the Day of Judgment.

### The Ishraq (Chaasht) Prayer

The verse mentioning *al-ishraq* is used as evidence for the *Salaat al-Duha* (Chaasht prayer). Hazrat Umme Haani <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہا</sup> narrated that the Prophet ﷺ came to our home, performed wudu, and prayed Chaasht, saying: "O Umme Haani, this is the prayer of Ishraq."

Hazrat Taa'os narrates from Hazrat Ibne Abbas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہما</sup> that he was asked: "Do you find mention of Chaasht prayer in the Quran?" The companions said no. He recited the verse of *al-ishraq* and said this was the prayer Hazrat Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> used to pray. He said: "I always wondered about Salaat al-Duha in the Quran, and I found it."

It becomes clear that Ishraq and Duha (Chaasht) are the same prayer at the same time. The early time is called Ishraq, the later time Duha (just before noon). Sometimes it was prayed early and sometimes late, giving the impression of two separate prayers — but it is one prayer.

**Number of rak'aat:** Minimum 2, minimum of perfection 4. One may pray more — 8 or even 12 rak'aat. All numbers have evidence from hadith.

Bukhari and Muslim record that the Prophet ﷺ would pray Chaasht with 4 rak'aat, and increase as Allah willed.

### The Splendor of His Kingdom

> "And We strengthened his kingdom and gave him wisdom and decisive speech."

Hazrat Ibne Abbas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہما</sup> narrates that 36,000 men would guard Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> at night. In the morning, he would tell them: "Go back, the prophet of Allah is pleased with you." Another narration says 40,000 men would come out of love and devotion to guard him, considering it a blessing.

Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> found this number excessive — there was no need for so many. While the prophet did not need them, his servants needed the honor of serving him.

**The incident of the cow claim:** A man from Bani Israel claimed another man owed him a cow. The defendant denied it. Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> asked the plaintiff for witnesses — he had none. Dawood told them both to leave and he would reflect on the matter.

Dawood fell asleep and was told in a dream: "Kill the plaintiff." He thought: "This is a dream; I should not be hasty." The second night the same command came, and again he did not act. The third night he was told: "Kill him, or punishment from Allah will come upon you."

Dawood sent for the man. He said: "Allah has commanded me to have you killed." The man said: "Will you kill me without witnesses or proof?" Dawood said: "Yes, by Allah, I will execute Allah's command upon you."

The man said: "Do not be hasty. Let me tell you the truth. I did not come here because of the cow claim. I killed this man's father by deception and kept it hidden. I have been caught by Allah's grip because of this sin." Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> ordered his execution.

After this incident, the awe of Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> settled deeply in the hearts of Bani Israel, and the splendor of his kingdom was established.

Allah gave him wisdom. The Quran says:

> "Whoever is given wisdom has been given abundant good."

Wisdom includes knowledge, good deeds in deen and dunya, and correct beliefs.

**Decisive speech:** Allah granted Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> decisive speech — the ability to convey Allah's commands with great effect. While animals have some awareness, they cannot communicate it to others as humans can. Among humans, some speak in a confused manner, while others can convey their meaning perfectly. Allah gave Dawood perfection in both inner faculties and outer speech.

### Iron Made Soft

> "And We made iron soft for him: 'Make full coats of mail and balance the links.'"

Iron would become soft in his hands like wax or kneaded dough — no need for fire or hammering. He would shape it as he wished, making very beautiful and balanced coats of mail — neither too big nor too small. Some commentators say he never even needed rivets.

Hazrat Maqaatil narrates: When Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> became king of Bani Israel, he would go out at night in the guise of a common man and ask people: "How is King Dawood?" Once he met an angel in human form. When asked, the angel said: "He is a very good man. If only one thing were not missing, he would be perfect." Dawood asked what that was. The angel said: "He eats from the treasury. If he ate from his own earnings, his virtues would be complete."

Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> prayed: "O Allah, teach me how to make armor and make it easy for me." Allah taught him and made iron soft in his hands. He would spend a third of his income for the welfare of Muslims, completing one coat of mail daily. His armor sold for 1,000, 4,000, or 6,000 dirhams. He would spend on himself, his family, and give to the poor and needy from this income. He made 360 suits of armor, sold them, and earned enough dirhams that he no longer needed the treasury — he also gave generously to the poor.

### The Incident of the Two Plaintiffs — The Truth

> "And has there come to you the news of the litigants when they climbed over the wall into the prayer chamber? When they entered upon Dawood, and he was frightened of them. They said: 'Fear not. We are two litigants, one of whom has wronged the other. So judge between us with truth and do not be unjust, and guide us to the right way. Indeed this is my brother; he has ninety-nine ewes and I have one ewe. So he says: Entrust it to me, and he has overpowered me in speech.'"

**Note:** Before investigating this incident as commonly narrated, the correct interpretation must be established, and the views of verified scholars presented.

The Quranic style uses an interrogative opening to draw the listener's attention for taking lesson.

**Context:** Two parties came by scaling the wall and suddenly entered Dawood's worship chamber unexpectedly. "Mehraab" refers to his prayer chamber where he struggled against his nafs.

**Dawood's routine:** He would spend one day on governance and judgments, the second day on household duties. The third day was exclusively for worship. He would post guards at his prayer chamber so nobody would disturb him. On that day, no one dared enter.

**The incident:** While engaged in worship, it was astonishing that people would scale the wall and enter without permission. Dawood was frightened. They said: "Do not fear. We are two parties who have come for judgment. Judge with justice and guide us to the straight path."

One said: "This is my brother. He has ninety-nine ewes and I have only one. He demands that I give him mine too, so his count reaches one hundred, and he speaks to me with such force that I cannot reply."

Dawood judged: "This is clear oppression. He cannot bear to see even one ewe with you. He has wronged you."

He said: "Most partners oppress each other — the one with more deprives the weaker of their little share — except those who believe and do good deeds. But such people are few."

At that moment, Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> realized he was being tested. He immediately sought forgiveness and fell into prostration.

### Refutation of the False Story

**The Bible version:** The book of 2 Samuel chapter 11 mentions that Dawood saw a woman bathing from his roof, sent for her (Uriah's wife), committed adultery with her, and then arranged for Uriah to be killed in battle.

**Scholarly refutation:** Imam Fakhruddin Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> decisively refuted this, calling it utterly false. Key arguments:

1. No sinful person would accept such an accusation against themselves; how could it be attributed to a prophet?
2. If accepted, it would prove two major crimes against Dawood: murder and immorality.
3. Allah revealed this verse to console the Prophet ﷺ and show Dawood's excellent example. If these crimes had occurred, Allah would not have comforted His Beloved with such a person.
4. The verses that praised Dawood as "Our servant, strong in worship, constantly turning back" would contradict such vile actions.

Hazrat Ali <sup>كرم الله وجهه</sup> said: "If anyone speaks about Dawood as storytellers do, I will give 160 lashes."

**The correct interpretation (Allama Raazi, Allama Abu Hayyan):**

A group of Bani Israel had become hostile to Dawood and were plotting to kill him. They saw his third-day seclusion as an opportunity. They scaled the wall, planning to assassinate him while the guards were unaware. When they reached his chamber, they found many people present and could not execute their plan. They fabricated the story of a dispute to explain their intrusion.

Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> realized their evil intent. He was initially angry and thought of taking revenge, but then forgave them and sought forgiveness himself — why had the thought of personal revenge even crossed his heart?

In another interpretation, they came through an unusual path at an unusual time. Dawood feared they might kill him (as a hostile group existed). When he realized they only wanted a judgment, he felt that his suspicion of them was beneath the dignity of prophethood, so he sought forgiveness.

**Ibn Arabi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> said:** "Preachers should not narrate false stories in their sermons. The Prophet ﷺ said: 'When a person lies, the angels flee thirty miles.' When a preacher knows angels attend his gathering, it is incumbent upon him to speak the truth."

**Conclusion:** The stories attributed to Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — that he committed immorality with a woman and had her husband killed, or that he forced someone to divorce his wife — are all baseless fabrications from Jewish sources. The true account is what Imam Raazi and the scholars of *Tafseer Kabeer* and *Behrul Muheet* have established.

### The Command of Khilafat and Justice

> "O Dawood, indeed We have made you a vicegerent on earth, so judge between people with truth and do not follow desire, lest it lead you astray from the way of Allah. Indeed, those who go astray from the way of Allah will have a severe punishment because they forgot the Day of Reckoning. And We did not create the heaven and the earth and what is between them in vain — that is the assumption of those who disbelieve. So woe to those who disbelieve from the Fire. Or should We treat those who believe and do righteous deeds like those who spread corruption on earth? Or should We treat the righteous like the wicked?"

Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was being told: "You are not from a royal dynasty who inherited this throne. You were an unknown shepherd, and We favored you with this vast kingdom. The way to thank Us is to judge with justice, not let personal likes and dislikes influence your decisions. If you sacrifice justice for desire, you will lose Allah's guidance and face severe punishment."

**Khaleefa vs. Badshah:** Allama Qaazi Sanaullah Paanipati writes that Hazrat Umar <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> once asked Hazrat Talha, Zubair, Ka'ab, and Salman <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہم</sup>: "What is the difference between a khaleefa and a king?" Salman replied: "A khaleefa judges with justice, distributes wealth equally, and is compassionate to his subjects as a man is to his family. He judges by the Book of Allah."

Hazrat Umar <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> also once asked: "I do not know whether I am a khaleefa or a king." Someone said: "The difference is clear. A khaleefa takes and gives with justice and spends in the right way — and by Allah's grace, you do this. A king oppresses people, takes from some and gives to others." Hearing this, Umar fell silent.

Islam prefers the word *khaleefa* (vicegerent) over *badshah* or *sultan* because those contain notions of self-importance. *Khaleefa* means deputy — one who implements his Master's commands within the authority given to him. This is the fundamental difference between Islamic and other systems of governance.

A ruler's primary duty is to do justice. No external influence — recommendation, greed, fear, or personal interest — should affect his judgment. A ruler who fails this has forgotten the Day of Recompense, regardless of what he claims.

The disbelievers think this worldly life is all there is — enjoy it, earn wealth, don't worry about halal and haram. Allah refutes this delusion: this universe is not created in vain. A believer and a corrupt person are not equal. The Day of Judgment will surely come — the righteous will be rewarded and the wicked disgraced.
