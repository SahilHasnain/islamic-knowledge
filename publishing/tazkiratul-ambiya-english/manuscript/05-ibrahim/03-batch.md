### The Punishment for Breaking the Idols

When Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> destroyed the false gods of the disbelievers and also overcame them with arguments, they decided to take revenge and proposed the harshest of all punishments — that he be burned in fire. Although only Allah ta'ala can inflict the punishment of fire, and it is not permissible for any person to punish someone with fire, Namrood and his people sentenced him to be burned. Allah ta'ala mentioned this:

> "They said, 'Build for him a structure and throw him into the blazing fire.'"

That is, build a huge wall around, light a fire in it, and throw Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> into it.

> "They said, 'Burn him and support your gods, if you are to act.'"

Consider how foolish these people were — they could not even understand that the idols they were helping, who could not even help themselves, how could they be worthy of being gods?

### The Incident of Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Being Thrown into the Fire

The dimensions of the enclosure built to burn Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> were related by Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>: its height was 30 cubits (45 feet), its width 20 cubits (30 feet), and its length 30 cubits (45 feet).

### The One Who Suggested Throwing Him into the Fire

Hazrat Mujahid says that Hazrat Abdullah bin Umar <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> said, "O Mujahid, do you know who was the first to suggest burning Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>?" I said, "I do not know." He said, "It was a man from the villages of Persia named Ikraad. In some places, his name is fully mentioned as Ikraad bin Atiya. There are two other statements about his name — according to one, it is Hayoon, and according to the other, it is Hader." Allah ta'ala caused that man to sink into the earth, and he will continue sinking until the Day of Judgment.

Before being thrown into the fire, Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was imprisoned. Then they began to build a four-walled structure like a furnace. When the structure was ready, they started collecting wood. They gathered all kinds of wood for 40 days. If an old woman fell ill, she would say, "If I recover from this illness, I too will bring wood to burn Ibrahim."

### What Kind of Fire Was It?

When all the people together had gathered a huge quantity of wood after 40 days of labor, the fire was lit. The flames of the fire began to converse with the heavens. Such a great and severe fire was it that no bird could even fly in the air above it.

### Satan's Guidance for Throwing Him into the Fire

When the fire blazed so intensely that its heat spread far and wide, and no human had the strength to approach it, the disbelievers became anxious that all their effort was being wasted, because they could not figure out how to throw Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> into the fire. Then Satan came and guided them: a mangonel should be prepared, Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> should be tied with ropes, placed in the mangonel, and thrown into the fire. It should be noted that this was the first mangonel ever made in the world. Afterward, it continued to be used in wars, and stones were hurled like balls through the mangonel.

### The Cry of the Creatures of the Heavens and the Earth

When Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was tied with ropes and placed in the mangonel, all of Allah ta'ala's creatures of the heavens and the earth — except jinn and humans — cried out and complained before Allah ta'ala: "O Master of the Universe! On earth, there is no one besides Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> who worships You. O Allah, today he is being burned because he took Your name." The angels of the heavens and the earth, the animals, the wild beasts, and the birds — all were astonished and distressed seeing this scene. They were unaware of Allah ta'ala's wisdom. They were thinking, "What will happen now? The one who takes Allah's name will be burned today. Then who will worship Allah ta'ala on earth?"

### The Angels Asked Permission from Allah ta'ala to Help Him

The angels of the heavens and the earth submitted before Allah ta'ala: "O Allah, permit us to help Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>." Allah ta'ala granted them permission: "If he wishes to seek help from you, then you may help him. But if he does not seek help from anyone besides Me, then I know him better. I am his Protector. Leave his matter to Me. Indeed, he is My *Khaleel* (intimate friend). At this time, on the entire face of the earth, I have no other *Khaleel* besides him. I am his Deity — he has no deity besides Me."

### Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Trust in Allah ta'ala

The angel appointed over the winds came to Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, and the angel appointed over the waters also presented himself. Both submitted: "Permit us to extinguish the fire." He said, "I have no need of your help. My Allah is sufficient for me, and He is the best Disposer of affairs."

Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came to him and said, "If you need my help, I will help you." He said, "I have no need of your help." Jibreel said, "Then at least ask your Lord ta'ala." He said, "He knows my state — He is sufficient for me without my asking."

SubhanAllah! How great was Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> trust in Allah ta'ala? It is said that seeking help from anyone other than Allah is not permissible. If it were permissible, Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> would have sought help from the angels. Would that these people understood that the rank of the noble prophets is higher than that of the angels — what need do they have to seek help from those of lower rank? Then how can an ordinary person have trust like Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>? He did not even ask Allah ta'ala — "He Himself knows. What need do I have to ask?"

### The Lizard Blew into the Fire

In a narration of Bukhari and Muslim, it is stated that the lizard used to blow into the fire. In Muslim, it is narrated from Hazrat Sa'ad <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> that the Noble Prophet ﷺ ordered it to be killed and called it an evil creature. In one narration, killing a lizard with the first blow carries more reward, killing it with the second blow carries less reward, and killing it with the third blow carries even less reward.

### The Amazing Scene of Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> in the Fire

Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> refused to take help from the angels. He did not even ask Allah ta'ala to save him before going into the fire. Only one thing was in his view: "Whatever my Lord is pleased with, I am pleased with that." When the disbelievers tied him and placed him in the mangonel to throw him into the fire, he recited these blessed words: "There is no deity besides You. Pure is Your Essence. All praise is for You. All things belong to Your dominion. You have no partner."

The disbelievers threw him into the fire. Allah ta'ala commanded the fire: "O fire, be cool and safe for Ibrahim." Along with commanding the fire to become cool, Allah ta'ala also commanded it to be safe, so that Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> would suffer no harm.

In Musnad Ahmad, the statement of Hazrat Ali <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> is mentioned: "If Allah ta'ala had not commanded *salaman* (safe), the fire would have become so cold that he would have died from the cold."

It is reported in a narration that when Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was thrown into the fire, the fire continued to burn on the outside, but its heat could not reach Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Rather, a garden was created inside the fire. When he was thrown in, the angels caught him by his sides and sat him down on the ground at a place where there was a spring of sweet water, and all around, rose, narcissus, and jasmine plants and flowers presented a beautiful scene. The fire only burned the ropes with which he was tied, and even from their burning, he suffered no harm.

It should be noted that when the fire was commanded to become cool for Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, there was no fire left in the world that did not go out — that is, all the fires of the world were extinguished at once.

### How Many Days Did Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Remain in the Fire?

It is narrated from Minhaal bin Amr that he was informed that Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> remained in the fire for 40 or 50 days. Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "In the days I spent in the fire, I was as happy and comfortable as I have never been in my entire life." Allah ta'ala sent the angel appointed over the shadows to him in the form of Ibrahim himself, so that he would sit with him and he would find companionship and not feel any distress from being alone. Jibreel brought him a silk shirt from Paradise and said, "O Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, indeed your Lord says: Do you not know that fire cannot harm My beloved ones?"

### Namrood Saw Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> in a Garden

Namrood looked from the height of his palace and saw Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> sitting in a garden, with another person (an angel) sitting beside him, and around him lay the stockpile of wood that had been gathered to burn him.

### Namrood Acknowledged Allah's Power Yet Remained Misguided

Seeing the blazing flames of the fire, Namrood called out, "O Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, can you come out of this fire?" He said, "Yes, I can come out." He said, "Get up and come out." (Perhaps he thought that when he came out, he would have to pass through the burning fire and would be burned.) Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> got up and walked out of the fire. Namrood asked him, "Who was that other man with you who looked just like you?" He replied, "He was the angel appointed over shadows, whom Allah ta'ala sent to me so that I would not feel any distress from being alone, but rather find companionship with him."

Despite acknowledging Allah's power, Namrood remained misguided. He said, "Since I have seen the honor and power of your Lord, I make a vow that I will offer a sacrifice to attain proximity to your Lord. Therefore, I am sacrificing 4000 cows before your Lord." Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said, "As long as you remain upon your religion, Allah ta'ala will not accept your sacrifice." He said, "I cannot abandon my kingship, but I will certainly offer the sacrifice." He slaughtered 4000 cows according to his vow and resolved not to punish Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> in the future. However, he remained upon his disbelief — faith was not destined for him.

In some narrations, when the disbelievers saw that the fire had not burned Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, they said, "Ibrahim has cast a spell on the fire." To test this, they threw an old man into the fire, and the fire scorched him to ashes.

### Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Refuted All False Deities

Among the people to whom Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was sent, some were idol worshippers, some star worshippers, some moon worshippers, some sun worshippers — and even Namrood was worshipped. He proved through arguments that idols cannot be deities. When the people did not benefit from the arguments, he broke the idols to remove his distress and comfort his heart.

The star worshippers invited him to worship the stars. He refuted them as well: "These stars are not worthy of being deities."

> "When the night covered him, he saw a star and said, 'Do you take this as my Lord?' Then when it set, he said, 'I do not like those that set.'"

That is, these stars themselves are bound to a natural system — they do not have the capacity to be a deity. The focus of my attention and love is only the Master of the Kingdom, the Creator of the Universe, Alone without partner. I do not love these stars, nor do I consider them the true effecters in any matter. When I do not love them, how can I consider worshipping them good?

The moon worshippers invited him to worship the moon. He refuted them as well: "You are misguided. The truth is that you yourselves should come to the straight path. Do you want to lead me astray from the right path? That is impossible."

> "Then when he saw the moon rising, he said, 'Do you call this my Lord?' Then when it set, he said, 'If my Lord had not guided me, I would surely be among the misguided.'"

That is, this moon is also subject to someone's command. Its brightness and shine shift from one region to another — at one time it illuminates one area while another is deprived of its light. How can something that itself does not remain in one state be a deity? This is Allah's favor upon me that He has guided me to the straight path and granted me the ability to remain upon it. Otherwise, among a people where misguided people are found everywhere, how can one individual remain upon the truth?

The sun worshippers invited him to worship the sun, saying, "This is very bright — it is certainly fit to be a great god." He refuted them as well: "I do not permit anyone to remain upon polytheism. Expecting polytheism from me is your foolishness."

> "Then when he saw the sun shining, he said, 'Do you call this my Lord? This is the greatest.' Then when it set, he said, 'O my people, I am free from what you associate (with Allah).'"

When he saw the sun shining, he said to the people, "Do you call this my Lord? Do you consider it the greatest god? This too sometimes illuminates one region while keeping another in darkness. When it illuminates another region, it plunges the first area into darkness. Can something that itself is bound to someone's command to revolve on its axis be a god? No, no, it can never be a god. O my people, give up associating partners with Allah ta'ala. I have already declared my dissociation. Your hope that I too will join you in acknowledging false deities is futile." He said, "The focus of my attention is only the Creator of the heavens and the earth. How can I associate any partner with Him? I am no polytheist." The people tried to frighten him by saying the false deities would harm him for opposing them. He replied in a few words:

> "I do not fear what you associate (with Him)."

### The Debate of Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> with Namrood

> "Have you not considered the one who argued with Ibrahim about his Lord, because Allah had given him kingship?"

This refers to the king of that time named Namrood bin Kan'aan bin Sanjareb. He was the first king to wear a crown and oppress his subjects. He claimed divinity. He was given the kingship of the entire world. His total lifespan was 800 years — 400 years in the pomp and splendor of his kingship, and 400 years a mosquito bit him, entering his brain through his nose, and he kept having shoes beaten on his head. He had a strong fortress built to challenge Allah ta'ala. His capital was in Babul.

### The Four Kings of the Entire World

Those who attained the kingship of the entire world are only four persons — two Muslims and two disbelievers:
1) Hazrat Suleman <sup>عليه السلام</sup>
2) Hazrat Sikandar Zulqarnain <sup>عليه السلام</sup>
3) Namrood
4) Shaddad bin Aad, whose name was Bakhtnasar.

Shaddad, while claiming divinity in opposition to Allah ta'ala, had a garden built for himself in the jungles of Adan. When the garden was ready, he went to see it. He had just placed both his horse's hooves inside his artificial garden when Izraail was commanded to seize his soul.

### When Did the Debate of Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> with Namrood Occur?

In *Kabeer* and *Ruhul Ma'ani*, two statements are quoted: one, that it occurred after the breaking of the idols and before being thrown into the fire; the second, that when he came out of the fire, this debate took place. The reason for the debate was that Namrood asked Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, "Who is your Lord that I should worship?"

### Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Proof About His Lord

> "When Ibrahim said, 'My Lord is the One who gives life and causes death.'"

That is, He creates life and death in bodies. This was the best guidance for one who does not recognize God. It told him: your own life testifies to the existence of your Lord. You were a lifeless drop of fluid — the One who gave this drop human form and granted it life is your Lord. And after life, the One who causes death to bodies is the Sustainer. The testimony of His power is present in your own life and death. To remain unaware of His existence is utter ignorance, foolishness, and extreme misfortune.

This argument was so powerful that Namrood could not answer it. Thinking that he would be left speechless and humiliated before the assembly, he resorted to quibbling:

> "He said, 'I give life and cause death.'"

### Namrood's Foolishness

Namrood summoned two persons, killed one, and spared the other, saying, "I too give life and cause death — that is, I seize someone and release him (I give life), and I kill someone (I cause death)." This was an extremely foolish statement. What comparison is there between killing and sparing, and creating life and death? His inability to bring the killed person back to life, and calling releasing someone "giving life," was enough to expose his disgrace. To the wise, it was clear that Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> argument was strong and conclusive — no answer was possible.

Since Namrood's argument was also found to be false, Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> pressed him in debate: "O false claimant of divinity! Creating life and death is not within your power — you cannot even imagine it. Do something easier than this and show me."

> "Ibrahim said, 'Indeed, Allah brings the sun from the east. So bring it from the west.' Then the disbeliever was dumbfounded. And Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people."

That disbeliever was left astounded by Allah. Otherwise, he could have quibbled in this way too: "I bring the sun from the east — tell your God to bring it from the west." But Allah ta'ala confounded him and left him speechless. Thus he was humiliated.

When he could not answer, he said, "I have no grain for you. Ask your Lord, whom you worship, to give you grain."

### The Sand Turned to Grain

As Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was returning from him, he stopped at a sand dune on the way. He filled a bag with sand from there and arrived home. He put the bag down and fell asleep. His wife Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> opened it and found the finest wheat inside. She ground it and prepared bread. When Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> woke up, food was served to him. He asked, "Where did this wheat come from?" His wife said, "From the bag you brought back filled." Then he understood that Allah ta'ala had provided him with sustenance.

### Namrood Was Deprived of Faith Once Again

Allah ta'ala sent an angel in human form to the oppressor Namrood. He came and said, "Your Lord says: Believe in Me, and We will maintain your kingdom." He said, "I am the Lord. Who is my Lord?" This incident occurred three times, but he remained deprived of faith.

### The End of Namrood and His People

A punishment of mosquitoes was sent upon Namrood's people. Such was the abundance of mosquitoes that the sun was hidden from them — sunlight did not reach the earth. Mosquitoes sucked their blood and ate their flesh, leaving only bones of everyone except Namrood. Namrood watched but could do nothing. Then a mosquito entered his brain through his nose and continued to eat his brain for 400 years. When a beating was delivered from above, it would stop biting; otherwise, it kept biting. So day and night, shoes and slaps were continuously struck on his head. The protocol of his court became that whoever came would first deliver a shoe to his head. For 400 years, he ruled in great comfort, and for 400 years, he was beaten. Then he died in disgrace. His lifespan was just over 800 years.

### Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Wished to See the Dead Brought to Life

> "And when Ibrahim said, 'O my Lord, show me how You give life to the dead.' He said, 'Do you not believe?' He said, 'Yes, but I ask that my heart may be at peace.'"

### Why Did Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Ask to See the Dead Brought to Life?

Imam Razi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> stated 17 reasons, but Allama Nawawi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> mentioned four as clear and evident, with the rest being less apparent.

**First reason:** He previously possessed inferential knowledge (*ilm istidlaali*). Now he wished to observe the process of how the dead are brought to life so that necessary intuitive knowledge (*ilm zaruri*) might also be attained. According to Imam Abu Mansoor, inferential knowledge can sometimes be subject to doubts, but necessary knowledge is free from doubts — knowledge acquired through direct observation is necessary.

It should be noted that for a prophet himself, there is no difference between inferential and necessary knowledge, because a prophet's knowledge is free from doubt. However, the reason for asking was so that no one could say, "You have not seen the dead brought to life — how can your knowledge be relied upon?"

**Second reason:** He wanted to know his rank with Allah and the status of his supplications being accepted. In this case, the meaning would be: "Do you not believe? Your rank with Me is great. You are My beloved and My *Khaleel*."

**Third reason:** He had no doubt before. He asked so that he might progress from *ilm al-yaqeen* (knowledge of certainty) to *ayn al-yaqeen* (the eye of certainty), because there is a great difference between the two. *Ayn al-yaqeen* is attained after direct observation, while *ilm al-yaqeen* does not require observation.

**Fourth reason:** When he established the proof against the polytheists that "My Lord is the One who gives life and causes death," then he submitted to Allah ta'ala, "Show me how You give life to the dead so that my proof before the disbelievers may be even stronger."

### The Dead Were Shown to Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Being Brought to Life

> "He said, 'Take four birds and make them attached to you. Then place a portion of them on each mountain. Then call them — they will come to you swiftly. And know that Allah is All-Mighty, All-Wise.'"

Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> took four birds — a peacock, a vulture (in one narration, a pigeon is mentioned instead of a vulture), a crow, and a rooster — and made them familiar with him. Then, according to Allah ta'ala's command, he cut them into pieces, and mixed their flesh, bones, etc., and placed them on four mountains. Then he called them: "O separate bones! O separate pieces of flesh! O severed veins! Join with one another so that Allah ta'ala may return your souls to you."

Hearing this, the bones moved toward each other — that is, the bones of each bird joined with its own bones. Feathers joined with feathers, pieces of flesh joined with other pieces, until blood joined with blood. In this manner, he was watching the dead being brought to life before his eyes, and he was granted the rank of *ayn al-yaqeen*. Then Allah ta'ala revealed to him: "O Ibrahim, you asked Me how I give life to the dead. Indeed, I created the earth and established four types of winds in it: 1) the north wind, 2) the south wind, 3) the east wind (*baade saba*), 4) the west wind (*baade bor*). On the Day of Judgment, all the dead and slain will gather at the sounding of the Trumpet, just as these four birds were gathered from the mountains before you. Creating all of them and giving them life after death is as easy for Me as creating or giving life to a single person."

**Note:** Some scholars have stated that all the parts of the four birds were mixed and placed on the mountains. When he called them with Allah's permission, they came to life and ran to him. The wisdom in making them familiar was that he would recognize upon their arrival that these were the same birds he had mixed and mingled with himself. Some said that he kept their heads with him. When he called, all their parts joined with their respective parts and came to their respective heads. Nothing is beyond Allah ta'ala's power — whatever form appears is a manifestation of His wondrous power.

### Why Were Birds Chosen from Among All Animals?

Why were four birds commanded to be made familiar, and not animals? There are two reasons. One: Allah ta'ala has given birds the power to fly in the sky and soar in the air. Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was also granted a high station — elevation of ranks and the courage to reach the kingdom (*malkoot*). Therefore, birds were commanded to be slaughtered and their flesh mixed and placed, so that his miracle might correspond to his ranks.

The second reason: When Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> slaughtered the birds, cut them into pieces, mixed them up, and placed them on mountain peaks, then called them — all the mixed pieces separated from each other and joined with their own pieces. Similarly, on the Day of Judgment, all scattered particles will gather, bodies will come into existence, and their souls will join them. Allah ta'ala's noble command supports this: "With eyes lowered, they will emerge from the graves as if they were locusts scattered."

### Why Were Four Commanded?

Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> only asked to see one dead person brought to life. But the Master of the Kingdom said, "O My *Khaleel*, you wished to see one dead person brought to life in consideration of your servitude. But I, due to My Lordship, show you four dead ones brought to life."

The second reason was that animals and other things are composed of the four elements — fire, earth, water, and air. Therefore, four were commanded to be slaughtered — "Just as I am giving life to these four, similarly I will give life to all compositions of the four elements."

### Why Were These Specific Four Chosen from Among All Birds?

The reason for selecting the peacock, vulture, rooster, and crow from among all birds is that man loves adornment, rank, and high status — these qualities are found in the peacock. Allah ta'ala said: "The love of desires has been made attractive to people."

Just as man is engrossed in eating a lot, similarly the vulture is also mostly concerned with eating. Likewise, just as man is concerned with fulfilling carnal desires, this trait is also found in the rooster. Man is more greedy for seeking and accumulating wealth — similarly, the crow is greedy for seeking and accumulating wealth, because besides the crow, no bird flies at night, and in severe cold, only the crow comes out during the day.

The selection of these four points to this wisdom: as long as man does not eliminate carnal desires, sensual desires, greed for wealth, and love of adornment and decoration, spirituality will not have any effect on his heart, nor will he attain comfort from the light of Allah ta'ala's majesty.

### Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Migration

> "And Ibrahim said, 'I am going to where my Lord has commanded me. He will guide me.'"

Explaining the meaning of the command *إِنِّي ذَاهِبٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّي سَيَهْدِينِ*, Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> says: "That is, I am going where my Lord has commanded me, where I can perform my worship in a better manner." Because to stay with a people who did not believe despite seeing his signs was now pointless. And since Allah ta'ala had also commanded, migration from there had become necessary.

Initially, he migrated from the people of Namrood and came to his uncle Haran in the city of Harran. Seeing Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> piety, Haran married his daughter Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> to him. Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> was a very beautiful woman. Among men, Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was the most handsome, and among women, Hazrat Saara — indeed, Hazrat Yusuf <sup>عليه السلام</sup> inherited his beauty from his grandmother Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup>. Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> continued his mission of preaching there as well, so his uncle Haran also expelled him from his house. During the journey of migration, the incident with the tyrant king of Egypt occurred, and Hazrat Saara was given Hazrat Haajra — this incident has already been mentioned earlier in the explanation of a noble hadith.

### The Migrating Caravan

When the migrating caravan set out from Haran, it consisted of only three persons — Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, Hazrat Saara, and Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. These three were the only people at that time who believed in Allah ta'ala's Oneness. Before departure, Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Hazrat Saara made a pact that each other's word would be obeyed. Along the way, Hazrat Haajra also joined them, so the caravan now consisted of four persons — all of whom believed in Allah.

### Prosperity in Philistine

When these four individuals journeyed from Egypt and arrived in Philistine, the people there recognized their worth and stature. They considered their arrival a source of blessing and offered much land to Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> as a gift. Through cultivation of this land, Allah ta'ala granted immense blessings. He acquired grain and livestock in great abundance. He provided travelers and the poor with shelter and food, thus attaining a unique status in hospitality.

### Hazrat Saara's Suggestion to Marry Hazrat Haajra

One day, Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> submitted, "By Allah ta'ala's grace and favor, we have no shortage of worldly necessities — food, drink, and dwelling are all abundant. However, we lack children. Therefore, you should marry Haajra <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup>. Perhaps Allah ta'ala will bless us with offspring — that is, a child born from Haajra's womb may become a source of comfort and tranquility for us." Thus, upon Hazrat Saara's suggestion, he married Hazrat Haajra.

### Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Supplication for Children

> "O my Lord, grant me a righteous child."

Allah ta'ala honored this supplication with acceptance and commanded:

> "So We gave him glad tidings of a forbearing boy."

This glad tidings refer to the birth of Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Allama Aalusi, while explaining the meaning of *haleem* (forbearing), states: "That is, he was given the glad tidings that a son would be granted to him who would be *haleem*. What greater example of forbearance could there be than this: when he was near the age of maturity, his father said to him, 'I see in my dream that I am slaughtering you. What is your opinion?' He submitted, 'Father, do as you are commanded. If Allah wills, you will find me among the patient.'"

### Glad Tidings of Ishaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> After Ismail

> "Then We gave her (Saara) glad tidings of Ishaq, and after Ishaq, of Yaqub."

The reason for giving glad tidings to Hazrat Saara was that the joy of children is greater for women than for men. Another reason was that Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> had no child of her own, so she was the one who received greater joy, since Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> already had a son — Ismail.

The mention of Yaqub in the glad tidings indicated that Hazrat Saara's <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> lifespan would be so long that she would also see her son Ishaq's son, Yaqub. Hazrat Saara's <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> age at that time was 90 years — in Tafseer Jalalain, 99 years is mentioned. Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was 120 years old. That is why Hazrat Saara said in astonishment: "How strange! Shall I bear a child when I am old and my husband is old? Indeed, this is a most astonishing thing."

### Ishaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Was Younger, Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Was Older

One year after the glad tidings of Ishaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> were given, Allah ta'ala granted his birth. Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was born 14 years before Ishaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — that is, 13 years after Ismail's birth, the glad tidings of Ishaq were given.

### Ismail, Ishaq, and Yaqub <sup>عليهم السلام</sup> Became Prophets

> "And We granted him (Ibrahim) Ishaq and Yaqub, and made each of them a prophet — a giver of news of the unseen."

> "And We bestowed upon them Our mercy and granted them true eminence and renown."

This indicates that Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> blessed lifespan was so long that he saw his grandson Yaqub <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. From this verse, it is also understood that this was the reward for migrating for Allah ta'ala's sake and leaving one's home and family: Allah ta'ala blessed him with sons, grandsons, wealth, and riches.

### Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Fulfillment of Promises

> "And mention Ismail in the Book. Indeed, he was true to his promise, a messenger and a prophet. He used to enjoin prayer and zakah upon his family, and he was beloved to his Lord."

Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> always fulfilled whatever promise he made. Once, he and a companion were traveling. When they neared a city, the companion said, "Sit here while I go to the city to buy food — or you go and I will wait." Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said, "I will wait for you; you go and buy food." The man went but forgot. After three days (in some narrations, after a whole year), he returned to find Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> still there. Astonished, he asked, "Are you still here?" He replied, "Yes, I had to wait as per the promise."

### Hazrat Haajra and Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Were Left in the Sacred Land

Allah ta'ala tested Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> by commanding him to leave his wife and son Ismail in the sacred land. At that time, there was no city or settlement there — it was a barren wilderness. The purpose of commanding him to leave them there was, firstly, to test him; secondly, to build the Kaaba and populate Makkah; and thirdly, to fulfill the pact made with Hazrat Saara during the migration — that her word would be obeyed. For although she had initially advised him to marry Haajra, after Ismail's birth, jealousy arose due to her own childlessness. She pleaded before Allah, "Command Ibrahim to take this son and his wife away from me." Allah ta'ala accepted her supplication and commanded Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> to leave them in the sacred land.

Where Zamzam is today, there was a tree. At that time, neither Makkah existed nor did any human dwell there, nor was there any water. He placed some dates in a pouch and some water in a waterskin, entrusted them to the mother and child, left them under a tree at the site of Zamzam, and turned back. Hazrat Haajra <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> followed him, asking, "O Ibrahim! Where are you going, leaving us here? There is no one here to care for us, no companion or friend, nor any settlement where we could find food and drink." She asked repeatedly, but he gave no answer. Then Hazrat Haajra <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> asked, "Did Allah ta'ala command you to do this?" He replied, "Yes." She said, "Then Allah will not let us be destroyed." Saying this, she returned to her place.

Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> reached a valley from where he could no longer see his wife and son. He turned his attention toward Baitullah Shareef — at that time, only its foundations were visible, resembling a mound — and prayed:

> "O our Lord, I have settled some of my offspring in a valley without cultivation, near Your Sacred House, O our Lord, so that they may establish prayer. So make the hearts of people incline toward them with love and affection, and provide them with fruits, so that they may give thanks."

Hazrat Haajra <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> continued nursing her baby Ismail until the water in the waterskin was exhausted and the dates were also finished. She herself became hungry and thirsty, and her milk also dried up. The baby was restless with hunger and thirst, moving his tongue over his lips. The mother could not bear her child's condition. She climbed a nearby hill, Safa, hoping to spot water or see someone. Then, with the same hope, she went to Marwa. When she reached the valley between them where she could not see the child, she would run; when she reached a point where she could see the child, she would walk at a normal pace. Even upon reaching Marwa, no water or person was visible. In this state of restlessness, she completed seven rounds between Safa and Marwa. On the final round at Marwa, she heard a voice. She said to herself, "Be quiet!" She thought perhaps she was hallucinating. "Who could be here? It must just be a sound I'm hearing." When she heard the voice again, she thought, "If there is anyone here who can hear a cry and respond, it can only be an angel." She looked and saw an angel standing near the baby. He struck his heel — or his wing — near Ismail's heel, and a spring of water gushed forth. Hazrat Haajra built a basin around the water, which was gushing forth. She drank herself, gave the baby to drink, and said to the water, "Zamzam" — stop, stop.

The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "May Allah have mercy on the mother of Ismail. If she had left Zamzam as it was (or if she had not scooped it quickly), Zamzam would have been a flowing stream."

When she drank from the spring, her milk also began to flow, and she fed the baby. The angel said to her: "Do not fear — you will not be destroyed. Indeed, this is the House of Allah. This child and his father will build it. Surely Allah does not waste the reward of His chosen ones."

After a while, the Jarham tribe passed by. Seeing birds flying overhead, they thought, "Birds are only found where there is water — there must surely be water here." They sent a scout who found a spring of water with a woman sitting nearby. They said, "Let us share in your water, and we will share our animals' milk with you." Hazrat Haajra <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> made a pact with them on this condition. This same Jarham tribe later arranged the marriage of a girl to Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.

**Benefit:** The effects of Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> supplication are clearly visible even today. The land of Makkah is mountainous and sandy, yet fruits of every kind — from the finest to the finest — are found there. People are drawn to this place from every direction. In every Muslim's heart, there is a yearning to visit Baitullah.

Hazrat Haajra <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup>, restless at seeing her child's thirst, circled Safa and Marwa. But Allah ta'ala was so pleased with the conduct of His Khaleel's wife and Ismail's mother that until the Day of Judgment, pilgrims will continue to circle there to revive this memory. The truth is that Hajj is nothing without the conduct of Allah's accepted servants. Walking with a striding gait during tawaf to revive the memory of the Noble Prophet ﷺ and his companions, and throwing pebbles at the Jamrat in Mina to revive the memory of Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — these very acts constitute Hajj.

### The Sacrifice of Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

After the migration, Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> prayed for a son: "O Allah, grant me a righteous child."

> "O my Lord, grant me a righteous child. So We gave him glad tidings of a forbearing boy."

His supplication contained three requests: O Allah, grant me a male child, may he reach the age of forbearance, and may he remain forbearing. Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> himself was *haleem* (forbearing). Allah ta'ala said about him:

> "Indeed, Ibrahim was most tender-hearted, forbearing."

He was granted a son who was also *haleem*, so that the son might be as noble and virtuous as the father and become a prophet of great stature. "Salah" — righteousness and proximity to Allah — is a most excellent quality. Therefore, Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> prayed for this quality for his son and also for himself:

> "O my Lord, grant me wisdom, and join me with the righteous."

> "Then when he reached the age of working with him, he said, 'O my son, I have seen in a dream that I am slaughtering you. So consider — what is your view?' He said, 'O my father, do as you are commanded. You will find me, if Allah wills, among the patient.'"

### The Age of Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> at the Time of the Sacrifice

According to some scholars, Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was 13 years old when the incident of the sacrifice occurred.

### The Purpose of the Test

Since the earlier noble verses mentioned that Allah ta'ala gave Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> glad tidings of a *haleem* son, He now applied a test to make it clear what a great, patient, and forbearing son the Lord had granted him — one who passed such a tremendous test with patience and a smiling face.

### Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Dream Over Three Nights

After the 7th of Dhul Hijjah passed, he saw a dream at night: someone was saying, "Indeed, Allah commands you to slaughter your son." In the morning, he pondered over it and remained in some doubt — was this truly Allah's command, or just an imaginary dream? For this reason, the 8th of Dhul Hijjah is called *Yaum al-Tarwiya* (the day of reflection). After the 8th passed, he saw the dream again at night. In the morning, he became certain that this was indeed a command from Allah ta'ala. Therefore, the 9th of Dhul Hijjah is called *Yaum al-Arfa* (the day of recognition). After the following night, he saw the dream a third time. In the morning, upon firmly resolving to act upon it, the 10th of Dhul Hijjah became *Yaum al-Nahr* (the day of slaughter).

### Why Act Upon a Mere Dream?

> "Indeed, Allah has made the dreams of the noble prophets true."

That is, their dreams are true, and they are obligated to act upon them.

### Three Types of Prophets' Dreams

1) The dream occurs exactly as seen — like our Noble Prophet ﷺ saw in Madina Tayyaba that he and his companions entered Makkah Mukarrama, and some companions shaved their heads while others trimmed their hair. This dream came true exactly one year later as seen: "Indeed, Allah has fulfilled His Messenger's true vision: you will surely enter al-Masjid al-Haram, if Allah wills, in safety, with your heads shaved or trimmed, without fear."

2) The dream is only a test, and its actual occurrence is not intended — like Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> saw in his dream that he was slaughtering his son. This was only a test. He carried out the test, but Allah saved Ismail and provided a ransom.

3) A similitude is given through some things in the dream — the exact thing shown does not occur, but it has some interpretation, and the actual occurrence is similar — like Yusuf's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> dream: "When Yusuf said to his father, 'O my father, I saw eleven stars and the sun and the moon — I saw them prostrating to me.'" In the dream, he saw the sun, moon, and eleven stars prostrating to him, but in reality, these things did not prostrate to him. Rather, his dream was fulfilled in this way: "And they fell down prostrating to him. And Yusuf said, 'O my father, this is the interpretation of my earlier dream. My Lord has made it true.'" Parents were shown in the form of the sun and moon in the dream, and eleven brothers in the form of eleven stars. The dream came true when all prostrated to him in respect — which was permissible in earlier shari'ahs, though it is forbidden in our shari'ah. Note that the prostration of worship is not permissible for anyone other than Allah ta'ala in any shari'ah.

### The Wisdom of Consulting the Son

Allah ta'ala commanded him to consult his son so that it might become evident how patient his son was in obeying Allah's command. This would bring coolness to his eyes. When he would see that his son had achieved the highest standard of forbearance, the son too would attain a great rank for enduring severe hardship with immense patience — reward in the Hereafter, and praise in this world as well.

Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> attained blessing by combining his firm resolve to be patient with the phrase "if Allah wills" (*insha Allah*). This points to the principle that when intending to do something in the future, it should be mentioned with *insha Allah*, because Allah ta'ala alone grants the ability to do good; similarly, avoidance of sins is also granted by His grace.

### Satan's Failure with Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

It is narrated from Hazrat Qatada <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> that Satan intended to succeed against Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and his son. He came in the form of a friend to stop him, but could not succeed against him. Then he tried to lead his son Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> astray, but could not prevail against him either. Then he took the form of a huge, fat, fresh-looking creature and blocked the valley so that they could not go forward. An angel was with Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> who said, "Strike him." He threw seven pebbles at him, and he moved out of the way. He tried to come forward again, and he again threw pebbles and removed him from the path. A third time, he similarly came forward and blocked the way, and he again threw seven pebbles and removed him from the path. Today, it has been made obligatory for pilgrims to act upon this Ibrahimic practice. SubhanAllah — how pleasing the practices of His beloved ones are to the Lord that He made them a part of a great act of worship!

### Ismail's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Advice to Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said to his father: "O my dear father! Before slaughtering, bind me tightly so that I do not struggle. Keep your clothes away from me so that they are not stained with my blood and my mother does not become distressed seeing them. Pass the knife swiftly across my throat so that death comes to me easily. When you go to my mother, give her my salam." After these words, father and son looked at each other. The father kissed the son; tears of love flowed, but there was no shortcoming in carrying out Allah ta'ala's command.

> "Then when they both submitted to Our command, and the father laid the son down on his forehead."

The suggestion to lay him on his forehead was also from Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, lest paternal love cause even a slight hesitation in wielding the knife.

> "And We called out to him: O Ibrahim! You have indeed fulfilled the dream. Indeed, this is how We reward the righteous. Indeed, this was a manifest trial. And We ransomed him with a great sacrifice, and We left for him (a good name) among later generations."

Before the knife was even applied, it was said to him, "You have made the dream come true" — as if he had already wielded the knife, and Jibreel Ameen had come and diverted its direction.

Allah ta'ala sent a large, fat, horned white sheep tinged with black as a ransom for Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. He was saved from slaughter and yet granted the reward of being slaughtered. And until the Day of Judgment, he was honored with the title of *Zabeehullah* (the one slaughtered for Allah's pleasure).

When Jibreel Ameen <sup>عليه السلام</sup> brought the ransom, he thought that Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> might act hastily, so he recited: "Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar." Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> raised his head toward the heavens, saw Jibreel bringing the ransom, and recited: "La ilaaha illallahu wallahu akbar." When Hazrat Ismail Zabeehullah <sup>عليه السلام</sup> heard it, he recited: "Allahu akbar wa lillaahil hamd."

The combined words of these three revered figures were made obligatory as *Takbeerat al-Tashreeq* upon those offering prayer — from the Fajr of the 9th of Dhul Hijjah to the Asr of the 13th — until the Day of Judgment, so that this remembrance may remain established.

**Note:** According to some sources, the second statement is attributed to Hazrat Ismail and the third to Hazrat Ibrahim.

### Which of Ibrahim's Two Sons Was the Sacrifice?

Although there is a difference of opinion on this matter — whether the sacrifice was of Ishaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> or Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — strong evidence makes it clear that it was Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Allama Razi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> has mentioned several proofs in this regard:

**1)** The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "I am the son of two sacrifices." Similarly, an Arab called him "O son of the two sacrifices" and he smiled. When asked how he was the son of two sacrifices, he ﷺ replied: "When Abdul Muttalib began digging the well of Zamzam, he vowed to Allah that if Allah made this task easy for him, he would sacrifice one of his sons. The draw fell in favor of Abdullah. His maternal family and some scholars decided that 100 camels be given as ransom. Thus, the Prophet's ﷺ father attained the honor of being the intended sacrifice. And it is also clear that the Prophet ﷺ is from the progeny of Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, not from Ishaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Therefore, the second sacrifice was certainly Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.

**2)** Hazrat Asma'i said: "I asked Abu Amar ibn Ula, 'Who was the sacrifice?' He replied, 'O Asma'i! Where has your intellect gone? Do you not know that Ishaq was not in Makkah? He was in Syria (Shaam). Only Ismail was in Makkah, engaged with his father in building the Kaaba. And the incident of the sacrifice also took place near Makkah, in Mina. Therefore, the incident of the sacrifice certainly relates to Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.'"

**3)** Allah ta'ala called Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> patient — not Ishaq. The Lord commanded:

> "And mention Ismail, Idrees, and Zulkifl — they were all among the patient."

And Allah ta'ala said concerning Ismail: "Indeed, he was true to his promise." The one who was to be slaughtered was the one who made a promise to his father:

> "You will find me, if Allah wills, among the patient."

When it became clear that the one to be slaughtered made a promise of patience to his father and fulfilled it, Allah called Ismail patient and true to his promise — therefore, the sacrifice was certainly Ismail.

**4)** Before Ishaq's birth, the Lord gave glad tidings to his mother in these words: "We gave her glad tidings of Ishaq, and after Ishaq, of Yaqub." If it is said that the command to sacrifice concerned Ishaq, then it must be determined whether the command was given before Yaqub's birth or after. If before his birth, Ibrahim's test could not take place, because he had already been told that Saara's son would be Ishaq and Ishaq's son would be Yaqub. If Ibrahim knows that Ishaq will have a son named Yaqub who is yet to be born, then Ishaq cannot be slaughtered — how could there be a test? And how could the Lord command contrary to His own command? If the command came after Yaqub's birth, that too cannot be proven, because Allah said: "When he reached the age of working with him." At the time of the sacrifice, Ibrahim's son was 13 years old (or in some narrations, 7). For Yaqub to be born and then command the sacrifice of Ishaq at this age is also contrary to reason.

**5)** While mentioning Ibrahim's migration, Allah said: "I am going to my Lord — He will guide me" — that is, to the land where my Lord commands. After migration, Ibrahim prays: "O my Lord, grant me a righteous child." Allah mentions the acceptance of this prayer: "So We gave him glad tidings of a forbearing boy." Then, mentioning the same son, He said: "Then when he reached the age of working with him, he said, 'O my son, I have seen in a dream that I am slaughtering you. So consider — what is your view?' He said, 'O my father, do as you are commanded. You will find me, if Allah wills, among the patient.'" After this entire incident, it becomes clear that this is Ismail, because the glad tidings of Ishaq were given specifically to Saara — Ibrahim had already received glad tidings of a son and had been granted one. If Ismail's age at the time of sacrifice was 13, then the glad tidings of Ishaq were given in the same year and he was born a year later. If Ismail was 7, then Ishaq was born 7 years after the incident.

**6)** It is also recorded in Ibn Kathir that the horns of the ram given as ransom at the time of the sacrifice remained fixed on the wall of the Kaaba for a long time. This also makes it clear that the sacrifice took place in Makkah Mukarrama. Had the sacrifice concerned Ishaq, it would have taken place in Syria, not Makkah.

How pleasing was Ibrahim's act of sacrifice to the Lord that until the Day of Judgment, those who possess the means and wealth will continue to practice it.

### The History of the Kaaba

#### Adam's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Construction

It has been narrated in Tafseer Azeezi from Ibn Asaakar and others that when Hazrat Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> descended to earth from Paradise, he submitted in the Divine Court: "O Allah, here I neither hear the glorification and exaltation of the angels, nor do I see any place of worship like I used to see Bait al-Mamoor in the heavens, around which the angels used to circumambulate." Allah ta'ala commanded: "Go where We show you the sign, build the Kaaba, circumambulate around it, and offer prayer facing it."

Hazrat Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> accompanied Adam to guide him and brought him to the place from which the earth was created — that is, the site of the Kaaba was the first place where foam appeared on water, and from that foam, the earth began. Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> struck his wing there, laying the foundation down to the seventh earth, which the angels filled with stones from five mountains: Mount Lebanon, Mount Tur, Mount Judi, Mount Hira, and Mount Tur Sina. They raised walls on each side as markers, and Adam <sup>عليه السلام</sup> would offer prayer facing it and circumambulate it.

According to some narrations, Bait al-Mamoor was placed upon that foundation. During the flood of Nooh, Bait al-Mamoor was lifted up, and the site of the Kaaba remained like a raised mound. People continued to visit it and pray for blessings.

#### Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Construction

When Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> left Hazrat Haajra and his son Ismail here, and a settlement developed, Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was commanded to take Ismail and build the Kaaba. A piece of cloud was sent to delineate the boundaries of the Kaaba, and Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> drew the outline. Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> built the structure on the foundations from Adam's time.

The height of the Kaaba was 9 cubits. From Rukn Aswad to Rukn Shaami: 33 cubits. From Rukn Gharbi to Rukn Yamani: 31 cubits. From Rukn Yamani to Rukn Aswad: 20 cubits. From Rukn Shaami to Rukn Gharbi: 22 cubits. Thus, the Kaaba at that time was rectangular (with opposite walls equal). One wall each of length and breadth was slightly shorter. Two doors were made, level with the ground — there were no locks or chains. Later, during the time of Tab'a Himyari, locks, chains, etc., were added. Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> built, and Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> passed stones, mortar, etc. SubhanAllah! With what magnificence was the House of Allah built? A prophet of Allah was the builder, and a prophet of Allah was the helper.

#### After Ibrahim's Construction

After his construction, partial renovations were made at various times. Once, the Amaliqa and Jarham tribes rebuilt it. After that, Qusai bin Kilab rebuilt it, making a roof from the wood of the *muql* tree, and instead of planks, date-palm wood was used.

#### The Quraish Reconstruction

A woman was burning incense in the Kaaba when a sudden spark ignited the roof, and the walls had already deteriorated. Therefore, the Quraish decided to build it anew. Waleed bin Mughira was appointed as the head of construction. It was decided that only lawful wealth would be spent. Since the wealthy people of that time possessed mostly wealth acquired through usury, the amount of lawful wealth collected was small. Due to the shortage of funds and some of their own objectives, the Quraish made several changes:

1) Part of the Kaaba's land was excluded — the building was made smaller. The area outside the Kaaba is called *Hateem*, where the *Meezab al-Rahmat* (drainspout) falls. Even today, it is marked by a small wall, and tawaf is performed outside it.

2) Instead of two doors, the Quraish made one door, and that too raised high, so that they could let in whomever they wished and prevent whomever they wished. This practice continues to this day — the door opens for kings, no matter how wicked they may be, while the pious and righteous never hear of the door being opened for them.

3) Inside the Kaaba, two rows of wooden pillars were made, with three pillars in each row.

4) The height of the Kaaba was doubled — from 9 cubits to 18 cubits.

5) Inside the Kaaba, near Rukn Shaami, a staircase was built to access the roof.

#### The Reconstruction of Hazrat Abdullah bin Zubair <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>

Hazrat Aisha Siddiqa <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> narrates: "Once, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ showed me the stones of Ibrahim's foundation adjoining the Kaaba (i.e., the Hateem) and said, 'The Quraish reduced it. If the people had not recently embraced Islam and there was no fear of their sentiments being stirred, I would have rebuilt the Kaaba on Ibrahim's foundations.'"

On the basis of this narration, Hazrat Abdullah bin Zubair demolished and rebuilt the Kaaba. He included the Hateem within the Kaaba, made two doors level with the ground, mixed fragrant soil with lime for plastering, perfumed the doors inside and out with ambergris, and covered it with a most valuable silk cloth.

Note: The first person to cover the Kaaba with a cloth was named As'ad, the king of Yemen, known by the title of Tab'a. He was the one who first established the urban foundation of Madina Tayyaba.

Abdullah bin Zubair's construction was completed on 27 Rajab 64 AH. Then, in 74 AH, Hajjaj bin Yusuf (the governor of Abdul Malik bin Marwan) demolished the structure and rebuilt it as the Quraish had built it. Later, Haroon al-Rashid wished to rebuild the Kaaba as Abdullah bin Zubair had built it (which was essentially the Ibrahimic construction), but the scholars of that time forbade it, saying, "Someone may oppose you and change it again. In this way, demolishing and rebuilding will become a game." After that, repairs continued, but the entire structure was not rebuilt from scratch.

#### The Present Structure of the Kaaba

In 1040 AH, Sultan Murad bin Ahmad Khan, the ruler of Constantinople, saw that the building had become very old. He demolished the entire structure except for the corner containing Hajr Aswad and rebuilt it — but on the same foundations and in the same style that Hajjaj bin Yusuf had built. Marble flooring was laid inside, and a most exquisite velvet ceiling was installed inside. The outer walls were built of *sange khaara* stone with lime mortar. A most beautiful black silk covering was placed over the entire Kaaba, inscribed with the *Kalima Tayyiba* — "La ilaaha illallah, Muhammadur Rasoolullah ﷺ" — and the sultan's name was on the golden border. The present Kaaba is the one built by Sultan Murad — that is, no complete reconstruction has been done since then. The covering (*ghilaf*) of the Kaaba used to come from Egypt every year with great fanfare. Once, it was also made in Lahore, Pakistan. The old method was that the old *ghilaf* was given to the custodians, and people would buy it as a blessed relic. Nowadays, the *ghilaf* is made in Saudi Arabia itself, bearing the name of King Saud.

### Maqaam Ibrahim and Hajr Aswad

Both are heavenly gems (*yaqoot*). They were extremely luminous; Allah ta'ala effaced their light, otherwise they would have illuminated the east and the west.

Maqaam Ibrahim is the stone upon which Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> stood while building the Kaaba. As the building rose higher, the stone also rose higher. This stone would soften when he stood on it, so that his feet would not be pained by its hardness. Hence, the marks of his blessed feet were imprinted on it. Standing on this stone, placed on Mount Abu Qubais, Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> called out: "O servants of Allah, come for Hajj!" Allah ta'ala commanded: "And proclaim the Hajj among the people — they will come to you on foot and on every lean camel, coming from every distant path."

After his proclamation, all those who were to perform Hajj responded with *labbaik*. Each person responded as many times as they were destined to perform Hajj. Those yet to come from the wombs of mothers and the loins of fathers, until the Day of Judgment, also responded with *labbaik*.

When the Noble Prophet ﷺ described the grandeur of Maqaam Ibrahim, Hazrat Umar <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> submitted, "Shall we not offer prayer behind it?" The Prophet ﷺ remained silent, awaiting revelation. That very day, the noble verses descended from Allah ta'ala: "And take the Maqaam of Ibrahim as a place of prayer."

By Maqaam Ibrahim is meant the stone upon which Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> stood and built the Kaaba. The marks of Ibrahim's blessed feet are recorded on it. After completing the seven rounds of tawaf, the two rak'ats of tawaf are offered behind Maqaam Ibrahim. In this too, a glimpse of following the Ibrahimic nation is found. Allah ta'ala granted such grandeur to the marks of His Khaleel Ibrahim's blessed feet that until the Day of Judgment, those performing tawaf are commanded to offer prayer (two rak'ats) behind Maqaam Ibrahim. After completing the seven rounds of tawaf, it is obligatory to offer two rak'ats of tawaf in Masjid al-Haram, though it is mustahab to offer them behind Maqaam Ibrahim.

### Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Stood on Maqaam Ibrahim Three Times

**1)** After many years, when Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came to meet Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, he had promised Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> that, by Allah's will, he would only see his son, meet him, and return without dismounting from his ride. When he arrived in Makkah, Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had gone hunting. He met his daughter-in-law and asked about their circumstances. She said, "The situation is not good — we are in hardship, surviving only by hunting." As he was leaving, he said, "Convey my salam to your husband, and tell him that the doorstep of his house is not good — it should be changed."

When Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> returned home, he himself asked, "Did any elder come today?" His wife replied, "Yes, he came, conveyed salam, and left a message." When she explained the message, he divorced her, saying, "He has instructed me to divorce you." The reason was that she had been ungrateful to the Lord. It is not befitting for a prophet's wife to complain about a meager livelihood; she should be patient.

Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came again to meet Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. This time too, Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was not at home. He met his daughter-in-law (Ismail's second wife) and asked about their circumstances. She said, "All praise is for Allah — we are passing through good times. We have control over the water of Zamzam. My husband brings game from hunting, and we are living very well." When Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was about to leave, his daughter-in-law insisted that he stay, but he said, "I am not permitted to dismount and step on the ground." She said, "Then place your feet on this stone so that I may wash them." The stone on which he placed his feet was Maqaam Ibrahim itself.

As he was leaving, he sent salam to Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and gave a message: "The doorstep of the house is good — keep it strong."

**2)** During the construction of the Kaaba, he stood on it, and it would soften so that his blessed feet would not be pained by its hardness. For this reason, the marks of his blessed feet were imprinted on it. When he wished to ascend, the stone would rise by itself; when he wished to descend, it would descend by itself. Unlike ordinary masons, he had no need to tie wooden planks.

**3)** After the construction of the Kaaba, standing on this stone on Mount Abu Qubais, he gave the call of Hajj to the people.
