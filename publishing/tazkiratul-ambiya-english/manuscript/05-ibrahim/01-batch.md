### Hazrat Ibrahim, Ismail and Is'haq <sup>عليهم السلام</sup>

Hazrat Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Hazrat Is'haq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> are both sons of Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. The name of Hazrat Is'haq's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> mother is Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup>, and the name of Hazrat Ismail's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> mother is Hazrat Haajra <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup>.

**The Lineage of Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>**
He is the son of Taarakh bin Nakhoor. His name is Ibrahim and his title is Abul Zaifaan (the very hospitable). His lineage is: Ibrahim bin Taarakh bin Nakhoor bin Saroo'a bin Ra'oo bin Taatea bin Aabar bin Saaleh bin Arfah Shaz bin Saam bin Nooh.

His birth took place 1709 years after the flood, and approximately 2300 years before Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, in the village of "Kauni" near the city of Babul.

In *Tafseer Khazainul Irfan*, it is stated that his birth took place at the location of "Soos" in the region of Amwaaz.

**Note:** Aazar is the name of Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> uncle. His father's name is Taarakh.

Allama Mehmood Ahmad Aalusi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> states: A large number of Ahlus Sunnah scholars rely on the fact that Aazar was not Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> father. The evidence of the multitude of Ahlus Sunnah is that none of the Noble Prophet's ﷺ forefathers were disbelievers. The noble command of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is: "I continued to be transferred from pure loins to pure wombs." And it is also clear that disbelievers and polytheists can never be pure. Allah ta'ala said: "Indeed, the polytheists are impure."

Some people said that "pure" (taahir) in the Prophet's ﷺ command means that his forefathers were free from fornication. Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup>, refuting this, states: "To restrict purity to being free from fornication is a claim without evidence — there is no reliable proof for it. The general nature of the words is considered, not the specificity of the causes."

The generality of the words indicates that absolute purity of every kind is intended. The Noble Prophet ﷺ continued to be transferred through loins pure from disbelief and fornication of every kind, and through pure wombs.

Imam Razi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> in *Tafseer Kabeer* had attributed the statement that the Prophet's ﷺ parents were believers to the Shia. Refuting this as well, Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> says: "To attribute this statement to the Shia — as Imam Razi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> also did — this happened due to lack of reflection and consideration. If attention had been paid, it would not have been so."

It should be noted that in Allama Suyooti's <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> work *Alhaawi*, with reference to a treatise, the statement of Allama Fakhruddin Razi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> is also recorded as being along with the majority regarding faith.

Hazrat Allama Maulana Muhammad Ashraf Siyalwi states: The work after *Tafseer Kabeer* is Allama Razi's later composition, so Allama's retraction from his earlier statement is proven.

"The majority of scholars hold the view that Aazar is the name of Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> uncle."

In the Holy Quran, the word "father" (اب) is used for grandfather, uncle, and father alike. The command of Allah ta'ala is:

> "Were you present when death approached Yaqoob, when he said to his sons, 'What will you worship after me?' They said, 'We will worship your God and the God of your fathers (آباء) Ibrahim, Ismail, and Is'haq.'"

In this noble verse, the word *aabaa* (plural of *ab*) is applied to Yaqoob's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> father Is'haq <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, his uncle (paternal) Ismail <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, and his grandfather Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>.

Muhammad bin Ka'ab Qarzi presented this verse as evidence and said: "In a noble hadith, the blessed statement of the Noble Prophet ﷺ is present in which he called his uncle Hazrat Abbas <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> 'my father' (أبي)."

> "Present my father Abbas to me."

Those researchers who claimed that Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> real father was not a disbeliever, but rather his uncle Aazar was a disbeliever, also presented the statement of Ibn Manzar as evidence. He presented in his commentary with a sound chain from Suleman bin Sarad: "When Namrood and his people intended to throw Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> into the fire, and they began to gather wood, an old woman was also gathering wood. When Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> found the whole nation opposing him, he said, 'Allah is sufficient for me, and He is the best Disposer of affairs.' When those people threw him into the fire, Allah ta'ala commanded the fire: 'O fire, be cool and safe for Ibrahim.' When the fire became a garden for him, his uncle said, 'This fire has stayed away from Ibrahim because of me.' So Allah ta'ala sent a spark of the fire toward him, which fell on his feet and burned him to ashes."

In this narration, the word *'amuhu* (his uncle) is clearly present, which makes it evident that Aazar was his uncle.

### A Brief Account of Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and the Splendid Proof of Aazar Being His Uncle

It is narrated from Muhammad bin Ka'ab, Qataada, Mujahid, Hasan, and others that indeed Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> continued to seek forgiveness for his uncle Aazar until he died. After his death, it became clear to him that he had died in a state of disbelief. A disbeliever is an enemy of Allah — there is no purpose in praying for him. So he stopped seeking forgiveness for him and dissociated from him. Allah ta'ala said:

> "And Ibrahim's seeking forgiveness for his father (uncle) was only because of a promise he had made to him. When it became clear to him that he was an enemy of Allah, he dissociated from him."

In this verse, *abeeh* (his father) refers to Aazar. Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> sought forgiveness for him due to a promise, because he had once said to Aazar: "I will ask my Lord to forgive you." The second reason was that Aazar had also once promised Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> to accept faith. The occasion of revelation of this noble verse is: It is narrated from Hazrat Ali Murtaza <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> that when this verse was revealed — "I will seek forgiveness for you from my Lord" — I heard a man seeking forgiveness for his parents even though both were polytheists. I said, "You seek forgiveness for polytheists?" He said, "Did Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> not pray for Aazar, even though he too was a polytheist?" I mentioned this incident to the Chief of the World ﷺ, and he ﷺ said:

> "Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> seeking forgiveness was in the hope of his (Aazar's) accepting Islam, which Aazar had promised him, and Ibrahim had promised to seek forgiveness for him. When that hope ended, he dissociated from him and stopped seeking forgiveness for him."

Aazar died from the spark of fire, and after that, Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> did not make any supplication for him. After the incident of the fire, Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> migrated toward Syria, then entered Egypt, where an incident with a tyrant king occurred, and he was given Hazrat Haajra. Then Allah ta'ala commanded him to leave Haajra and her son Ismail near the Ka'aba. When he left his son and wife at the place where Makka Mukarrama is now settled, he made some supplications there, among which are these blessed words: "O our Lord, forgive me and my parents."

From this, it becomes as clear as daylight that the one for whom he stopped praying — the enemy of Allah — was his uncle Aazar, who was addressed as "father." And after how long after stopping that prayer is he now praying for forgiveness for his father and mother? That is his real father. If Aazar, who was a disbeliever and enemy of Allah, was his real father, then what is the meaning of praying for him again after dissociating from him?

### Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Was Shown the Kingdom of the Heavens and the Earth

Allah ta'ala said:

> "And thus We showed Ibrahim the kingdom of the heavens and the earth, so that he would be among those who have certainty."

The meaning of *malkoot* is great kingship and absolute sovereignty. That is, he was made to observe all the things of the heavens and the earth — to the extent that he saw the realities of the moon, sun, mountains, trees, and rivers, and observed all of the earth and heavens. "That is, all signs and all wonders were shown to him. Indeed, seven heavens were unveiled for him, and he saw all the things of the heavens until his sight reached the Divine Throne. Similarly, seven earths were unveiled for him, and he saw everything in the earths."

Ibn Mardoowiya <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> narrated from Hazrat Ali <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> that the Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "When Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had observed the kingdoms of the heavens and the earth, he saw a person committing sin — that is, someone far away, hiding, engaged in disobedience — but he observed him. So he prayed against him. Allah ta'ala destroyed him. Then he became aware of another person's sin and intended to pray against him as well, when Allah ta'ala forbade him: 'O Ibrahim! Your supplications are answered. Do not pray for the destruction of My servants, because My servants are of three types: either they are sinners but I will accept their repentance; or they themselves remain sinners, but among their descendants are such righteous people who will fill the earth with glorifications — that is, they will praise, extol, and declare the oneness of Allah ta'ala so much that through their influence, others too will become engaged in Allah's remembrance; or they die in a state of sin and remain in My grasp of power — after that, it is up to My will whether I forgive them or punish them.'"

SubhanAllah! When this is the rank of Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> knowledge, then what must be the state of the knowledge of the Chief of the Prophets, the Beloved of the Compassionate, Mustafa ﷺ? The combined knowledge of all the noble prophets is like a single drop compared to the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, and his knowledge is like an ocean.

### Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Refuted the Idol Worshippers

> "And remember when Ibrahim said to his father Aazar: 'Do you take idols as gods? Indeed, I see you and your people in manifest error.'"

Through this noble verse, proof was established against the polytheists of Arabia, because many of them respected Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and acknowledged his virtue. They were being told: "Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> forbade idol worship and considered it a great defect and error. If you acknowledge his greatness, then abandon idol worship."

> "When Ibrahim said to his father: 'O my father, why do you worship that which neither hears nor sees, nor can it benefit you at all? O my father, indeed there has come to me of knowledge that which has not come to you. So follow me, I will guide you to a straight path. O my father, do not be a servant of Satan. Indeed, Satan is ever disobedient to the Most Merciful.'"

In all these verses, "father" refers to his uncle Aazar. He advised him: "The idols you worship cannot see your worship, cannot hear your praise, and are incapable of hearing any kind of speech. They cannot help you or anyone else, cannot save you from any kind of trouble, and cannot make you carefree. Since they possess none of these qualities, they can never be worthy of worship. Rather, worshipping them is in reality the worship of Satan, because he is pleased by it. Worshipping idols is the result of his deception and deceit. Therefore, abandon the worship of Satan, because Satan is disobedient to Allah ta'ala — he has been rejected himself and is constantly trying to mislead others. He wants someone or other to fall into his snare, become distant from Allah ta'ala, and become deserving of His punishment."

> "And recite to them the news of Ibrahim, when he said to his father and his people: 'What do you worship?' They said, 'We worship idols and remain devoted to them.' He said, 'Do they hear you when you call, or benefit you or harm you?' They said, 'Rather, we found our fathers doing so.' He said, 'Do you see what you have been worshipping, you and your ancient forefathers? Indeed, they are all enemies to me, except the Lord of the Worlds — He who created me, so He guides me; and He who feeds me and gives me drink; and when I am ill, He cures me; and He who will cause me to die and then bring me to life.'"

In these noble verses, Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is being mentioned with praise, because he was the high ancestor of the Quraish and the builder of the Ka'aba. The Quraish were very proud of being his descendants. Therefore, his beliefs were being stated before them so that those who call him their high ancestor and take pride in this relation may also believe in Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> *Rabbul Akbar* (the Greatest Lord). Just as his robe was completely pure from the stains of disbelief and polytheism, similarly they too should remove their stains and adopt pure monotheism.

Allama Qazi Sanaullah in *Tafseer Mazhari* states: "*Li-abeeh* refers to Aazar, who was his uncle, because he had raised him — therefore he was called 'father.'"

How beautiful is the manner of Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> admonition! He makes them acknowledge the helplessness of their deities. When they could not deny these things — because their idols were incapable of hearing or causing benefit or harm — they began to defend themselves by saying, "We found our fathers doing this, so we are not prepared to deviate from their way at any time." He <sup>عليه السلام</sup> advises them with affection: "Do not be ignorant. Baseless stubbornness is not good. The results of blind imitation are very dangerous. In worldly matters, you constantly use your intellect and understanding — when it comes to this fundamental issue of life, you should also light the lamp of your thinking."

Then he <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "Say whatever you wish about these blind and deaf idols — I consider them my enemies and ill-wishers. My relationship of worship is only with that True Deity who takes everything in creation to its perfect degree, also provides all its necessities, and delivers the necessary means for its growth and development."

Compared to these blind and deaf deities, how meaningful is the introduction of Allah ta'ala with the attribute of *Rabbul Aalameen* (the One who takes everything to its perfect degree)? Ponder again over the verse. He did not say, "These idols are your enemies" — rather, he said, "They are my enemies." The manner of a noble advisor is just this: he does not directly attack others, but begins with himself. Speaking in this indirect manner is far more effective than speaking directly.
