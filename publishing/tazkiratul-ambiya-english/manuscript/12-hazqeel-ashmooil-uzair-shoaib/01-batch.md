## Hazrat Hazqeel <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

### The Prophet Who Raised the Dead

> "O beloved, did you not see those who left their homes while they were thousands, fearing death? Then Allah said to them, 'Die!' Then He brought them back to life. Indeed, Allah is gracious to people, but most people are ungrateful."

Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہما</sup> narrates: A king of Banu Israel called his people to jihad. They came out of their homes and said: "We will not go to the land you want to take us to — there is a plague spreading there. When the plague ends, we will go." Allah ta'ala inflicted death upon all of them — thousands in number — right there outside their city. They remained in that state for 8 days until their bodies swelled. When Banu Israel from other areas learned of their death, they came to bury them, but the stench was so unbearable they could not approach. Instead, they built a wall around them.

In another narration: Hazrat Hazqeel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> called his people to jihad, but they showed cowardice and refused. So Allah ta'ala sent death upon them. When the deaths multiplied, they began fleeing their cities out of fear. As thousands were leaving the city, Hazrat Hazqeel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> prayed: "O Allah! O God of Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, You see Your servants' disobedience. Show them a sign of Your power so they know they cannot escape Your grasp." After this prayer, Allah ta'ala sent death upon them — as they left the city in the thousands, they all died.

When Hazrat Hazqeel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> saw thousands of his people lying dead, he was distressed. He thought: if only they could be brought back to life. Allah ta'ala revealed to him: "O Hazqeel, say to the bones: 'O bones, Allah commands you to come together.'" At his command, all the bones joined together and formed complete skeletons, though without flesh and blood. Then Allah revealed: "Now say: 'O bodies, Allah commands you to wear the garment of flesh.'" And Allah clothed them with flesh. Then Allah revealed: "Now say: 'O bodies, Allah commands you to stand up.'" And thus Allah brought them back to life. When they rose, they were saying:

**سُبْحَانَكَ اللَّهُمَّ رَبَّنَا وَبِحَمْدِكَ لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ**

Even after being revived, the effects of death remained — their complexion stayed yellow as it had been at death. The foul odor that had developed in their bodies at death also remained, and it is said that this odor persists among their descendants to this day.

Note: After death, a person is no longer accountable — commands and prohibitions cease. However, these people were made accountable after being revived. This is either because they had not experienced the full severity of death's agonies, or because Allah erased that memory, or because this death was imposed as a punishment contrary to the natural order — thus the rulings of death did not apply to them.

This incident of reviving the dead occurred in the time of Hazrat Hazqeel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and was his miracle.

### Kunniyat

Hazrat Hazqeel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was known as **ibnul ujooz** (son of the old woman) because his mother was advanced in age at his birth.

He was also known by the title **Zulkifl** because he took responsibility for 70 prophets and saved them from being killed. However, it is also believed that there is a separate prophet named Zulkifl <sup>عليه السلام</sup> mentioned earlier.

### Lesson

No one can escape death. As Allah says:

> "Say: 'Indeed, death from which you flee will surely meet you.'"

There is no delay or advance. Our false notions — "so-and-so died before their time," "if only so-and-so had not traveled, this accident would not have happened" — are baseless. At the appointed time, every soul must enter the mouth of death.

## Hazrat Ashmoo'il <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

> "O beloved, did you not see a group of Banu Israel after Moosa when they said to their prophet: 'Appoint a king for us so that we may fight in the way of Allah.' The prophet said: 'Is it not likely that if fighting is prescribed for you, you will not fight?' They said: 'Why should we not fight in Allah's way when we have been driven from our homes and children?' But when fighting was prescribed for them, they turned away — except a few of them. And Allah knows the wrongdoers."

### The Messenger Before Him

After Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, Yoosha' <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came, upholding Allah's commands and teaching according to the Torah. After him came Kaalib, then Hazqeel, then Ilyaas, then Al-Yasa'a <sup>عليهم السلام</sup>.

### Oppression by Amalqa

After these prophets, the nation of Jaloot (Amalqa) overpowered Banu Israel. Banu Israel lived between Egypt and Palestine. The Amalqa captured many of their cities, took 440 of their noble sons as prisoners, imposed jizya upon them, and even seized their Torah.

At that time, they had no prophet to manage their affairs. All the men of the prophetic household had passed away. Only one woman from the prophetic lineage remained, and she was pregnant. She constantly prayed: "**Ashmoo'il**" — in Hebrew, "**Ashmoo**" means "hear" and "**eil**" means "Allah" — meaning "O Allah, accept the prayer."

### Ashmoo'il's Prophethood

When the child was born, he was given this name. When he grew up, his mother gave him the Torah and appointed him to the service of Baitul Muqaddas. Allah ta'ala granted him prophethood.

When he came to his people as a prophet, the people said: "If you are truthful in your claim, then appoint a king for us under whose leadership we may fight the Amalqa." The prophet of Allah said: "You will not fulfill your pledge." They insisted: "We will surely fight — we have been driven from our homes, our children have been taken captive." But as the prophet had said, they all broke their pledge — except 313 who remained steadfast.

Note: The name of this prophet has differences of opinion, but Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> said: "The name of this prophet of Allah was Ashmoo'il. In Hebrew, he is called Ismail. He was from the descendants of Haroon <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. This is the view of most scholars."

### Taloot is Chosen as King

> "And their prophet said to them: 'Indeed, Allah has appointed Taloot as your king.' They said: 'How can he have kingship over us when we are more deserving of kingship than him, and he has not been given abundance of wealth?' He said: 'Indeed, Allah has chosen him over you and increased him abundantly in knowledge and physique. And Allah gives His kingdom to whom He wills. And Allah is All-Encompassing, All-Knowing.'"

First, they themselves asked the prophet of Allah to appoint a king. But when they were told that Allah had chosen Taloot, they began to object: "How can he be our king?"

Their objection had two grounds:

1. **Lineage**: Prophethood in Banu Israel had been in the line of Laawi bin Yaqoob. From this line came Dawood and Sulaiman <sup>عليهما السلام</sup>. Taloot was not from either of these tribes.

2. **Poverty**: Taloot was a poor man — a leatherworker or dyer. They said: "He has not been given abundance of wealth. How can he be our king?"

The prophet of Allah replied: "Taloot is deserving of kingship because Allah has chosen him specifically and preferred him over you. The kingdom belongs to Allah — He gives it to whom He wills. To object to His choice is not the way of people of intellect. And the obvious matter you can understand is this: a king must have knowledge and physical strength. Taloot possesses both of these more than you. This is why he is worthy of kingship."

### Why Knowledge and Strength?

Knowledge and strength are true virtues; wealth and status are outward virtues. True virtues take precedence. Knowledge and power are intrinsic to a person — they do not leave. Wealth and status are transient — they come and go. They are not connected to a person's essence but are accidental attributes. Therefore, knowledge and power are superior.

Most importantly, the purpose of a king is to run the system of the state and know the art of warfare — how to achieve victory, protect the nation from enemies, and defend the borders. Therefore, qualification for kingship is based on knowledge and capability, not wealth and status.

### Women and Leadership

When it is established that a ruler must be knowledgeable and courageous, it follows that appointing a deficient-minded people as rulers is foolishness and ignorance.

Hazrat Abu Sa'eed Khudri <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was going toward the Eidgah on Eid-ul-Adha or Eid-ul-Fitr. On the way, he passed by some women and said: "O gathering of women! Give charity abundantly. I see many women in Hell." The women asked: "Why, O Messenger of Allah?" He replied: "You curse excessively and are ungrateful to your husbands."

Then he said: "I have not seen anyone more deficient in intellect and religion than you, yet capable of causing wise men to lose their intellects."

The women asked: "What is the deficiency in our religion and intellect?" He replied: "Is not the testimony of a woman half that of a man? This is the deficiency of intellect. And is it not true that during your menstrual period you cannot pray or fast? This is the deficiency of religion."

Whoever the Prophet ﷺ declares deficient in intellect remains deficient even if placed in a position of authority.

Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddique <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: When the Prophet ﷺ was informed that the Persians had made the daughter of Kisra their queen, he said: "That nation will never succeed that has appointed a woman as their ruler."

Hazrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> narrates: The Prophet ﷺ said: "When your rulers are righteous, your wealthy are generous, and your affairs are decided by consultation, then living is better for you. But when your rulers are evil, your wealthy are miserly, and your affairs are in the hands of women, then dying is better for you."

### The Ark as a Sign of Taloot's Kingdom

> "And their prophet said to them: 'The sign of his kingdom is that there will come to you a chest (ark) containing tranquility from your Lord and relics left by the family of Moosa and the family of Haroon, carried by angels. Indeed, in that is a great sign for you, if you are believers.'"

The prophet of Allah informed them that their criteria for leadership were wrong — the correct measure was knowledge and courage, and Taloot excelled in both.

The Bible states: "He was a thirty-year-old youth, unmatched in beauty and handsomeness. His stature was so tall that others could barely reach his shoulders. He was from the line of Binyamin (Yaqoob's youngest son)."

The prophet of Allah told them: "Taloot's selection is not a human choice — the Lord of Honor Himself has chosen him for your leadership. You should not object to His gift."

But Banu Israel would not give up their obstinacy so easily. They immediately demanded proof that Allah had truly chosen Taloot. Their prophet replied: "The sign of his kingship is this: the ark containing your peace and tranquility, the relics of Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Haroon <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, which the Amalqa seized from you — the angels will return it to you. If you have faith, you need no further sign."

When the angels brought the ark — or drove the ox-cart on which the ark was placed — to Banu Israel, they were satisfied about Taloot's kingship and gained confidence that they would surely be victorious. The ark contained the relics of the prophets: Moosa's staff and garments, and Haroon's turban.

**Lesson**: This verse shows that objects connected to Allah's chosen servants carry blessing — through them, prayers are accepted and victory over enemies is granted. The Companions kept the Prophet's ﷺ nails and blessed hair as tabarruk. Hazrat Khalid bin Waleed <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> had a cap on his head containing one of the Prophet's blessed hairs. He said: "Whenever I wear this cap into battle, Allah grants me victory through the blessing of that hair."

### The River Test

> "When Taloot set out with the armies, he said: 'Indeed, Allah will test you with a river. Whoever drinks from it is not with me, and whoever does not drink from it is with me — except one who scoops a handful with his hand.'"

But those who had failed in the test became cowards and said: "We have no strength today against Jaloot and his army." This shows that disobeying Allah and His Messenger's commands makes one incapable of fighting the disbelievers. This is exactly why today all Islamic nations live in fear of the disbelievers.

Those who succeeded in the test were granted such blessing that they prepared themselves for jihad. They realized that death must come one day, so why not die in obedience to Allah and attain eternal life? When one must meet the Lord, let that meeting be one of success and His pleasure — what kind of meeting is that which brings Allah's wrath and punishment?

They attained such a high level of certainty that they said: "How often has a small group overcome a large group by Allah's permission. Indeed, many have left us — we are few remaining — but when Allah's help reaches us, we will surely be victorious."

**Purpose of the test**: Banu Israel had a habit of opposing their prophet. They were tested so that the truthful and the hypocrite, the obedient and the disobedient, could be distinguished. Those who remember Allah should be kept close; opponents should not be brought along — because retreating before facing the enemy would not cause much trouble for other Muslims, but retreating in the middle of battle would cause great harm, and the retreaters would face even greater disgrace.

### Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Kills Jaloot

> "And Dawood killed Jaloot, and Allah gave him kingship and wisdom and taught him what He willed."

Hazrat Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> used to graze goats. He would throw stones at wolves and lions with a slingshot. His seven brothers were with Taloot in the battlefield. When word reached him a bit late about their situation, his father Eisha sent him: "Go and check on your brothers."

Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came and saw his brothers standing in a battle line. Jaloot was taunting Banu Israel: "If you are on the truth, let someone come out to face me." Dawood asked his brothers: "Why don't you kill this uncircumcised disbeliever?" But no one was ready to face him because of his bravery.

When Dawood went to another line, he saw Taloot urging people to kill Jaloot. Dawood asked: "What will you give the one who kills him?" Taloot replied: "I will marry him to my daughter and give him half the kingdom."

As Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was walking, three stones called out to him: "Pick us up — the killing of Jaloot is in us." He picked up those stones. He struck one stone at Jaloot's chest, and he was killed. Dawood also killed several others, and thus Jaloot and his army were defeated. In this way, Allah ta'ala granted Dawood <sup>عليه السلام</sup> kingship and prophethood.
