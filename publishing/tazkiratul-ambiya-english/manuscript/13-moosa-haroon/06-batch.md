## Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — Continued

### Qaaroon (Korah)

Qaaroon was from the people of Moosa, but he transgressed against them. He was given immense treasures — the keys to his hoards were a burden for a troop of strong men. His wealth was so vast that ten strong men could not carry the keys.

When his people advised him, "Do not exult — Allah does not love the exultant. Seek the abode of the hereafter with what Allah has given you, and do not forget your share of the world," he replied: "I was only given this because of the knowledge I possess."

Allah caused the earth to swallow him and his mansion. He sank gradually — first to his knees, then his waist, then his chest, then his neck, and finally completely. He cried out and begged Moosa for mercy, but Moosa, in his anger at Qaaroon's arrogance, did not relent.

Some narrations say that Moosa had cursed him earlier. When the earth swallowed Qaaroon, he cried, "O Moosa! By His mercy, save me!" But Allah revealed: "He did not show mercy to the orphan, so do not show mercy to him."

**Lesson**: Qaaroon's story is a warning against pride in wealth and knowledge.

### Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

It was mentioned to Moosa that there was a man more knowledgeable than him. Moosa said: "I do not know of anyone more knowledgeable than me." Allah said: "There is a servant of Ours — at the junction of the two seas — who has knowledge you do not possess."

Moosa asked: "How can I meet him?" He was told: "Take a dead fish with you. When the fish comes alive and escapes into the sea, there you will find him."

Moosa took his young servant Yusha' bin Noon and set out with a fish in a basket. At the junction of the two seas, the fish came to life, leaped from the basket, and entered the sea, making a tunnel in the water. They continued walking until they became tired.

Moosa said: "Bring our morning meal — we have certainly suffered fatigue in this journey." Yusha' said: "Do you remember when we rested at the rock? I forgot the fish there — none but Satan made me forget to mention it. It made its way into the sea amazingly."

Moosa said: "That is what we were seeking." They returned, retracing their footsteps.

They found a servant of Ours (Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup>) — a man covered in a cloak, lying on the shore. Moosa greeted him. Khizr said: "Where do you come from — a land of peace?" Moosa said: "I am Moosa." Khizr said: "The Moosa of Bani Israel? You have knowledge from Allah which He taught me, and I have knowledge from Allah which He taught you. You cannot bear to accompany me patiently."

Moosa said: "You will find me patient, if Allah wills, and I will not disobey you in any matter."

Khizr said: "If you follow me, do not ask me about anything until I myself mention it to you."

### The Boat

They boarded a boat. Without warning, Khizr scuttled the boat — breaking and damaging it. Moosa said: "Did you scuttle it to drown its passengers? You have done a dreadful thing."

Khizr said: "Did I not say you cannot bear to be with me?"

Moosa said: "Do not take me to task for what I forgot."

### The Boy

They continued until they met a boy playing with other children. Khizr seized the boy by the head and killed him. Moosa said: "You killed an innocent soul without cause? You have done an evil thing."

Khizr said: "Did I not tell you that you cannot bear to be with me?"

Moosa said: "If I ask you about anything after this, do not accompany me further."

### The Wall

They came to a town whose inhabitants refused them hospitality. There they found a wall about to collapse. Khizr repaired it, setting it straight without asking for payment. Moosa said: "You could have taken payment for this."

Khizr said: "This is the parting between you and me. Now I will explain the meaning of all that you could not bear."

### The Explanation

**The Boat**: It belonged to poor fishermen. A tyrant king was seizing every sound boat by force. By damaging it, Khizr made it defective so the king would not take it, preserving the livelihood of the poor owners.

**The Boy**: His parents were believers. The boy would have grown up to oppress them with rebellion and disbelief. Allah replaced him with a better child — more righteous and loving.

**The Wall**: Beneath it was a treasure belonging to two orphan boys in the city. Their father was a righteous man. Allah willed that the orphans reach maturity and retrieve their treasure as a mercy from their Lord. Khizr repaired the wall so the treasure would not be discovered.

### Was Khizr a Nabi or Wali?

Most scholars hold that Khizr <sup>عليه السلام} was a prophet — he received revelation (wahi) and performed actions based on divine instruction, not personal judgment. His ability to recognize Moosa through revelation is cited as evidence of prophethood. Allama Raazi transmitted the opposing arguments but did not reject the view of his prophethood.

Some hold that Khizr is still alive, though the stronger view is that he has passed away.

### Moosa's Death <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

When the angel of death came to Moosa, he said: "Respond to the call of your Lord." Moosa struck the angel and knocked out his eye. The angel returned to Allah and said: "You sent me to a servant who does not want to die." Allah said: "Return and tell him to place his hand on the back of a bull — for every hair his hand covers, he will be granted a year of life."

Moosa said: "And then what?" The angel said: "Then death." Moosa said: "Then let it be now."

He asked: "O my Lord, let me be buried near the Holy Land, within a stone's throw of it." It is narrated that his grave is near the red dune — close to Baitul Maqdas.

### Virtue of Burial Near the Righteous

The chapter concludes with a note on the desirability (mustahab) of burying the deceased near righteous people, so that when mercy descends upon them, the one buried nearby may also receive its blessing. The Prophet ﷺ said: "I saw Moosa standing in his grave, praying."
