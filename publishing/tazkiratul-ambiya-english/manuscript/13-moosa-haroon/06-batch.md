## Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — Continued

### Qaaroon (Korah) Among the People of Moosa

Qaaroon was from the people of Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, but he transgressed against them. Allah ta'ala said: *"Indeed, Qaaroon was from the people of Moosa, but he oppressed them."*

Allah ta'ala had given him such immense treasures that the keys to his hoards would weigh down a troop of strong men. It is narrated that ten strong men could not carry the keys to his storehouses. Despite this, he was not grateful. His people advised him: *"Do not exult. Indeed, Allah does not love the exultant. Seek the abode of the Hereafter with what Allah has given you, and do not forget your share of the world, and do good as Allah has done good to you, and do not seek corruption in the land. Indeed, Allah does not love the corrupters."*

But Qaaroon replied: *"I was only given this because of the knowledge I possess."* He attributed his wealth to his own intelligence and capability, forgetting that it was Allah who had bestowed it upon him.

### Qaaroon Is Swallowed by the Earth

When Qaaroon came out before his people in all his pomp and splendor, those who desired the life of this world said: *"Oh, if only we had the like of what Qaaroon has been given. Indeed, he is a man of great fortune."* But those who had been given knowledge said: *"Woe to you. The reward of Allah is better for those who believe and do righteous deeds. And none shall attain it except the patient."*

Then Allah caused the earth to swallow him and his mansion. The earth consumed Qaaroon gradually — first to his ankles, then to his knees, then to his waist, then to his chest, then to his neck, and finally completely. As he was sinking, he cried out: "O Moosa! O Moosa!" begging for mercy. But Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, in the intensity of his anger for the sake of Allah's religion, did not relent. The earth had already swallowed him entirely.

Allah ta'ala revealed to Moosa: *"O Moosa, you have shown great hardness of heart. He was calling out to you repeatedly, but you showed him no mercy. You paid no attention to his cries and lamentations."* Another narration states: *"Be warned — by My honor and My majesty, if anyone had called out to Me even once, I would certainly have answered his call and fulfilled his need."*

Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> gave the most beautiful reply to Allah's address. He said: *"O my Lord, it was for Your sake that I acted in anger."* That is: "O Allah, when they were tearing apart the fabric of Your religion, trampling Your commands, going astray themselves and leading others astray — then I presented myself before Your court and said: 'O Generous Master, I am Your Messenger conveying Your commands to them, but they do not desist from their ways. So take them into Your grasp.' O Kind Lord, who knows better than You that I did not become angry because they had slandered me — in that, they had already humiliated themselves. My anger was for the sake of Your Essence. How could I listen to their cries and pleas?"

### Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Is Exonerated from the Harm of His People

Allah ta'ala said: *"O you who believe, do not be like those who harmed Moosa, then Allah exonerated him from what they said. And Moosa was honorable in the sight of Allah."*

When a certain man objected to the distribution of the Prophet's <sup>ﷺ</sup> wealth, the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> expressed displeasure and said: *"May Allah have mercy on Moosa. He was harmed more than this, and he bore it with patience."*

One incident of harm inflicted upon Moosa has already been mentioned — that he was slandered. But Allah exonerated him from that.

A second incident is narrated in Musnad Ahmad, Bukhari, and Tirmizi from Hazrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup>. The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: *"Moosa was extremely modest. He would keep his body so covered that no one could see his skin — such was his care due to his modesty. But some people among Bani Israel caused him mental distress. They said: 'He covers his body only because of some defect — either he has leprosy or he suffers from scrotal hernia. That is why he never bathes naked with us.' All of them would bathe together, naked."*

Allah ta'ala willed to exonerate His Prophet from the defect they had alleged. One day, Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> went to bathe alone. He placed his clothes on a stone and bathed. When he finished and went to retrieve his clothes, the stone moved away, carrying them with it. Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> ran after the stone, crying: "My clothes, O stone! My clothes, O stone!" The stone finally stopped where the elders of Bani Israel were sitting in a gathering. When they saw Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> naked, they realized that he was perfectly formed — there was no defect in him. Thus Allah exonerated him from the false accusation they had made.

Hazrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> said: "By Allah's oath, the stone bore six or seven marks from the blows of Moosa's staff."

A third incident: they accused Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> of killing his brother Haroon <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Ibn Mamba, Ibn Jurair, Ibn Manzar, Ibn Abi Haatim, Ibn Marduwiya <sup>رضي الله عنهم</sup> and Hakim — who declared this narration authentic — narrated from Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله عنهما</sup> who transmitted from Hazrat Ali <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup>: "Moosa and Haroon <sup>عليهما السلام</sup> went up a mountain. There Haroon <sup>عليه السلام</sup> passed away. Bani Israel said: 'Moosa has killed Haroon, for he was more beloved to us and more gentle in temperament.' This baseless speech caused Moosa great distress. Allah commanded the angels, who lifted Haroon's bier and carried it past the gatherings of Bani Israel, and the angels were announcing the news of Haroon's death."

Thus Allah exonerated Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> from this as well — the angels themselves carried and buried Haroon's body.

They also harmed Moosa by calling him a sorcerer and a madman. They harassed him by saying: *"You and your Lord go and fight; we are sitting here."* And they caused him pain by saying: *"We will never endure one kind of food."* And: *"We will never believe in you until we see Allah openly."*

From all these painful statements, Allah granted Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> deliverance and gave comfort to his heart.

Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was *wajeeh* (honorable) in the sight of Allah. The word *wajeeh* has been explained in various ways: one of high rank, one of elevated station and dignity, one accepted by Allah, one whose prayers are answered — Allah accepted all his supplications except the one in which he asked to see his Lord in this world. *Wajeeh* also means that he spoke directly with Allah without intermediary. Hazrat Maulana Ahmad Raza Khan Barelvi's translation encompasses all these meanings: Moosa was one of honor and dignity in the sight of Allah.

### Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — Their Meeting

Hazrat Sa'eed bin Jubair <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> narrates that he said to Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله عنهما</sup>: "Noofa Bakaali thinks that the Moosa of Bani Israel is not the same Moosa who met Khizr." Ibn Abbas replied: "He is the enemy of Allah; he lies. I heard Ubay bin Ka'ab <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> saying: I heard the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> say:

"Once Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> stood up to deliver a sermon to Bani Israel. He was asked: 'Who is the most knowledgeable among the people?' He replied: 'I am the most knowledgeable.' Allah ta'ala admonished him for this, because he did not attribute the answer to Allah — he should have said: 'Allah knows best.' So Allah revealed to him: 'There is a servant of Ours at the junction of the two seas who possesses knowledge that you do not have.' Moosa said: 'O my Lord, how can I find him?' He was told: 'Take a fish with you in a bag. Where the fish disappears from you, that is his place.'"

*"And when Moosa said to his young companion: I will not stop until I reach the junction of the two seas, even if I travel for ages. Then when they reached the junction of the two seas, they forgot their fish, and it made its way into the sea, tunneling through. Then when they had passed on, Moosa said to his servant: Bring us our morning meal. Indeed, we have suffered much fatigue in this journey. He said: Did you see? When we took shelter at the rock, indeed I forgot the fish — and none made me forget it except Satan, that I should mention it. And the fish made its way into the sea in an amazing manner."*

The young man Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> took with him was Yusha' bin Noon, who served him and remained in his company, learning knowledge from him. After Moosa, he became his vicegerent.

"Junction of the two seas" means the meeting point of two seas — the Persian Sea and the Roman Sea. There they would find Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. They were told: "Where your fish disappears, search for him there." Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> firmly resolved to reach that place and said: "I will continue my effort until I arrive there, no matter how distant it may be."

They took roasted fish and bread in a bag for provisions. When they reached a rocky place, they rested and fell asleep. The roasted fish came alive in the bag, wriggled into the river, and the water's flow stopped, forming an arch. Hazrat Yusha' bin Noon <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had woken up and saw the fish come alive and enter the water, but he forgot to tell Moosa.

They continued traveling until the next meal time. Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "We are tired from the journey and hungry — let us take out the bread and fish." Then Yusha' remembered and said: "The fish came alive and went into the sea! A roasted fish coming alive and entering the water — what an amazing and astonishing thing!"

Hazrat Yusha' <sup>عليه السلام</sup> attributed his forgetfulness to Satan. One reason may be that the thought of home and family had occupied his heart, and he attributed these thoughts to Satan. Another possibility is that he was so immersed in contemplating the wonders of Allah's power that nothing else remained in his mind. The real agent was Allah, but out of humility, he said: "I should have paid attention to other matters as well — not doing so is, in a sense, attributable to Satan."

**Benefit:** The word *fata* (young man, translated as "servant" or "attendant") refers to Yusha' bin Noon. The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> addressed his attendant with the respectful term "young man" rather than "servant," teaching us that despite differences in rank, we should not use words that humiliate those below us. Would that this lesson reached the police officers who often address respectable citizens with foul language. And would that this lesson reached even one of the thousands of teachers in schools and religious institutions who use abusive language, who consider students lower than their own children and relatives, who are corrupters of character, not teachers of character — they may be instruments for driving students away from knowledge, but they can never be the means of imparting it.

### Returning to Where the Fish Came Alive

*"Moosa said: That is what we were seeking. So they returned, retracing their footsteps, and found a servant from among Our servants to whom We had granted mercy from Us and taught knowledge from Our presence. Moosa said to him: May I follow you so that you may teach me something of the right guidance that you have been taught? He said: Indeed, you will never be able to bear with me patiently. And how can you be patient over that which your knowledge does not encompass? Moosa said: You will find me patient, if Allah wills, and I will not disobey you in any matter."*

Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Hazrat Yusha' <sup>عليه السلام</sup> returned to the place where the fish had come alive and entered the water. Various signs marked the spot where the water's flow had stopped. Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "This is the place we seek." They began searching until they came to a rock where they saw a man lying wrapped in a cloak. Moosa greeted him. Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "Where has the greeting of peace come from in this land?" (No one here greets with peace.) Moosa said: "I am Moosa." Hazrat Khizr said: "The Moosa of Bani Israel?" He replied: "Yes." Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "The knowledge that Allah has given you, He has not given me; and the knowledge that He has given me, He has not given you."

Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "May I follow you so that you may teach me some of the right guidance you have been given?" Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> knew that Moosa had been given knowledge of the outward law and would not be able to bear matters of the unseen, so he said: "How can you be patient with what your knowledge does not encompass?"

Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "If Allah wills, you will find me patient."

### Hazrat Khizr Imposes a Condition

*"He said: If you follow me, do not ask me about anything until I myself mention it to you."*

Since Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had already said, "If Allah wills, I will be patient, and I will not disobey any command of yours," Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> permitted him to accompany him.

### Khizr Damages the Boat

*"So they set out, until when they boarded a ship, he scuttled it. Moosa said: Have you scuttled it to drown its people? You have certainly done a dreadful thing. He said: Did I not say that you can never bear with me patiently? Moosa said: Do not take me to task for what I forgot, and do not make my matter difficult for me."*

Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> set out walking. Yusha' is not mentioned — either because he was a follower and his mention was unnecessary, or because Moosa had sent him back to Bani Israel.

Bukhari and Muslim narrate from Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله عنهما</sup> that they were walking along the seashore. A boat passed near them. The boatmen recognized Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and consulted among themselves, deciding to take them aboard without charge. When they boarded, a bird came and sat at the edge of the boat, dipping its beak into the sea. Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "My knowledge and your knowledge, compared to the knowledge of Allah, is like the water this bird has taken in its beak compared to the water of the sea."

This was only for the purpose of illustration. In reality, the knowledge of both of them compared to Allah's knowledge is even less than that. It should be noted that the apparent meaning cannot be taken here — that their knowledge diminished Allah's knowledge by the amount the bird's beak took from the sea — because this meaning is not correct in any way. Diminution of Allah's knowledge is impossible. Allama Nawawi has presented the first meaning in Sharah Muslim with these words.

Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had loosened a plank from the boat. Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> expressed astonishment: "These people boarded us without charge, and now you have broken their boat! Do you wish to drown them?"

It should be noted that the damage was done near the shore. Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had temporarily fixed a nail back into it.

"You have done a very evil thing" — this is a strong expression used when something dreadful or weighty occurs. Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "Did I not tell you that you cannot bear with me patiently when you see things outwardly?" Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "A lapse occurred from me — do not hold me to account for it."

### Khizr Kills a Boy

*"So they set out, until when they met a boy, he killed him. Moosa said: Have you killed an innocent soul who has killed no one? You have certainly done an evil thing. He said: Did I not tell you that you can never bear with me patiently? Moosa said: If I ask you about anything after this, do not keep me in your company. Indeed, you have received enough excuse from me."*

When both emerged from the boat and were walking along the shore, they passed by a settlement where children were playing. Among them was a boy named Jisoor — the most handsome and clean among all the children. Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> seized him by the head and severed it. According to one narration, he laid the boy down and slaughtered him with a knife.

Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, in great anger, cried out loudly: "What an evil deed you have done! You have killed an innocent, minor child who was pure and free from sin — there is no retaliation required for him! You have done something utterly contrary to reason!"

Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> replied: "Did I not tell you that you cannot bear with me?" Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "If I ask you about anything after this, then leave my company — for you will have received your excuse from me."

### Khizr Repairs the Wall

*"So they set out, until when they came to the people of a town, they asked its people for food, but they refused to host them. Then they found in it a wall about to collapse, so he set it straight. Moosa said: If you wished, you could have taken payment for it. He said: This is the parting between me and you. Now I will inform you of the interpretation of that over which you could not be patient."*

This town was called Antaakiya or Ila. Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was so hungry that it was permissible — indeed, necessary for survival — to ask for food from the people. In such circumstances, it becomes obligatory upon them to provide food as well. For this reason, Moosa objected when Khizr repaired the wall without asking payment, saying: "These people did not feed us despite our severe hunger — it was necessary to take wages from them."

When they saw the wall leaning to one side, about to collapse, Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> simply passed his hand over it, and the wall straightened. This was indeed a miracle from him.

Upon Moosa's objection, Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "Now, according to your promise, the time of parting between me and you has come. But I will explain the reason and wisdom behind each of the three things I did, so that you may know the purpose of your coming — to acquire knowledge."

Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> narrates: "I have seen in certain books of narrations that when the verse *'its people refused to host them'* was revealed, the descendants of that town came to the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> with gold, because they were deeply ashamed that their ancestors were such lowly people that they could not even feed two or three hungry travelers. They said: 'O Prophet of Allah, we wish to change the letter *Ba* to *Ta* in exchange for this gold' — meaning they wanted the word to become *'they brought food to them for hospitality'* instead of *'they refused to host them.'* They hoped that this change would remove the blame and shame from their town.

"The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> firmly refused and said: 'This can never happen — that change should be made in the word of Allah. If changing a single dot necessitates alteration in the word of Allah, it would prove falsehood in the speech of the Lord. Then there would be no reliance on the commands of Allah, and the Lordship of Allah and the servitude of the servant would all be rendered null and void.'"

Their demand was itself based on foolishness. How could it be possible for any human to change the Quran whose preservation Allah Himself has undertaken? And to expect this especially from the Prophet of Allah was an even greater sign of distance from reason.

### If Only Moosa Had Been Patient

The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: *"May Allah have mercy on us and on Moosa. If he had not been hasty, he would have seen even more wonders. But the promise he had made to his companion overtook him."* Moosa had said: "If I ask you about anything after this, do not keep me in your company — indeed you have received enough excuse from me." In accordance with that promise, after the third question, they parted.

Elaborating on the blessed words of this hadith, Allama Nawawi <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> says: "From this hadith, the scholars have established that in supplication — that is, in matters of the Hereafter — a person should give priority to himself. But in worldly matters, preferring others over oneself is better."

### Hazrat Khizr Explains His Actions

*"As for the boat, it belonged to some poor people who worked on the sea. I intended to make it defective, for behind them was a king who was seizing every sound boat by force."*

Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> explained: "The boat belonged to ten poor people, some of whom were disabled. They supported themselves through its labor. When they return, they will face a disbelieving king named Julandi, who seizes sound boats but leaves defective ones alone. So I decided to make it defective, so that he would not seize it and their livelihood would continue."

Note that he said "I intended to make it defective," not "I made it defective." Outwardly it had become defective, but in reality it was made beneficial for them.

*"As for the boy, his parents were believers, and we feared that he would oppress them with rebellion and disbelief. So we intended that their Lord should give them in exchange one better in purity and nearer in mercy."*

Hazrat Ubay bin Ka'ab narrates that the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: "Indeed, the boy whom Khizr killed had been marked for disbelief. Had he lived, he would have forced his parents into rebellion and disbelief."

Allah ta'ala knew that he would grow up to be a disbeliever. Of course, he could not be called a disbeliever during childhood.

*"As for the wall, it belonged to two orphan boys in the city, and beneath it was a treasure for them, and their father was a righteous man. So your Lord willed that they should reach their maturity and retrieve their treasure as a mercy from your Lord. And I did not do it on my own accord. That is the interpretation of that over which you could not be patient."*

From the mention that "their father was a righteous man," we learn that the righteousness of parents causes Allah's mercy to descend upon their children.

Muhammad bin Munkadir said: "Indeed, Allah protects the children and grandchildren of His righteous servant, and his tribe and neighbors, because of his righteousness. As long as they remain in the neighborhood of that righteous person, they remain under Allah's protection."

**Benefit:** Regarding the three incidents, Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> explained:
1. First explanation: "I intended to make the boat defective"
2. Second explanation: "We intended"
3. Third explanation: "Your Lord willed"

What is the reason for this difference in expression? The brief answer is that although Allah is the true Creator of good and evil and benefit and harm alike, the way of the people of refinement and spiritual knowledge is that when mentioning good and benefit, they attribute it to Allah, and when the occasion of evil and harm arises, they attribute it to themselves. Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: *"When I am ill, it is Allah who cures me"* — attributing illness to himself and health to Allah, even though the One who causes illness is also Him.

Similarly, when explaining the damage to the boat, Khizr said *"I intended"* — attributing it to himself, because breaking the boat appeared outwardly blameworthy. When it came to repairing the wall — which was purely good — he attributed it to Allah. As for the killing of the boy, it had two aspects: good, because his parents were being given a righteous child in exchange for a disobedient one; and evil, because outwardly an innocent child was being killed. Thus the plural form *"We intended"* was used so that the good aspect was attributed to the Divine Essence and the evil aspect attributed to himself. Allama Badruddin <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> preferred this explanation.

### Was Hazrat Khizr a Prophet or a Saint?

On this issue, scholars have evidence on both sides. Some say he was a *wali* (saint), others say he was a *nabi* (prophet). The research scholars incline toward him being a prophet. Presenting this issue, the thinker of Islam and exegete of the Quran, Hazrat Peer Karam Shah Sahib, states:

"The majority of scholars hold that this servant was Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. His name is Baliyya bin Malkaan. Because wherever he would go, the land would become green, he was called Khizr, and this epithet became his famous name.

Some scholars hold that he was a *wali*, but Allama Paani Pati and other research scholars hold that he was a *nabi*, because knowledge acquired through the *ilham* (inspiration) of a *wali* is speculative and subject to error. Committing a serious act like killing cannot be justified through *ilham* alone. Therefore, he must be considered a prophet, and the knowledge of a prophet is certain."

**Evidence of those who consider him a prophet:**

(1) Allah ta'ala said: *"We granted him mercy from Us"* — here "mercy" means prophethood, just as in another place "mercy" is used in the sense of prophethood, when the disbelievers commented on the Prophet's <sup>ﷺ</sup> prophethood: *"Why was this Quran not sent down to a great man from either of the two cities?"*

(2) He gave Moosa the direction: "If you follow me, do not ask me about anything." This command establishes his prophethood, because a *wali* does not have the authority to impose conditions on a prophet.

(3) He recognized Moosa through revelation — revelation only comes to prophets. This makes his prophethood clear.

It should be noted that Allama Raazi <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> has also transmitted the opposing arguments, but since the writer is convinced of his prophethood, he suffices with these proofs. Those who believe in his sainthood rather than prophethood should consult Tafseer Kabeer and Ruhul Ma'ani for a detailed study.

### Is Hazrat Khizr Still Alive?

On this matter, Peer Karam Shah Sahib writes: "As for whether Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> is still alive or has passed away, there are two groups of scholars, and both have amassed piles of evidence to prove their position. Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> has presented these proofs in his commentary with great elaboration and detail. But despite studying this debate spread over many pages, no satisfaction is attained, and one does not reach a conclusion that settles the heart.

"May Allah make the shrine of Qaazi Sanaullah Paanipati <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> a place of descent for His lights. He has written something on this matter that removes the contradiction of the evidence and brings tranquility to the heart. In my opinion, quoting the evidences of both parties here would cause excessive length. It is sufficient to write that passage from Tafseer Mazhari. I hope that just as his research removed my anxiety, studying it will also remove your difficulty."

After quoting the evidences of both parties, Qaazi Sanaullah writes: "The solution to this difficulty is impossible without the words of Hazrat Mujaddid Alfesaani. When Hazrat Mujaddid was asked about Khizr — whether he is alive or has passed away — he turned his attention to the Divine presence for the disclosure of the reality.

"Hazrat Mujaddid Alfesaani saw that Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was standing before him. He asked him about his true state. Khizr said: 'I and Ilyaas are not among the living, but Allah has granted our souls such power that we become embodied and perform the works of the living — for instance, when Allah wills, we guide the misguided, help the afflicted, teach the knowledge of *ladunni*, and grant spiritual connection to whomever Allah intends. We have been made the helpers of that *Qutb* of the *awliya* who is the axis of the world. Allah has made him the axis of the world, and the world's existence continues through his blessing and grace. Today, an elder from Yemen is the *Qutb*, who follows the Shafi'i school, and we pray with him according to the Shafi'i school.'"

Through this authentic unveiling, the contradiction and difficulty of the various opinions were removed. All praise is for Allah, who is the Great and Most High.

**Benefit:** Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, despite being the possessor of abundant knowledge and deeds, and despite his exalted rank and the presence of complete means of nobility in his favor, still went to acquire knowledge from Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and adopted humility before him. From this we learn that humility is better than pride — a person can attain high stations only through humility.

There are two types of students: one who knows nothing and has no experience of argumentation — he cannot refute a lecture or raise objections. The second type of student already possesses abundant knowledge, has experience in reasoning and objecting, yet still wishes to meet a perfect person to attain further perfection in knowledge. Such a person's acquisition of knowledge is very difficult, because when he sees something or hears some speech, it often does not appear correct to him outwardly, and he raises objections and argues — for he is unaware of the reality, whereas in truth that very speech or action is correct. The teacher who possesses perfect knowledge becomes averse to such a student's argumentation. After tolerating it twice or thrice, he tells him: "Go away — I cannot teach you. You already possess more knowledge than I can give."

The best student is one who does not raise objections against his teacher, who does not try to prove his own superiority. We have seen that only those who were respectful, needy, and humble attained the station of teaching. The disrespectful, insolent, and faithless, even if they acquired worldly wealth and riches, found the necklace of the teacher's displeasure around their necks, becoming a cause of their humiliation.

Many were those whom we thought would become teachers and succeed in religion and world, but it did not happen — because their ill manners placed them in the station of disgrace. The same applies to the disciple — he must observe respect and honor toward his *shaykh*.

Qaazi Sanaullah Paanipati <sup>رحمة الله عليه</sup> mentions in Tafseer Mazhari: "This verse makes clear that for the disciple, it is necessary that he stop objecting to his *shaykh*. When Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> wanted to acquire knowledge from Khizr, he observed great respect, honor, and neediness."

Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> presented himself as a follower, saying: "May I follow you?" — seeking permission to remain in obedience. It was as if he said: "Do you permit me to consider myself your follower?"

In this, he expressed great humility. By saying "teach me," he effectively acknowledged that the knowledge of *ladunni* that Khizr possessed, he himself did not possess — this too is a way of showing respect.

(1) He said *mimma 'ullimta* ("from what you have been taught") — meaning: "I do not say that you should give me all your knowledge so that I become your equal. Rather, I wish to acquire some knowledge from you, just as a beggar asks something from a rich man." By asking in this manner, Moasa set the highest standard of humility.

(2) By saying *mimma 'ullimta*, he acknowledged that "Allah has given you the knowledge of *ladunni* — I affirm this."

(3) By saying "right guidance" (*rushd*), he acknowledged that Khizr is on the path of goodness and expressed his own desire for guidance.

(4) By saying "teach me from what Allah has taught you," he effectively requested: "Treat me as Allah has treated you — your favor upon me will be like Allah's favor upon you." This is why one who acknowledged the favor of his teacher said: "I am the slave of the one from whom I have learned even a single letter."

(5) Following (*ittiba'*) means doing something solely because someone else did it, without having any proof of one's own. We say "La ilaha illallah," and the Jews also say the same words — yet we are not their followers because we have our own proof. Our Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> commanded us that none is worthy of worship except Allah, and our performing five daily prayers is following the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> because without his words and actions, we have no other proof. Moosa sought permission to follow Khizr — meaning: "I will follow you by observing your actions and hearing your words. I will not need any other proof." From this issue it becomes clear that "indeed, it is necessary for the student to accept the teacher's words from the beginning and abandon argumentation and objection."

Asking for understanding is permissible — if the teacher did not explain properly due to lack of attention, the matter may be asked again in a commendable manner.

(6) Moosa said *attabi'uka* ("I will follow you") — meaning he would follow him in all matters, not just some.

(7) When narrations make it clear that Hazrat Khizr <sup>عليه السلام</sup> already knew that Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was the prophet of Bani Israel, the possessor of the Torah, one whom Allah had honored by speaking to him directly, one given great miracles — despite his exalted rank and high station, Moosa's showing such great humility demonstrates how intensely he strove to acquire knowledge. This was befitting his dignity, for the more knowledge one has, the more the light and blessedness of knowledge appears on one's face, and the more such a person strives to acquire further knowledge and fully honors those who possess it.

(8) Moosa said: "May I follow you on condition that you teach me?" He mentioned following and service first, and knowledge acquisition later. This makes clear that the student should first adopt the service of the teacher as his emblem and then keep the attainment of knowledge in view.

Wretched is the student who considers serving his teacher an evil thing or who does not do what the teacher says and resorts to evasion. Nevertheless, the teacher must also draw students close through good character — those who approach cannibals also stay far away. Similarly, the teacher must pay maximum attention to the education of those who serve him. A teacher who prioritizes his own work and harms the education of his servant is in reality that student's enemy, not benefactor. The true favor to the servant is to strictly forbid things that harm his education, to show kindness when he memorizes a good lesson, and to love him like his own child through affection.

A teacher cannot be a benefactor to students if he wastes time with anecdotes and stories and does not teach the book. Nor is a teacher a benefactor who, after completing his lesson, puts someone to work and compensates for another teacher, or after finishing the lesson starts stories and tales, wasting others' rights and committing oppression. After finishing their studies, the students certainly realize which teacher wasted their time and which teacher paid attention to teaching them. Would that the students realized this during the course of their education itself!

(9) Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> did not ask for wealth, property, or status in exchange for following Khizr — he only sought the acquisition of knowledge. Those students are truly foolish who, by virtue of proximity to the teacher, keep complaining about other students and never miss an opportunity to harm them. O teachers of the faith, tell me by justice: in which *shari'ah* is it permissible to appoint a spy? Sometimes spying on the teacher, sometimes on students — this is far from justice. The student of today will become the teacher of tomorrow. Only one who forgets his own past can commit such base acts.

O cannibal teacher of today! Glance at your own past — how worthy were you? How angelic was your character? Did you never make a mistake? Did you never show negligence? Did you never err? When you were just like today's students, then why are you speaking nonsense today? Why are you raining blows today? Why do you consider humans as donkeys today? Was your teacher as vile as you are? Think! Reflect! What is humanity?

### The Death of Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

Hazrat Abu Huraira <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> narrates that the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: *"The Angel of Death (Izraail) was sent to Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. When he came to him, Moosa struck him and knocked out his eye."*

Another narration states it this way: *"The Angel of Death came to Moosa and said: 'Respond to the call of your Lord.' Moosa struck him such a slap that his eye was destroyed. Izraail returned to Allah and submitted: 'You sent me to a servant who does not want to die.' Allah restored his eye and said: 'Go to My servant and tell him to place his hand on the back of a bull — for every hair that comes under his hand, he will be granted a year of life.' Moosa asked: 'Then what?' Allah said: 'Then death will come.' Moosa said: 'Then let death come now.' Then he asked: 'O my Lord, let me be buried in the land of Baitul Muqaddas, within a stone's throw of it.' The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: 'O my Companions, if I wished, I could show you his grave — it is by the side of the road near the red sand dune.'"*

**Benefit:** Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> wished to be buried in Baitul Muqaddas because that place was honored by the burial of many prophets. His prayer makes clear that it is commendable to be buried in a place of virtue, near the righteous.

### Burying Near Righteous People in Blessed Places Is a Commendable Act

Hazrat Umar <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> wished to be buried in the blessed chamber of the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>, and Hazrat Aayesha Siddiqa <sup>رضي الله عنها</sup>, preferring others over herself, gave permission. Regarding this, in Fathul Baari Sharh Bukhari it is stated: "From this noble hadith, it is learned that one should desire to be buried near righteous people, with the thought that when mercy descends upon them, I too may benefit from it. When righteous people visit their graves and pray for them, I too will benefit from that prayer."

**Note:** From the hadith in Sahih Muslim, it is as clear as daylight that the Angel of Death has no power over the prophets. Some prophets welcomed Izraail when he came with the command of the Lord. Others admonished him for coming to them without permission and made it clear that he has no power over them. Then they preferred going to Allah. Even when offered an extension of life, they did not accept. This shows that their intention was not to obtain more life but to make clear the station of the prophets to the common people.

**Great Benefit:** It is narrated from Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله عنهما</sup> that on the day of the Prophet's death, Allah commanded the Angel of Death: "Present yourself before My Beloved on earth. Beware — do not appear without permission, and do not take his soul without permission." So Izraail stood at the door in the form of a Bedouin Arab and said: *"O mine of messengership, O place of the coming and going of angels, O people of the prophetic household — peace be upon you. Permit me to enter, may the mercy of Allah be upon you."*

At that time, Sayyida Fatima Zehra <sup>رضي الله عنها</sup> was at the head of the Prophet. She replied: "The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> is occupied with his own condition — he cannot meet you now." He asked permission a second time and received the same reply. He asked a third time, now in a loud voice. All those present in the house trembled at the terror of that voice. The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> regained consciousness, opened his blessed eyes, and asked: "What is the matter?" The situation was submitted to him. He said: "O Fatima, do you know who this is? This is the one who destroys pleasures, crushes desires, severs gatherings, widows wives, and orphans children."

### Moosa Praying in His Grave

Hazrat Sulaiman Taimi <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> narrates that he heard Hazrat Anas <sup>رضي الله عنه</sup> say: The Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: *"I passed by the grave of Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, and he was praying in his grave."*

Another narration states: "On the night of Mi'raj, I passed by Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>."

And in another narration, the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> said: "I saw Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> praying. I saw Eisa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> praying. I saw Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> praying."

It should be noted that Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was seen praying in his grave, and also seen praying with the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> in Baitul Muqaddas, and also seen welcoming him in the heavens. There are two possible reasons for this: either the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup> was traveling on Buraaq, while Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was traveling without any mount through his prophetic power — therefore he reached Baitul Muqaddas and the heavens before the Prophet <sup>ﷺ</sup>, meaning the speed of prophethood surpasses even the speed of Buraaq. The second possibility is that Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> could be present in his grave, in Baitul Muqaddas, and in the heavens all at the same time.
