## Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup> — Continued

### Moosa Strikes the Copt

> "He entered the city at a time when its people were unaware, and found two men fighting — one from his own faction and the other from his enemy. The one from his faction called for help against the one from his enemy. So Moosa struck him with his fist and finished him. He said: 'This is from Satan's work. Indeed, he is a clear enemy who leads astray.' He said: 'My Lord, I have wronged myself, so forgive me.' So He forgave him. Indeed, He is the Oft-Forgiving, the Most Merciful. He said: 'My Lord, as You have favored me, I will never be a supporter of the criminals.'"

### Why Moosa Entered the City Secretly

Moosa entered the city secretly for various reasons. The most authentic is that from early on, Moosa had the opportunity to stay with his mother and hear her accounts. He was well aware of the situation. He also knew of the high station of prophethood among his noble ancestors. When he saw Firaun sitting as a god and making people worship him, his monotheistic mind could not tolerate this open *shirk* for long. His resolute temperament led him to confront Firaun about this improper behavior, and he began refuting the misguidance of Firaun and his people.

Banu Israel would listen to him and follow him. Gradually the word spread. He had also confronted Firaun directly about his claim to divinity, leading to a complete break. He was considered a criminal and a rebel. To protect himself from Firaun's evil, he went into hiding. If he had to enter the city for some urgent need, he would do so at a time when no one would know. This incident occurred when he entered the city while people were resting. Allama Qurtubi and other scholars have preferred this view.

According to Ibn Is'haaq, this was after he had openly expressed his difference with Firaun and condemned Firaun's idols and the worship of Firaun. This led to severe backlash, so he chose to remain hidden to protect himself.

### The Incident

When Moosa entered the city, he saw two men fighting — an Israelite and a Copt. The Israelite called for his help. Moosa stepped forward to stop the Copt from his aggression. When the Copt did not listen, Moosa struck him with a fist. He had no intention to kill, but the blow proved fatal.

Hazrat Qataada <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> explained the cause: The Copt was ordering the Israelite to carry a heavy bundle of wood. The Israelite refused, and the Copt — being an agent of the ruler — began beating him. Moosa arrived, and the Israelite appealed to him. Moosa helped him not merely because the one seeking help was an Israelite, but because helping any oppressed person is obligatory in every religion.

### Why the Killing Was Attributed to Satan

Allah had permitted the killing of disbelievers but had deferred it until a later time. After some time, killing became commendable (*mustahab*), but doing it before its time meant abandoning what was preferable. Therefore, Moosa attributed this act to Satan's influence — "my action of abandoning the preferable was from Satan."

Another explanation: Moosa was referring to the *victim's* actions, not his own — "This man who was killed, his deeds were satanic because he opposed Allah's commands, so he deserved to be killed."

A third: Moosa meant "army of Satan" — the slain man was from the satanic faction. In Arabic idiom, "fulaan min amlish shaitaan" means "so-and-so is from Satan's party."

**"رَبِّ إِنّ۪ي ظَلَمْتُ نَفْسِي فَاغْفِرْ لِي"** — One meaning: "O Allah, I missed the preferable act by killing him hastily; forgiveness is sought for abandoning the better course."

Another meaning: "O my Lord, I have incurred trouble for myself by killing this accursed man. If Firaun finds out, he will kill me. So conceal this matter from him." And Allah kept this news from reaching Firaun.

### The Secret is Revealed

> "The next morning, he entered the city fearful and looking around. Suddenly, the one who had sought his help the day before was crying out for help again. Moosa said: 'Indeed, you are clearly misguided.' Then when Moosa intended to seize the one who was an enemy to both of them, he said: 'O Moosa, do you intend to kill me as you killed someone yesterday? You want only to be a tyrant in the land and do not wish to be a reformer.'"

Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہما</sup> said: Firaun's people informed him that one of Banu Israel had killed one of their men. Firaun ordered the killer and witnesses to be found. The Copts searched but found no evidence.

The next day, Moosa encountered the same Israelite fighting with another Copt. When he saw Moosa, he again called for help. Moosa said: "Indeed, you are clearly misguided" — you fight with people every day, bringing trouble upon yourself and your helpers. Why don't you exercise caution? Moosa rebuked him firmly.

Then Moosa felt pity — the Copts were oppressive, so he should help. When Moosa stepped forward to seize the Copt, the Israelite misunderstood and feared that Moosa intended to kill him too (since he had killed a Copt the previous day). He cried out: "Do you intend to kill me as you killed someone yesterday?" This exposed the secret — that Moosa had killed the Copt.

### The Warning and Flight to Madyan

> "And a man came running from the farthest end of the city. He said: 'O Moosa, indeed the chiefs are conspiring to kill you. So leave — I am of those who wish you well.'"

The man who warned him was from Firaun's own people — a believer who concealed his faith. He told Moosa they were discussing his execution and Firaun had already decided.

> "So he left the city, fearful and watchful. He said: 'My Lord, save me from the wrongdoing people.' And when he headed toward Madyan, he said: 'Perhaps my Lord will guide me to the right path.'"

Madyan refers to the city where Hazrat Shoaib <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was living. It was named after Madyan bin Ibrahim. The distance between Egypt and Madyan was eight days' journey. Firaun had authority over that city, which is why Allah guided Moosa there.

Moosa had never seen this city before nor knew the way. He had no mount and no provisions. He survived on tree leaves. Allah appointed Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> to guide him. The Copts searched for him but could not find him because there were three roads to Madyan — he took the middle path while they searched the other two. When the Lord Himself was protecting him, how could they find him?

### Moosa at the Well of Madyan

> "When he arrived at the water of Madyan, he found a crowd of people watering their flocks. Apart from them, he found two women holding back their flocks. He said: 'What is the matter with you?' They said: 'We cannot water until the shepherds take their flocks away, and our father is very old.' So he watered for them, then turned to the shade and said: 'My Lord, I am in need of whatever good You send down to me.'"

When Moosa arrived at a well in Madyan, he saw a large crowd gathered, watering their animals — camels, cows, goats, and sheep. He noticed two women standing apart, holding their flocks back. They did not want to push through the crowd — it was improper for women to mix freely with men, and drawing water from the well required great strength. A stone covered the well's mouth that took ten men to move, and the bucket also required ten men to lift. They also did not want their animals to mix with others.

Moosa asked why they were standing apart. They explained that their father was very old and they could not draw water themselves. They waited for everyone to finish so their animals could drink what remained.

Moosa asked the people to show compassion, but they said: "If you are so sympathetic, water for them yourself." They placed the heavy stone back on the well. Moosa alone moved it and drew water with the bucket that normally required ten men. He prayed for blessing, and their goats drank from a single bucket and were fully satisfied.

Then he sat in the shade and prayed: "O Allah, send me food" — he had been eating only tree leaves for seven days.

SubhanAllah — behold the greatness of a prophet: seven days hungry, yet stronger than ten men, tired from travel and under scorching sun — yet nothing stopped him. He set an unmatched example of compassion.

### Shoaib <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Summons Moosa

The two women were Shoaib's daughters. When they returned home earlier than usual, their father asked why. They told him about the noble, strong man at the well who had watered for them, so they did not have to wait for everyone to finish.

Shoaib <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: "Go and call that man." It appears that Allah had already informed His prophet that the arriving man was Moosa <sup>عليه السلام>, also a prophet, to whom he would soon be commanded to announce prophethood.

> "Then one of the two came to him, walking with modesty. She said: 'My father invites you so that he may reward you for watering for us.' When Moosa came to him and narrated his story, he said: 'Do not fear. You have escaped from the wrongdoing people.'"

Although the woman said her father wanted to reward him for watering, Moosa did not go for payment — he went to visit Shoaib <sup>عليه السلام>. He disliked going for wages. When he arrived at Shoaib's home, Shoaib presented food. Moosa said: "A'uzu billah" (I seek refuge with Allah). Shoaib asked why. Moosa replied: "The people of my household do not sell their religion for the world. We do not take payment for any good deed."

Shoaib said: "But it is my custom and that of my forefathers to feed guests." At this, Moosa sat and ate, though the thought kept recurring: "May this not become wages for my good deed, affecting its sincerity."

Shoaib then asked: "Who are you and where are you from?" Moosa replied: "I am Moosa bin Imraan bin Qaahas bin Laawi bin Yaqoob." He then narrated his entire story — the killing of children, being cast into the river, the search for a wet nurse, his upbringing with his mother, the killing of the Copt, and the Copts searching for him.

After hearing everything, Shoaib said: "Do not fear — you have been saved from the wrongdoing people." Meaning: Firaun and his army will not have sway over this region. This was indeed a marvel of Allah's power — despite having millions of soldiers, Firaun could not find Moosa who was merely an eight-day journey away.

### Shoaib's Daughter's Counsel

> "One of the two said: 'O my father, employ him. Indeed, the best of those you employ is the one who is strong and trustworthy.'"

Shoaib's daughter had witnessed Moosa's strength — he alone drew the bucket that required ten men and moved the heavy stone. She had also witnessed his piety: when speaking to her, his gaze was lowered, and when walking with her, he said: "Walk behind me — tell me from behind" so his gaze would not fall upon her.

Note: All of this was happening through Allah's revelation. It would be incorrect to say Shoaib sent his young daughter alone to call a stranger. Allama Raazi says: "Shoaib was commanded by Allah through revelation to send his daughter to call that man. His daughter was chaste, pure from all flaws, and trustworthy." And the one she was calling was also Allah's beloved, perfect prophet.

Shoaib mentioned two qualities: strength (*quwwat*) and trustworthiness (*amaanat*). Although two more qualities — intelligence (*fataanat*) and wisdom (*zeeraaki*) — are necessary for a complete person, these are encompassed within perfect trustworthiness, which cannot exist without intelligence and wisdom.

SubhanAllah — what an excellent choice Shoaib's daughter made! Bravery to fight the disbelievers, piety, intelligence, and wisdom — these are what make a person complete. And she brought the matter as counsel to her father, knowing that according to their shariah, he would employ the man, and through service, marriage would take place.

### The Marriage Proposal

> "He said: 'I wish to marry one of these two daughters of mine to you, on the condition that you serve me for eight years. If you complete ten, it is from you. I do not wish to make it hard for you. You will find me, Allah willing, among the righteous.' Moosa said: 'This is an agreement between me and you. Whichever of the two terms I fulfill, let there be no further demand on me. And Allah is witness over what we say.'"

Shoaib had no son, so he wished to keep Moosa with him. He proposed marriage to one of his daughters, with the *mahr* being eight years of service — eight years obligatory, and two additional years voluntary. Moosa accepted and said: "Let whichever term I complete be sufficient — do not force me to stay longer." The agreement was finalized, the marriage took place, and Moosa began serving in his father-in-law's house.

Shoaib gave Moosa a staff to drive the goats and shake leaves from trees for them to eat. This staff was made of teak wood, which Adam <sup>عليه السلام> had brought with him, and it had been passed down through the prophets until it came to Shoaib and then to Moosa. This very staff later became his miracle.
