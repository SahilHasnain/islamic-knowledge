## Hazrat Moosa wa Haroon <sup>عليهما السلام</sup>

### Banu Israel

The children of Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> are called Banu Israel, because "Israel" is his title or second name. The word "Israel" is composed of "Isra" and "Eil". "Isra" has four meanings: servant/worshipper, chosen one, human, and migrant. "Eil" means Allah. Thus Hazrat Yaqoob <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was named Israel because he was a devoted servant of Allah, His chosen one, a man of great status, and one who turned toward Allah.

Some scholars say "Isra" means "to travel by night" and "Eil" means Allah — so the name means "one whom Allah caused to travel by night."

### Hazrat Moosa <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

Moosa's father's name was **Imraan**, who was the chief of his tribe, from the descendants of Laawi bin Yaqoob. There is difference of opinion on his mother's name — Yaarakha, Iyaarakht, Nookhind, Lokha, Maryam — the most authentic is Yaarakha.

### Hazrat Haroon <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

He is Moosa's full brother, three years older. He was very wise and forbearing, and Banu Israel loved him greatly.

### Saamri — His Name Was Also Moosa

Saamri, who later led Banu Israel to worship the calf, was also named Moosa — Moosa bin Ja'far. He was from the tribe of Saamra, hence called Saamri. His entire tribe and surrounding people worshipped the cow.

He was born when Firaun was killing every male child of Banu Israel. His mother hid him in a mountain cave. For the upbringing of such hidden children, Allah had appointed Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام>. Jibreel would come to his cave and place his hand in Saamri's mouth, and he would suck milk and honey from it.

Some scholars write that Jibreel would put his own fingers in the child's mouth and he would suck milk and honey.

Note: When a person is deprived of blessedness in his original nature, even the best upbringing cannot benefit him. The Moosa who was raised by Jibreel became a disbeliever, while the Moosa <sup>عليه السلام> who was raised by Firaun became Allah's messenger.

Saamri outwardly believed in Moosa but was a confirmed hypocrite.

### Firaun

"Firaun" was the title of the kings of Egypt, just as "Qaisar" was for Rome, "Kisra" for Persia, "Taba'a" for Yemen, "Khaqaan" for Turkey, and "Najaashi" for Abyssinia.

No king of Egypt was more evil-tempered, hard-hearted, and oppressive than the Firaun of Moosa's time. His name was Waleed bin Mus'ab or Mus'ab bin Rayyaan. Some wrote his name as Qaboos. He was from the Coptic tribe. Between the Firaun of Yusuf's time and this Firaun, more than 400 years had passed.

### State of Banu Israel Before Moosa's Birth

Banu Israel were servants to the Copts. The Copts assigned them various types of work: some were put to construction, some to plowing fields, some to farming and harvesting. They were even assigned dirty work like cleaning latrines and clearing mud. Stone-cutting and carrying stones was their responsibility. Those who could not do such work had jizya imposed on them. If a person did not pay jizya before sunset, his hands were tied to his neck and remained that way for a month.

The women of Banu Israel were treated like slave girls — all domestic work like spinning, sewing, etc., was assigned to them.

### The Killing of Banu Israel's Children

Firaun had a dream: a fire came from the direction of Baitul Muqaddas, surrounded Egypt, and burned all the Copts but did not harm Banu Israel. Distressed, he asked dream interpreters. They said: "A child will be born among Banu Israel who will cause the end of your kingdom."

Firaun ordered that every male child born among Banu Israel be killed. Thousands were killed — 12,000 or 17,000.

Allah ta'ala reminded Banu Israel of this: "When We saved you from the people of Firaun who afflicted you with the worst torment, killing your sons and sparing your women — and in that was a great trial from your Lord."

The sparing of women was because the Copts did not see girls as a threat.

### Moosa's Birth

> "And We inspired the mother of Moosa: 'Suckle him, then when you fear for him, cast him into the river. Do not fear nor grieve. Indeed, We will return him to you and make him a messenger.'"

Before his birth, conviction was placed in his mother's heart: after birth, continue nursing him; when you fear the spies of Firaun or the neighbors (due to crying), cast him into the river. We will protect him, return him to you, and make him a messenger.

How long she nursed him before casting him into the river is not mentioned in the Quran. One narration from Ibn Jareej says: "He cried at four months, so fearing the neighbors, he was placed into the Nile."

### The Midwife

When Moosa's birth approached, a midwife came — one of those appointed by Firaun for Banu Israel's women. When Moosa was born, rays of light shone from between his eyes. Seeing this, every joint of the midwife trembled, and love for Moosa was placed in her heart.

She said: "O woman, I came to kill this child, but I have been overcome with intense love for him. Protect your child." She left.

Just then, Firaun's spies arrived at the door. Moosa's sister saw them coming and said: "Mother, the Copts are coming." His mother panicked and, in a state of shock, wrapped the baby in clothes and threw him into a burning tandoor (oven). When the Copts entered, they did not go near the burning oven and searched the house thoroughly. They saw no child. Moosa's mother showed no change in her complexion — though usually a woman's face pales after childbirth — and her milk was not visible.

They asked: "Why did that midwife come to your house?" She replied: "She was my friend who came to visit me." This was not a lie — the midwife was indeed her friend.

### Saved by the Tandoor

When the Copts left, she regained her senses and asked her daughter: "Where did the child go?" — she did not even know where she had thrown him. Her daughter replied: "I don't know." Then they heard a faint cry from the tandoor. She saw that the fire had become cool and safe for the child. She took him out.

Now, fearing that Firaun was searching everywhere for the child, she decided to place him in a chest and set it on the river. Allah had already firmly placed this conviction in her heart that this way the child would be safe and would one day return to her.

### The Carpenter Betrays

She went to a carpenter to get a chest. He asked what she needed it for. She told him the truth — that she would place her son in it and set it in the river, hoping he might escape the Copts. He sold her the chest, but evil intention arose in his heart. He went to the Copts appointed to kill children to inform them.

When he reached them, Allah sealed his tongue. He gestured with his hands. The Copts thought him mad, beat him, and drove him away. When he returned home, Allah restored his speech. He went a second time to inform them — again his tongue was sealed, and they beat him. He returned, and his speech was restored. He went a third time — this time his tongue was sealed and he went blind. They beat him and drove him away. Now he sincerely repented: "O Allah, if You restore my sight and speech, I will tell no one." Allah accepted his repentance and restored both.

### The Ark in the River

Moosa's mother placed him in the chest and gave him to the river.

Firaun had only one daughter and no other children. He loved her greatly. She used to present three requests to her father every day. She suffered from a severe skin disease (leprosy/baras). Firaun consulted physicians and magicians. They said: "O king, she cannot be cured until something resembling a human being is found from the river, and its saliva is applied to the affected area. This will happen on such and such day and month, when the sun is bright."

When that day came, Firaun arranged a gathering by the riverbank. His wife was also with him. Firaun's daughter and her maids went and sat by the river. A canal from the Nile flowed toward Firaun's palaces. His daughter and maids began bathing. They saw a chest bobbing on the waves, which had come to rest against a tree.

Firaun ordered: "Bring that chest to me immediately." Boatmen quickly brought the chest. They tried to open it but could not. They tried to break it but failed. Firaun's wife, Aasiya, saw a light shining from inside the chest that others could not see. When Aasiya opened it, she found a small baby with light shining between his eyes.

### Aasiya Adopts Moosa

Allah placed love for the child in everyone's hearts. Firaun's daughter applied the baby's saliva to her affected areas and was immediately cured. She hugged the child.

Some people told Firaun: "This might be the very child we are trying to avoid. It may have been thrown into the river out of fear of you." Hearing this, Firaun resolved to kill the child. But his wife Aasiya pleaded for the child's life and adopted him as her own.

Thus the first stage was complete. Through the blessing of Moosa's saliva, Allah demonstrated His power: the child you sought to eliminate by killing thousands of babies has been brought to your very home — you cannot kill him.

Aasiya was a very righteous woman from the lineage of the prophets. She was kind to the poor and needy. She said to Firaun: "This child is so beautiful — he is worthy of being kept. We have no child of our own, so let us make him our son." Firaun and his chiefs accepted this.

Allah ta'ala said: **"So the family of Firaun picked him up, that he might become for them an enemy and a cause of grief. Indeed, Firaun and his army were sinful. And the wife of Firaun said: 'He is a comfort to my eye and yours. Do not kill him — perhaps he will benefit us, or we may take him as a son.' And they were unaware."**

Had Firaun and his vizier Haman and their chiefs known that this was the very child destined to destroy their kingdom, they would never have taken him in — or if they did, they would surely have killed him. But the power of the Creator cannot be overcome.

> "And I cast upon you love from Me, so that you might be brought up under My eye."

Hazrat Ibn Abbas <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہما</sup> said: "Allah made him beloved and caused the creation to love him. When Allah bestows His love upon someone, love for him is placed in people's hearts."

Hazrat Qataada <sup>رضی اللہ تعالیٰ عنہ</sup> said: "Moosa was given such beauty, handsomeness, and luminosity in his eyes that whoever saw him would love him."

### Mother and Sister's Anxiety

> "And the heart of Moosa's mother became empty. She was about to disclose his matter had We not strengthened her heart so that she would be among the believers. And she said to his sister: 'Follow him.' So she watched him from a distance while they were unaware."

When his mother learned that the child had fallen into Firaun's hands, she was overcome with fear that he might be killed. But when Allah informed her that Firaun's wife had taken him as a son, her heart was comforted.

Had Allah not strengthened her heart, she might have cried out — "Alas, my child!" — alerting everyone. Or when she learned that Aasiya had become kind to the child, she might have revealed her joy. But Allah prevented her from doing so.

Moosa's sister's name was Maryam (or Kulsoom). His mother sent Maryam: "Go see where the chest goes and how they treat the child." Maryam watched from afar so as not to be noticed.

Note: Moosa's mother's name was also Maryam, and her father was Imraan. Moosa's sister was also named Maryam, and his father was also Imraan. Some mistakenly thought that Maryam the mother of Eisa was Moosa's sister — this is incorrect, as there is a vast time gap between these two prophets.

### Moosa's Upbringing is Entrusted to His Mother

> "And We had previously forbidden all wet nurses for him. So she (his sister) said: 'Shall I direct you to a household that will nurse him for you and be well-wishers for him?' So We returned him to his mother so that her eye might be comforted and she would not grieve."

Allah placed in Moosa's nature a dislike for every wet nurse except his own mother. Firaun ordered wet nurses to be brought, but the baby would not drink from any of them and became restless with hunger. Firaun, concerned because of Aasiya, held the child and said: "If only we could find a wet nurse whose milk he would accept."

Moosa's sister seized this opportunity and said: "I know of a household that will take care of this child and will not be negligent or treacherous."

Haman said: "This girl knows the child's family — seize her and she will lead us to them." But Moosa's sister cleverly replied: "I meant that the household are well-wishers of the king, not of the child." Being from the prophetic household, her intelligence saved both herself and her brother.

Firaun said: "Bring the woman you are referring to." She went and brought her mother. When she arrived, the baby smelled the familiar scent of his mother and immediately latched on and began nursing. Firaun asked in amazement: "Who are you that this child accepted your milk when so many wet nurses failed?"

His mother replied: "I am a woman who uses good fragrance, keeps myself and my clothing clean, and my milk is pure and sweet. Every child I have nursed has drunk my milk." Firaun entrusted Moosa to her and even arranged for her expenses.

Thus Allah fulfilled His promise: "Cast him into the river, and We will return him to you." This was to strengthen the mother's certainty — since this promise was fulfilled, the child would surely become a messenger. Most people are unaware of Allah's power — all schemes are nothing before His might.
