### Chapter 6: Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

Allah ta'ala commands:

> "So Looth believed in him."

The meaning is that Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> confirmed all of Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> words and affirmed his claim to prophethood. All the noble prophets are free from sins both before and after prophethood. Therefore, it cannot be taken to mean here that Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> (God forbid) embraced faith from disbelief — that when Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> preached monotheism, Looth came to believe in it. Such a meaning is contrary to the dignity of a prophet.

The statement "Looth believed in Ibrahim" means that he accepted Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> as truthful in every claim. Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was the son of Haran bin Tarkh, Ibrahim's brother. Although in Kashshaf it is mentioned that he was his sister's son, this view is rejected in Kamalain.

It should be noted that Haran was also the name of Hazrat Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> uncle, who was the father of Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup>. In earlier times, many members of a household would share the same name — sometimes a father and son, and sometimes a brother and sister would have the same name.

**Note:** Some scholars have stated that when Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was not harmed by the fire, Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> believed after seeing this miracle. This gives the impression that (God forbid) he believed after disbelief. Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> rejects this, saying: "What some scholars said — that Looth believed when he saw that the fire did not burn Ibrahim — is contrary to reason and revelation, because it would imply he had no faith before, which is unbefitting for a prophet."

Thus, the meaning of Looth's belief is either what has already been explained, or it could mean: "He attained such heights of rank that ordinary people cannot reach that station."

### The Migration of Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> migrated with Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> from Babul to Harran, then from there to Egypt, where Hazrat Saara <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> was also with them. From Egypt, they came to Philistine, and now Hazrat Haajra <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> was also with them. Then Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> came to a city about 18 miles away from Ibrahim's city for the purpose of preaching. The name of that city was *Sadum* (Sodom), which the Quran refers to as *المُوتَفِکَة* (the overturned cities).

### Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Did Not Call the People to Worship

Hazrat Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> only preached to his people to refrain from evil; he did not command them to worship in the manner that Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> did. The reason was that Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> had already preached the Oneness of Allah and worship to the people, and it had become widely known. Since Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> lived during Ibrahim's time and the region was nearby, and he himself was from the same people (family), he focused on restraining them from their specific evils rather than re-emphasizing well-known matters, so that they might be saved from worldly and otherworldly punishment. But the people did not desist, and ultimately punishment was sent upon them.

He said to his people:

> "And Looth was saved when he said to his people, 'Indeed, you commit an indecency that no one in the worlds has committed before you. Do you approach men with evil desire, and cut off the way, and commit evil in your gatherings?' His people had no answer except to say, 'Bring Allah's punishment upon us, if you are truthful.'"

The prophet was concerned with saving the people from punishment, but the people sometimes said, "Bring the punishment upon us," and sometimes said, "O Looth, if you do not desist, you will certainly be expelled" — meaning, "We will expel you from our city and not let you live here."

### The Evils of Looth's People

The greatest evil among them was homosexuality (*liwaatat*). They also had the habit of sitting on the paths of travelers, stopping them, and forcibly committing evil with them. Sitting on the road and highway robbery, killing people and looting their wealth were also part of their evil habits.

1) These evils of the people of Looth are still found among people today — a rebellion against the pure shari'ah.

2) Attending men's gatherings only to taunt each other — looking at one another and selecting for evil. Pigeon-flying — spending the entire day flying pigeons and betting on them. People today still commit this evil.

3) Dyeing the tips of fingers with henna like women. Note that it is permissible for a man to apply henna as a remedy, but it is forbidden for adornment. In the present age, boys prefer girls' clothing, gait, and mannerisms — which are forbidden.

4) Throwing pebbles here and there, harassing passersby. Similarly, shooting pellets from guns to annoy people. This practice is found among ruffians today even more than among the people of Looth.

5) Slapping each other — someone came and slapped another, and another came and slapped back. This was their idea of fun, which was in reality a sign of their vulgarity.

6) Passing gas loudly in gatherings among people. They considered this a matter of pride, whereas for noble people it is a cause of shame. Involuntary loud passing of gas due to illness, inability to suppress it, or the risk of worsening an illness — these are valid excuses, and in such cases many acts are excused with different rulings.

7) Stripping naked in jest — removing one's cloak, shalwar, etc., being naked, and being immodest. This too was a practice of the people of Looth, which today's so-called Muslims call "culture."

8) Using obscene language in jest and cursing each other. This evil is also commonly found among today's vulgar boys. They consider themselves modern and progressive. If a noble person stops them, they label him with terms like "backward" and "conservative," saying, "The world has moved far ahead in immodesty, but you are still 1400 years behind." Study scientific subjects, progress, elevate your nation and country — but do not invite Allah's punishment by becoming immodest.

In short, every kind of immodesty was found among that people. Every act contrary to modesty was considered praiseworthy in their eyes. Chewing gum and chewing miswak openly before people in public gatherings was also an evil practice of the people of Looth. This is an act that disgusts onlookers and creates aversion, sometimes causing nausea in sensitive people.

Chewing gum — taking it out of the mouth repeatedly, putting it back in, and sometimes making bubbles — is exactly the same as the practice of the people of Looth. In truth, it is a sign of foolishness. A donkey is often compared to a foolish person because it urinates on its own thighs. One who cannot protect himself from urine is considered foolish. Many donkeys make bubbles from their mouths and then take them back in, producing foam in the mouth. If you look at a donkey and then at these "progressive" gum-chewers, you will see no difference. Therefore, calling them donkeys and considering them foolish is a sign of intelligence.

Some people walk around with a miswak in their mouths at all times — in public gatherings, on the bus, etc. — though putting a miswak in the mouth, taking it out, putting it back in without rinsing is almost equivalent to disturbing others.

**When is miswak recommended?** Understanding the Prophet's ﷺ instructions about miswak makes it clear that constantly chewing miswak, sometimes putting it in the mouth and sometimes taking it out, is a flaw. An act that disturbs others is not something the noble prophets did.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "If I did not fear hardship for my ummah, I would have made miswak obligatory with every wudu." He also said: "If I did not fear hardship for my ummah, I would have delayed Isha prayer and made miswak obligatory for every prayer." Note that both of these are still recommended (*mustahab*); only the obligation is negated so as not to cause hardship.

Hazrat Shuraih bin Hani <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> says that Hazrat Aisha <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنها</sup> was asked what the Prophet ﷺ would do first when entering the house. She replied: "Miswak" — whether returning from the mosque or from a journey. He paid special attention to cleanliness and was thorough in brushing his teeth.

**Times when miswak is recommended:**
1) When teeth become yellow
2) When the mouth develops an odor
3) After sleeping
4) When preparing for prayer
5) Whenever making wudu — whether for prayer or to touch the Quran

### The Wife of Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

The name of Hazrat Looth's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> wife was *Waahila*. She was a disbeliever. There was hypocrisy in her heart. She did not keep her word — she would inform the people about the guests who came to Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup>, thus betraying him. She was pleased with the people's evil. Therefore, she was also caught in the punishment along with the people.

### The Angels of Punishment Came to Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

The same angels who came to Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> to give glad tidings of Ishaq <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and after him Yaqub <sup>عليه السلام</sup> also came to punish Looth's people. They came in human form. Hazrat Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was very hospitable — no guest had come to him for 15 days — so he quickly presented a roasted calf to them. When he saw that these strangers' hands did not reach the food, he understood that they were angels.

**Objection:** Allah ta'ala says, "And in his heart he began to fear them." This indicates that he did not recognize them as angels and, considering them strangers, became afraid — not knowing who they were or where they had come from.

**Answer:** In *Taweelat al-Najmiya*, it is stated that Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> did not fear for his life as a human. If he feared for his life, when he was being thrown into the fire by mangonel, he would have feared for his life then — but he said, "I entrust myself to the Lord of the Worlds." His fear was only for his people, out of mercy and compassion, lest his people be afflicted with some punishment. The angels' response clarifies this theme, for they said, "Do not fear; indeed, we have been sent to the people of Looth."

### Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Argument with the Angels

> "Then when the fear left Ibrahim and the glad tidings came to him, he began to argue with Us concerning the people of Looth."

That is, when Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup> learned that his own people were not being punished, he became concerned about Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> and his family. Allama Aalusi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> also favors this view — that he was concerned for his people and he recognized the angels. Allama Aalusi says: "My inclination is that he had already recognized them as angels; the fear was only because he could not ascertain why they had descended — had they come to punish the people?"

Although Ibrahim's <sup>عليه السلام</sup> questioning of the angels was not a real argument, it appeared as one. He said to them, "Will you destroy this town if there are 300 believers in it?" They said, "No, we will never do that." He said, "Will you destroy it if there are 200 believers?" They said, "No, we will not destroy it." He said, "Will you destroy it if there are 40 believers?" They said, "No, we will never destroy it." He said, "Will you destroy the city if there are 10 believers?" They said, "No." Then he said, "What if there is just one believer?" They said, "No." Then he said, "Looth is there." The angels said, "We know who is there. We will save Looth and his family except his wife — she will be caught in the punishment with the people."

### The Angels Came to Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup>

> "When Our messengers came to Looth, he was grieved for them and felt constrained. He said, 'This is a day of great distress.' And his people came rushing toward him, and they had been accustomed to evil deeds. He said, 'O my people, these are my daughters — they are purer for you. So fear Allah and do not disgrace me concerning my guests. Is there not among you a single upright man?' They said, 'You know we have no right to your daughters, and indeed you know what we desire.' He said, 'If only I had strength against you or could take refuge in some strong support.'"

When the people learned that handsome young men had come to Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> as guests, they came running with evil intentions and intense desires. The one who informed them was Looth's wife Waahila.

It should also be noted that those people did not commit this evil in secret; rather, they openly engaged in this obscenity. That is why Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said, "Is there not among you a single upright man?" He counseled them in a most affectionate manner: "These are the daughters of my nation — they are your wives. You are trampling their rights. You are not fulfilling conjugal rights. Your inclination toward young men and your commission of evil is the means of your wickedness. Turn toward your wives so that their rights may be fulfilled and you may become righteous. These are my guests — do not disgrace me by committing evil with them. If I had any power, I would make you flee by beating you. If I had a strong fortress or shelter, I would take my guests there."

### The Meaning of Seeking Refuge

Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said, "Or take refuge in some strong support." The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "May Allah have mercy on Looth — he wished to take refuge in some strong support."

Outwardly, one might wonder why Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> said this — did he (God forbid) display impatience? Mulla Ali Qaari <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> answers: "Human nature requires that one seek help from a strong tribe for essential matters." This is not contrary to the dignity of the prophets. Rather, all the prophets after Looth also sought help from members of their tribes, and there is no prohibition from Allah in this.

### The Angels' Response and the Punishment of Looth's People

> "The angels said, 'O Looth! We are messengers of your Lord. They will not reach you. So set out with your family during the night, and let none of you look back — except your wife. Indeed, she will be struck by what strikes them. Indeed, their appointment is the morning. Is the morning not near?' Then when Our command came, We turned the city upside down and rained upon it stones of baked clay, layer upon layer, marked from your Lord. And they are not far from the wrongdoers."

The angels had been silently watching this scene. When the ruffians' insolence and Looth's distress and helplessness reached their peak, the angels spoke: "O Looth! Do not be distressed. Open the door and let these buffoons come forward. We are not young boys that they can seize us. We are angels sent by Allah, and we have been sent to turn these cities upside down. Do this: when part of the night has passed, take your family and leave from here. But your wife cannot go with you — her fate will be the same as the other criminals. The moments of respite for these wrongdoers are over; only morning remains, and there is not much time until dawn."

When the punishment came, their cities were laid waste. Their sky-scraping buildings were toppled to the ground. A torrential rain of hard stones was sent upon them until they were reduced to black dust. Their four cities — Sa'doon, Umoorah, Auma, and Zabo'im — were situated at what is now the Sea of Mordad or the Sea of Looth. Even today, clouds of smoke rise from the Sea of Looth and frequent earthquakes occur there.

### Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> Departed at Night

Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> left at night with the members of his household except his wife. Allah ta'ala folded the earth, and thus he reached Ibrahim <sup>عليه السلام</sup>. Then Jibreel <sup>عليه السلام</sup> lifted all their cities with his wing, raising them so high that the inhabitants of heaven could hear the roosters and barking dogs of those cities. Then he turned them upside down, dropped them, and rained stones upon them, destroying and obliterating them completely.

Note that four cities have been mentioned previously. The fifth city, which was the largest, was called *Sadum* (Sodom), which the Quran refers to as *مُوتَفِکَات* (the overturned cities).

**Note:** The stones that were rained upon them bore marks that distinguished them from other stones. They had inscriptions or engravings on them, or each person's name was written on them — and the stone fell upon the one whose name it bore, killing him.

### The Interpretation of "My Daughters"

When the people of Looth learned that young guests had come to him, they rushed with evil intentions. He tried to dissuade them and said:

> "O my people, these are my daughters — they are purer for you."

A'la Hazrat Maulana Ahmad Raza Khan Bareilvi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> writes: "He said, 'O my people, these are the daughters of my nation — they are purer for you.'" Other scholars have translated it as "my daughters."

A'la Hazrat's translation is according to the commentaries and befits the dignity of Allah's prophet. The alternative translations would imply that Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> was referring to his own daughters. Although there is one view that he offered his daughters in marriage, this view is rejected due to various arguments. He had two daughters — the word *banati* (my daughters) is plural, and plural requires at least three. How could two daughters suffice for the whole people? Does it mean only two chiefs of the people, or the whole people? Was marriage with disbelievers permissible?

Abu Shaikh has narrated from Ibn Abbas <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>, and Ibn Abi Hatim from Ibn Zubair, and Ibn Asaakar from Siddi, that when Looth <sup>عليه السلام</sup> mentioned *banati*, he meant the women of his nation. He used the demonstrative pronoun *ha'ulaa* (these) referring to them as if they were present, and attributed them to himself, calling them his daughters. The meaning is that every prophet is like a father to his nation, as in the recitation of Hazrat Ibn Mas'ood <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>: "The Prophet is more entitled to the believers than their own selves, and he is a father to them, and his wives are their mothers." Hazrat's <sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup> recitation also reflects this.

A'la Hazrat Maulana Ahmad Raza Khan Bareilvi's <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup> translation is supported by Allama Razi <sup>رحمه الله تعالى</sup>, who has established proofs for this chosen view. From Tafseer Kabeer: "Regarding Looth's statement, 'These are my daughters, they are purer for you,' there are two views. Hazrat Qatada said he meant his real daughters. However, Hazrat Mujahid and Sa'eed bin Zubair said he meant the women of his nation — they were his daughters in the sense that they were attributable to him due to their acceptance of his call and following him. The principle in grammar is that a minimal connection suffices for attribution, and he was their prophet, and a prophet is a father to his nation, as the Quran says: 'The Prophet's wives are their mothers'; thus, the prophet is their father."

Allama Razi said: "This is the view I prefer, and there are several proofs for its soundness."

**First proof:** It is highly unlikely for a person to offer his own daughters to ruffians, sinners, and wicked people. This is not befitting for pious people — how could the great prophets do such a thing?

**Second proof:** He said "my daughters" — his own real daughters could not have sufficed for such a large multitude, but the women of the nation could have sufficed for all of them.

**Third proof:** There is a sound narration that he had two daughters — one named *Zanta* and the other *Za'oora*. The plural term *banat* (daughters) is not truly applicable to two daughters, since a plural requires at least three individuals.
