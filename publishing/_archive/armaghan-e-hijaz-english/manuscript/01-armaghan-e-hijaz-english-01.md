# Armaghan-e-Hijaz: English Translation

## Kalam 001

**Source index:** 0  
**Source title:** Iblees Ki Majlis-e-ShuraThe Devil’s Conference (1936)  
**Title:** The Devil's Council  
**Slug:** iblees-ki-majlis-e-shura  
**URL:** https://allamaiqbal.org/armaghan-e-hijaz/iblees-ki-majlis-e-shura/  
**Source lines:** Urdu 139; transliteration 138

**Iblees (to his counsellors)**

This ancient game of elements, this lowly world,
The blood of the hopes of the dwellers in the Mighty Throne!

Today the Maker who gave it the name of the world
Is bent on bringing it to ruin.

I showed the European the dream of kingship;
I broke the spell of mosque, temple, and church.

I taught the poor the lesson of destiny;
I gave the affluent the madness of capitalism.

Who can cool its burning fire,
When Iblees's inward fire rages in its tumult?

Whose branches have risen high through our watering—
Who can bow that ancient tree to the ground?

**First Counsellor**

What doubt is there that this Ibleesian order is firm?
By it the people's habit of servitude has grown stronger.

Since eternity, prostration has been written into these poor people's fate;
Their nature demands a prayer without standing upright.

Desire cannot arise anywhere in the first place;
And if it arises somewhere, it dies or remains raw.

It is the miracle of our ceaseless striving that today
The Sufi and the Mullah alike are servants of kingship.

This was precisely the opium suited to the temperament of the East;
Otherwise, the “science of theology” is no less than “qawwali”!

What if the tumult of circumambulation and pilgrimage still remains?
The believer's unsheathed sword has grown blunt.

For whose despair is this new decree an argument?
“In this age, jihad is forbidden to the Muslim man!”

**Second Counsellor**

So, the uproar of popular sovereignty is harmless, then?
You are unaware of the world's new seditions!

**First Counsellor**

Yes, but my vision of the world tells me this:
If kingship merely wears another veil, what danger is there in it?

We ourselves have dressed monarchy in democratic clothes,
Now that man has become a little self-knowing and self-observing.

The reality of civic rule is something else;
It does not depend upon the existence of a lord or a king.

Whether it is the assembly of the nation or the court of Parvez,
He is a king whose gaze rests upon another's harvest.

Have you not seen the democratic order of the West?
Its face is bright, but its inward self is darker than Genghis!

**Third Counsellor**

If the spirit of kingship remains, what anxiety can there be?
But what answer is there to the mischief of that Jew?

That Moses without a theophany, that Christ without a cross—
Not a prophet, yet holding a book in his bosom.

What can I say of the unbeliever's gaze that burns through veils?
For the nations of East and West, it is a Day of Reckoning!

What greater corruption of nature could there be than this?
The servants have torn the ropes of their masters' tents!

**Fourth Counsellor**

See its breach within the halls of great Rome;
We showed Caesar's family once again the dream of Caesar.

Who is wrapped in the waves of the Mediterranean?
“At times he rises like a cypress, at times he cries like a lute.”

**Third Counsellor**

I have little faith in his foresight,
Who has exposed European politics in this way.

**Fifth Counsellor (addressing Iblees)**

O you whose breath's fire keeps the world's work in order!
When you wished, you made every hidden thing manifest.

Through your heat, water and clay became a world of fire and music;
By your teaching, the fool of Paradise became a knower of affairs.

No one knows Adam's nature more intimately than you,
More than the Lord celebrated among the simple-hearted servants.

Those whose only work was sanctification, glorification, and circumambulation—
Through your honour they are forever fallen and ashamed.

Though all the sorcerers of Europe are your disciples,
I no longer trust their intelligence.

That scheming Jew, that manifestation of Mazdak's spirit—
His madness is about to tear every robe to shreds.

The desert crow is becoming the equal of the falcon and the vulture;
How swiftly the temper of the age changes!

It has spread in turmoil across the expanse of the heavens—
What we had foolishly taken to be merely a handful of dust.

Such is the dread of tomorrow's sedition that today
Mountains, meadows, and streams are trembling.

My master! The world whose whole order rests upon your sovereignty
Is about to be overturned.

**Iblees (to his counsellors) - I**

The world of colour and fragrance lies within my hand of dominion—
What is earth, what are sun and moon, what is your layered sky?

We shall see the spectacle of East and West with our own eyes
When I have heated the blood of Europe's nations.

The leaders of politics, the shaykhs of the church—
My single cry can drive them all mad.

What the fool thinks is a workshop of glass,
Let him break it and see the cups and jugs of this civilization!

The collars that nature's hand has torn
Cannot be mended with the needle of Mazdakian logic.

How can the wandering communists frighten me—
These confused in fortune, bewildered in mind, dishevelled in hair?

If anything threatens me, it is that community
In whose ashes a spark of desire still survives.

Here and there, even now, some of that nation may be seen
Who perform ablution for the oppressor with the tears of dawn prayer.

He knows, to whom the inward face of the age is clear:
Mazdakism is not tomorrow's sedition—it is Islam!

**Iblees (to his counsellors) - II**

I know this community is not the bearer of the Quran;
Capitalism itself is the faith of the believing man.

I know that in the East's dark night
The sleeves of the sanctuary's elders are without the Radiant Hand.

Yet this is my fear before the demands of the present age:
What if the Law of the Prophet becomes manifest somewhere?

Beware! A hundred times beware the Prophet's law—
Guardian of woman's honour, tester of men, creator of men!

It brings the message of death to every kind of slavery—
To no emperor or monarch, and to no roadside mendicant.

It purifies wealth of every impurity,
Making the affluent trustees of riches and property.

What greater revolution of thought and action could there be?
This earth belongs not to kings, but to Allah!

It is well if this law remains hidden from the world's eye;
It is fortunate that the believer himself is deprived of certainty.

It is best that he remain entangled in theology,
That he remain entangled in interpretations of the Book of Allah.

**Iblees (to his counsellors) - III**

May his proclamations of Allah's greatness shatter the talisman of the six directions,
May the dark night of that God-conscious one never grow bright.

Is the son of Mary dead, or eternally alive?
Are the attributes of the Divine Essence separate from Truth, or the very Essence?

Does the coming one mean the Messiah of Nazareth,
Or a renewer bearing the qualities of Mary's son?

Are the words of the Speech of Allah originated or eternal?
In which creed does salvation await the afflicted community?

In this age, are these chiseled idols of theology
Not enough for the Muslim: Lat and Manat?

Keep him estranged from the realm of action,
So that all his pieces are checkmated upon life's board.

It is best that the believer remain a slave until the Resurrection,
Leaving this unstable world for the sake of others.

For him, the best poetry and mysticism are those
That conceal the spectacle of life from his eyes.

At every breath I fear the awakening of this community—
The truth whose religion is the reckoning of the universe.

Keep him intoxicated in morning remembrance and reflection;
Make his monastic temperament still more entrenched.

## Kalam 002

**Source index:** 1  
**Source title:** Budhe Baloch Ki Nasihat Baite Ko  
**Title:** An Old Baloch's Advice to His Son  
**Slug:** budhe-baloch-ki-nasihat-baite-ko  
**URL:** https://allamaiqbal.org/armaghan-e-hijaz/budhe-baloch-ki-nasihat-baite-ko/  
**Source lines:** Urdu 22; transliteration 22

May the desert wind be dear to you;
No Delhi or Bukhara is better than this wilderness.

Go wherever you wish, swift as a flowing torrent;
This valley is ours, and that desert too is ours.

Honour is a great thing in the world's struggle and striving;
It places a crown of Dara upon the dervish's head.

Learn this hidden art from some perfected one:
They say glass can be turned into stone.

The destiny of nations lies in the hands of individuals;
Every individual is a star in the fate of the nation.

The diver who does not leave the company of the shore
Remains deprived of the wealth of the sea.

If a nation becomes free by giving its faith away,
Such a bargain is a Muslim's loss.

Once again the world faces a battle between spirit and body;
Civilization has once again roused its beasts.

Allah trusts the believer's courage;
Iblees relies on Europe's machines.

What is the destiny of nations? No one can say;
If the believer has insight, a sign is enough.

Ask the ancient song for sincerity in action:
“How wondrous if kings should favour beggars!”

## Kalam 003

**Source index:** 2  
**Source title:** Tasveer-o-MusawwarPAINTING AND THE PAINTER  
**Title:** Painting and the Painter  
**Slug:** tasveer-o-musawwar  
**URL:** https://allamaiqbal.org/armaghan-e-hijaz/tasveer-o-musawwar/  
**Source lines:** Urdu 20; transliteration 20

**Painting**

The painting said to the painter:
“My display is born of your art;

Yet what injustice this is—
That you remain hidden from my sight!”

**Painter**

“The seeing eye is a burden upon the one who sees;
What has world-seeing done to the spark?

Sight: pain, grief, fire, fever, and burning—
O foolish one, be content with tidings.”

**Painting**

“Tidings are the weakness of reason and understanding;
Sight is the heart's everlasting life.

The striving and movement of this age
Is not worthy of the saying, ‘You shall not see Me.’”

**Painter**

“You exist through the excellence of my art;
Do not despair of your maker.

There is only this condition for seeing me:
Do not hide from your own sight.”

## Kalam 004

**Source index:** 3  
**Source title:** Alam-e-Barzakh(THE STATE OF BARZAKH)  
**Title:** The State of Barzakh  
**Slug:** alam-e-barzakh  
**URL:** https://allamaiqbal.org/armaghan-e-hijaz/alam-e-barzakh/  
**Source lines:** Urdu 43; transliteration 42

**The Corpse, from his grave**
What thing is it? What tomorrow of today is Resurrection?
O my ancient sleeping chamber, what is Resurrection?

**The Grave**
O corpse of a hundred years! Do you not know?
Resurrection is the hidden demand of every death!

**The Corpse**
The death whose hidden demand is Resurrection—
I am not caught in the snare of that death.

Though I am a corpse a hundred years old,
I am not weary of this dark chamber of dust.

If the spirit must mount the feeble body once again,
Then if this is Resurrection, I am not its buyer.

**A Voice from Beyond**
Neither the serpent's lot, nor the scorpion's; neither the beasts' lot, nor the wild animals':
Everlasting death is only for enslaved nations.

The blast of Israfeel cannot bring them to life,
Whose bodies were empty of spirit even while they lived.

To rise after dying is the work of free men alone,
Though every living being's destination is the embrace of the grave.

**The Grave**
*(to its corpse)*
Ah, tyrant! You were an enslaved servant in the world;
I had not understood why my dust was so burning.

Your corpse has made my darkness darker still;
Your corpse has torn the veil of the earth's honour.

Beware, a hundred times beware the corpse of the enslaved!
O Israfeel! O Lord of the universe! O pure spirit!

**A Voice from Beyond**
Though Resurrection has thrown the order of being into upheaval,
Through this very tumult the secrets of existence are unveiled.

In the earthquake, mountains and passes fly like clouds;
In the earthquake, fresh springs appear in the valleys.

Every new construction requires total destruction;
Within it lies the opening of life's difficulties.

**The Earth**
Ah, this everlasting death! Ah, this battle of life!
Will the struggle of the universe ever end?

Reason cannot gain release from its own idols;
The learned and the common alike are servants of Lat and Manat.

How humiliated has the Adam of divine qualities become!
The stability of such a world weighs heavily upon heart and sight.

Why does dawn not come to the night of humankind?

## Kalam 005

**Source index:** 4  
**Source title:** Maazool Shahenshah(A Deposed Monarch)  
**Title:** A Deposed Monarch  
**Slug:** maazool-shahenshah  
**URL:** https://allamaiqbal.org/armaghan-e-hijaz/maazool-shahenshah/  
**Source lines:** Urdu 6; transliteration 6

Blessed is that fortunate monarch
Whose sacrifice has exposed the secrets of kingship.

The “Shah” is a clay idol in the British temple,
Whom its priests may smash to pieces whenever they wish.

This musk-scented opium is for us slaves;
O English sorcerer, carve us another master!
