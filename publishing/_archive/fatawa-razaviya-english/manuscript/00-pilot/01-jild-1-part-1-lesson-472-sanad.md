# The Poor Servant's Chain for the Illuminating Fiqh

## Source Record 17231 | Hadith No. 97

### The Chain of Transmission

The chain of this poor servant in the illuminating fiqh is continuous through
the distinguished Hanafi scholars, the renowned muftis, authors, Imams, and
illustrious spiritual masters.

All praise belongs to Allah Most High. There are many routes of this chain. One
of its most exalted routes is that I narrate fiqh from the Lamp of the Sacred
Land, the Hanafi Mufti of Makkah al-Mukarramah, our master Shaykh Abd al-Rahman
al-Siraj. He narrates from the eminent mufti, our master Shaykh Abdullah al-
Siraj. He narrates from the Mufti of Makkah, Sayyidi Jamal ibn Abdullah ibn
Umar. He narrates from the illustrious Shaykh Muhammad Abid al-Ansari al-Madani.
He narrates from Shaykh Yusuf ibn Muhammad ibn Ala al-Din al-Mizjaji, who
narrates from Shaykh Abd al-Qadir ibn Khalil. Shaykh Ismail ibn Abdullah, known
as Ali Zadah al-Bukhari, narrates from him.

Shaykh Ismail narrates from the Gnostic of Allah Most High, Shaykh Abd al-Ghani
ibn Ismail ibn Abd al-Ghani al-Nabulsi. He is the author of *al-Hadiqah al-
Nadiyyah*, *al-Matalib al-Wafiyyah*, and many noble and pure writings. He
narrates from his father, the author of *Sharh al-Durar wa al-Ghurar*, who
narrates from two illustrious Shaykhs, Ahmad al-Shubri and Hasan al-Sharnablali.

Thus, there are twenty-seven intermediaries between this chain and Imam Abu
Hanifah, the Imam of the greatest rank. Between Imam Abu Hanifah and the
Illustrious, Radiant Leader of the Messengers ﷺ, there are four intermediaries.

## Source Record 17232 | Hadith No. 98

The annotator of *al-Durar wa al-Ghurar* is the author of *Nur al-Idah*, its two
commentaries, *Maraqi al-Falah* and *Imdad al-Fattah*, and other excellent works.
His first route is through Shaykh Umar ibn Najm, author of *al-Nahr al-Fa'iq*,
Shams al-Hanuti, author of *Fatawa*, and Shaykh Ali al-Maqdisi, commentator on
*Nazm al-Kanz*.

His second route is through Shaykh Abdullah al-Nahriri, Shaykh Muhammad ibn Abd
al-Rahman al-Maysari, Shaykh Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Hamawi, and Shaykh Ahmad
al-Muhibbi. All seven narrate from Shaykh Ahmad ibn Yunus al-Shalbi, author of
*Fatawa*. He narrates from Siraj al-Din Abd al-Barr ibn al-Shihnah, commentator
on *al-Wahbaniyyah*. He narrates from Kamal ibn al-Humam, the author of *Fath
al-Qadir*, who is known as the absolute verifier. He narrates from al-Siraj,
the reader of *al-Hidayah*, who narrates from Ala al-Din al-Sayrafi, who narrates
from Sayyid Jalal al-Din al-Khabbazi, commentator on *al-Hidayah*.

### Footnote 1

In my chains, this name is written with the letter fa, and that is the more
well-known form. It is also pronounced Sirami, with a mim, as it appears in *Fath
al-Qadir*, *Tahtawi*, and *Radd al-Muhtar*. With a fa, Siraf, on the pattern of
Shiraz, is a town in Persia on the seashore near Kerman. The renowned grammarian
Abu Sa'id was from there. With a mim, Siram is a city in Rum. Nizam Yahya ibn
Yusuf ibn Fahd al-Nahwi, a student of al-Taftazani, was from that city.

### Footnote 2

This is how it appears in this chain of mine. In another chain of mine, through
al-Siraj al-Hanuti, Ibrahim al-Karki, author of *al-Fayd*, Shaykh Muhibb al-Din
al-Aqsarai, the reader of *al-Hidayah*, and al-Sayrafi, the wording is: “I
narrate from the Sayyid...”

## Source Record 17233 | Hadith No. 99

The chain continues from al-Hidayah through Shaykh Abd al-Aziz al-Bukhari, author
of *al-Kashf wa al-Tahqiq*, then through Imam Abd al-Sattar ibn Muhammad al-
Kardari, Imam Burhan al-Din, author of *al-Hidayah*, Imam Fakhr al-Islam al-
Bazdawi, and Shams al-A'immah al-Halwani.

Jalal al-Din ibn Shams al-Din al-Kurlani narrates from Abd al-Aziz ibn Muhammad
ibn Ahmad al-Bukhari. This Sayyid Jalal al-Din is the author of *al-Kifayah*, a
commentary on *al-Hidayah*. He was a student of Husam al-Din al-Sughnaqi, author
of *al-Nihayah*, the first commentary on *al-Hidayah*. Al-Khabbazi, Umar ibn
Muhammad ibn Amr, author of *al-Mughni fi al-Usul*, also wrote a commentary on
*al-Hidayah*. Both were students of the author of *al-Kashf wa al-Tahqiq*.

And Allah Most High knows best. 12, from him, may his spiritual outpouring
continue. (T)

### Footnote

This is how it appears in my chain, and it also appears in the chains of Sayyid
al-Tahtawi and Sayyid al-Shami: from Fakhr al-Islam, and from Shams al-A'immah
al-Sarakhsi, and from Shams al-A'immah al-Halwani, and so on. I say: this is an
addition within a continuous chain, because Imam Fakhr al-Islam acquired
knowledge directly from Shams al-A'immah al-Halwani, without an intermediary.
Al-Dhahabi wrote this in the biography of al-Halwani in *Siyar A'lam al-Nubala*.

## Source Record 17234 | Hadith No. 100

Shams al-A'immah al-Sarakhsi, Fakhr al-Islam al-Bazdawi, and his brother Sadr
al-Islam acquired knowledge from Imam al-Halwani. Al-Dhahabi recorded that Imam
al-Halwani passed away in Bukhara in 456 AH. Fakhr al-Islam passed away in Kash
in Rajab 482 AH. He was born around 400 AH. Therefore, he was approximately
fifty-six years old when al-Halwani passed away. 12, from him, may his spiritual
outpouring continue.

### Footnote

This is how it appears in this chain of mine, as well as in the chains of
al-Tahtawi and al-Shami. It is established in the chain of al-Shami's Shaykh,
and it is well known that his kunyah was Abu Muhammad and his name was Abdullah
ibn Muhammad. This is also how it appears in another chain of mine, through
Izz al-Din Ahmad ibn al-Muzaffar and the aforementioned Abd al-Aziz al-Bukhari.
Both narrate from Hafiz al-Din al-Bukhari, from Shams al-A'immah al-Kardari, from
Badr al-A'immah Umar al-Warsaki, from Imam Rukn al-Din Abd al-Rahman al-Kahani,
from Fakhr al-Qudat al-Arsabandi, and from Imad al-Islam Abd al-Rahim al-Zawzani.

**The remainder of the chain is on the following page.**

## Source Record 17235 | Hadith No. 101

The chain continues from Abu Hafs al-Bukhari, from his father Ahmad ibn Hafs,
the renowned Imam Abu Hafs al-Kabir, from Imam al-Hujjah Abu Abdullah Muhammad
ibn al-Hasan al-Shaybani, from Imam Abu Hanifah, from Hammad, from Ibrahim, from
Alqamah and al-Aswad, from Abdullah ibn Masud رضی الله تعالی عنہ, from the Prophet
ﷺ.

The chain also continues from Qadi Imam Abu Zayd al-Dabbusi, from Ustad Abu Jafar
al-Istirushni, from Abu al-Hasan Ali al-Nasafi, from Imam al-Fadli. He said: Imam
Abu Muhammad Abdullah ibn Muhammad ibn Yaqub al-Sabdhmuni al-Harithi informed us,
and the chain continues.

Perhaps he had two kunyahs: (1) Abu Muhammad, and (2) Abu Abdullah. And Allah
Most High knows best. 12, from him, may his spiritual outpouring continue. (T)
