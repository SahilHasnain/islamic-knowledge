# Roshan Tar Agahi: The Fatwa Is According to the Imam's Position

## Source Records 17236–17240 | Hadith Nos. 103–107

All praise belongs to Allah, the Most Kind towards us in the Hanafi religion.
He strengthened us through Imams who, by the permission of the Generous,
Self-Sufficient Lord, correct what is crooked and continually provide support.
Among them He placed our Imam Abu Hanifah like the heart within the body. Blessings
and peace be upon the Imam of the noble Messengers ﷺ.

This noble treatise establishes that a fatwa is always according to the position
of the Imam, even when the position of the two companions differs from it, and
even when a fatwa appears to have been issued according to another position.
The reasons for issuing a fatwa according to another position may be a change
in circumstances, necessity, prevailing practice, or similar considerations.
In reality, even then, the ruling remains the position of the Imam.

The noble saying of the Prophet ﷺ has rightly reached us:

> Seek a verdict from your heart, even if the issuing jurists have given you a verdict.

استفت قلبك وان افتاك المفتون

This is a most excellent opening. It indicates that the Imam's fatwa is the
weightier one, even if other verdicts oppose it.

Imam Ahmad narrated this hadith in *Musnad* and Imam Bukhari narrated it in
*al-Tarikh*, from Wabisah ibn Ma'bad al-Juhani رضی الله تعالی عنہ with a hasan
chain. Its wording there is: “Seek a verdict from yourself.” Imam Ahmad also
narrated through Abu Tha'labah al-Khushani رضی الله تعالی عنہ, with a sahih chain,
from the Prophet ﷺ:

> Righteousness is that by which the soul finds tranquillity and the heart finds
> peace; sin is that by which the soul does not find tranquillity and the heart
> does not find peace, even if those issuing verdicts declare it correct.

البر ما سکنت الیہ النفس واطمأن الیہ القلب والاثم مالم تسکن الیہ النفس ولم یطمئن الیہ القلب وان افتاک المفتون

The author of *al-Bahr al-Ra'iq* records two positions at the beginning of
*Kitab al-Qada*: according to *al-Sirajiyyah*, the mufti issues a fatwa without
qualification according to the position of Abu Hanifah. According to *al-Hawi
al-Qudsi*, if the Imam is on one side and the two companions on the other, the
strength of the evidence is considered.

The author then asks how the mashayikh could issue a fatwa according to a
position other than that of the Imam while being his followers. He explains that
the mashayikh were required to know the Imam's evidence, and that requirement
was not fulfilled in their case. In our time, the mufti transmits the position
chosen by the mashayikh.

Imam Kardari records from Asim ibn Yusuf in *Manaqib al-Imam* that he had never
seen a gathering more distinguished than the gathering of the Imam. His four
most distinguished companions were Zufar, Abu Yusuf, Afiyah, and Asad ibn Amr.

Imam Muhammad said that no one may attribute to us a statement he did not hear
from us. From our books, one may narrate only what one personally heard from us,
or what one knows with knowledge equal to ours.

## Source Records 17241–17247 | Hadith Nos. 108–114

Even if we do not know the evidence for the Imam's position, issuing a fatwa
according to it is permissible, indeed necessary. The position verified in
*al-Hawi al-Qudsi* is based on the same requirement. The mashayikh issued
according to another position because knowing the Imam's evidence was a
condition for them, and that condition was absent in their case. It is not a
condition for us.

The verifier Ibn al-Humam objected in several places to the mashayikh issuing
fatwas according to the position of the two companions. He stated that one may
depart from the Imam's position only when its evidence is weak. In the prayer
of Isha, the Imam's position has stronger evidence because it is more cautious.
The same applies to the determination of the final time of the takbirs of
Tashriq. Ibn al-Humam was qualified to investigate evidence; whoever lacks this
qualification must issue according to the Imam's position.

This qualification means knowing the positions, distinguishing their ranks,
and having the ability to prefer one over another.

Allamah Shami and Khair al-Din al-Ramli objected that the command to issue
according to the Imam's position appears to conflict with the Imam's statement
that no one may issue according to his position without knowing its basis.
The answer is that the ruling issued by one who is not a mujtahid is not a true
fatwa. It is only a transmission of the mujtahid's position.

We do not possess the qualification for deep investigation into evidence and
deriving subsidiary rulings from principles that the mashayikh possessed.
Therefore, our responsibility is to transmit the position chosen by the
mashayikh. They are the followers of the school who undertook its explanation
and organization through their ijtihadi ability.

Allamah Qasim stated that the mujtahid scholars examined differing positions,
preferred one, and declared it sound. We must follow and act upon the position
that they preferred, just as we would have followed their fatwa had they issued
it during their lifetime.

*Fatawa Ibn Shalbi* states that a qadi or mufti may not depart from the Imam's
position unless one of the mashayikh explicitly states that the fatwa is
according to another position. If the other position has not been preferred and
the Imam's evidence is considered stronger, a judgment according to the other
position is not enforceable.

The same is mentioned in *Radd al-Muhtar* and *Manhat al-Khaliq*. The authors of
the texts of the school sometimes choose a position other than the Imam's. When
the mashayikh issued contrary to the Imam's position, it was because they found
the evidence for that position absent in their circumstances.

The sound view is that the mufti of our time transmits the fatwa chosen by the
mashayikh, for they possess greater knowledge. Allamah Ibn Shalbi stated that
the basic principle is to act according to the position of Abu Hanifah رضی الله
تعالی عنہ. The mashayikh generally prefer his evidence over the evidence of
those who oppose him and answer the opposing arguments.

Preference has the force of explicit verification. Therefore, the qadi or mufti
may depart from the Imam's position only when a mashayikh explicitly states that
the fatwa is according to another position.

Shaykh Ala al-Din al-Haskafi wrote at the beginning of *Sharh al-Tanwir* that
we must follow the positions that the mashayikh deemed weightier and sound,
just as we would have followed them had they issued fatwas during their lives.
If they transmit several positions without preference, one considers changes in
custom and people's circumstances, what is easier and more beneficial, what is
widely practiced, and what has the stronger basis.

Those who cannot distinguish between positions must refer to one who can do so.
And Allah Most High knows best.

### First Introduction: The Difference Between Transmitting a Position and Issuing a Fatwa

Transmitting a position and issuing a fatwa according to it are not the same.
We sometimes transmit positions outside our school, but no one assumes that we
are issuing fatwas according to them.

A fatwa is to rely upon a matter and tell the questioner: “In the case you have
asked about, this is the ruling of the Sacred Law.” No one may do this without
knowing the ruling through a proof of the Sacred Law. Otherwise it is speaking
without knowledge and attributing a falsehood to the Sacred Law.

Allah Most High says:

> Or do you say about Allah that which you do not know?

أم تقولون على الله ما لا تعلمون

And He says:

> Say (O Beloved): Has Allah permitted this for you, or do you forge a lie
> against Allah?

قل آلله أذن لكم أم على الله تفترون

### Second Introduction: Two Types of Evidence

Evidence is of two types: detailed evidence, whose knowledge is particular to
the people of investigation, and general evidence. Its explanation continues
