# Roman Urdu Translation Style Guide — Tawakkul

## Tone

- Translate into natural, respectful Roman Urdu prose.
- Preserve the tone of the author (Shaykh Faheem) — warm, practical, and reflective. Do not summarize or flatten.
- Preserve adab when referring to Allah, Huzoor ﷺ, the Prophets <sup>عليهم السلام</sup>, the Companions, and the righteous.
- **Preserve the author’s narrative voice.** Translate the author as though he is speaking directly in Roman Urdu. Do not introduce translator commentary such as `is kitab ka musannif`, `source ke mutabiq`, or `the source says` in the body. Where the English says “this writer,” “I,” or “we,” retain that first-person authorial voice naturally, e.g. `Main yeh maanta hoon` or `hum dekhte hain`. Keep source and citation explanations only in footnotes when they are genuinely part of the reference.

## Adab (per AGENTS.md)

1. **Death reference** — Never attribute death directly to the Prophet. Use "departed (from this world)", "passed away", or "was called back"; never "died" or "death of the Prophet".
2. **No diminishing phrasing** — Never use "no more than" for the Prophet.
3. **Preserve ﷺ** — Always keep the `ﷺ` symbol after the Prophet's name wherever it appears in the source.
4. **Vocative address** — Render `قُلْ` / `يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ` as "Say (O Beloved)" or "O Prophet".
5. **Elevated epithets** — Prefer honorific renderings consistent with Ahlus-Sunnah convention.
6. **Reference** — When uncertain, compare against `content/books/kanzul-iman-english.json`.

## Honorifics

- Use `ﷺ` after the Prophet's name.
- Use `<sup>عليه السلام</sup>` for Prophets and angels.
- Use `<sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>` for a male Companion.
- Use `<sup>عز وجل</sup>` after Allah where appropriate.
- For other names/figures referenced by the author (e.g. Umar ibn al-Khattab, Anas ibn Malik, Abdur Rahman ibn Awf), use the Roman Urdu renderings natural to the source context.

## Arabic preservation

- Keep Quranic verses in Arabic script exactly as printed in the source (correcting the garbled PDF extraction where it is recognisable).
- Do NOT romanize Arabic terms, Quranic phrases, or Islamic terms.
- When a transliteration line (e.g. "Bismillāhi Tawakkaltu Alallāh") appears in English-script Arabic, remove it — keep only the Arabic script, followed directly by the Roman Urdu meaning.

## Plain, easy language

- Prefer simple, everyday Roman Urdu words over heavy or technical ones.
  - `utarne`, not `ubtar-ne`
  - `ho jaye` / `hoga` / `ho kar rehta hai`, not `waqoo' mein aana`
  - `jo`, not `go` (jo ke)
  - `faisla karne wala`, not `Faisla-Kunanda`
- **Prefer Roman Urdu in the body text, but preserve very familiar English words when they are more natural or intuitive than a forced translation.** This especially applies to everyday technology and modern usage, such as `Wi-Fi`, `internet`, `online`, `network`, `router`, `company`, `program`, and `technology`. Less familiar or unnecessary English should still be replaced with simple Roman Urdu, and English glosses may be added in brackets where helpful, e.g.:
  - `deri`, not `delay`
  - `pahiye`, not `tyron`
  - `sarak par chalne ki salaahiyat (roadworthy)`
  - `zaahiri taur par`, not `naturally`
  - `khabar sunane wala (newsreader)`
  - `mitti khisakne (landslide)`
  - `naqsha-e-raah (roadmap)`, not `roadmap`
  - `karkhaanon ka bara daur`, not `sanat ka bara daur`
  - `pareshani`, not `stress`
- Where the source adds a parenthetical English gloss, keep a simple Roman Urdu gloss (or the English term if clearer) — e.g. `juraym ki sharah (crime rate)`.

## Footnotes

- Inline footnote markers are rendered as superscript numbers immediately after the word/sentence they belong to: `Shām<sup>2</sup>`.
- Footnote definitions are listed as a numbered list at the end of the section: `1. Jāmi At-Tirmizhī, Book 37, Hadīth 2707`.
- Never leave bare footnote numbers floating inside the paragraph text (e.g. `Shām 2 ki taraf`).

## Batch sizing

- Keep future chapter translations to a maximum of two substantial sub-batches whenever practical.
- Each sub-batch should cover a long, logically self-contained portion of the narrative rather than a short page range.
- Preserve continuity by checking the end of the first sub-batch before starting the second.
- Chapter Three currently has five short sub-batch files; do not repeat this structure for later chapters.

## Salawat glyph

- The PDF text layer renders the salawat as a standalone `k` (e.g. "the Prophet Muhammad k"). Convert all such occurrences to ﷺ.
